> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **결론 (thesis)**: 페니실린 알레르기 (Penicillin Allergy) 환자를 만났을 때 진짜 1차 문제는 알레르기가 아니라 **"알레르기 라벨"** 이고, 수십 년간의 반사적 대체약이던 클린다마이신 (Clindamycin)은 이제 틀린 답이다. 대책은 ① 라벨 평가 → ② 세팔로스포린 (Cephalosporin) 재검토 → ③ 마크로라이드 (Macrolide) → ④ 클린다마이신은 최후 수단, 이 순서다.
> - **라벨 ≠ 알레르기**: 인구의 약 10%가 페니실린 알레르기를 보고하나 (Ramsey 2022; 다른 보유 논문은 10–20%로 기재) 그 대다수는 진성 알레르기가 아니다 — **검사하면 80–99%가 알레르기 아님으로 확인**된다 (Salgado-Peralvo 2021). [확인]
> - **교차반응 통설은 틀렸다**: 페니실린–세팔로스포린 이중 알레르기는 메타분석 환자 6,147명 중 **0.7%**, 확진된 페니실린 알레르기에 한정해도 **3%** — 과거 교과서의 8–10%가 아니다 (Ramsey 2022). [확인] 단, 우리 보유 논문은 이 낮은 수치를 **곁사슬 (side-chain) 구조가 아니라 역학**(미확인 라벨·드문 세팔로스포린 알레르기)으로 설명한다 — "R1 곁사슬 때문"이라는 흔한 설명은 위키 근거가 없다. [확인]
> - **세팔로스포린 금기는 좁다**: 2021 미국심장협회 (American Heart Association, AHA) 성명은 세팔로스포린을 **페니실린/암피실린에 아나필락시스 (Anaphylaxis)·혈관부종 (Angioedema)·두드러기 (Urticaria) 병력이 있을 때만** 금기로 규정한다 (Wilson 2021). [확인] 그 외 미확인·저위험 라벨은 세파졸린 (Cefazolin)·세팔렉신 (Cephalexin)이 안전하다 — 기관별 전환 사업에서 세파졸린 사용 34%→80%+ 증가에도 과민반응 증가 0 (Ramsey 2022). [확인]
> - **클린다마이신의 몰락 ①(가이드라인)**: 2021 AHA는 감염성 심내막염 (Infective Endocarditis, IE) 예방의 **전 처방에서 클린다마이신을 삭제**했다 — 원문 그대로 *"Clindamycin is no longer recommended for antibiotic prophylaxis for a dental procedure."* 단회 예방 투여만으로도 클로스트리디오이데스 디피실 (C. difficile) 감염·사망이 발생하기 때문. [확인]
> - **클린다마이신의 몰락 ②(안전성)**: 영국 국민보건서비스 (NHS) 7년 처방 + 이상약물반응 (Adverse Drug Reaction, ADR) 연계 자료 — 클린다마이신 치명적 ADR **2.9/백만**(최고, 대부분 C. difficile 장염)으로 아목시실린 (Amoxicillin) 0.1/백만의 **약 29배** (Thornhill 2019). [확인]
> - **클린다마이신의 몰락 ③(임플란트 결과)**: 페니실린 알레르기 + 클린다마이신군의 조기 임플란트 실패가 **24.68% vs 8.03% (상대위험도 RR 3.84)**, 별도 코호트에서 **2.10% vs 0.80% (교차비 OR 3.10)**, 술후 감염 최대 **6배**, 즉시 임플란트는 **5.7–10배** (Salgado-Peralvo 2021 체계적 문헌고찰, 5편). [확인] **다만 5편 중 4편이 자가보고 페니실린 알레르기 (Self-Reported Penicillin Allergy, SRPA)** — 알레르기와 약제가 교란되어 있어 저자들도 "페니실린 알레르기 자체를 위험인자로 단정할 수 없다"고 명시한다. 즉 이 3–6배는 **약(클린다마이신)의 벌점이지 알레르기의 벌점이 아니다.** [확인]
> - **시술별 대체약** (스페인 임플란트학회 (Spanish Society of Implants, SEI) 합의, Salgado-Peralvo 2022): 통상 임플란트 식립 → 아지트로마이신 (Azithromycin) 500 mg 술전 1시간 (GRADE C); 즉시 임플란트 → 아지트로마이신/클라리트로마이신 (Clarithromycin)/메트로니다졸 (Metronidazole) 수일 요법 (GRADE D); 상악동 거상 → 시프로플록사신 (Ciprofloxacin) 500 mg/12h × 9일 (GRADE D, **상악동 세균총이 마크로라이드 내성이라 마크로라이드 부적합**); 보철 단계 → 항생제 불필요 (GRADE D). [확인]
> - **살아 있는 불일치 (진짜 모순)**: 위키 안에서 클린다마이신 권고가 갈린다 — 회피 진영(Wilson 2021, Thornhill 2019, Salgado-Peralvo 2021·2022) vs 여전히 1차 대체약으로 명시하는 진영(Segura-Egea 2017 유럽근관학회, Torof 2023, Mohammadpour 2025, Díaz 2025). **적응증별로 갈린다**: IE 예방에서는 삭제됐고, 구강외과·치료 목적에서는 아직 유효 약제로 남아 있다. 연도순 승계가 아니라 축이 다른 권고다. [확인]
> - **경험적 치료의 함정**: 알레르기 대체약은 내성 문제도 안는다 — 브라티슬라바 10년 자료에서 구강안면감염 분리균의 클린다마이신 내성 **25.8%**(환자기준 39%), 메트로니다졸 25.5%, 아목시실린-클라불란산 (Amoxicillin-Clavulanate) 4.9% (Vavro 2024) [확인]; 에리스로마이신 (Erythromycin)은 Prevotella 62%·Fusobacterium 66%로 커버리지 부족 (Chunduri 2012) [확인]. **예방(prophylaxis)에서 통하던 대체약이 치료(treatment)에서는 안 통할 수 있다.**
> - **예외 — 임신·B군 연쇄상구균 (Group B Streptococcus, GBS)**: 임신 중 GBS 예방의 정답은 **페니실린 G**이며 대체약이 열등하다 (Ramsey 2022). [확인] 그래서 가임기 여성의 페니실린 라벨은 치과가 아니라 산과에서 값을 치른다 — **라벨 해제 (Delabeling)의 가치가 가장 큰 집단**이다.
> - **한국 맥락**: 국내 3차병원 10년 배양자료에서 비리단스 연쇄상구균 (Viridans Streptococcus)의 페니실린 G 감수성은 68.4%→52.0%로 하락한 반면 3세대 세팔로스포린은 93.5–95.5%로 유지 (Kwon 2023) [확인] — 한국 환경에서 세팔로스포린으로의 이동은 알레르기 논리와 내성 논리 **양쪽에서** 지지된다. 단 **한국인 페니실린 알레르기 유병률·라벨 해제 데이터는 위키에 0편.** [확인]
> - **임상 즉시 적용 4룰**: ① 라벨을 병력으로 파고들어라(무슨 약, 언제, 어떤 반응, 이후 베타락탐 투여력) ② 미확인·저위험 라벨 = 세팔렉신/세파졸린이 1차 ③ 클린다마이신은 반사적 대체약이 아니라 최후 수단 ④ 진성 아나필락시스·혈관부종·두드러기 병력 또는 중증피부이상반응 (Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reaction, SCAR: 스티븐스-존슨증후군/독성표피괴사융해 (SJS/TEN), 약물반응호산구증가전신증상 (DRESS)) 병력이면 베타락탐 재노출 금지 + 알레르기내과 의뢰.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 17 wiki papers (2 consensus statements, 3 SR+MA, 3 SR, 3 narrative reviews, 4 retrospective/cross-sectional microbiology and pharmacoepidemiology datasets) on how dentistry should handle the penicillin-allergic patient: the operative problem is the **allergy label**, not the allergy — roughly 10% of the population carries one, 80–99% of self-reported labels are disproved on testing, and the reflex substitution the label triggers is now the larger hazard.

Two long-taught numbers are wrong or newly narrow: penicillin–cephalosporin cross-reactivity is 0.7% overall and 3% in confirmed penicillin allergy (not the historical 8–10%), and the 2021 AHA statement removed clindamycin from every infective-endocarditis prophylaxis regimen on single-dose *C. difficile* risk — a decision quantified by NHS data showing clindamycin's fatal-ADR rate of 2.9 per million (≈29× amoxicillin) and reinforced by implant cohorts where penicillin-allergic patients on clindamycin failed 3–4× more often, though that signal is confounded because 4 of 5 studies used unverified self-reported allergy.

The resulting countermeasure hierarchy is cephalosporin → macrolide → clindamycin only as last resort, applied procedure-by-procedure (IE prophylaxis, routine and immediate implant placement, sinus augmentation, guided bone regeneration, oral-surgical prophylaxis, therapeutic management of odontogenic infection, pregnancy/Group B Streptococcus); the wiki holds a genuine unresolved disagreement, since the endodontic and oral-surgical literature still names clindamycin as the first penicillin-allergy alternative while the cardiology, pharmacovigilance, and implant literature argues against it.

## 세줄요약

위키 17편(합의문 2·체계적문헌고찰+메타분석 3·체계적문헌고찰 3·내러티브리뷰 3·후향/단면 미생물·약물역학 자료 4)을 종합: 치과에서 페니실린 알레르기 환자의 실제 1차 문제는 알레르기가 아니라 **알레르기 라벨**이다 — 인구의 약 10%가 보유하나 자가보고 라벨의 80–99%는 검사에서 부정되며, 그 라벨이 촉발하는 반사적 대체 처방이 더 큰 위해가 되었다.

오래 가르쳐온 두 수치가 바뀌었다: 페니실린–세팔로스포린 교차반응은 전체 0.7%·확진 페니실린 알레르기에서 3%로 과거 8–10% 통설과 다르고, 2021 미국심장협회 성명은 단회 투여만으로도 클로스트리디오이데스 디피실 감염이 생긴다는 이유로 감염성 심내막염 예방의 전 처방에서 클린다마이신을 삭제했다 — 영국 NHS 자료의 클린다마이신 치명 이상반응 2.9/백만(아목시실린의 약 29배)과, 페니실린 알레르기 + 클린다마이신군의 조기 임플란트 실패 3–4배 신호가 이를 뒷받침하나 후자는 5편 중 4편이 미확진 자가보고 알레르기라 교란되어 있다.

따라서 대책의 위계는 세팔로스포린 → 마크로라이드 → 클린다마이신(최후 수단)이며 시술별로 달라진다(심내막염 예방·통상/즉시 임플란트·상악동 거상·골유도재생술·구강외과 예방·치성감염 치료·임신 및 B군 연쇄상구균); 위키 안에는 미해소 불일치가 실재한다 — 근관·구강외과 문헌은 여전히 클린다마이신을 1차 대체약으로 명시하는 반면 순환기·약물감시·임플란트 문헌은 이를 반대한다.

## Thesis

The penicillin-allergic dental patient is usually not a penicillin-allergic patient. Roughly one in ten people carries the label, and 80–99% of self-reported labels are disproved when tested — so the first countermeasure is to interrogate and, where possible, dissolve the label, not to reach for a substitute drug. When a substitute is genuinely needed, two pieces of long-taught doctrine have to be discarded: that penicillin–cephalosporin cross-reactivity is 8–10% (it is 0.7% overall, 3% in confirmed allergy), and that clindamycin is the natural fallback (it now carries the highest fatal-ADR rate among dental antibiotics, was struck from every 2021 AHA prophylaxis regimen, and travels with a 3–6× implant failure and infection signal). The evidence therefore reorders the hierarchy to **cephalosporin → macrolide → clindamycin as last resort** — but that reordering is uneven across the wiki, and the endodontic and oral-surgical literature has not yet followed it.

## 1. The Label Problem — the first countermeasure is not a drug

The penicillin allergy that dentistry actually manages is almost always a **historical annotation**, not a demonstrated IgE-mediated hypersensitivity. [확인] Ramsey 2022 puts the labelled prevalence at about 10% of the US general population and states plainly that the majority of retrospectively studied labelled patients are not truly allergic; [확인] the implant-side systematic review is more quantitative — 10–20% of patients report penicillin allergy and **80–99% are no longer allergic when tested** (Salgado-Peralvo 2021, source tier). The label is also socially sticky: it is entered once, rarely revisited, and thereafter silently redirects every antibiotic decision that patient will ever receive.

Three consequences follow, and they are the reason this page exists.

1. **The label, not the allergy, is what drives outcomes.** [확인] In the five studies pooled by [[drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2021-penicillin-allergy-risk-factor-early-implant-failure]], allergy status and antibiotic choice are perfectly confounded — every "allergic" patient received clindamycin, every non-allergic patient received amoxicillin — and allergy was verified by testing in only 1 of 5. The authors therefore refuse the obvious headline: *"it is not possible to state that penicillin allergy per se constitutes a risk factor for early dental implant failure."*
2. **The substitution is a real clinical exposure with its own harm profile.** That exposure is quantified in §3.
3. **The label is frequently attached to a decision that should not have produced a prescription at all.** [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/tampi-2019-antibiotics-pulpitis-apical-abscess-sr]] found pooled pain differences of MD ≈ 0.09–0.39 (all non-significant) for antibiotics in symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis with symptomatic apical periodontitis, and localised acute apical abscess; [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/teoh-2021-oral-antibiotic-empirical-acute-dentoalveolar-infections]] found that with drainage established, no-antibiotic management matched broad-spectrum; [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/momand-2024-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-implant-failure]] returned RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.30–1.47) and **NNT 143** for routine implant prophylaxis. **The cheapest penicillin-allergy countermeasure is to notice that no antibiotic was indicated.**

## 2. Cross-Reactivity Reappraisal — the 8–10% figure is wrong

The historical teaching that 8–10% of penicillin-allergic patients react to cephalosporins is the single belief most responsible for pushing dental patients onto clindamycin. [확인] Ramsey 2022 reports the modern meta-analytic figure: among 6,147 patients across 77 studies, penicillin–cephalosporin dual allergy was **0.7% overall**, rising to **3% among patients with a *confirmed* penicillin allergy**. Penicillin allergy was present in only 4.4% of patients who reacted to cefazolin.

The substitution has been tested at scale, not merely modelled. [확인] In institutional before/after programmes summarised by Ramsey: perioperative cefazolin use in penicillin-allergy-labelled patients rose from 34% to over 80% across 756 patients with **no recorded hypersensitivity reactions**; a Vancouver programme raised cefazolin +18% while cutting clindamycin −62% with no anaphylaxis or severe allergic reactions; and removing the EHR penicillin/cephalosporin cross-reactivity alert (Macy) increased cephalosporin prescribing without any increase in hypersensitivity. A retrospective series of 734 procedures in 690 labelled patients found **no difference in hypersensitivity reactions** whichever agent — clindamycin, vancomycin, or cefazolin — was used.

**A mechanistic correction the wiki can make.** [확인] The now-standard explanation for low cross-reactivity — shared R1 side-chain determinants rather than the shared beta-lactam ring — **is explicitly not the mechanism our source gives**: the wiki page records that the R1 side-chain concept is *not discussed* in Ramsey's review, which instead attributes the low figure to epidemiology (unverified labels plus genuinely rare cephalosporin allergy). [미검증] The side-chain account is standard allergy teaching, but no held paper states it, so this page does not assert it as evidence.

**Where the contraindication actually sits.** [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/wilson-2021-ie-prophylaxis-aha-scientific-statement]] draws the line narrowly: cephalosporins are contraindicated only in patients with a **history of anaphylaxis, angioedema, or urticaria** to penicillin or ampicillin. [확인] Ramsey adds the second absolute exclusion used in every cefazolin-substitution policy: any severe cutaneous adverse reaction (SJS/TEN, DRESS) — do not re-expose to any beta-lactam.

## 3. The Fall of Clindamycin — three independent lines converging

Clindamycin was the reflex penicillin-allergy substitute in dentistry for decades. Three separate literatures dismantled it between 2019 and 2022, and they do not share a data source — which is what makes the convergence persuasive.

**(a) Guideline removal.** [확인] The 2021 AHA scientific statement dropped clindamycin from **every** IE prophylaxis regimen, verbatim: *"Clindamycin is no longer recommended for antibiotic prophylaxis for a dental procedure."* The stated rationale is that a single prophylactic dose can cause *Clostridioides difficile* infection including death, and that clindamycin causes more frequent and more severe reactions than the other prophylaxis options. Penicillin-allergic patients are routed instead to **cephalexin 2 g, or azithromycin/clarithromycin 500 mg, or doxycycline 100 mg** orally; **cefazolin or ceftriaxone 1 g IM/IV** when unable to take oral medication.

**(b) Pharmacovigilance.** [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]] cross-linked 7 years of NHS England dental prescribing with MHRA Yellow Card reports: clindamycin's fatal ADR rate of **2.9 per million prescriptions** is the highest of any dentally prescribed antibiotic and roughly **29× amoxicillin's 0.1** — almost entirely *C. difficile* colitis, with the highest GI ADR rate as well (155.5/million). This is the evidence base underneath the AHA's decision.

**(c) Surgical outcomes.** [확인] [[drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2021-penicillin-allergy-risk-factor-early-implant-failure]] pooled five datasets: implant-level early failure **24.68% vs 8.03% (RR 3.84)** and patient-level 18.86% vs 5.17% (Salomó-Coll 2018, clindamycin in 100% of allergic patients); **2.10% vs 0.80% (OR 3.10)** with postoperative infection 3.40% vs 0.60% — *"six times higher"* (French 2016); immediate implants **10.50% vs 1%** (French 2016) and 8.52% vs 2.95% (Wagenberg & Froum 2006). [확인] The SEI consensus then converted this into a formal recommendation **against** reflex clindamycin substitution in implant surgery ([[drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2022-consensus-preventive-antibiotic-therapy-dental-implant]], PICO 6, GRADE C).

**State the confounding honestly.** [확인] The (c) line is the weakest of the three: 4 of 5 studies used self-reported, untested penicillin allergy, so "allergy" and "clindamycin" cannot be separated, and the review's own conclusion says so. Read correctly, the 3–6× signal is best interpreted as a **penalty attaching to the substitute drug and to the unexamined label**, not to allergy — which is precisely why delabeling (§5) is the intervention with the most leverage. The (a) and (b) lines do not depend on this confounded dataset at all.

**A fourth, quieter argument: the substitute is also microbiologically weaker.** [확인] In a 10-year Bratislava cohort of 744 hospitalised orofacial infections, clindamycin resistance was **25.8% per isolate / 39% per patient** and metronidazole 25.5%, against amoxicillin-clavulanate's 4.9% ([[drug/antibiotics/vavro-2024-antibiotic-susceptibility-orofacial-infections]]). [확인] Erythromycin fares worse still — Prevotella 62%, Fusobacterium 66% ([[drug/antibiotics/chunduri-2012-bacterial-spectrum-orofacial-infections]]) — which removes the oldest European allergy alternative from serious consideration for treating an established infection.

## 4. The Live Disagreement — clindamycin recommended vs clindamycin avoided

This is a genuine, unresolved conflict inside the wiki, and it should not be smoothed over.

| Position | Pages holding it | What they say |
|---|---|---|
| **Avoid clindamycin** | [[drug/antibiotics/wilson-2021-ie-prophylaxis-aha-scientific-statement]] · [[drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]] · [[drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2021-penicillin-allergy-risk-factor-early-implant-failure]] · [[drug/antibiotics/salgado-peralvo-2022-consensus-preventive-antibiotic-therapy-dental-implant]] | Removed from all IE regimens; highest fatal-ADR rate; 3–6× implant failure/infection; formal GRADE C recommendation against |
| **Clindamycin is the penicillin-allergy alternative** | [[drug/antibiotics/segura-egea-2017-antibiotics-in-endodontics-a-review]] · [[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]] · [[drug/antibiotics/mohammadpour-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-oral-surgery-sr]] · [[drug/antibiotics/diaz-2025-antibiotics-sinus-lift-infection-umbrella]] | ESE review names clindamycin for confirmed penicillin allergy; clindamycin 600 mg single dose for implants; "clindamycin reserved for penicillin allergy only" across OMFS; clindamycin 300–600 mg q6–8 h for sinus lift |

**The resolution is by axis, not by year.** [확인] The removal is *indication-specific*: the AHA's decision governs **antibiotic prophylaxis before a dental procedure in a cardiac-risk patient**, where a single dose buys a very small absolute benefit and any *C. difficile* death is therefore unacceptable arithmetic. It does not govern **therapeutic** dosing for an established spreading infection, where the anaerobic and Gram-positive coverage clindamycin provides is being bought for a real, immediate indication. The wiki's own stewardship overview already registers this split, noting clindamycin "remains valid" specifically in oral-surgery indications ([[overviews/drug-antibiotic-stewardship-overview]]).

Two caveats keep even that reading unstable. [확인] Salgado-Peralvo 2022 is a *prophylactic* recommendation and still rejects clindamycin, which puts it against Torof 2023 on the same axis (implant prophylaxis) — an unreconciled head-to-head. [확인] And Vavro 2024's 25.8% clindamycin resistance is a *therapeutic* datum, which erodes the very indication where clindamycin was supposed to remain safe. The honest statement is that **there is no held paper that adjudicates this**; the position this page takes is that clindamycin has fallen from "first alternative" to "last resort", with a residual therapeutic role for penicillin-allergic patients with established infection when culture data or local antibiogram supports it.

Note also that the pre-shift baseline was near-universal. [확인] In Segura-Egea's collation of European prescribing surveys, clindamycin was the named allergic alternative in the UK (2008), Turkey (2013), Lithuania (2010), Spain (2010), and Croatia (2015); erythromycin in Belgium and earlier UK surveys; metronidazole in one. Not one survey named a cephalosporin. The shift documented on this page is recent, is not yet reflected in the endodontic literature we hold, and will take years to reach chairside habit.

## 6. The Delabeling Pathway

Delabeling is the intervention with the highest leverage, because it removes the problem rather than routing around it.

**Step 1 — take the history properly.** [확인] Ramsey's own data show why: after a perioperative anaphylaxis event, history alone identifies the culprit agent in only ~18% of patients, and the agent cannot be identified at all in 28–50%. A label that says "penicillin allergy" and nothing else is, statistically, an unexamined claim. The clinically decisive questions are which drug, how long ago, what reaction (cutaneous only vs airway/hypotension vs blistering/mucosal), and whether any beta-lactam has been tolerated since.

**Step 2 — risk-stratify.** [확인] Three tiers emerge from the held sources:

| Tier | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low-risk, unverified | Remote, cutaneous-only, no anaphylaxis/angioedema/urticaria, no SCAR | Cephalosporin (cephalexin PO / cefazolin IM-IV) is appropriate first-line; cross-reactivity well under 1% |
| Confirmed or high-risk unverified | Test-positive, or history of anaphylaxis/angioedema/urticaria | ~3% cross-reactivity — allergist consultation before a cephalosporin; use a macrolide meanwhile |
| Any SCAR history | SJS/TEN, DRESS | **No beta-lactam re-exposure of any kind** — the universal exclusion in every substitution policy |

**Step 3 — refer or challenge.** [확인] Pre-operative evaluation delabels most low-risk patients: Savic delabelled 55 of 56; a Canadian programme performed oral challenge in 146 of 190 patients, after which 77% received cefazolin, saving a mean 22 minutes of operating-room time per case where vancomycin was avoided. [확인] After any perioperative or peripartum anaphylaxis, allergy/immunology referral is strongly advised — 45 of 47 MGH patients tolerated future anaesthesia after formal evaluation.

**Step 4 — expect resistance from clinicians, not just from bacteria.** [확인] In a UK survey of 4,798 anaesthesiologists, only 40% would administer penicillin to a patient with a low-risk reaction history, and only 47% would administer it to a patient **already delabelled by an allergist**. The label survives its own disproof. Any dental delabeling effort must anticipate that a corrected record will still be treated as an allergy by the next clinician who reads it.

**Why this matters most in pregnancy.** [확인] Penicillin G is the preferred agent for Group B Streptococcus prophylaxis and the benefit outweighs the low anaphylaxis risk — so a woman of childbearing age carrying an unexamined penicillin label will pay for it in obstetrics, not in the dental chair. A dental visit is a realistic opportunity to notice the label and route her to evaluation years before term.

## 7. Korean Clinical Context

[확인] The wiki holds one Korean dataset directly relevant here: [[drug/antibiotics/kwon-2023-microbial-isolates-antibiotic-sensitivity-odontogenic]], a 10-year tertiary-centre review of 192 hospitalised odontogenic fascial-space abscess patients (302 isolates, Dankook University Hospital). Viridans streptococcus dominated (51.7%, rising 41.8% → 60.9% over the decade) and its **penicillin G sensitivity fell from 68.4% to 52.0%**, while third-generation cephalosporin sensitivity held at **cefotaxime 93.5% / ceftriaxone 95.5%**. The authors conclude penicillin should not be the first-line empirical choice for fascial-space abscess in this population.

The practical reading for a Korean clinic: **the pivot toward cephalosporins is supported here on two independent axes at once** — the allergy axis (cross-reactivity is far lower than taught, so cephalosporins are usually available even to labelled patients) and the resistance axis (cephalosporin sensitivity is holding locally while penicillin sensitivity erodes). Those axes are unrelated in origin, which makes their agreement worth acting on.

What the wiki does **not** hold, and what should not be improvised:

- [확인] **No Korean penicillin-allergy prevalence or delabeling data.** The ~10% labelled figure is US; the 80–99% disproved-on-testing figure is drawn from the international implant literature. Whether Korean self-reported penicillin allergy behaves the same way is unmeasured in anything we hold.
- [미검증] **Access to allergy testing / oral challenge from a dental setting in Korea** is not addressed by any held paper. The delabeling pathway in §6 is built entirely from surgical, anaesthetic, and obstetric programmes abroad; it assumes an allergy/immunology service the referring dentist can actually reach, and this page cannot say how available that is domestically.
- [미검증] No held paper describes Korean professional-body guidance (대한치과의사협회, 질병관리청 or equivalent) on penicillin-allergy antibiotic selection in dentistry. The AHA and SEI documents above are the wiki's only guideline-level sources on this question.

One transferable point does hold regardless of country. [확인] [[local-anesthesia/cherrez-ojeda-2024-knowledge-attitudes-anaphylaxis-local-anesthetics]] (Latin America, n=480) found that only 43.5% of dentists stocked epinephrine, 50.1% knew the intramuscular route, and 9.6% felt confident managing an anaphylactic reaction — with an *inverse* age gradient, so senior clinicians scored worse. Any policy that moves more penicillin-allergy-labelled patients back onto beta-lactams (correctly, on the evidence above) has to be paired with a stocked emergency kit and current anaphylaxis training, because it slightly raises the frequency of the rare event the office must be able to survive.

## Evidence Gaps

1. **No RCT anywhere in the wiki compares alternative antibiotics on clinical dental outcomes in penicillin-allergic patients.** Every comparative number on this page — implant failure, infection, prophylaxis efficacy in the allergic subgroup — comes from observational cohorts in which allergy status and drug choice are perfectly confounded. The trial that would settle it (azithromycin vs cephalexin vs clindamycin in labelled patients, with allergy verification) does not exist in anything we hold.
2. **Salgado-Peralvo 2021's confounding is unresolved and probably unresolvable retrospectively.** 4 of 5 studies used self-reported allergy; allergy was verified in 1. The 3–6× signal cannot be apportioned between the drug and the label without prospective verification.
3. **No Korean-population data on penicillin allergy at all** — no prevalence, no delabeling yield, no dental-specific antibiotic-selection guidance from a Korean professional body. Only local microbiology (Kwon 2023) is held.
4. **Delabeling infrastructure in dentistry is entirely unstudied in our sources.** Every delabeling programme cited is anaesthetic, surgical, or obstetric. Whether a dental practice can initiate risk stratification, refer effectively, or run any form of direct challenge — and at what cost and yield — is not addressed by any held paper.
5. **The sinus-lift allergy recommendation is internally contradictory in the wiki** (ciprofloxacin per SEI 2022 vs clindamycin per Díaz 2025), and neither rests on outcome data in penicillin-allergic sinus-lift patients. A paper measuring infection outcomes by agent in this subgroup would resolve a real chairside question.
6. **The mechanistic account of low cross-reactivity is missing.** Our only cross-reactivity source attributes the 0.7% figure to epidemiology and does not discuss side-chain determinants; an allergy-immunology primary source would let the wiki state *why* cephalosporins are usually safe, not merely *that* they are.
7. **Doxycycline appears in the AHA regimen but has no dental-outcome evidence in the wiki.** It is listed as an oral IE-prophylaxis alternative by Wilson 2021, yet the sinus-augmentation overview separately found no held source supporting doxycycline as a penicillin-allergy substitute in dental surgery — an unexplained asymmetry worth an ingest.
