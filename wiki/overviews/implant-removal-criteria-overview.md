> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 이분법 먼저**: "실패 중(Failing) — 치료한다" vs "실패(Failed) — 뽑는다". 이 분류가 치료계획의 출발점 (Hong 2024 플로우차트).
> - **절대 발거 기준 (Hard Criteria)**: ① 임플란트 동요(Mobility) — 골유착 (Osseointegration) 완전 소실, 즉각 발거; ② 임플란트 골절 (Implant Fracture) — 제거·대체 외 선택지 없음; ③ 골소실 ≥⅔ 임플란트 길이 AND/OR 동요 — Cafferata 2025 PI-X 군 포함 기준, 임상에서 널리 준용.
> - **상대 발거 기준 (Soft Criteria)**: 치료 불응성 (Treatment-Refractory) 임플란트주위염 — 비외과·외과 치료 후에도 진행성 골소실·화농 지속 시 "발거 고려" (Hong 2024 플로우차트 종착 노드).
> - **위험 증폭 인자** (Zhou 2026, n=406): 구치부(Molar Site) (OR=3.21), 기저 화농 (Baseline Suppuration) (OR=4.51), 피개의치 (Overdenture) (OR=3.59) — 이 인자 3개가 겹치면 수술 후에도 임플란트 소실 위험이 수 배 상승.
> - **치료단계 계층 (Korean Academy consensus, Kim 2024)**: 점막염(Mucositis) → 비외과 → 외과 → **발거+결손 재건** 순; 각 단계 실패 시 다음 단계로.
> - **임플란트주위염 진단 기준** (EFP 기준, Cafferata 2025 적용): 탐침출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BOP) + 화농 + 탐침깊이 (Probing Depth, PD) ≥6 mm + 변연골소실 (Marginal Bone Loss, MBL) ≥3 mm → 발거를 고려하는 상한 기준의 출발점.
> - **임플란트 골절·보철 부적합**: 전체 실패의 10~13% 차지(Solderer 2019, Shenoy 2025); 주위염과 달리 내과적 치료 없이 즉각 제거 대상.
> - **주위염이 후기 발거 원인의 81–83%** (Solderer 2019, Shenoy 2025) — 비외과·외과 치료에도 불구하고 주위염이 진행되면 결국 발거 쪽으로 귀결.
> - **근거 공백 (Evidence Gap)**: "발거 기준"을 전향적으로 검증한 SR·RCT가 **없다**. 골소실 ⅔ 기준은 연구 포함 기준에서 역용(Cafferata 2025)된 것이지, 무작위 연구에서 도출된 역치가 아님. 임상에서는 골소실 양보다 치료 반응성 부재가 더 실제적 결정 트리거.
> - **발거 후 재식립**: 생존율 71–100% (2차 시도, Solderer 2019) — 발거 자체가 예후 불량을 뜻하지 않음; 부위 준비·임플란트 직경 선택이 관건.
> - **기법 선택은 별도 오버뷰**: 역토크·trephine·piezo 선택 알고리즘은 [[overviews/failed-implant-removal-sinus-retrieval-overview]] 참조.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 6 papers on when to remove a dental implant: the fundamental clinical binary is "failing" (treat aggressively) vs "failed" (extract), with hard removal criteria including mobility (complete loss of osseointegration), implant fracture, and bone loss ≥⅔ implant length and/or mobility (PI-X criterion, Cafferata 2025), and soft criteria encompassing treatment-refractory peri-implantitis that persists despite stepwise non-surgical and surgical intervention (Hong 2024 flowchart endpoint).

Peri-implantitis accounts for 81.9–82.9% of late implant removal (Solderer 2019; Shenoy 2025), and high-risk site factors — molar location (OR=3.21), baseline suppuration (OR=4.51), and overdenture prosthetics (OR=3.59) — predict implant loss independently of surgical protocol (Zhou 2026, n=406 implants); the Korean Academy of Periodontology consensus (Kim 2024) codifies removal + defect reconstruction as the final tier when lower-level treatments fail.

A critical evidence gap exists: no prospective SR or RCT has validated a precise bone-loss threshold mandating removal; the ⅔-length criterion is derived from study inclusion criteria (Cafferata 2025), not outcome trials, and treatment non-response is clinically the more actionable removal trigger than a radiographic threshold alone; post-removal reimplantation survives at 71–100% when the site is correctly prepared (Solderer 2019).

## 세줄요약

6편 종합: 임플란트 발거 결정의 핵심 이분법은 "실패 진행 중(Failing, 적극치료)" vs "실패(Failed, 발거"이며, **절대 발거 기준**은 동요도 (Mobility, 골유착 완전 소실), 임플란트 골절(Implant Fracture), 골소실(Bone Loss) ≥⅔ 임플란트 길이 AND/OR 동요 (PI-X 기준, Cafferata 2025); **상대 발거 기준**은 비외과·외과 치료에도 지속되는 치료 불응성 임플란트주위염 (Hong 2024 플로우차트 종착점).

임플란트주위염이 후기 발거 원인의 81.9–82.9%를 차지하며 (Solderer 2019, Shenoy 2025), 구치부 위치 (OR=3.21)·기저 화농 (OR=4.51)·피개의치 (OR=3.59)가 수술 프로토콜과 무관하게 임플란트 소실을 독립 예측 (Zhou 2026, n=406개); 한국치주과학회 합의문 (Kim 2024)은 발거+결손 재건을 단계적 치료 계층의 최종 단계로 명시.

핵심 근거 공백: 발거를 의무화하는 정밀한 골소실 역치를 전향적으로 검증한 SR·RCT가 없다 — ⅔ 기준은 연구 포함 기준(Cafferata 2025)에서 역용된 것이지 결과 연구 도출값이 아니므로, 임상에서는 방사선학적 역치보다 **치료 무반응성**이 더 현실적 발거 결정 트리거; 발거 후 재식립 생존율은 71–100% (Solderer 2019).

## Thesis

**Clinical question:** When is a dental implant definitively beyond salvage, and what criteria — absolute versus contextual — justify removal?

The decision to remove an implant sits at the end of a treatment cascade. Unlike natural teeth, where periodontal severity scores (e.g., stage IV, grade C) have been formalized into extraction indications, implant removal lacks an equivalent validated threshold system. This overview synthesizes what our held papers do and do not establish, and where the field's evidence ends.

---

## 1. The Fundamental Binary: Failing vs Failed

The starting point for any removal decision is the **failed vs failing classification** (Hong 2024, [[implants/peri-implantitis/hong-2024-peri-implantitis-treatment-protocol-flowchart]]):

| Status | Definition | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Failing (실패 진행 중)** | Peri-implantitis present but osseointegration partially intact; no mobility; still treatment-responsive | Stepwise non-surgical → surgical; re-evaluate at each phase |
| **Failed (실패)** | Complete or near-complete loss of osseointegration; mobility; or refractory to all treatment tiers | Extraction |

This binary is the first node in the Hong 2024 treatment protocol flowchart. A failing implant enters the treatment cascade; a failed implant does not — extraction is the index decision, not a fallback.

---

## 2. Hard Criteria for Removal (Absolute Indications)

Three scenarios mandate removal without further salvage attempts:

### 2.1 Mobility (Complete Loss of Osseointegration)

Clinical mobility of the implant body — not the abutment or prosthesis — signals complete osseointegration failure. No surface decontamination or regenerative procedure can restore osseointegration to a mobile implant. This is the clearest, universally accepted absolute indication; it appears implicitly throughout all reviewed papers as the endpoint defining "failed."

### 2.2 Implant Fracture

Implant body fracture (distinct from abutment fracture) accounts for 0.4–1% of implant failures (Solderer 2019, [[implants/survival/solderer-2019-removal-failed-dental-implants-revisited]]). It is a structural failure — no biological treatment applies. The fractured segment must be retrieved, which itself requires technique selection (CTRT typically insufficient; trephine or piezo required). See [[overviews/failed-implant-removal-sinus-retrieval-overview]] for technique details.

### 2.3 Bone Loss ≥⅔ Implant Length and/or Mobility — the PI-X Criterion

Cafferata 2025 ([[implants/peri-implantitis/cafferata-2025-impaired-treg-mediated-immune-regulation]]) enrolled an explantation group (PI-X, n=23) defined by:

> **Bone loss ≥⅔ implant length AND/OR mobility**

This threshold appears in a histological study as an inclusion criterion — it is the bone-loss level at which a Frankfurt Goethe University tertiary center decides explantation is indicated. It represents current expert practice, not a threshold derived from a prospective outcome study (see §5, Evidence Gap). Numerically, for a 10 mm implant, ≥⅔ corresponds to ≥6.67 mm of bone loss — near-complete loss of periimplant bony support.

| Criterion | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mobility | Any clinical mobility of implant body | Universal; implied in all papers |
| Bone loss | ≥⅔ implant length | Cafferata 2025 PI-X inclusion criterion |
| Implant fracture | Any fracture of implant body | Solderer 2019; Shenoy 2025 |

---

## 3. Soft Criteria — Treatment-Refractory Peri-implantitis

The more common clinical scenario is an implant without mobility, with moderate-to-severe peri-implantitis that does not respond to treatment. The Hong 2024 flowchart ([[implants/peri-implantitis/hong-2024-peri-implantitis-treatment-protocol-flowchart]]) provides the decision pathway:

```
Non-surgical debridement ± antibiotics
        ↓ re-evaluate
  Success? → SPT
  Persistent? ↓
Defect-morphology-guided surgery
        ↓ re-evaluate
  Success? → SPT
  Persistent? → Consider extraction
```

**Treatment success** at each re-evaluation node is defined as: PPD ≤5 mm + no BOP + no suppuration + no progressive bone loss. Failure to meet these criteria after appropriate surgical treatment is the operational definition of "treatment refractory," which triggers extraction consideration.

The Kim 2024 Korean Academy of Periodontology consensus ([[implants/peri-implantitis/kim-2024-peri-implant-diseases-consensus-korean-academy-periodontology]]) formalizes this as a tiered hierarchy:

| Tier | Intervention |
|---|---|
| 1 | Non-surgical debridement (mucositis → peri-implantitis) |
| 2 | Mechanical/chemical debridement ± surgical access |
| 3 | Surgical: resective / reconstructive |
| 4 | **Implant removal + defect reconstruction** |

Tier 4 is not a rescue — it is the definitive end of the cascade for implants that have passed through tiers 1–3 without resolution.

---

## 4. Risk Factors That Accelerate Toward Removal

Zhou 2026 ([[implants/peri-implantitis/zhou-2026-surgical-protocols-peri-implantitis-cohort]]) identified independent predictors of implant loss in a real-world cohort (n=406 implants) undergoing peri-implantitis surgery:

| Risk Factor | OR for Implant Loss | p |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline suppuration | **4.51** | 0.002 |
| Molar site | **3.21** | 0.03 |
| Pre- + post-op antibiotics* | 3.13 | 0.02 |

*The antibiotic association reflects confounding by indication — antibiotics were preferentially prescribed in more complex, suppurative cases.

An implant with baseline suppuration at a molar site, already treated surgically, faces dramatically elevated loss probability. These factors do not themselves mandate removal but should calibrate the threshold for escalating to the next treatment tier or shortening re-evaluation intervals.

Additionally, Zhou 2026 demonstrates **treatment success declines sharply over time** — from 54.7% at 1 year to approximately 27–42% at 5 years. Chronicity of disease is a further argument for earlier removal decisions in high-risk sites rather than repeated re-treatment.

Solderer 2019 adds epidemiological context on *why* implants reach explantation:

| Failure cause | Proportion |
|---|---|
| Peri-implantitis (late failure) | 81.9–82.9% |
| Prosthetically inadequate malposition | ~10% |
| Inexperienced surgeon | 12.2% vs 2.4% experienced |
| Implant fracture | 0.4–1% |

Peri-implantitis overwhelmingly dominates late removal. Malposition and inexperienced-surgeon failure are preventable — they require different upstream interventions, not removal criteria per se.

---

## 5. Evidence Gap: The Missing SR on Removal Thresholds

No systematic review or randomized controlled trial has prospectively validated a specific bone-loss threshold mandating dental implant removal.

The ⅔-length criterion (§2.3) is derived from a study's *inclusion criterion* — it describes who a tertiary center already classified as needing explantation, not a threshold shown to improve outcomes versus treating to a lower bone-loss level. The following comparison illustrates where the evidence stops:

| Evidence type | What we have | What we lack |
|---|---|---|
| Radiographic threshold (bone loss) | ⅔ implant length used in PI-X study inclusion (Cafferata 2025) — tertiary expert practice, not RCT-derived | Prospective SR/RCT showing bone loss X mm → better outcomes with removal vs continued treatment |
| Mobility | Universal agreement; implied in all papers | RCT not needed (mobility = no osseointegration = treatment irrelevant) |
| Treatment non-response | Operationalized in flowchart (Hong 2024): PPD >5 mm or BOP or suppuration or progressive bone loss after surgical treatment | No trial of "remove immediately vs re-treat once more" |
| Time-to-decision | No evidence | How many surgical re-treatments before declaring refractory? |

**Clinical implication:** In the absence of hard radiographic thresholds, the most defensible removal indication is **documented treatment non-response** after appropriate stepwise care — not a bone-loss number alone. The bone-loss threshold serves as a risk-stratification signal to set expectations, not as a standalone trigger.

---

## 6. Special Scenario: Non-Peri-implantitis Indications

A minority of explantations occur for non-infectious reasons (Solderer 2019):

- **Malposition** (~10% of failures): prosthetically untenable placement; may be discovered immediately after loading or years later as restorative requirements change.
- **Patient preference or systemic change**: implant retained in a patient who subsequently develops a contraindication (e.g., planned bisphosphonate therapy, radiation field change); removal decisions governed by the specific systemic risk, not peri-implantitis criteria.
- **Implant fracture**: structural; see §2.2.

These scenarios have no treatment cascade — once the indication is identified, removal proceeds directly.

---

## 7. After Removal: Reimplantation Expectations

Removal is not the end of the clinical story. Solderer 2019 reports:

| Attempt | Survival range |
|---|---|
| 2nd implant (previously failed site) | 71–100% |
| 3rd implant (twice-failed site) | 50–100% |
| Overall success (few reported) | 90–100% |

Key principles for reimplantation success (Solderer 2019):
- Prefer **larger diameter, rough-surface** replacement implant for primary stability
- **Immediate vs delayed** reimplantation equivalent given adequate bone and no residual infection
- Extended defects need **buccal-plate GBR** before reimplantation
- "Cluster effect" (simultaneous multi-implant failure) hints at systemic/genetic factors to control before re-surgery

Technique selection for the removal itself — counter-torque ratchet (CTRT), trephine, piezosurgery — is covered separately in [[overviews/failed-implant-removal-sinus-retrieval-overview]].

## 8. Decision Framework Summary

```
IMPLANT WITH SUSPECTED FAILURE
        ↓
[1] MOBILITY? ─── YES ──► FAILED → Extract immediately
        │ NO
        ↓
[2] FRACTURE? ─── YES ──► FAILED → Extract (technique per site)
        │ NO
        ↓
[3] BONE LOSS ≥⅔ IMPLANT LENGTH? ── YES ──► Strong indication; discuss with patient
        │ NO/UNCLEAR                         Consider extract vs one more surgical attempt
        ↓
[4] PERI-IMPLANTITIS DIAGNOSIS?
   (BOP/SUPP + PD ≥6 mm + MBL ≥3 mm) ── NO ──► Monitor + reinforce hygiene
        │ YES
        ↓
[5] NON-SURGICAL → re-evaluate (PPD ≤5 / no BOP / no SUPP / no progressive MBL)
        │ PERSISTENT
        ↓
[6] SURGICAL (defect-morphology guided) → re-evaluate
        │ PERSISTENT = TREATMENT REFRACTORY
        ↓
[7] CONSIDER EXTRACTION
    ↑ weight: molar site / suppuration / overdenture (Zhou 2026 ORs)
    ↑ weight: long disease chronicity / successive surgical failures
```
