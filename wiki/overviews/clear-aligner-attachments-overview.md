> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 어태치먼트(Composite Attachment)는 "있느냐 없느냐"가 가장 큰 변수다. 유무 비교에서는 이동 발현·장치 유지력이 일관되게 개선되지만(Jedlinski 2023 SR 26편·Nucera 2022 SR 임상 5편), **어떤 형태의 어태치먼트냐**(최적화형 vs 재래형)는 임상 정확도 차이로 아직 입증되지 않았다(Muthuswamy Pandian 2025 SR+MA)
> - **이동유형별 기여**: 전치 치근 토크(Root Torque)·회전(Rotation)·근원심 이동·후방 고정원(Anchorage)에서 이득이 가장 뚜렷, 압하(Intrusion)는 근거 약함, 정출(Extrusion)·후방 협설 확장은 당시 연구 없음(Nucera 2022)
> - **정출의 전제조건**: 어태치먼트 없이는 8개 마진 디자인 **전부**가 계획과 반대인 압하력을 냄 — 정출에서 어태치먼트는 "최적화 옵션"이 아니라 **필수 조건**(Baik 2024, in-vitro)
> - **최적화형 vs 재래형**: 견치 재회전 정확도 61.2%(재래) vs 71.5%(최적화), SMD −0.31, p=0.06; 전치 정출 57.5% vs 62.4%, p=0.60 — **둘 다 ClinCheck 예측량 미달**. 회전 전용 SR(Benedetti 2025)도 12편 중 11편이 어태치먼트를 썼지만 효과 "비일관"
> - **형태·크기(FEA)**: 단일 견치 유한요소해석(Finite Element Analysis, FEA)에서 **같은 형태라면 작을수록 변위 증가**; 사각-베벨형은 회전, 삼각-베벨형은 경사이동(Tipping), 최소 power ridge(0.5×0.5×5 mm)는 토크에 유리(Pede 2024)
> - **대구치 원심이동(Distalization)**: 협측+구개측 대합 반타원형(opposed semi-elliptical)이 가장 평행한 이동을 만들지만 이동량은 타 디자인의 ~75%; 확장 동시 적용 시 경사·회전 2–3배 증폭; **완전맹출 제3대구치**는 제2대구치 원심 변위를 10–22%(최대 총변위 17%) 저해(Kuguoglu 2024, 단일모델 FEA)
> - **재료**: 일반 플로어블 vs 벌크필 플로어블은 임상적으로 **동등**(split-mouth RCT, 800개·140일, 탈락률 18.75%, HR 0.84, p=0.293) — 구치부 탈락이 소구치보다 많음(Fialho 2026). 실험실 근거는 필러가 많을수록 결합강도·전달 정확도↑(Jedlinski 2023; 필러 ~72% 이상에서는 추가 이득 없음), 나노하이브리드/고점도 레진이 기계·마모 저항 우수(Frias 2026)
> - **전달 템플릿(Transfer Tray)**: 단단하고 두꺼운 템플릿일수록 전달 정확도↑, 대신 제거가 어려워 첫 접착 시 탈락 위험 — 정확도 vs 탈락의 **trade-off**(Jedlinski 2023, 원문 Discussion)
> - **보철물 위 접착**: 리튬 디실리케이트·지르코니아에서는 불산(Hydrofluoric Acid, HFA) 에칭 + 패커블 레진(Filtek Z350)이 최고 전단결합강도(Shear Bond Strength, SBS)(Kaur 2026, in-vitro 9편)
> - **근거 수준의 경고**: 이동 관련 근거의 다수가 FEA·in-vitro이고(Jedlinski 26편 중 FEA 11·in-vitro 8), FEA 2편은 **단일 모델·단일 힘**이다. 임상 RCT는 재료 생존(Fialho)과 최적화형 비교 내 1편뿐 — 설계 권고는 "방향성"으로만 쓸 것
> - **긴장 지점**: ① 실험실은 고필러/고점도 레진을 지지하나 임상 RCT는 플로어블 두 종만 비교(고점도 vs 플로어블 임상 RCT 미보유) ② FEA는 "작을수록 변위↑"인데 Muthuswamy 포함 1편은 "크고 모서리 날카로운 재래형이 측절치 정출에 더 정확" — 이동유형·연구설계가 달라 직접 모순은 아님

## Three-line Summary

This overview synthesizes the held evidence on clear-aligner composite attachments — six attachment-dedicated papers (2 SRs, 1 SR+MA, 2 single-model FEA studies, 1 split-mouth RCT) plus seven supporting papers on attachment-dependent extrusion, rotation accuracy, resin/substrate bonding, FEA mechanics, attachment-margin demineralization, and scan fidelity. The consistent signal is that attachment *presence* improves movement expression and retention (and is a precondition for extrusion), while attachment *type* (optimized vs conventional) has not shown a clinical accuracy advantage, and neither type reaches the planned movement. Material choice between conventional and bulk-fill flowable composites does not change clinical survival (18.75% overall failure over 140 days, molars worse), but design and material recommendations above that level rest mostly on in-vitro and single-model FEA evidence.

## 세줄요약

투명교정 어태치먼트(Composite Attachment) 근거를 종합 — 어태치먼트 전용 6편(SR 2·SR+MA 1·단일모델 FEA 2·split-mouth RCT 1)에 정출·회전 정확도·레진/보철물 접착·FEA 기전·어태치먼트 주위 탈회·스캔 정확도 관련 보조 7편을 더했다. 일관된 신호는 어태치먼트의 **존재**가 이동 발현과 유지력을 높이고 정출의 전제조건이라는 것이며, **종류**(최적화형 vs 재래형)는 임상 정확도 우위가 없고 둘 다 계획 이동량에 못 미친다. 일반 vs 벌크필 플로어블 레진은 임상 생존율이 같지만(140일 탈락률 18.75%, 구치부 불리), 그 이상의 설계·재료 권고는 대부분 in-vitro·단일모델 FEA에 기대고 있다.

## 2. Presence vs Type — the Central Distinction

The held evidence answers two different questions, and conflating them produces an apparent contradiction.

**Question A — attachment vs no attachment.** Every held source that asks this finds a benefit. Jedlinski 2023 reports that all included studies showed better control and a larger range of movement with attachments, including on teeth serving only as anchorage. Nucera 2022 (5 clinical trials) localizes the benefit to anterior root torque, rotation, mesiodistal movement and posterior anchorage. Baik 2024 shows the extreme case: for premolar extrusion, **all eight margin designs produced an intrusive force without an attachment** — the aligner pushed the tooth the wrong way. With an attachment present, an extended margin gave a median +0.96 N extrusive force.

**Question B — which attachment.** Here the signal disappears. Muthuswamy Pandian 2025 pooled optimized (SmartForce) vs conventional attachments: canine derotation 71.5% vs 61.2% accuracy (SMD −0.31, p=0.06, I²=0%, GRADE low) and anterior extrusion 62.4% vs 57.5% (SMD 0.14, p=0.60, I²=76%). Incisor intrusion (one study) reached only 33–40% accuracy regardless of attachment. Benedetti 2025's rotation SR found attachments in 11 of 12 studies but described their effectiveness as inconsistent, with no controlled comparison confirming optimized-over-conventional superiority.

**Reading them together:** the benefit is carried by having an attachment at all, not (on current evidence) by the commercial refinement of its geometry. This is consistent with Cao 2025's FEA synthesis, in which attachments work by rebalancing crown-vs-root stress to convert tipping into translation — a mechanism any adequately engaged attachment provides. A non-significant p=0.06 on derotation with a 10-point accuracy gap is an underpowered result, not proof of equivalence; two pooled studies cannot settle it.

Displacement in Pede 2024 was greatest incisally and least at the root in every model — i.e., even with attachments the FEA predicts crown-dominant movement, which matches the clinical under-expression of root-level movements.

## 4. Molar Distalization

Kuguoglu 2024 is the only held paper that compares attachment geometries head-to-head for distalization (single adult maxilla; 0.25 mm distalization, ±0.50 mm expansion):

- **Opposed (buccal + palatal) semi-elliptical** attachments gave the most parallel second-molar movement (least tipping/rotation) but only ~75% of the displacement of the vertical rectangular or buccal-only semi-elliptical designs — a control-vs-magnitude trade-off.
- **Adding expansion** increased distal/buccal displacement 2–3 fold but increased tipping/rotation proportionally, most with the opposed design, which also had the highest stress of the six models.
- **A fully erupted third molar** reduced distal crown displacement by 10–22% (17% of maximum total displacement) and increased distopalatal rotation.

Clinical translation for Class II distalization planning (see axis 2 of [[overviews/clear-aligner-indications-limitations]]): choose a palatal-component attachment when bodily control matters more than speed; consider staging expansion and distalization sequentially; and account for erupted third molars before committing to an aligner-distalization timeline. All three points rest on one computational model without clinical validation.

## 5. Material and Bonding

**Resin choice.** Fialho 2026 is the only held clinical RCT on attachment material: conventional flowable (Filtek Supreme Flowable) vs bulk-fill flowable (Filtek Bulk Fill Flowable), split-mouth, 800 posterior attachments, Invisalign SmartTrack, 140 days. Survival was 83.5% vs 80.5% (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.60–1.16, p=0.293); overall failure 18.75%; molars failed more often than premolars in both groups. Regular flowable is therefore an adequate, cheaper choice *relative to bulk-fill flowable*.

The laboratory literature points in a partly different direction. Jedlinski 2023 (bonding studies, re-read from the PDF) found that more filler gives better shear bond strength and transfer accuracy, with no significant further gain above ~72% filler, and recommended flowable or orthodontic bonding composites with as much filler as possible. Frias 2026 (15 studies, 11 in-vitro) found all resins within clinically acceptable SBS (~6–14 MPa) but nanohybrid/high-viscosity resins better for mechanical/wear resistance and dimensional stability, with flowables rougher and more stain-prone — favoring high-viscosity resins for high-load posterior attachments.

**Transfer template.** Jedlinski 2023: harder, thicker transfer trays transfer attachment shape more accurately, but a less rigid tray was associated with easier removal and fewer detachments at first bonding (based on clinicians' tactile assessment in one study). Condensable composites increase chair time and are harder to use with a rigid tray. No held paper measures template transfer accuracy clinically; Frias 2026 lists attachment-reproduction accuracy among its outcomes but only in-vitro.

**Restored teeth.** For attachments on lithium disilicate or monolithic zirconia crowns, HF etching plus Filtek Z350 (packable) gave the highest SBS across 9 in-vitro studies, and packable outperformed flowable variants (Kaur 2026). Jedlinski 2023 likewise stresses chemical pretreatment of ceramics (HF or air abrasion) rather than standard composite bonding.

## 6. Attachment Side-Costs

- **Demineralization at attachment margins.** Unal 2025 (prospective controlled trial, n=40, non-randomized, 10 per group) found DIAGNOdent readings around attachments worsened (+4.35) with fluoride toothpaste alone over 6 months, while CPP-ACP, nano-hydroxyapatite and fluoride varnish all improved them (nHAp numerically best, −5.36). Small pilot, but it argues for active remineralization from the start where attachments are bonded.
- **Scan fidelity.** Oguz 2026 (in-vitro, five scanners) found greatest scan deviation clustered in interproximal/undercut and attachment regions — relevant for in-house refinement rescans.
- **Rebonding burden.** Fialho 2026's 18.75% failure over 140 days (posterior only) is a chair-time cost that should be expected, especially on molars.

## 7. Tensions and Gaps

1. **Laboratory filler advantage vs clinical material equivalence.** In-vitro evidence favors higher-filler/high-viscosity resins (Jedlinski, Frias; Kaur on crowns), but the only clinical RCT compared two flowables. The wiki holds **no clinical RCT of flowable vs high-viscosity/packable attachment resin**. Fialho's equivalence should not be extended to that comparison.
2. **Size direction.** Pede 2024's FEA shows smaller attachments of the same shape give more displacement; one study inside Muthuswamy Pandian 2025 found larger, sharper-edged conventional attachments more accurate for lateral incisor extrusion. Different movement (rotation/tipping/torque vs extrusion), different design (single-canine FEA vs clinical cohort) — not a direct contradiction, but there is no held rule for sizing across movement types. Pede's page also flags an internal inconsistency between the paper's Conclusion and its own attachment-dimension table.
3. **"Attachments improve rotation" vs "attachment effect inconsistent".** Nucera answers presence; Benedetti and Muthuswamy mostly answer type or observe attachments in nearly all arms without controls. Resolved by the presence-vs-type distinction in §2, but no held RCT randomizes attachment vs no attachment for rotation.
4. **Single-model FEA.** Both FEA studies use one tooth or one maxilla under one load case; Jedlinski notes that high-RoB FEA studies commonly lacked model validation. Treat millimetre and percentage figures from Pede and Kuguoglu as directional.
5. **Transfer accuracy is untested clinically** in the held papers, and the rigid-vs-flexible tray trade-off rests on a tactile, non-instrumented comparison.
6. **Unstudied movements.** Posterior bucco-lingual expansion with attachments (Nucera) and optimal attachment number/position remain undefined.

## 8. Clinical Decision Points

1. **Extrusion planned?** → Attachment is mandatory (Baik); still expect ~60% expression (Muthuswamy) and plan refinements.
2. **Rotation-heavy case, especially canines?** → Use an attachment, but don't rely on optimized-vs-conventional choice; slow the staging and over-correct (Benedetti; Muthuswamy).
3. **Anterior root torque?** → Attachment/power ridge is the lever (Nucera; Pede; Cao); magnitude remains unpredictable.
4. **Molar distalization?** → Palatal-component (opposed) attachment when bodily control matters; separate expansion from distalization if tipping is a concern; check third-molar status (Kuguoglu).
5. **Resin?** → Conventional flowable is clinically equivalent to bulk-fill flowable (Fialho); consider higher-filler resin for high-load posterior sites on laboratory grounds (Frias; Jedlinski), recognizing this is not clinically tested.
6. **Attachment on a ceramic crown?** → HF etch + packable composite (Kaur; Jedlinski).
7. **Prevention.** → Start active remineralization around attachments (Unal); budget chair time for rebonding, particularly molars (Fialho).
