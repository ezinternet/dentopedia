> - **이분하악관 유병률의 앵커 수치**: 40편 CT/CBCT 비례 메타분석 (Aung 2023, SR+MA) — 환자 단위 20.7% (95% CI 15.9–26%), 반악 (hemi-mandible) 단위 14.3%. 즉 CT 촬영 환자 약 5명 중 1명이 BMC 보유. 단 이질성 극심 (I²≈99%) + 깔때기그림 비대칭(출판편향)으로 정밀도는 제한. [근거강함이나 이질성 큼]
> - **양측성·성별·측방성**: BMC 양성자 중 22.8%가 양측 대칭; 남성·우측에서 유의하게 많음 (Aung 2023). 부속관 평균 길이 12.14 mm, 평균 직경 1.54 mm.
> - **영상 modality가 유병률을 좌우**: 파노라마 방사선사진은 BMC를 거의 놓침 — MRI 연구 (Wamasing 2018) 에서 파노라마 검출 0건, CT조차 1건, 반면 MRI는 BMC 6.4%·NC 58.4–97.6% 검출. CBCT는 파노라마 대비 BMC 검출률 현저히 높음 (Kuribayashi 2010: CBCT 15.6%). **결론: 하악 술전 계획의 표준 영상은 파노라마가 아니라 CBCT** (Menditti 2025 서술리뷰도 동일 주장).
> - **후구치관 (Retromolar Canal) 은 별도 주의 대상**: CT 연구 (Cuozzo 2024) — 환자 53.6%가 BMC 또는 TMC 보유, 부위 단위 BMC 36.2%·TMC 4.5%; 후구치관이 다른 부속관보다 유의하게 넓음 (1.44±0.65 mm). 하악 후방 수술 전 반드시 평가.
> - **분류 체계 통합**: Menditti 2025는 하악관 변이를 4범주 — 이분관·삼분관·하악내 부속관 (intramandibular)·치아관 (dental canal) — 로 제안하고, 하치조신경 (IAN) 과 무관한 협/설측 "영양기능" 관은 별개 범주로 분리 취급 (일부 연구서 유병률 ~95%).
> - **영양관–전신질환 연관은 미해결 논쟁 (cluster 내부 상충)**: 4편 중 3편은 양성, 1편은 null.
> - 양성 근거: Kaur 2019 (n=600) — NC 유병률 당뇨 (DM) 93.5% / 고혈압 (HTN) 88.5% / 대조군 44.5%; Hasan 2022 (n=200) — DM 84%·HTN 66%·치주염 52% vs 대조 20% (모두 p<0.001); Kumar 2014 (n=2989) — HTN 55.2% vs 36.2% (p=0.001, ~2.17배).

> - **임상 결론**: ① 하악 후방 임플란트·발치·신경블록 전 CBCT로 BMC/TMC·후구치관을 능동 확인 (파노라마 신뢰 금물), ② 영양관 다수 관찰 시 전신질환 스크리닝 단서로 참고하되 진단 근거로 삼지 말 것, ③ BMC는 약 1/5 환자에서 존재하는 흔한 변이 — "정상" 가정이 곧 위험.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 10 radiographic-anatomy papers on posterior-mandible neurovascular variants along two axes: bifid/trifid mandibular canal (BMC/TMC) variants + imaging detectability, and nutrient canal (NC) prevalence + its contested systemic-disease association.

BMC affects ~20.7% of CT-imaged patients (Aung 2023 SR+MA, 40 studies; 14.3% hemi-mandible level, male/right-side predominance) and is largely invisible on panoramic radiography (0 detected vs MRI/CBCT — Wamasing 2018, Kuribayashi 2010), making CBCT the standard for pre-surgical mandibular planning; the NC–systemic-disease link is unsettled — three studies report higher NC prevalence in DM/HTN (Kaur 2019, Hasan 2022, Kumar 2014) while one case-control finds no association (Abdar-Esfahani 2013), with NC tracking age and alveolar bone loss rather than hypertension per se.

Clinically: image the posterior mandible with CBCT (not panoramic) to detect BMC/TMC and the retromolar canal before implant/extraction/IANB, and treat a heavy nutrient-canal pattern as an ancillary screening clue — never a standalone diagnostic sign — for undiagnosed DM/HTN.

임상: 임플란트/발치/IANB 전 하악 후방을 파노라마가 아닌 CBCT로 촬영해 BMC/TMC와 후구치관을 검출하고, 다수 영양관 소견은 단독 진단 징후가 아니라 미진단 DM/HTN의 보조 스크리닝 단서로만 취급.

## Summary

The posterior mandible carries the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle (IANVB), and its anatomy is far more variable than the "single canal" mental model implies. This overview synthesizes ten radiographic-anatomy papers into two clinically distinct questions:

1. **How often, and how, does the mandibular canal branch?** — the bifid/trifid mandibular canal (BMC/TMC) and accessory/retromolar canal literature, where the dominant lesson is that the answer depends heavily on imaging modality.
2. **Do nutrient canals (NC) on routine periapical films signal systemic disease?** — a contested cross-sectional literature where the cluster itself contains a genuine contradiction.

Both feed the same clinical endpoint: reducing nerve injury, hemorrhage, and anesthesia failure before mandibular surgery, implant placement, or an inferior alveolar nerve block (IANB).

## Axis A — Bifid/Trifid Mandibular Canal & Accessory Canals

### The prevalence anchor (Aung 2023, SR+MA)
A proportional meta-analysis of **40 CT/CBCT studies (17,714 patients; 31,973 hemi-mandibles)** is the strongest single estimate the wiki holds:
- **Patient-level BMC prevalence 20.7%** (95% CI 15.9–26%); **hemi-mandible-level 14.3%** (95% CI 10.7–18.3%).
- Bilateral symmetry in **22.8%** of BMC-positive patients — a unilateral finding does not rule out the contralateral side.
- Mean accessory-canal **length 12.14 mm, diameter 1.54 mm**; higher prevalence in **males** and on the **right side**.
- Caveat: very high heterogeneity (I² ≈ 99%), moderate risk of bias (JBI 54.69%), and funnel-plot asymmetry (publication bias). The point estimate is directionally solid ("roughly 1 in 5") but the interval is what matters clinically.

### Modality drives the number (the central methodological lesson)
The same anatomy yields wildly different prevalences depending on how you image it:
- **Panoramic radiography badly under-detects.** Wamasing 2018 (high-resolution 3D-VIBE MRI, 154 patients/249 hemi-mandibles) found BMC in 6.4% by MRI but **0 of those cases were visible on panoramic radiography and only 1 on CT** — panoramic is effectively blind to BMC.
- **CBCT markedly outperforms panoramic.** Kuribayashi 2010 (CBCT, 252 patients/301 sides, Nortjé classification) detected BMC in **15.6%**, "markedly higher than prior panoramic-radiograph studies," and endorsed CBCT for detailed pre-surgical evaluation.
- **Accessory canals are a whole network, not a single branch.** Eshak 2014 (4051 CBCT scans, University of Michigan) found accessory canals in **42.9%** overall — **29.8% mandible, 13.1% maxilla** — connecting inner and outer cortical plates.
- Net rule: **a "clear" panoramic film does not exclude a bifid canal.** If the surgical stakes involve the IANVB, the modality must be CBCT.

### The retromolar canal deserves separate attention (Cuozzo 2024)
Retrospective CT of 123 patients / 246 canals:
- **53.6% of patients** had ≥1 BMC or TMC; site-level BMC **36.2%**, TMC **4.5%**.
- Most common subtypes: forward canal (type III, 12.6%) and **retromolar canal (type I, 10.2%)**.
- Retromolar canals were **significantly wider (1.44 ± 0.65 mm)** than other accessory canals — clinically relevant to hemorrhage/anesthesia in third-molar surgery, ramus grafting, and posterior implants. Assess it explicitly before posterior mandibular surgery.

### A unifying classification (Menditti 2025, narrative review)
Synthesizing ~50 articles (1931–2023), Menditti proposes four categories of mandibular canal (MC) variant — **bifid MC, trifid MC, intramandibular accessory canals, dental canals** — and argues that extramandibular buccal/lingual "nutrient-function" canals (independent of the IAN; prevalence up to ~95% in some series) form a **distinct, non-connected category** requiring separate consideration. Its clinical thesis matches the imaging data above: **CBCT, not panoramic, is essential** for pre-surgical/implant planning to avoid hemorrhage, IANB failure, and nerve injury. Limitation: narrative (not systematic) review, lower-tier journal, no formal quality appraisal.

## Axis B — Nutrient Canals & the Systemic-Disease Controversy

Nutrient canals (NC) are vascular channels visible on mandibular anterior intraoral periapical radiographs (IOPARs). A recurring claim is that they are more frequent in hypertension (HTN) and diabetes (DM) — potentially an incidental screening clue. **The cluster does not agree with itself.**

### The positive signal (3 studies)
- **Kumar 2014** (cross-sectional, n=2989; 1440 hypertensives vs 1549 controls): NC incidence **55.2% in HTN vs 36.2% in controls (p=0.001, ~2.17×)**. Crucially, NC also **rose with alveolar bone loss (22.1% with no bone loss → 75.8% with >2/3 root bone loss)** and with age up to 60 — and showed **no correlation with HTN severity**, plus an unexpected *drop* in HTN >10 years (17.6%). So even the largest positive study frames NC as suspicion-raising, not diagnostic.
- **Kaur 2019** (n=600): NC presence **DM 93.5% > HTN 88.5% > controls 44.5%**; both disease groups significantly higher than controls (P<0.05) but not different from each other; NC were predominantly **interradicular**, and NC did **not** correlate with disease duration/severity or gender → supports NC as an adjunct, not standalone marker.
- **Hasan 2022** (n=200): NC prevalence **DM 84%, HTN 66%, chronic periodontitis 52% vs control 20%** (all p<0.001; DM highest, inter-group p=0.0028); correlated with disease duration in DM and HTN. Single-center, χ² without confounder adjustment.

### The contradicting null (Abdar-Esfahani 2013)
Case-control (n=64: 32 HTN vs 32 normotensive, Iran): NC incidence **37.5% in HTN vs 53.1% in normotensives — direction reversed and not significant (P=0.209)**; no association with HTN duration or control status. NC correlated instead with **older age** (47.1 vs 42.6 y, P=0.002). This page is explicitly flagged in the wiki as *contradicting* Kumar 2014.

### Reconciling the conflict
- The positive studies are larger but almost all are **single-center cross-sectional χ² comparisons without multivariable confounder adjustment**; NC co-tracks with **age and alveolar bone loss**, both of which also track with HTN/DM prevalence — classic confounding.
- The most defensible reading: **NC is not a validated diagnostic sign of any systemic disease.** A heavy NC pattern on a routine IOPAR is at most an *ancillary prompt* to check whether an older patient has undiagnosed/uncontrolled DM or HTN — never a basis for diagnosis. Weight of evidence leans "association present but weak and confounded," with at least one credible null.

## Clinical Bottom Line

| Question | Answer | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| How common is a bifid mandibular canal? | ~20.7% of patients (CT), 14.3% hemi-mandibles; 22.8% bilateral | SR+MA, high heterogeneity |
| Can I trust a panoramic film to rule it out? | No — panoramic detects ≈0% of BMC | consistent (MRI/CBCT vs panoramic) |
| What imaging for pre-surgical mandibular planning? | CBCT (MRI superior in research but not routine) | consistent |
| Which accessory canal needs specific attention? | Retromolar canal — wider, common | retrospective CT |
| Do nutrient canals diagnose hypertension/diabetes? | No — at most an ancillary clue; ≥1 null study; confounded by age/bone loss | contested cross-sectional |

**Decision rule:** For any posterior-mandibular procedure near the IANVB (implant, third-molar surgery, ramus/block graft, IANB in a difficult case), obtain and actively read a **CBCT** for BMC/TMC and retromolar canal — do not assume a single canal from a panoramic film. Treat nutrient canals as an incidental screening prompt only.
