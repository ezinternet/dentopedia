> - 지르코니아 크라운 한정 메타분석(Tomar 2025)에서는 SR이 6개월 변연골소실(Marginal Bone Loss, MBL) 유의하게 낮고(SMD -0.63, p=0.002), 전체 합병증 위험 46% 낮음(RR 0.54, p=0.02) — 단 1–5년 장기 MBL은 두 군 동등해짐(즉 초기 이점이지 영구적 이점은 아님).

## Three-line Summary

This overview synthesizes 17 papers (2 SR, 8 SR+MA, 5 RCT, 1 prospective cohort, 1 consensus guideline) on implant-supported fixed prosthesis retention type (screw vs cement), intermediate abutment use, and restorative material (metal-ceramic, veneered/monolithic zirconia, lithium disilicate, FRC) and their effect on survival and complications.

Across every retention and material comparison studied, overall survival was statistically equivalent (typically 96–99% at 3–5 years, 89.5–100% for complete-arch prostheses at 5–15 years); what differed consistently was complication type — cement retention and veneered ceramics shifted risk toward biological/technical complications (peri-implantitis, chipping), while screw retention and monolithic designs shifted risk toward mechanical complications (screw loosening) but reduced chipping and, at early timepoints, marginal bone loss.

Clinically, material/design selection should be driven by which complication profile is least acceptable for a given patient and site — not by expected survival, which does not meaningfully differentiate the options studied.

## 세줄요약

이 개요는 임플란트 지지 고정성 보철물의 유지 방식(나사 vs 시멘트), 중간 지대주 사용, 수복 재료(금속도재, 비니어드/모놀리식 지르코니아, 리튬디실리케이트, 섬유강화복합레진)가 생존율·합병증에 미치는 영향을 다룬 17편(SR 2, SR+MA 8, RCT 5, 전향적 코호트 1, 합의 가이드라인 1)을 종합한다.

모든 유지방식·재료 비교에서 전체 생존율은 통계적으로 동등했다(3–5년 96–99%, 완전궁 보철 5–15년 89.5–100%가 일반적); 대신 일관되게 달라진 것은 합병증의 "종류"였다 — 시멘트 유지와 비니어드 세라믹은 생물학적/기술적 합병증(임플란트주위염, 치핑) 쪽으로, 나사 유지와 모놀리식 디자인은 기계적 합병증(나사 풀림) 쪽으로 위험을 이동시켰지만 치핑과 초기 변연골소실은 감소시켰다.

임상적으로 재료·디자인 선택은 (연구된 옵션 간 유의한 차이가 없는) 예상 생존율이 아니라, 해당 환자·부위에서 어떤 합병증 유형이 가장 받아들이기 어려운지에 따라 결정되어야 한다.

## Thesis

Across the 17 papers reviewed, implant-supported fixed prosthesis outcomes research has converged on a consistent, almost formulaic pattern: **survival is a poor discriminator between retention types and materials; complication phenotype is the real decision variable.** No comparison in this evidence base — screw vs cement (Hamed 2020; Tomar 2025; Khurshid 2025), monolithic vs veneered zirconia (Tajti 2023; Hosseini 2022; Gseibat 2025; Pjetursson 2023, 2026; Laumbacher 2021), zirconia vs metal-ceramic (multiple), abutment-level vs implant-level connection (Molinero-Mourelle 2026), or even FRC vs zirconia for cantilever restorations (Kasem 2025) — produced a clinically or statistically decisive survival difference. Survival estimates cluster tightly in the mid-to-high 90s% across 3–5 year (single units) and 5–15 year (complete-arch) horizons regardless of the variable manipulated.

What does differ, reproducibly, is the *type* of complication a given design invites. Cement retention shifts risk toward biological complications — subgingival cement remnants, peri-implant mucositis, and peri-implantitis — because excess cement is difficult to detect and remove around deep implant margins (Hamed 2020; Khurshid 2025; Tomar 2026 confirms this pattern extends to complete-arch prostheses, where peri-implantitis is "higher with cement retention"). Screw retention shifts risk toward mechanical complications — chiefly screw loosening (18% vs 4% at 12 months in Khurshid 2025; 5–15% at the complete-arch level in Tomar 2026) — but buys retrievability and, in zirconia crowns specifically, a measurable early (6-month) marginal-bone-loss advantage that is not sustained long-term (Tomar 2025). Similarly, veneered ceramic designs (whether zirconia or lithium disilicate) consistently chip and fracture significantly more than monolithic designs (Pjetursson 2023 p<0.01; Pjetursson 2026; Tajti 2023 OR 0.31; Laumbacher 2021; Tomar 2026 P<.05), a signal so uniform across single crowns, multi-unit FDPs, and complete-arch prostheses that it should be treated as a settled design principle rather than a per-study finding.

Intermediate (abutment-level) abutments occupy a narrower niche: the evidence (only 3 RCTs, 126 patients, 341 implants — Molinero-Mourelle 2026) shows a real but small MBL benefit (MD -0.13 mm at 36 months, not clinically significant on its own) alongside a more convincing reduction in probing depth (soft-tissue health), with no cost in survival, mechanical complications, or patient satisfaction. This makes AL abutments a soft-tissue-health optimization, not a survival or hard-complication lever.

## Clinical Decision Points

### 1. Screw- vs cement-retained: which for which case

Survival is not the deciding variable — Hamed 2020's 12-study SR found no significant survival difference between SR and CR, and every downstream paper in this set (Khurshid 2025, Tomar 2025, Tomar 2026) reconfirms it. The decision instead turns on which complication phenotype the clinician and patient are more willing to manage:

- **Favor SR (screw-retained)** when peri-implant disease risk is elevated (history of periodontitis/peri-implantitis, deep sulcus, augmented/GBR sites), when retrievability matters for long-term maintenance or repair, and specifically for **zirconia crowns**, where Tomar 2025's SR+MA showed SR crowns have significantly lower MBL at 6 months (SMD -0.63, p=0.002) and 46% lower overall complication risk (RR 0.54, p=0.02). Budget for mechanical maintenance: Khurshid 2025 found screw loosening in 18% of SR crowns at 12 months vs 4% for CR (p=0.032).
- **Favor CR (cement-retained)** when angulation or esthetics make an occlusal/palatal access hole undesirable, or where the restorative workflow favors cementation — but use retrievable cement and avoid deep subgingival margins, since Khurshid 2025 found marginal gap (20% vs 8%) and cement washout (14% vs 2%) significantly more common with CR, and Tomar 2026's umbrella review confirms peri-implantitis runs consistently higher with cement retention at the complete-arch level too.
- Peri-implantitis differences trend toward CR being worse in every study (10% vs 4% in Khurshid 2025, though NS at 12 months; 4–18% range in Tomar 2026, "higher with cement retention") but rarely reach significance in short follow-ups — longer-term data would likely sharpen this signal.
- The long-term MBL advantage of SR is **not durable**: Tomar 2025 shows the 6-month SR advantage disappears by 1, 3, and 5 years, so SR's biggest proven benefit is early bone preservation at augmented/compromised sites, not a permanent structural edge.

### 2. Material selection for the framework/veneer — monolithic beats veneered, almost regardless of the core material

This is the single most reproducible finding across the entire evidence base. Whether the substrate is zirconia or lithium disilicate, whether the prosthesis is a single tooth- or implant-supported crown, a multi-unit implant FDP, or a complete-arch restoration, **monolithic (unlayered) fabrication significantly reduces ceramic chipping/fracture versus veneered fabrication**:

- Pjetursson 2023 (posterior implant multi-unit FDPs): veneered restorations had significantly more chipping/fracture than monolithic (p<0.01); monolithic zirconia had the best 3-year survival point estimate (98.9%) though not statistically superior to PFM (98.3%) or veneered Zr (97.5%).
- Pjetursson 2026 (tooth-supported single crowns, 64 studies, 11,560 crowns): monolithic LDS is the single top performer at 5 years (98.5%), statistically comparable to veneered densely-sintered zirconia (97.3%) and metal-ceramic (97.1%); the paper explicitly attributes reduced fracture to monolithic *design*, applicable to both LDS and zirconia, not to any one material's intrinsic strength.
- Tajti 2023 (SR+MA of RCTs, posterior implant crowns): monolithic zirconia vs metal-ceramic — equivalent survival (RR 1.0) and MBL (MD -0.05mm), but monolithic zirconia has 69% lower technical-complication risk (OR 0.31), driven mainly by absence of veneer chipping.
- Two independent RCTs confirm the chipping-free profile directly: Hosseini 2022 (5-year premolar implant crowns) — zero chipping in the zirconia group vs one event in metal-ceramic; Gseibat 2025 (5-year posterior digital-workflow crowns) — zero chipping in zirconia vs three events in metal-ceramic, with equivalent survival (96% vs 97%) in both RCTs.
- Laumbacher 2021's overview of 38 SRs (~10,000 zirconia restorations) independently names chipping as the single most frequent technical complication across all zirconia restoration types, and states monolithic design is the fix.
- Tomar 2026's complete-arch umbrella review shows the same pattern scales up: veneer chipping 15–35% with veneered zirconia, **significantly higher than monolithic zirconia (P<.05)** — the single-crown-level finding holds at the full-arch level.
- Sheykhian 2026's SR+MA specifically on screw-retained ceramic-**veneered** ISFDPs reports a 13% pooled complication rate dominated by chipping, screw loosening, and fracture — consistent with (and mechanistically explained by) the veneered-design vulnerability identified elsewhere.

**Caveat on material vs material (holding design constant):** when veneering is not the variable, material choice matters less. Feldspathic/silica-based crowns are the one clear outlier — significantly inferior at 90.4% 5-year survival (p<0.0001) vs monolithic LDS's 98.5% in Pjetursson 2026 — so this older material is not a competitive first choice regardless of retention or design. Lithium disilicate for implant-supported multi-unit FDPs remains comparatively under-studied (wide 95% CI 84.8–99.9% in Pjetursson 2023), whereas its tooth-supported single-crown evidence is mature (95–98% 5-year survival per Laumacher 2025). Waltenberger 2025's German S3 guideline formalizes this asymmetry: for single crowns, LDS, silicate ceramics, and all zirconia generations are recommended (~96–97% 3-year survival), but for short-span FDPs and full-arch restorations, **only 3Y-TZP zirconia has sufficient clinical evidence** — high-translucency zirconia (4Y/5Y-TZP) and lithium disilicate multi-unit FDPs are flagged as evidence-limited, with polymer-infiltrated ceramics explicitly not recommended for any implant indication.

For fiber-reinforced composite (FRC) as a framework alternative to zirconia — evidence here is limited to a single **tooth-supported** (not implant-supported) 36-month RCT of cantilever inlay-retained FDPs (Kasem 2025): overall survival 97.5%, no significant difference between monolithic zirconia and FRC (p>0.05). This is directly relevant to conservative single-tooth replacement decisions but should not be extrapolated to implant-supported prostheses without implant-specific data.

### 3. Intermediate abutments — a soft-tissue-health lever, not a survival lever

Molinero-Mourelle 2026 is the only paper in this set addressing abutment-level (AL, intermediate abutment) vs implant-level (IL, direct) connection specifically in screw-retained multi-unit FDPs, and the evidence base is thin (3 RCTs, 126 patients, 341 implants, Sweden/Spain). Findings:

- **MBL**: statistically significant but clinically small AL advantage at 36 months (MD -0.13mm, 95% CI -0.23 to -0.03, p=0.01, moderate certainty), no difference at 12 or 60 months.
- **Probing depth**: a clearer, larger AL advantage at both 36 months (MD -0.66mm, p=0.01) and 60 months (MD -0.60mm, p<0.001), though certainty of evidence is low.
- **No difference** in bleeding on probing, implant survival (RD=0.00 at all timepoints), mechanical complications, or patient satisfaction at any follow-up.
- Proposed mechanism: AL abutments reposition the implant-abutment micro-gap coronally, away from the alveolar crest, reducing microbial-driven inflammatory bone resorption and preserving biologic width via a "one abutment, one time" protocol.

**Practical read**: intermediate abutments are worth considering when soft-tissue health (reduced probing depth, presumably reduced peri-implant inflammation risk) is the priority — e.g., patients with a history of peri-implant mucosal problems — but should not be marketed as a bone-loss or survival intervention given the small/inconsistent MBL effect and the thin 3-trial evidence base.

### 4. Complete-arch prostheses — anticipated complication burden over time

Tomar 2026's umbrella review (7 SRs, ≥5-year follow-up, 4–8 implants/arch) is the only source in this set addressing full-arch prostheses directly, and its findings are a scaled-up mirror of the single-crown/FDP evidence above:

- Prosthetic survival 89.5–100% and implant survival 95.2–99.5% over 5–15 years — broadly consistent with (if slightly wider-ranged than) the single-unit figures elsewhere in this evidence base.
- Screw loosening 5–15%, veneer chipping 15–35% (significantly higher for veneered vs monolithic zirconia, P<.05), framework fracture <5% (metal-ceramic ≈ metal-resin, P=.38), peri-implantitis 4–18% (higher with cement retention), marginal bone loss 1.2–1.5mm at 5–10 years.
- **Implant number does not matter for survival**: 4 vs ≥5 implants per arch showed no significant difference in implant survival, prosthetic survival, or MBL (P>.05) — supporting reduced-implant-count ("All-on-4"-type) protocols on biomechanical grounds when properly executed.
- This paper is a qualitative/narrative umbrella synthesis (no new pooled meta-analysis), and AMSTAR-2 confidence across the 7 included reviews ranged low (1) to moderate (5) to high (1) — treat point estimates as directional ranges, not precise pooled effects.

Read together with the single-crown evidence, the complete-arch data confirm that the retention-type and design principles established at the crown/FDP level (screw vs cement biological-vs-mechanical trade-off; monolithic-vs-veneered chipping gradient) generalize up to full-arch reconstructions rather than being an artifact of small single-unit studies.

## Gaps & Future Research

- **Lithium disilicate in multi-unit implant FDPs** remains under-studied relative to its mature tooth-supported single-crown evidence base — Pjetursson 2023's pooled 3-year survival estimate carries a wide CI (84.8–99.9%), and Waltenberger 2025's guideline does not recommend LDS for short-span FDPs or full-arch restorations due to insufficient evidence (only 3Y-TZP zirconia clears that bar).
- **No head-to-head RCT directly compares intermediate abutments across materials or retention types** — Molinero-Mourelle 2026's evidence is limited to 3 RCTs, all bone-level systems with metal-ceramic screw-retained prostheses, from only 2 countries (Sweden, Spain); whether the AL soft-tissue benefit generalizes to zirconia frameworks or tissue-level implants is unknown.
- **FRC as an implant-supported framework material** has essentially no data in this evidence base — Kasem 2025's FRC-vs-zirconia comparison is tooth-supported (cantilever inlay-retained FDP on natural abutment teeth), not implant-supported, so its 97.5% survival and non-inferiority to zirconia cannot be directly extrapolated to implant prostheses.
- **Long-term (>12 months) peri-implantitis differences between SR and CR** are consistently directionally worse for CR but frequently fail to reach statistical significance in short follow-ups (Khurshid 2025: 10% vs 4%, p=0.146 NS at 12 months) — longer prospective data are needed to confirm whether this trend becomes significant over 5+ years.
- **Newer 2026 papers in this set (Molinero-Mourelle, Sheykhian, Tomar umbrella, Da Silva) are recent additions with limited independent replication** — their findings are internally consistent with the older literature but have not yet been cross-validated by a second SR+MA on the same specific question.
- **Zirconia implants themselves** (as opposed to zirconia crowns/abutments on titanium implants) remain clinically unvalidated even in narrow, high-stakes indications like maxillary lateral incisor agenesis — Da Silva 2026's systematic review of 25 clinical studies found zero clinical use of zirconia implants despite favorable FEA numbers, reflecting a broader gap in zirconia-implant (not just zirconia-restoration) long-term survival data.
- **Complete-arch complication data are drawn from older primary studies** (the 7 SRs in Tomar 2026 synthesize primary literature published up to ~2019, filtered for ≥5-year follow-up) — contemporary digital-workflow, high-translucency zirconia, and monolithic full-arch protocols are underrepresented relative to newer single-crown RCTs (e.g., Gseibat 2025's digital workflow).
