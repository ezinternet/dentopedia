> - 2 mm 기준은 측정 사슬 오차가 그보다 훨씬 작다는 전제 위에서만 의미가 있다. 사슬은 네 링크이고 근거 상태가 서로 다르다. 첫 링크만 2 mm 기준을 통과한다. [확인]
> - 첫 링크(손 조작): SR(de Moraes Melo Neto 2021)은 양수 조작 대 턱점 유도 비교 6편에서 양수 조작이 3편에서 우월, 2편 동등, 1편 열등이라 권고하지만 모두 저품질(Jadad ≤2)이고 무치악 자료가 없다. Jász 2024(34명, 50 µm 초음파 측정)는 양수 조작과 혀끝 기법이 MIP와 차이가 없었고, 턱 후퇴 기법은 후방으로 벗어났으며 모든 편차가 ±1 mm 이내였다. [확인]
> - 둘째 링크(기록 재료): Rani 2024는 wax가 탄성체보다 약 6배 나쁘다고 보고하지만 단위를 밝히지 않았고, 탄성체 세 종은 서로 구별되지 않았다(p=0.90–0.99). 저자의 "polyether가 최선" 결론은 뒷받침되지 않는다. Tank 2024는 방향은 같지만 n=30, SD≈9로 확정하지 못한다. [확인]
> - 셋째 링크(디지털 검증): Morsy 2024에서 구강 스캐너는 MIP 민감도가 100%였지만 측방 운동 민감도는 28%(NPV 56%)였다. 기준 표준이 교합지라서 진짜 정답이 아니다. [확인]
> - 넷째 링크(기준 자체): Rovira-Lastra 2026에서 교합지는 참양성 81%, 거짓양성 15%였다. 200 µm 교합지가 100 µm보다 거짓양성이 적었고(9.4% vs 20.6%), 양악 교합 일치를 요구하면 거짓양성이 2.0%로 떨어졌다. 거짓양성의 약 2/3는 교두 첨단 흔적이었다. [확인]
> - 치료 제약(Singh 2024 Cochrane): 교합 조정은 효과가 없고, 안정화 스플린트는 매우 낮은 확실성의 작은 효과만 있으며, 비가역 교합 변경은 근거 없이 위험이 있어 강한 비권고다. 따라서 CR–MIP 편차는 진단과 설계 정보이지 교합 조정의 지시가 아니다. [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 측정 사슬의 정확도 주장은 링크별로 따로 해야 하며, 손 조작 링크만 근거가 뒷받침한다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the measurement-chain paragraphs and the record-verification table of the parent CR/CO-MIP reference-position synthesis.
The 2 mm threshold presupposes a chain of manipulation, record material, mounting, and contact verification with much smaller error, and only the manipulation link meets that standard.
Articulating paper, the reference standard used to verify occlusion, is itself inaccurate, and occlusal adjustment has no benefit, so a CR–MIP slide is diagnostic information rather than an indication to treat.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 CR/CO-MIP 종합의 측정 사슬 단락과 기록·검증 표를 담는다.
2 mm 기준은 손 조작, 기록 재료, 장착, 접촉 검증이 그보다 훨씬 작은 오차를 가진다는 전제에서만 의미가 있고, 그 기준을 통과하는 링크는 손 조작 하나뿐이다.

## Synthesis

**And the debate has a floor it has largely not inspected: its own instruments.** Every position above is discriminated by a number — Pullinger's 2 mm sagittal slide separating normal from abnormal (Fornai), Radej's ≥2 mm vertical/horizontal and ≥0.5 mm transverse thresholds for clinically significant condylar displacement. Those thresholds are only meaningful if the chain that produces them — **manipulation technique → record material → mounting → contact verification** — has error well below them. That chain has four links, and they are not in the same evidential state: one is bounded, three are not. Sorting them is the difference between a useful objection and a rhetorical one.

**The first link is the one with the best evidence, and it holds.** How the clinician's hands seat the mandible is the input to everything downstream, and it is the only link in this chain carrying systematic-review-level evidence. [[complete-denture/de-moraes-melo-neto-2021-centric-relation-condylar-reproducibility-sr]] found that the entire published within-method literature reduces to a single comparison — bimanual manipulation vs chin-point guidance — with bimanual superior in 3 of 6 studies, equivalent in 2, inferior in 1; the authors recommend **bimanual manipulation for dentate non-TMD patients**, with the honest caveat that all six studies were low quality (Jadad ≤2) and none enrolled edentulous patients. [[complete-denture/jasz-2024-centric-relation-methods-digital-mandibular-motion]] then measured the question directly rather than tallying it: 34 dentate TMD-negative subjects, seven CR techniques, each read against the subject's own MIP in 3D on a **50 µm ultrasound mandibular motion analyser**. Dawson bimanual manipulation and tongue-tip at the hard/soft-palate border deviated non-significantly from MIP on all three axes; Gothic-arch tracing and tongue-at-rugae pulled anteriorly; **active and passive chin retrusion pulled posteriorly** — the forced-retrusion failure mode that motivated the abandon-CR camp in the first place, caught in the act and attributable to technique rather than to the concept.

**The number that matters is the one bounding all seven: every deviation stayed within ±1 mm.** That is below the 2 mm line this debate discriminates on. So the manipulation link does *not* invalidate the threshold — it clears it, at least in dentate TMD-negative subjects on one 50 µm instrument at one centre. This should be read as a partial concession against this overview's own earlier framing: "the ruler is as coarse as the line" was stated too broadly. What is unvalidated at the 2 mm scale is the *rest* of the chain, and the objection is stronger for being narrowed to where it actually bites. Note too what this does to the Zonnenberg–Goldstein axis: Goldstein's position is that CR's problem is recording inconsistency rather than the concept, and these two papers are the closest thing the wiki holds to a test of that claim — they find the inconsistency real, technique-dependent, **and largely fixable by naming the technique**, which is Goldstein's argument rather than Zonnenberg's. It is corroboration on the mechanism, not a verdict on the terminology dispute, which is semantic and not settled by millimetres.

**The remaining three links are where the objection survives.** Interocclusal record material alone separates wax from elastomer by roughly sixfold (Rani 2024: Aluwax 0.939 vs polyether 0.136, PVS 0.152–0.165 deviation from control, all four significantly different from control, though **the paper never states its unit** and its own "polyether is best" conclusion is unsupported by its Tukey result showing the three elastomers indistinguishable at p=0.90–0.99). Tank 2024 points the same direction clinically but cannot carry weight: its largest mean discrepancy (hard wax) failed significance while a smaller one (alu wax) reached it, at n=30 with SD≈9. On the verification side, intraoral scanners record MIP with near-perfect operating characteristics yet collapse to **28% sensitivity in lateral excursion** (Morsy 2024) — and the reference standard they are judged against is articulating paper, whose own accuracy is now measured rather than merely doubted. [[occlusion/rovira-lastra-2026-articulating-paper-accuracy-occlusal-points]] scored articulating paper against a scanned polyvinyl-siloxane registration in 32 natural dentitions: **81% true positives and 15% false positives** at the contact-point level. Two of its findings are directly actionable and one is counter-intuitive — **200 µm paper produced fewer false positives than 100 µm** (9.4% vs 20.6%), because thinner paper generates more incidental cusp-tip smears, and requiring a mark to **coincide on both arches** before counting it as a contact collapsed false positives from 15% to **2.0%**. Two-thirds of false positives were cusp-tip artefacts. This matters to the debate rather than only to the chairside: it means the indicator against which scanners, splints and equilibrations are all judged mislabels roughly one contact in seven as real, and that a clinician who adjusts to unconfirmed marks is grinding to noise — while a free, one-line change of criterion removes most of it. Most pointedly, Biren 2025 shows that raising OVD leaves the *number* of occlusal contacts unchanged while their *positions* shift measurably: the metric most clinicians reach for to confirm an occlusion is insensitive to exactly the thing that moved. Taken together with Qadeer & Türp's 11–70 contact-count range, the honest reading is that **measurement standardisation across these three links is not an appendix to this debate but a precondition for settling it** — a 2 mm rule cannot arbitrate positions when the material, the digital capture and the verification metric are unvalidated at that scale, even though the manipulation link now clears it. This claim is directional and deliberately unquantified for the material and digital links: those studies are small, two are three-page supplement papers, and one omits its unit entirely.

**Finally, a constraint from the top of the ladder that the whole debate must sit under.** Everything above concerns which position to *build to* and how to record it. It is a separate question whether a discovered discrepancy licenses *treating* the occlusion, and here the evidence is both higher-grade and clearer than anything else on this page: [[tmj/singh-2024-occlusal-interventions-tmd-cochrane-sr]], a Cochrane review of occlusal interventions across TMD subtypes, finds **occlusal adjustment shows no benefit**, stabilisation splints show only very-low-certainty small benefit and no advantage over non-occluding placebo splints, and irreversible occlusal modification (grinding, crowns prescribed for TMD) has no evidence of benefit and carries risk of harm — issued as a **strong recommendation against**. The consequence for this synthesis is precise and worth stating flatly: a CR–MIP slide is **diagnostic and design information, not an indication to equilibrate**. Node 3's ">2 mm → assess" means assess and stop; it does not mean adjust. Jiménez-Silva's unresolved association runs the same way — an association of uncertain causality cannot justify an irreversible intervention that a Cochrane review finds ineffective. The one place the evidence still supports building deliberately to CR/CO is *restorative necessity* (the existing MIP is being lost or replaced anyway, per Kattadiyil), not therapeutic ambition.

### Can we actually record and verify the position we chose?

The debate's thresholds (2 mm slide; Radej's ≥2 mm / ≥0.5 mm) presuppose a measurement chain accurate well below them. These papers test that presupposition — and are the reason the "Measurement not standardized" gap below is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. The chain has four links; **rows are ordered by link, and only the first link currently clears the 2 mm threshold.**

| Paper | Design | n / scope | Link in the chain | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| de Moraes Melo Neto 2021 | **SR** (PRISMA, PROSPERO) — highest grade in this table | 6 studies, dentate non-TMD | **manipulation technique** | Entire within-method literature = bimanual vs chin-point only. Bimanual superior in 3, equal in 2, inferior in 1 → **bimanual recommended for dentate non-TMD**. All 6 studies low quality (Jadad ≤2); no edentulous data; no graphic/physiologic head-to-head exists |
| Jász 2024 | Cross-sectional (50 µm ultrasound motion analyser) | 34 dentate, TMD-negative | **manipulation technique** | 7 CR techniques vs each subject's MIP in 3D: **Dawson bimanual and tongue-tip at hard/soft-palate border non-significant on all axes**; Gothic-arch and tongue-at-rugae deviate anteriorly; **active/passive chin retrusion deviates posteriorly**. **All deviations ≤±1 mm — this link clears the 2 mm line.** Single centre; MIP is a relative benchmark, not an anatomic gold standard |
| Rani 2024 | In vitro (JPBS 3-pp supplement) | 10 records/material, mock prep 46-48 | record **material** | Aluwax 0.939 vs polyether (Ramitec) 0.136, PVS 0.152–0.165 deviation from control — wax ≈6× worse; F(4,45)=261.5, p<0.001; **but** the 3 elastomers are indistinguishable (p=0.90–0.99), contradicting the paper's own "polyether is best"; **unit never stated** |
| Tank 2024 | Cross-sectional (JPBS 3-pp supplement) | 30 dentate, 20–45 y | record **material**, clinically | Same direction (waxes worst, PVS/bis-acrylic best) but **does not establish it**: only alu wax differed significantly from MIP; hard wax had the largest mean discrepancy yet was non-significant (n=30, SD≈9). Durable point is conceptual — closing onto any material and releasing the bite introduces vertical error at all |
| Morsy 2024 | In vitro | master casts, articulator | **digital capture / verification** | IOS multi-occlusion: MIP sensitivity/NPV 100%, specificity/PPV 99%; protrusive acceptable; **lateral sensitivity 28%, NPV 56%** — misses ~3 of 4 true contacts. Reference standard is articulating paper, not ground truth |
| Rovira-Lastra 2026 | Cross-sectional criterion validity (scanned PVS reference, test–retest) | 32 natural dentitions | **the reference standard itself** | Articulating paper: **81% true positives, 15% false positives** at contact-point level. **200 µm beats 100 µm** (FP 9.4% vs 20.6%) — thinner paper smears more cusp tips; **requiring bilateral arch coincidence drops FP to 2.0%**; ~66% of FPs are cusp-tip artefacts; "pulling" technique adds nothing; mandibular arch ~5% more true positives. Quantifies the indicator every other row is judged against |
| Wang 2024 | Cross-sectional (abstract only — PMC lacks the Chinese body) | 10, ideal occlusion | **digital capture** | 3 IOS vs facebow+bite-registration mounting for MIP inter-arch distance: no significant difference (p=0.89–0.98). **Null ≠ equivalence** — SD ±6.4 mm dwarfs the 0.6–1.4 mm group differences; all three read consistently smaller than control (hypothesis-generating only) |
| Mei 2022 | In vitro (**industrial-CT reference standard**) | 9 scan strategies, 1 volunteer's casts | **virtual articulation** | VIR accuracy is *regional*, not global: bilateral-posterior (BP) scanning is the best full-arch default; the region you scan is the region that is accurate. Methodologically the strongest of the digital set — its reference is true ground truth, not another imperfect indicator |
| Biren 2025 | Cross-sectional | 18, 8 splints each | **verification metric itself** | Raising OVD 2/5 mm left the *number* of occlusal contacts unchanged while contact *positions* deviated (Z-direction clinically relevant at 5 mm intraoral); virtual facebow did not reduce deviation. Contact count is insensitive to what actually moved. Deviations are relative to a reference group, not to truth |
