## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 발견**: ARP 후 CBCT 하운스필드 단위(Hounsfield Unit, HU)의 신뢰도는 이식재 종류에 따라 결정적으로 달라진다 — 이종골(Xenograft, Bio-Oss)은 HU를 믿지 말고, 동종골(Allograft, FDBA)/PRF는 중등도 신뢰 가능.
> - **이종골(Xenograft) ARP 후**: 잔류 입자가 방사선 불투과성을 인위적으로 높임 → CBCT에서 D2처럼 보여도 실제 드릴 저항은 D3/D4일 수 있음; Abdulkarim 2021 — HU ↔ 임상 골 질(Misch) 상관관계 **비유의** (이종골 부위 p=0.02 vs 비이식).
> - **동종골(FDBA)/PRF ARP 후**: 이식재가 완전히 흡수·대체되므로 HU가 실제 신생골을 반영 → ISQ와 중등도 유의 상관(r=0.39–0.41, p<0.05); 신생골%와 강한 상관(r=0.776, p<0.001); Ivanova 2020.
> - **수치 기준**: Misch D1–D4(>1250 / 850–1250 / 350–850 / 150–350 HU)는 **비이식 정상 골** 기준 — 이종골 부위에서 이 역치를 그대로 적용하면 오판 위험.
> - **ARP 부위는 본질적으로 저밀도 골**: Ko 2024에서 ARP 부위 75%가 D3, 25%가 D4 — 이식재 종류와 무관하게 외과적 프로토콜을 저밀도 골 기준으로 설계해야 함.
> - **형태 예측인자가 HU보다 더 안정적**: 골 오목 깊이(Bone Concavity Depth, BCD) ≥1.03mm + 골 오목 각도(Bone Concavity Angulation, BCA) <155.3°가 낮은 이식재 흡수와 유의한 상관(Gan 2023); 이 형태 지표는 이식재 종류의 영향을 덜 받음.
> - **78/80 식립 가능 ≠ HU 기준**: Hartmann 2026의 "78/80 추가 증대 없이 임플란트 식립" 성공률은 CBCT 체적 기반 판단이며, 실패한 2부위는 "저골질"로 기술되었으나 HU 역치는 제시하지 않음 — Bio-Oss 흡수된 부분의 HU가 진짜 골인지 구분이 안 됨을 시사.
> - **ISQ 직접 측정이 최우선 기준**: ARP 부위(특히 이종골) 임플란트 준비 판단에서 CBCT HU보다 신뢰도가 높은 지표는 식립 직후 ISQ(목표 ≥65 Ncm 또는 ISQ ≥65); 초기 ISQ가 낮아도 10주 2차 안정성으로 회복 가능(Ko 2024).
> - **대기 시간**: FDBA/PRF ARP에서 4개월 시점에 HU가 ISQ를 예측했음(Ivanova 2020) — 이종골 ARP에서의 최적 대기 시간은 별도 연구 필요.
> - **임상 결론**: Bio-Oss 계열 ARP 후 CBCT는 체적(식립 공간 충분한가?) 판독에만 사용하고, 골 질(이 공간에 지금 임플란트를 식립할 수 있는가?) 판단에는 CBCT HU를 단독 기준으로 삼지 말 것 — FDBA/PRF는 예외.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 4 papers on CBCT Hounsfield unit (HU) reliability for implant readiness assessment after alveolar ridge preservation (ARP): the key moderating variable is graft material type.

Xenograft (Bio-Oss) residue inflates CBCT HU without proportional increase in native bone stiffness — HU does not correlate with clinical bone quality (Misch) in post-xenograft sites; FDBA/PRF ARP shows preserved HU→ISQ correlation (r≈0.40) and strong HU→histomorphometric new bone correlation (r=0.776).

Misch D1–D4 HU ranges are calibrated for native bone; applying them to post-ARP sites requires graft-type qualification — xenograft sites need direct ISQ measurement rather than CBCT HU for bone quality judgement.

## 세줄요약

4편 종합 — ARP 후 CBCT 하운스필드 단위(Hounsfield Unit, HU) 신뢰도의 핵심 조절 변수는 이식재 종류.

이종골(Xenograft, Bio-Oss) 잔류 입자: HU를 인위적으로 높여 임상 골 질(Misch 분류)과의 상관을 소실시킴; 동결건조골(FDBA)/PRF는 완전 흡수 후 HU가 신생골을 반영 → 안정성 지수(ISQ) 중등도 상관(r≈0.40) + 신생골% 강한 상관(r=0.776).

Misch D1–D4 HU 역치는 비이식 골 기준이며, 이종골 ARP 부위 판단에는 CBCT HU 단독 사용을 지양하고 식립 직후 ISQ 직접 측정을 우선해야 한다.

## Background: The Misch Classification as Native-Bone Reference

The standard framework for preoperative bone quality assessment is Misch's D1–D4 classification, now measured with CBCT-derived HU:

| Misch Type | HU Range | Description | Implant Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | >1,250 | Dense cortical | Standard; heat management |
| D2 | 850–1,250 | Thick cortical + coarse trabecular | Standard |
| D3 | 350–850 | Thin cortical + fine trabecular | Undersized drilling; extended healing |
| D4 | 150–350 | Fine trabecular only | OD; aggressive protocol modification |

Raghavan 2025 narrative review establishes these ranges as the clinical reference for surgical protocol selection. **Critical caveat**: these thresholds were established in native, non-grafted bone. Two papers published in 2020–2021 reveal that this framework requires qualification before application to post-ARP sites.

## The Graft-Type Dependency Problem

### Xenograft (Bio-Oss) ARP — HU is unreliable

Abdulkarim et al. (2021, J Contemp Dent Pract, n=118 sites, 38 grafted) is the key study. Sites with prior bone grafting showed paradoxically **higher HU but softer clinical bone quality** than non-grafted sites:

- Grafted sites: higher HU vs non-grafted (p<0.001) — graft opacity inflates the metric
- Grafted sites: softer clinical quality (Misch grade, intraoperative tactile) vs non-grafted (p=0.003)
- HU–clinical quality correlation: **significant** in non-grafted sites (p<0.001) but **non-significant** in grafted sites

Subgroup breakdown by graft material is the critical finding:

| Graft type | HU–quality correlation vs no-graft | p |
|---|---|---|
| Allograft | Not significantly different | ≥0.07 |
| **Xenograft** | **Significantly different** | **0.02** |

**Mechanism**: Xenograft particles (e.g., Bio-Oss HA) are slowly resorbing; at 4–6 months post-ARP, substantial mineral scaffold persists. These particles are highly radiopaque — they dominate CBCT HU without contributing equivalent stiffness or native bone volume. The result: a D2-appearing site on CBCT may have D3/D4 clinical resistance at surgery.

**Allograft (FDBA) is different**: FDBA undergoes osteoclastic resorption and replacement by native bone. By the time of implant placement, the allograft-derived scaffold has largely or fully been replaced — HU reflects actual bone.

### FDBA/PRF ARP — HU is a moderate-to-strong predictor

Ivanova et al. (2020, Folia Med, n=30 maxillary patients) performed the only study simultaneously measuring CBCT HU, ISQ, and histomorphometric % new bone formation at the same post-ARP implant sites. Socket preservation used FDBA or PRF (both allograft-class, fully resorbing). CBCT was taken at **4 months** post-ARP.

| Correlation | r | p | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| CBCT HU ↔ ISQ (vestibulo-oral axis) | 0.392 | 0.032 | Moderate; significant |
| CBCT HU ↔ ISQ (mesiodistal axis) | 0.407 | 0.026 | Moderate; significant |
| CBCT HU ↔ % new bone (histomorphometry) | **0.776** | **<0.001** | **Strong; highly significant** |

The strong HU↔histomorphometry correlation (r=0.776) is the mechanistic anchor: CBCT HU after FDBA/PRF ARP reflects **real bone biology** — the attenuation signal comes from genuine newly formed bone, not residual foreign mineral. This translates into the moderate HU→ISQ predictive relationship (r≈0.40; HU explains ~16% of ISQ variance, with implant design and cortical engagement contributing the rest).

**Consistency with Abdulkarim 2021**: FDBA is allograft-class, and Abdulkarim showed allograft sites maintain HU–quality correlation equivalent to non-grafted sites (p≥0.07). These two papers are mechanistically consistent, not contradictory.

## Morphological Predictors — More Graft-Material-Agnostic

Beyond HU, CBCT morphologic parameters offer predictors of ARP success that are less dependent on graft opacity:

From Gan et al. (2023), incorporated in the socket-preservation ARP overview:

| Morphologic predictor | Threshold | Correlation with lower graft resorption |
|---|---|---|
| Bone Concavity Depth (BCD) | ≥1.03 mm | r = −0.872, p<0.001 |
| Bone Concavity Angulation (BCA) | <155.3° | r = 0.686, p<0.001 |

Age, sex, implant brand, and graft material were non-predictive. The BCD/BCA effect is geometric — a deeper concavity and more contained (narrower-angle) defect provides a tent-effect mechanical environment that limits membrane collapse and graft dispersal. This morphologic assessment can be made on pre-ARP CBCT and is independent of what graft material was placed.

Hartmann et al. (2026, retrospective n=80 Bio-Oss Collagen sites) demonstrated 78/80 implant-placeable sites without further augmentation — a volume adequacy judgment based on CBCT 3D Slicer segmentation. Two sites failed for "low bone quality" but no HU threshold was reported, consistent with the hypothesis that Bio-Oss HU is unreliable for quality prediction.

## ARP Sites Are Inherently Low-Density Environments

A critical contextual finding from Ko et al. (2024) in the primary-stability-ARP overview: in a retrospective cohort of 30 ARP-treated sites with DBBM-C (Bio-Oss Collagen), Misch bone quality at implant placement was:
- **Type 3 (D3): 75% of sites**
- **Type 4 (D4): 25% of sites**

This was consistent regardless of whether the implant extended beyond the graft boundary. The implication: **ARP sites are D3/D4 environments by default** — surgical protocol should be calibrated accordingly (undersized osteotomy, extended healing, osseodensification consideration), and the expectation of finding D1/D2 bone on CBCT at 4–6 months post-xenograft ARP should be viewed with suspicion.

## Decision Framework: CBCT HU After ARP

```
ARP was performed
│
├─ What graft material?
│   │
│   ├─ FDBA (freeze-dried bone allograft)
│   │   → HU is moderately reliable (r≈0.40 with ISQ, r=0.776 with new bone)
│   │   → Misch D1-D4 HU ranges applicable with caution (n=30, maxilla only)
│   │   → Confirm with ISQ at placement
│   │
│   ├─ PRF (autologous fibrin, no radiopaque particles)
│   │   → CBCT HU reflects native bone regeneration directly
│   │   → Most reliable HU reading after ARP
│   │
│   └─ Xenograft (Bio-Oss, DBBM, β-TCP with HA)
│       → HU unreliable for bone QUALITY (Abdulkarim 2021)
│       → CBCT is still useful for bone VOLUME (is there enough space?)
│       → Do NOT use HU alone to decide implant readiness
│       → Use: morphologic assessment (BCD/BCA) + ISQ at placement
│
└─ Regardless of graft type: ARP sites are D3/D4 — plan accordingly
```

## What CBCT CAN Still Do After Xenograft ARP

Even when HU is unreliable for quality assessment in xenograft sites, CBCT remains valuable for:

1. **Volumetric adequacy** — is there enough bone volume for the planned implant diameter and length? (Hartmann 2026: 78/80 sites passed this test)
2. **Morphologic geometry** — BCD/BCA for graft resorption prediction
3. **Adjacent anatomy** — nerve proximity, sinus floor height, root proximity
4. **Cortical plate presence** — presence or absence of buccal cortex is visible regardless of graft type

The reframing: after xenograft ARP, CBCT is a **space and anatomy** tool, not a **bone quality** tool.

## Clinical Recommendations (Evidence Summary)

| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is CBCT HU reliable after Bio-Oss ARP? | ❌ No — quality unreliable | Abdulkarim 2021 (retrospective, n=38 xenograft sites) |
| Is CBCT HU reliable after FDBA ARP? | ✅ Moderate (r≈0.40 with ISQ) | Ivanova 2020 (prospective, n=30, 4 months) |
| Is CBCT HU reliable after PRF ARP? | ✅ Likely yes (no residual particles) | Ivanova 2020 (FDBA/PRF combined) |
| What is the best readiness gate for xenograft ARP? | ISQ at placement; morphology (BCD/BCA) pre-ARP | Abdulkarim 2021; Gan 2023 |
| What Misch type should be expected at ARP sites? | D3 (75%) or D4 (25%) | Ko 2024 (n=30, DBBM-C) |
| Does low initial ISQ at ARP sites predict failure? | No — 2° stability compensates by 10 weeks | Ko 2024 |

## Limitations of This Evidence Base

- **Abdulkarim 2021**: pilot study (n=38 graft sites), single institution, graft subtype not granularly specified; allograft was grouped, xenograft was grouped
- **Ivanova 2020**: n=30 only, abstract-only (no full text), maxilla only, FDBA and PRF not separated; 4-month timing only
- **No RCT** directly comparing CBCT HU-guided vs ISQ-guided implant placement timing after ARP
- **No study with Bio-Oss-specific post-ARP CBCT HU thresholds** — the literature gap remains open
- **Misch D1-D4 ranges** (Raghavan 2025) are from native bone; no validated post-ARP–specific HU classification exists
