## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: 측방창 천공을 술 전에 예측하는 해부·행동 인자(모체 클러스터 2)를 모은 하위 종합. 절 번호는 모체 기준을 유지한다. [확인]
> - 중격: 상악동당 유병률 33.2%, 환자당 41.0%(Henriques 2022, 62편 SR+MA, 상동 22,460개). 86%가 횡방향이라 표준 측방창 절개선을 가로지른다. 약 3개 상동 중 1개는 변형 접근이 필요하다. [확인]
> - 중격은 천공 위험을 높인다(Yang 2024, OR 4.03). Nemati 2023 다변량 Cox에서 중격 HR은 8.07이었다. [확인]
> - 막 낭종(MRC)은 Nemati 2023 다변량 모델에서 HR 27.75로, 같은 모델의 중격보다 강한 해부 예측인자였다. [확인]
> - 흡연: Wang 2023 SR+MA(9편, n=1,424)는 OR 1.58을 보고했다. Nemati 2023(고위험 코호트)의 HR 25는 설계와 대상 차이를 반영하므로, 일반화 가능한 쪽은 Wang의 풀링 추정치다. [확인]
> - 막 두께는 연속적 추세이고 명확한 cutoff가 없다. 천공 막은 얇았고(MD −0.91 mm), 아주 얇거나(<0.5 mm) 아주 두꺼운(>3 mm) 막에서 천공이 많은 U자형 관계가 제안된다(Ke 2024, 11편, 1,841 SFE). [확인]
> - 잔존 치조능 높이: 천공군이 낮았다(MD −0.89 mm, Ye 2025, 10편, 1,809 SFE). 풀링 천공률은 19.2%다. [확인]
> - 상악동 점막 기원: 점막 비후는 대부분 인접 치아·치주 질환의 표지다(Khalil 2024, 992 치아; 근단병소 AOR 32.7). 발치 부위가 가장 낮다. 따라서 CBCT의 점막 비후는 연기 사유가 아니라 치과 원인을 찾으라는 신호다. [확인]
> - OMC 해부와 MSO: 오스티움 폭·구 길이·사골 기포 크기가 점막 비후와 양의 상관을 보였고, UPSA 3·4·6형은 주의 신호다(Gulbey 2026, n=1,957). 거상 후 1주에 MSO가 1.8 → 1.1 mm로 일시 좁아졌지만 9개월에 회복되었고 부비동염은 없었다(Sakuma 2020, n=72). [확인]
> - PSAA: 지름이 큰 PSAA는 바닥과 치조능에 더 가까워 출혈 위험이 높다. 창 설정 전 CBCT로 위치를 확인하는 것이 표준 대응이다(Gulec 2025, n=255 상동). [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 가장 생산적인 술 전 판독은 중격의 유무와 위치이고, 다음은 측벽 두께(리머 사용 시), 잔존 골 높이, 막 낭종, PSAA 위치다. 흡연이 가장 큰 교정 가능 위험이다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the anatomical and behavioral perforation-risk cluster of the parent lateral-window sinus-lift synthesis: septa, mucosal thickness, lateral wall, floor and ridge height, mucous retention cysts, the posterior superior alveolar artery, and smoking.
Septa and mucous retention cysts are the strongest anatomical predictors, while smoking is the strongest modifiable one; ridge height and membrane thickness add graded risk without clean cutoffs.
The most productive preoperative read is septum presence and location, followed by lateral-wall thickness, ridge height, retention cysts, and PSAA position.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 측방창 상악동 거상 종합의 해부·행동 위험 절을 담는다: 중격, 점막 두께, 측벽, 바닥과 치조능 높이, 막 낭종, 후상치조동맥(PSAA), 흡연.
중격과 막 낭종이 가장 강한 해부 예측인자이고, 흡연이 가장 강한 교정 가능 인자다. 치조능 높이와 막 두께는 명확한 cutoff 없이 단계적 위험을 더한다.
가장 생산적인 술 전 판독은 중격의 유무와 위치이며, 다음은 측벽 두께, 골 높이, 막 낭종, PSAA 위치다.

## Cluster 2 — Predicting perforation before incision (anatomy, CBCT, and behavior)

CBCT-derived and behavioral risk factors converge on a growing list.

### Septa — the dominant anatomical risk factor

- **Prevalence denominator.** Henriques 2022 (SR+MA, 62 studies, 22,460 sinuses) establishes the pooled per-sinus septa prevalence at **33.2%** (95% CI 27.8–38.5%, I²=98%) and per-patient prevalence at **41.0%** (95% CI 36.0–46.0%). Orientation is predominantly transverse (86%) — exactly the morphology that bisects a standard lateral window osteotomy line. Sex is not a predictor (OR 0.785, p=0.098). Combining Henriques 2022 with Yang 2024's perforation OR, roughly **1 in 3 sinuses requires a modified approach**.
- **Perforation risk.** Yang 2024 (SR+MA, 10 studies, 2,168 lateral SFEs): presence of a septum carries **OR 4.03 (95% CI 1.77–9.19)** for perforation. Altayar 2023 (n=880 sinuses, Yemeni population) found septa in 47% of sinuses and 63.9% of patients — mean height 5.2 mm, most floor-origin, mid-region, coronal-direction, complete-type. Turker 2026 (n=300, modified 7-grade classification) found 91.7% had medial/lateral septa, 40% in the premolar region, with septum **location** statistically tied to risk (p<0.001). Nemati 2023 (prospective cohort, n=140) quantified septa as an independent predictor with **HR 8.07 (95% CI 2.93–22.29, p<0.001)** in a multivariate Cox model including anatomy and behavior simultaneously.

### Sinus mucosal thickness (SMT) and OMC drainage

- **Baseline SMT distribution.** Munakata 2021 (n=40 edentulous sinuses, CBCT) — mean SMT 1.09 ± 1.30 mm; 25% exceeded the 2 mm pathological threshold; SMT was independent of sex, time since extraction, or extraction reason.
- **What drives mucosal thickening — odontogenic and periodontal sources.** Mucosal thickening (MT) is overwhelmingly a marker of adjacent tooth/periodontal disease rather than a primary sinus disorder, which reframes it as a preoperative *risk-stratification* finding rather than a contraindication. Maska 2017 (retrospective CBCT, n=29, mean follow-up 3.3 yr) found 93.1% of sinuses had MT (65.5% severe >5 mm) yet implant and grafting survival were 100%, with **periodontal disease history the single significant predictor (p=0.004)** — smoking, ridge height, and endodontic history were all non-significant. Khalil 2024 (retrospective CBCT, 124 patients, 992 posterior maxillary teeth, multivariable logistic regression) supplies the highest-resolution etiology data: periapical lesion (AOR **32.7**, the dominant factor), first molar position (AOR 3.97), second molar (AOR 2.30), and severe periodontitis (AOR 2.75) are independent MT predictors; crucially, **extracted sites carry the lowest MT odds**, so tooth presence itself drives the odontogenic MT signal (even lesion-free teeth carry AOR 19.8 vs extracted). MT prevalence was 72.6% of patients. Akbari 2022 (retrospective CBCT, 141 implant candidates, 240 sinuses) adds an anatomical correlate: lower residual ridge height correlates with greater MT (inverse relationship), using >3 mm as the pathological threshold — though this contradicts Maska 2017's null ridge-height finding (p=0.889), likely a population/threshold difference. The clinical reading: MT severity is a downstream readout of dental disease, so MT on CBCT should trigger a hunt for the odontogenic source, not automatic deferral of augmentation.
- **Relative occupancy (MSMT/MOD ratio) and OMC patency beat absolute thickness for sinusitis prediction.** Lee 2020 (retrospective, 11 years, Korean tertiary referral center, n=120) introduced the **MSMT/MOD ratio** — maxillary sinus mucosal thickness divided by the ostium-to-floor distance — as a relative-occupancy index. The ratio significantly separated three management groups (no preoperative antibiotics 0.19 / preoperative antibiotics 0.39 / FESS-then-implant 0.78, all pairwise significant), whereas MOD alone discriminated nothing (all NS). One case with a *favorable* ratio (0.23) still developed sinusitis due to ostium obstruction — confirming OMC patency is an independent predictor that overrides the ratio. The center's systematic dentist–ENT collaboration plus CBCT screening yielded a 1.6% postoperative sinusitis rate vs the ~5.1% literature mean. Kim 2023 (retrospective longitudinal, n=9, same Korean center) extends this with outcome data: in ENT-cleared patients with preoperative MT, MSMT did **not** change significantly after implant surgery (4.3 → 4.5 mm, NS) across augmentation/grafting/no-grafting groups, and **zero sinusitis** occurred despite Haller cells (4/9), concha bullosa (2/9), and paradoxical middle turbinate (4/9) — i.e., a thickened membrane vetted through ENT screening is a safe, stable baseline rather than a progressive risk.
- **Thickened membrane is not a contraindication.** Khiabani 2024 (prospective controlled trial, n=40 sinuses, <5 mm vs >5 mm groups) found that LSFE in the thickened group (mean 6.8 mm) caused significant membrane normalization to 3.4 mm at 6 months (p<0.001) without rhinosinusitis in either group. Perforation rates and augmentation adequacy were equivalent. The thickened group had more intraoperative bleeding (p=0.003) and resistance to elevation (p=0.001) — important surgical difficulty differentials to anticipate.
- **Thickness as a continuous trend with no usable cutoff.** Ke 2024 (SR+MA, 11 CBCT studies, 1,841 lateral SFEs, all NOS=7) isolates membrane thickness as a single lateral-window perforation factor: the membrane is significantly **thinner in perforation cases (MD −0.91 mm, 95% CI −1.48 to −0.33, I²=94%)**, yet dichotomizing at 2 / 1.5 / 1 mm yields no significant association at any cutoff (ORs 0.66–0.97, all NS). The proposed explanation is a **U-shaped relationship** — both very thin (<0.5 mm) and very thick (>3 mm) membranes perforate more, with lowest risk ~1–1.5 mm. Clinical reading: thickness matters as a continuous trend but offers no clean threshold, so a thin-membrane alert should be a graded risk modifier, not a go/no-go cutoff.
- **Regional MT variation (premolar > molar).** Abullais 2024 (retrospective CBCT, n=75, 150 sinuses, Saudi Arabia) found premolar MT (1.46±0.49 mm) significantly greater than molar MT (1.17±0.53 mm, p<0.001). Facial index type had no significant correlation with MT or lateral wall thickness — confirming CBCT is mandatory regardless of gross facial morphology. Females had significantly thicker lateral wall thickness (LWT) in both premolar and molar regions (p=0.018/0.032). Age group 40–49 had the thinnest premolar MT (p=0.021), a "thin-membrane" alert window for risk counseling.
- **Upstream OMC drainage as a driver of membrane thickening.** Gulbey 2026 (retrospective CBCT, n=1,957 sinuses) demonstrated that osteomeatal complex (OMC) structural variables — ostium width, infundibulum length, ethmoidal bulla diameter — all significantly positively correlate with maxillary sinus mucosal thickness. UPSA types 3, 4, and 6 (orbital, middle-turbinate, skull-base attachments) significantly associated with polypoid thickening and ostium obstruction. Haller cells showed significant positive correlation with MT; concha bullosa did not. 54% of sinuses had some degree of mucosal thickening. **Clinical implication**: pre-SFE CBCT evaluation should extend beyond the sinus floor to map OMC drainage anatomy; UPSA types 3/4/6 are a red flag for ENT consultation.
- **Post-operative MSO edema.** Sakuma 2020 (retrospective CBCT, n=72 sinuses) showed that lateral-window SFE causes transient maxillary sinus ostium (MSO) diameter reduction from 1.8 mm to 1.1 mm at 1 week post-op (p=0.0001), with infundibulum occlusion in 19.4% of sinuses at that timepoint. By 9 months, MSO recovered to 1.5 mm and occlusion fell to 1.4%, with zero sinusitis cases. Accessory maxillary ostia (AMO) were found in 9.7% of sinuses, positioned ~10.9 mm posteriorly — potentially providing supplementary drainage when the primary MSO is transiently obstructed.

### Lateral wall thickness and vascular anatomy

- **Lateral wall thickness.** Monje 2016 (reamer-drill technique, dual-center retrospective) reported 12.5% SMP rate overall and identified ≥1.25 mm lateral wall as the safe-zone threshold for the reamer approach.
- **PSAA vascular anatomy.** Gulec 2025 (retrospective CBCT, n=255, 510 sinuses, Turkey) characterizes the posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA), the primary source of intraoperative hemorrhage in lateral-window SFE. PSAA is bilateral in 70.2%, intraosseous in ~63%, diameter <1 mm in ~55%, and positioned on average ~16 mm above the alveolar crest. Larger-diameter PSAAs sit significantly closer to the sinus floor and alveolar crest, raising hemorrhage risk. No significant association between PSAA presence and sinus septa or sinus pathology was found. CBCT-based PSAA localization before window placement is the standard-of-care implication.

### Sinus floor anatomy and residual ridge height

- **Sinus floor anatomy.** Manor 2024 (case-control, n=39 CBCTs) — thin floor, retention pseudocyst, septae, and concave/triangular shape associate qualitatively with perforation; the only statistically independent factor in that small sample was **MS floor width**.
- **Residual ridge height (RRH).** Ye 2025 (SR+MA, 10 studies, 1,809 lateral-window SFEs) is the first pooled estimate of RRH as a perforation predictor: mean RRH in the perforated group was significantly lower (MD −0.89 mm, 95% CI −1.47 to −0.31, I²=90%). The categorical small-RRH OR of 2.47 was non-significant overall but reached OR 4.68 (p<0.05) after removing a statistical outlier. Pooled SMP rate across included studies was **19.2%**. The biological rationale is straightforward: lower RRH demands greater membrane elevation arc, increasing tear probability. Ye 2025 explicitly notes that other factors (septa, wall thickness, smoking) likely interact — RRH should be considered alongside, not instead of, the full preoperative CBCT checklist.
- **Ridge-height pneumatization question.** Schriber 2019 (50 dentate vs 50 edentulous posterior maxillas, CBCT volumetrics) found **no significant difference in sinus volume, surface area, or maximal diameter** between groups — pushing back on the "sinus pneumatizes after extraction" narrative and reattributing post-extraction vertical loss to alveolar crest resorption rather than sinus expansion.
- **Population/regional anatomy.** Lee 2020 (n=60 Korean young adults, 3D CBCT) — inferior sinus volume/height/width all significantly larger in males and correlate with bizygomatic breadth; useful regional reference. Ananda 2015 (n=60 East Asian) — at the maxillary first molar site, mean residual bone height ~7 mm and root apex projects into the sinus in 50%; implants >7 mm require sinus lift or osseodensification.

### Mucous retention cysts and multi-factor risk models

- **Mucous retention cyst (MRC).** Nemati 2023 (prospective cohort, n=140) identified MRC as the strongest anatomical perforation predictor: **HR 27.75 (95% CI 8.73–88.23, p<0.001)** — substantially exceeding septa (HR 8.07) in the same model.

### Smoking — the dominant modifiable risk factor

- **Wang 2023 SR+MA.** The first pooled estimate of smoking and SMP (9 studies, n=1,424): **OR 1.58 (95% CI 1.10–2.25, I²=5%, Egger p=0.827)**. Low heterogeneity and no publication bias signal make this the most reliable modifiable-risk estimate in the field.
- **Alalshaikh 2024 SR+MA — multi-factor ranking.** A predominantly-retrospective SR+MA that ranks the lateral-window perforation predictors together: the three most prominent are thin membrane (<1.5 mm), sinus septa (≈**3× risk**), and smoking (≈**1.6× risk — roughly one perforation in every seven smokers** treated). Lateral wall thickness and residual ridge height were significant on limited evidence (3 studies), while **window size and number of missing teeth did NOT affect perforation likelihood**, and piezosurgery vs rotary was only marginally protective (P=.05). This independently corroborates the septa/smoking/RRH signals above and supplies a "what does NOT matter" anchor (window size).
- **Nemati 2023 prospective HR.** In the simultaneous multivariate model: smoking HR **25.00 (95% CI 7.58–82.51, p<0.001)** — the second-strongest risk factor after MRC, and the strongest modifiable one. The discrepancy between Wang 2023 OR (1.58) and Nemati 2023 HR (25) likely reflects study design and case-mix differences (Nemati cohort was exclusively RBH <5 mm, very high-risk population); the Wang pooled estimate is the more generalizable number.

**Synthesis [미검증]:** the single highest-yield preoperative read is "is there a septum and where," followed by lateral wall thickness if a reamer approach is planned, RRH for elevation-arc risk, MRC presence, and PSAA localization for window placement safety. OMC anatomy adds a finer-grained layer. Smoking is the primary modifiable risk; counseling and cessation support before SFE is evidence-backed. SMT >5 mm up to <10 mm is no longer a contraindication — it requires anticipating greater surgical difficulty but not deferral.
