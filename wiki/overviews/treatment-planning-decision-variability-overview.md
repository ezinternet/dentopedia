> - **핵심 소견 — 치아의 운명은 치아보다 술자로 더 잘 예측된다**: 근관치료 후 근단주위염 치아의 표준 방사선사진을 본 431명에서 일반의(GDP) 발치율 17.03% vs 근관전문의(E) 5.3% (일반의 OR 4.37·학생 OR 4.41, 둘 다 p<0.001). **독립 재현됨** — 저자들이 인용한 선행 연구(Rodríguez)도 5.63% vs 14.69%로 거의 같은 **약 3배 격차** (Dufey-Portilla 2026).
> - **격차를 키우는 인자는 전부 "치아 쪽"인데 반응은 술자 쪽에서만 나온다**: 간접수복물(GDP OR 2.12)·부적절 근충(OR 1.81)·중등도 병소(**OR 7.26**)가 모두 일반의를 발치로 밀지만, 전문의는 병소 크기에 **반응하지 않았다**(원문: "no E opted for extraction regardless of lesion size").
> - **⚠️ 그러나 "보존이 정답"이라는 전제는 이 위키가 가진 근거로는 지탱되지 않는다** — 이번 종합에서 가장 중요한 발견. Dufey-Portilla는 일반의 발치를 "불필요"로 규정하며 그 근거로 의도적 재식(Intentional Replantation, IR) 성공률 **"88–98%"**를 인용한다. 그런데 같은 배치로 인제스트한 IR의 최신 SR+MA(39편/2,305치)는 **통합 성공 0.78·생존 0.89**이고, **39편 중 31편이 high risk of bias(low 0편)**이며, 생존이 시간에 붕괴한다(3개월 0.98 → 1년 0.91 → **8년 0.63**) (Zhang 2025).
> - **즉 "전문의가 옳다"는 것이 아니라 "양쪽 다 확실하지 않다"** — 전문의의 보존은 실제보다 낙관적으로 인용된 수치 위에 서 있고, 일반의의 발치는 대안을 모르거나 못 쓰는 데서 나온다.
> - **보존 대안의 실제 수치** (계획 시 쓸 것): 의도적 재식 성공 0.78·생존 0.89(8년 0.63, 근거질 낮음), 자가치아이식(성숙치) 생존 95%·성공 92%·치근흡수 16% — **단 어느 쪽도 임플란트와 비교한 적이 없다**(단일군 추정) (Zhang 2025, Li 2026).
> - **AI는 이 문제를 풀지 못한다 — 구조적으로**: AI 교정 발치 결정의 통합 민감도 **70%**(발치 필요 환자 10명 중 3명을 놓침, I²≈96.7%), 그런데 그 **기준표준이 "임상의의 실제 발치 결정"**이다. 불안정한 인간 판단을 재현하도록 훈련된 모델의 70% 일치는 *옳은 결정의 70% 정확도가 아니다* (Ziaei 2025).
> - **AI는 "결정"보다 "탐지"를 훨씬 잘한다**: 탐지 민감도 0.85·특이도 0.94 vs 결정 민감도 0.70. 그리고 'AI 치료계획' SR+MA 27편 중 **실제 치료계획 근거는 3편뿐**(GRADE 낮음) (Alabdulkareem 2026).
> - **그럼에도 유일하게 건설적인 경로**: AI를 *결정자*가 아니라 *합의 조율자*로 쓸 때 — 술자 민감도 60.7% → 85.9%, 관찰자간 kappa +0.12~0.19, 판독시간 53.8초 → 1.5초. 단 4편 기반·GRADE Low–Moderate (Alabdulkareem 2026).
> - **임상 결론**: 발치를 결정하기 직전이 이 페이지를 쓸 지점이다. ①내 발치 결정이 전문의 기저율(약 5%)과 3배 벌어져 있는지 자문 ②그렇다고 무조건 의뢰·보존이 아니라, 보존 대안의 **실제** 수치(0.78/8년 0.63)로 환자와 상의 ③공공부문 연관(22.96% vs 15.24%)은 "지식 부족"이 아니라 **자원·접근성 제약에 대한 합리적 반응**일 수 있으니 도덕화하지 말 것 ④다섯 논문 어디에도 **환자 선호**가 없다 — 그 빈자리를 채우는 것이 [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/elwyn-2025-shared-decision-making-primer-clinicians]]의 공유의사결정(Shared Decision-Making, SDM)이다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 6 papers (plus 2 shared-decision-making sources) asking a question the wiki's 182 topic-specific decision ladders all presuppose an answer to: when clinicians look at the same tooth, do they produce the same plan? They do not — general dental practitioners extracted teeth with post-treatment apical periodontitis at 17.03% versus endodontists' 5.3% (OR 4.37, p<0.001), a roughly threefold gap independently replicated in a separate cited dataset (5.63% vs 14.69%), with tooth-level features (medium lesion OR 7.26, indirect restoration OR 2.12, inadequate root filling OR 1.81) driving generalists toward extraction while endodontists did not respond to lesion size at all.

The reflex reading — that specialists are right and generalists over-extract — is not supported by the evidence this wiki actually holds: the same paper justifies calling those extractions "unnecessary" by citing intentional replantation success of 88–98%, whereas the current SR+MA of intentional replantation (39 studies, 2,305 teeth) pools success at 0.78 and survival at 0.89 with **31 of 39 studies at high risk of bias and none at low**, and survival falling to 0.63 by eight years; autotransplantation of mature teeth reaches 95% survival but as a single-arm estimate never compared with implants.

Artificial intelligence cannot arbitrate the disagreement because it is trained on it: pooled sensitivity for AI extraction decisions is 70% (I²=96.7%) against a reference standard that is literally "conventional assessments by clinicians," and only 3 of 27 studies in the broader AI treatment-planning SR+MA assessed a treatment-planning outcome at all — though AI used as an *assistant* rather than a decider raised clinician sensitivity from 60.7% to 85.9% and improved interobserver kappa by 0.12–0.19, which is the one constructive finding in this literature.

## 세줄요약

6편 종합(+SDM 2편) — 위키의 182개 결정 사다리가 전부 전제하는 것을 되묻는다: 같은 치아를 보면 같은 계획이 나오는가? 아니다. 근관치료 후 근단주위염 치아에서 일반의 발치율 17.03% vs 근관전문의 5.3%(OR 4.37, p<0.001), 인용된 별도 데이터셋에서도 5.63% vs 14.69%로 **약 3배 격차가 독립 재현**됐다. 중등도 병소(OR 7.26)·간접수복물(OR 2.12)·부적절 근충(OR 1.81)이 일반의를 발치로 미는 동안 전문의는 병소 크기에 반응하지 않았다.

**"전문의가 옳다"로 읽으면 안 된다** — 이 위키가 실제로 가진 근거가 그걸 지탱하지 못한다. 그 논문은 일반의 발치를 "불필요"라 하며 의도적 재식 성공률 88–98%를 근거로 들지만, 현행 SR+MA(39편/2,305치)의 통합 성공은 **0.78**, 생존 0.89이며 **39편 중 31편이 high risk of bias(low 0편)**, 8년 생존은 **0.63**으로 떨어진다. 자가이식(성숙치)은 생존 95%지만 임플란트와 비교된 적 없는 단일군 추정이다.

AI는 이 불일치를 중재할 수 없다 — 그 불일치로 훈련되기 때문이다: AI 발치 결정의 통합 민감도 70%(I²=96.7%)이고 기준표준이 문자 그대로 "임상의의 관행적 판단"이며, 광의의 AI 치료계획 SR+MA 27편 중 치료계획 결과를 실제로 평가한 건 **3편뿐**이다. 다만 AI를 결정자가 아닌 **보조자**로 쓸 때 술자 민감도 60.7% → 85.9%, 관찰자간 kappa +0.12~0.19 — 이 문헌에서 유일하게 건설적인 소견이다.

## Thesis

This wiki contains 182 decision-ladder overviews. Every one of them is built on an implicit premise: that a clinician who reads the evidence will arrive at the indicated plan. **These papers test that premise and it does not hold.** The finding is not a curiosity about survey methodology — it is a statement about what a treatment plan actually is. For a tooth with post-treatment apical periodontitis, the single best predictor of whether it is extracted is not the lesion, the restoration, or the root filling. It is **who is looking at the film**.

Three things follow, and the third is the one this wiki is uniquely positioned to say.

**1. The variation is large, systematic, and replicated.** Dufey-Portilla 2026 put seven standardised periapical radiographs in front of 431 people — 47 students, 349 GDPs, 35 endodontists — and asked for one choice among retreatment, apical surgery/intentional replantation, and extraction. GDPs chose extraction at 17.03% against endodontists' 5.3% (OR 4.37; students OR 4.41; both p<0.001). Crucially, the authors cite an independent prior dataset (Rodríguez et al., CBCT-based) reporting 5.63% versus 14.69% — the same ~3× gap in a different sample with a different method. Two independent measurements of the same phenomenon is a different epistemic object than one survey, and it means this cannot be dismissed as a Chilean vignette artefact.

The internal structure of the variation is more revealing than its size. Every tooth-level variable moved the generalists — indirect restoration (OR 2.12), inadequate root filling (OR 1.81), medium lesion (**OR 7.26**), large lesion (OR 1.56) — while the endodontists' decisions were, in the authors' words, unmoved: *"no E opted for extraction regardless of lesion size."* Two groups looked at the same radiographic features and only one group's decisions were a function of them. (Note a genuine tension inside the source: it also reports endodontist extraction "as low as 5.3%", which cannot be reconciled with a literal 0% across every lesion stratum unless those few specialist extractions were driven by factors other than lesion size. The paper does not resolve this; do not quote the 0% as an absolute.)

**2. The obvious moral — "generalists over-extract, refer more" — is only half-earned.** The authors frame the gap as "unnecessary extractions" to be reduced by education, and that framing quietly assumes the endodontist's answer is correct. The study cannot establish that: specialists self-selected into endodontics, so skill, confidence, equipment, referral base, and professional identity are all confounded with group membership, and the design measures **stated intent from a radiograph alone** — no patient, no cost, no access, no preference, no outcome. The public-sector finding (22.96% extraction vs 15.24% private) points at something the education framing misses entirely: in a setting where microscopes, specialists, and retreatment funding are scarce, extraction may be the **rational** plan rather than the ignorant one.

**3. And here is what only this wiki can add: the retention alternatives are weaker than the paper that invokes them claims.** Dufey-Portilla justifies conservative treatment by citing apical microsurgery and intentional replantation "associated with high success rates (78.4–91.3% and 88–98%, respectively)." We now hold the current systematic review of intentional replantation, and it does not say that. [[endodontics/zhang-2025-intentional-replantation-periapical-periodontitis-prognosis-sr-ma]] pools **success at 0.78 (0.70–0.84)** and survival at 0.89 (0.85–0.93) across 39 studies and 2,305 teeth — and the evidence underneath is severely compromised: **31 of 39 studies at high risk of bias, 8 moderate, none low**. Worse for a planning decision, the pooled survival conceals a time course: 0.98 at three months, 0.91 at one year, **0.63 at eight years**. The figure a referring dentist is being asked to trust ("88–98%") is roughly the paper's three-month-to-one-year number presented as if it were the durable one.

The same caution applies to the other preservation route. [[endodontics/li-2026-root-canal-timing-autotransplanted-teeth-prognosis-sr-ma]] gives autotransplantation of mature teeth 95% survival (91–97%) and 92% success — genuinely encouraging, and the best number in this synthesis — but it is a **single-arm pooled estimate from non-randomised studies that never compares autotransplantation with implants**, carries 16% root resorption (8–30%), and shows success falling from 99% at ≤3 years to 77–80% beyond. Prospective studies in it reported 98% survival against retrospective studies' 91% (P=0.02), which is the signature of a literature whose best-designed studies and its most numerous studies disagree.

So the honest synthesis is not "retain more." It is: **both poles of this disagreement rest on evidence too weak to adjudicate it, and the disagreement persists precisely because the evidence cannot settle it.** Variation is what a field looks like when its evidence underdetermines its decisions.

**Can AI settle it? No — and the reason is structural, not technical.** [[artificial-intelligence/ziaei-2025-ai-orthodontic-extraction-treatment-planning-sr-ma]] pools AI extraction-decision performance at 70% sensitivity (61–78) and 90% specificity, with I² of 96.7%/93.7%. Seventy percent sensitivity means missing about three of every ten patients who genuinely need extraction. But the deeper problem is the reference standard: the full text specifies the comparator as *"conventional assessments by clinicians."* The models are trained to reproduce human extraction decisions — the very decisions Dufey-Portilla shows vary threefold by who makes them. **Agreement with a contested standard is not accuracy.** An AI trained on generalists and an AI trained on specialists would both score well against their own teachers and disagree with each other, and no metric in this literature would detect that.

The breadth paper confirms the limit rather than escaping it. [[artificial-intelligence/alabdulkareem-2026-ai-dental-treatment-planning-decision-making-sr-ma]] is titled for treatment planning, but of its 27 studies **only 3 assessed a treatment-planning outcome** (GRADE: low); its headline pooled figures — sensitivity 0.85, specificity 0.94, F1 0.90 — are all *detection and segmentation* metrics, and its segmentation Dice of 0.89 carries a 95% CI of **0.13–1.00**, an interval so wide the pooled value carries no information. Read together, the two AI papers give the cleanest one-line summary available: **AI detects (0.85) considerably better than it decides (0.70)**, and the wiki's 29 existing AI pages are overwhelmingly about the former.

One constructive path does survive. Alabdulkareem's decision-impact studies report clinician sensitivity rising 60.7% → 85.9% with AI assistance, interobserver kappa gains of 0.12–0.19, and interpretation time falling 53.8 s → 1.5 s. That is AI as an **agreement-narrowing assistant**, not as a decider — and agreement is exactly what this overview shows is missing. It rests on 4 studies at Low–Moderate GRADE, so it is a direction, not a conclusion.

A final observation, offered as a hypothesis rather than a finding: the human and machine biases run **opposite**. Generalists over-call extraction (endodontic context); AI models under-call it (orthodontic context, sensitivity 70% vs specificity 90%, a conservative bias Alabdulkareem also notes recurring across tasks — one commercial tool at >98% specificity but 33.33% sensitivity). These are different specialties, different populations, and different definitions of the positive class, so they are not directly comparable — but if the pattern held, deploying such models as a screen in general practice would push in the opposite direction from the practitioner's own bias, which is a research question worth stating.

## Clinical Decision Points

**You are about to extract a tooth with post-treatment apical periodontitis. This page is for that moment.**

1. **Know your own base rate before you judge the tooth.** The specialist base rate for extraction in this scenario is ~5%; the generalist rate is ~17%. If you extract substantially more often than one in twenty such teeth, that is not necessarily wrong — but it is a fact about you, not about your patients, and it is the strongest predictor in the data (Dufey-Portilla 2026).
2. **Watch the specific triggers that move generalists and not specialists.** A medium (4–5 mm) apical lesion multiplied generalists' odds of extracting by **7.26**, while specialists did not respond to lesion size. If lesion size is doing the work in your decision, pause — it demonstrably does not do that work for people who treat these teeth all day.
3. **Do not defer automatically either.** The retention case is routinely cited at "88–98%"; the current systematic review says success **0.78**, from a literature with **no low-risk-of-bias study**, and survival **0.63 at eight years**. If you refer, refer with the real numbers, and make sure the patient hears the eight-year figure rather than the one-year one (Zhang 2025).
4. **Know both preservation routes and what they cost.** Intentional replantation: success 0.78, worse with older age and — firmly — with combined periodontal-endodontic lesions (SRR 0.80, p=0.001), so a perio-endo tooth is the poor candidate. Autotransplantation of a mature tooth: survival 95%, success 92%, but **16% root resorption** and prophylactic postoperative root canal treatment associated with the lowest resorption (11%) — and the scope is complete-root-formation teeth only (Zhang 2025, Li 2026).
5. **Neither review compares its option to extraction-plus-implant.** Nobody has run that comparison. Any "save the tooth versus place an implant" conversation is therefore reasoning across two separate single-arm literatures, and should be presented to the patient as such.
6. **Treat the resource dimension as legitimate.** Public-sector clinicians extracted more (22.96% vs 15.24%). Where retreatment, microscopes, or specialist access are unavailable or unaffordable, extraction can be the right plan. "Reduce unnecessary extractions" is an education slogan that misreads a resource problem.
7. **Use AI to narrow disagreement, not to make the call.** As a decider it runs at 70% sensitivity against a contested human standard. As an assistant it lifted clinician sensitivity 60.7% → 85.9% and interobserver κ by 0.12–0.19. The second use is supported; the first is not (Ziaei 2025, Alabdulkareem 2026).
8. **The missing party in all six papers is the patient.** Not one of these studies recorded a preference, a cost tolerance, or a value. If the profession's own recommendation varies threefold by training, then the "recommend, then discuss" model is negotiating from an unstable anchor — which is the strongest available argument for structured shared decision-making rather than a weaker one (see [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/elwyn-2025-shared-decision-making-primer-clinicians]], [[behavioral-dentistry/communication-relationship/asaad-2019-shared-decision-making-sdm-dentistry]]).

## Gaps & Future Research

- **No study links planning variability to patient outcomes.** Every paper here measures intent (vignette), accuracy (against another human), or single-arm survival. **Nobody has shown that the tooth the specialist retained does better at ten years than the tooth the generalist replaced.** Until someone does, "unnecessary extraction" remains an assertion about process, not a demonstrated harm — and that is the single most important missing study in this synthesis.
- **The AI reference-standard problem is structural and unaddressed.** As long as ground truth is "what clinicians decided," model performance is bounded by, and inherits the biases of, whichever clinicians labelled the data. No study in this set trains or validates against outcomes. Reporting *whose* decisions formed the training labels should be a minimum standard.
- **No head-to-head of preservation versus replacement.** Intentional replantation, autotransplantation, apical surgery, and extraction-plus-implant have never been compared in one design. The pooled figures (0.89, 0.95) come from different populations and indications and cannot be ranked against one another.
- **The intentional-replantation literature has no low-risk-of-bias study at all** (0/39). This is a field-level failure, not a limitation of one review; a single well-conducted prospective cohort would change the evidence base more than another meta-analysis of the existing 39.
- **Resource and access variables are unmeasured.** The public/private difference is the only hint, and it is descriptive. Cost, reimbursement, specialist availability, and travel distance plausibly explain more variance than knowledge does, and none is modelled.
- **Patient preference is absent everywhere.** No included study measured what patients wanted, what they were told, or what they would have chosen given the true numbers. The wiki's shared-decision-making pages describe the process; this literature shows why the process matters — but the two have never been studied together.
- **Variability outside endodontics is unmapped here.** The variation finding rests on one clinical scenario (post-treatment apical periodontitis) plus one procedural cohort. Whether the same threefold gap appears in restorability judgements, periodontal prognosis, or implant-versus-bridge planning is unknown from these sources.
