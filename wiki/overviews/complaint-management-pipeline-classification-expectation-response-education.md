## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 환자 민원을 4단계 파이프라인 — (1) 분류(Classification) → (2) 기대 파악(Expectation) → (3) 대응(Response) → (4) 교육(Education) — 으로 운영화한 통합 페이지(민원과학 21편 + 치과 사전동의 1편 + 치과 적용층 + 소송 tail).
> - 핵심 명제: 불만이 지속되는 이유는 원래의 임상 오류 자체보다 *민원을 어떻게 분류하고, 민원인이 진짜 무엇을 원했는지, 응답을 어떻게 표현했는지*에 있다. 클리닉이 통제할 수 있는 가장 강한 lever는 대응·교육 층위.
> - 비대칭 핵심: 민원인은 압도적으로 **재발 방지와 정직한 설명**을 원하지(Friele 2006: 설명 65% > 사과 41%, 금전 요구는 7%) 돈이나 사과 연극을 원하지 않는데, 조직의 전형적 응답은 정반대로 "fauxpology"(사이비 사과)와 주관화로 간다.
> - 분류 축: Reader 2014 SR(59연구·88,069건)이 임상 33.7%·관리 35.1%·관계 29.1% 3 domain 제시; HCAT(Healthcare Complaints Analysis Tool, Gillespie 2016)이 7범주×4중증도 도구화; 민원이 직원이 놓친 안전사건의 60%에서 더 큰 위해를 보임(Van Dael 2022).
> - 대응의 금지 목록(blocklist): "I'm sorry you feel…"식 사이비 사과, 불만을 환자의 주관적 인상으로 재구성, "일회성 예외" 변명, 화제 전환·조기 종결 — 방어성은 개인 문제가 아니라 구조적 문제(Gillespie 2025).
> - 대응층에 유일하게 무작위배정 근거가 있다(Zheng 2025, `rct`, 개입 334 vs 대조 341): 민원인의 언어·어조로 행동유형을 분류하는 DISC 행동언어모델 (DISC Behavioural-Language Model) 을 표준 민원처리에 얹었더니 총 만족도 83.24%→93.39% (p<0.001), 보상률 8.15%→3.56%, 상급기관 탄원율 15.32%→2.49%; DISC 독립효과 보정 오즈비 (adjusted Odds Ratio, aOR) 3.06 (95% CI 1.66–5.64).
> - DISC 4유형 응대 스크립트가 이 페이지에서 가장 즉시 적용 가능한 도구다 — D(주도형): 즉시 개선 약속 / I(사교형): 경청 의사 표시 / S(안정형): 인정·사과·안심 / C(신중형): 사실 + 구조화된 설명. 감성사전(Boson) 층은 중국어 전용이라 재학습이 필요하지만 DISC 층은 언어 비의존이라 그대로 이식된다.
> - 교육은 CODE 모델(Elias 2025): 운영/절차 훈련(어떤 자원·구제책이 있는가)과 대인/디에스컬레이션 훈련을 *함께* — 한쪽만 하면 관료적 실패 또는 관계적 실패가 재현됨.
> - 치과 적용층: 치과의사가 민원율 최고(42.7/1000/년, Thomas 2018), 근관치료(Endodontics)가 의료과실 청구 노출 최대(Alrahabi 2019) — 러버댐·정확한 기록·표준진료 준수가 곧 임상 안전이자 법적 방어.
> - 소송 tail(한·일 판례): 한국 부제소 합의는 26.5%만 실제 후속 소송을 차단했고(Kwon 2017), 무상 재치료는 배상액에 산입되지 않음 — 합의서는 의례가 아니라 내용(범위·포기 청구·약인)으로 작성해야 함.
> - 수술적 치과의 반복 책임 근거(Park 2016): 감염 즉시 배양·치료, 적시 의뢰, 감염관리 기록, **명시적 술전 동의** — 감염위험 동의 누락은 독립적으로 책임 성립.
> - 사전동의(Informed Consent, IC)는 민원의 상류 차단막이자 소송 방어선이다: 치과의사 대상 법적 청구의 약 90%가 정보전달 결함에서 비롯되고, 지금까지 의료과실보다 IC 결함으로 유죄판결을 받은 술자가 더 많다(Otero 2022) — 서명은 마지막 고리일 뿐, 분석한 치과 동의서 9종 전부가 일반인 가독성 기준(INFLESZ 55점) 미달이었다.
> - 미해결: 치과 특이적 HCAT validation 부재, CODE의 대조군 효과 데이터 부재, 한국어 민원 감성분석 모델 부재. 대응 protocol의 무작위배정 근거는 Zheng 2025 **단일 중국 3차병원·비치과 1편뿐** — 치과·다기관 재현이 없다(이전 판의 "RCT 근거 부재"는 오기였다).

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 22 papers (21 complaint-science + 1 dental informed-consent) organizing patient-complaint research into a four-stage operational pipeline: classification (Reader 2014 SR, 88,069 complaints: clinical 33.7% / management 35.1% / relationship 29.1%; HCAT 7-category instrument) → expectation (complainants want recurrence prevention and honest explanation first; money only 7%, Friele 2006) → response (defensiveness is structural, not individual, Gillespie 2025; fauxpology and subjectivising are the failure modes) → education (CODE model: operational + interpersonal training in parallel, Elias 2025).

Dental applied layer shows dentists carry the highest complaint rate (42.7/1,000/year, Thomas 2018), endodontics the highest malpractice exposure, and Korean covenant-not-to-sue agreements are legally binding in only 26.5% of cases (Kwon 2017).

Clinical bottom line: the strongest lever a clinic controls is the response-and-training layer — the one randomised trial on this axis (Zheng 2025, n=334 vs 341) raised satisfaction 83.24%→93.39% and cut escalation 15.32%→2.49% by behaviour-typing complainants (DISC) before responding; structured complaint classification converts complaints into safety signals (60% of complaints show greater harm than staff noticed, Van Dael 2022), and rubber-dam use plus accurate records — with a readable, procedure-specific consent process (Otero 2022) — is simultaneously clinical safety and legal defense.

## 세줄요약

22편 종합(민원과학 21 + 치과 사전동의 1): 환자 민원을 분류(Reader 2014 SR 88,069건 — 임상 33.7%·관리 35.1%·관계 29.1%; HCAT 7범주) → 기대 파악(민원인은 재발방지·정직한 설명 우선, 금전 7%뿐, Friele 2006) → 대응(방어성은 구조적 문제, "fauxpology"·주관화가 실패 패턴, Gillespie 2025) → 교육(CODE 모델: 운영·대인 훈련 병행, Elias 2025)의 4단계 파이프라인.

치과 적용층: 치과의사 민원율 최고(42.7/1,000/년, Thomas 2018), 근관치료 의료과실 노출 최대; 한국 부제소 합의는 26.5%만 후속 소송 차단(Kwon 2017).

임상 결론: 대응·교육층이 클리닉이 통제할 수 있는 가장 강한 lever — 이 축의 유일한 무작위배정 연구(Zheng 2025, 334 vs 341)에서 DISC 행동유형 분류 후 응대가 만족도 83.24%→93.39%, 탄원율 15.32%→2.49%를 만들었다. 분류는 민원을 안전사건 신호로 전환하고(불만의 60%에서 더 큰 위해 확인, Van Dael 2022), 러버댐·기록 충실도와 읽히는 시술별 동의 과정(Otero 2022)은 임상 안전이자 법적 방어다.

## Thesis
Patient complaints are not noise to be deflected but a structured information source for safety and quality. The evidence converges on a single actionable claim: dissatisfaction persists less because of the original clinical error than because of *how the complaint is classified, what the complainant actually wanted, and how the response is worded*. The strongest lever a clinic controls is therefore the response-and-training layer, supported by a structured classification habit. This overview organises twenty-two papers into the pipeline **classification → expectation → response → education**, then adds a **dental applied** layer (epidemiology, medico-legal, procedural risk) and a **litigation** tail (settlement validity, infection liability, national claim trends, informed-consent liability).

The core asymmetry to internalise: complainants overwhelmingly want recurrence prevented and an honest explanation, not money or apology theatre ([[complaint-management/friele-2006-patient-expectations-fair-complaint]]); yet typical organisational responses do the opposite — they subjectivise the grievance and deploy the "fauxpology" ([[complaint-management/mccreaddie-2021-qualitative-study-nhs-complaint]]) because responders are structurally bound to protect the organisation ([[complaint-management/gillespie-2025-complaint-handlers-bind-defensive]]). Closing that gap is a training and system-design problem ([[complaint-management/elias-2025-successful-handling-patient-complaints]]), not a matter of individual goodwill.

## Clinical Decision Points

For a dental practice translating this evidence into operating procedure:

1. **Log, don't just resolve.** Adopt a lightweight HCAT-style classification (problem category × severity × stage of care) so complaint patterns become visible. A handful of recurring categories will dominate, and a small number of clinicians may drive most complaints (Thomas 2018) — both only show up if complaints are coded, not filed as free text.

2. **Lead the response with explanation + recurrence prevention, not apology theatre.** Friele 2006 is explicit: complainants rank an impartial process, a real explanation, and a change that prevents recurrence above an apology, and very few want money. Open with what happened and what will change.

3. **Pre-empt the expectation gap.** Friele 2015 shows the public expects to be handled badly. Name this directly early ("I want this to be useful, not defensive") to disconfirm the negative prior before it becomes self-fulfilling.

4. **Audit your own wording against the defensive catalogue.** McCreaddie 2021 and Gillespie 2025 give a concrete blocklist: the "fauxpology" ("I'm sorry you feel…"), recasting the complaint as the patient's subjective impression, "one-off exception" mitigation, redirecting/closing the episode. Treat these as red-flag phrases in any written reply.

5. **Type the complainant before you draft the reply (the one randomised finding on this axis).** Zheng 2025 is the only randomised controlled intervention in this synthesis (334 vs 341) and it tested exactly the thing the rest of the page can only argue for: adding a **DISC behavioural-language model** on top of standard receive→register→address→feedback handling. Satisfaction rose 83.24%→93.39% (p<0.001), compensation payouts fell 8.15%→3.56%, and escalation to a higher authority fell 15.32%→2.49%, with DISC carrying an independent aOR of 3.06 (95% CI 1.66–5.64). The operational core is a four-type read of the complainant's language and tone, each with a different correct opening: **D (dominant, "I'll only speak to whoever is in charge")** → acknowledge the point and commit to immediate improvement; **I (influential, loud, attention-seeking)** → visibly listen and respond actively; **S (steady, "could you come take a look?")** → acknowledge fault, apologise, reassure it is being taken seriously; **C (compliant, "give me a reasonable explanation")** → supply facts in a well-structured explanation. Note the split in transferability: the Boson sentiment-lexicon and KANN-DBSCAN clustering layers are Chinese-language-bound and would need retraining, but the DISC response logic is language-independent and transfers directly to a dental complaint encounter [확인 wiki+sources 양 티어 대조].

6. **Fix the system, not just the sentence.** Defensiveness is structural (Gillespie 2025; Van Dael 2022 JRSM). Give whoever handles complaints clear authority, time, and a non-punitive route — otherwise they will default to self-protection regardless of training.

7. **Train both tracks (CODE).** Elias 2025: pair operational/procedural training (what resources/remedies exist) with interpersonal/de-escalation training. One without the other reproduces either bureaucratic or relational failure.

8. **Know the litigation tail.** Treatment/procedure complaints dominate in dentistry (Thomas 2018), endodontics carries the highest malpractice exposure (Alrahabi 2019), and unresolved cases escalate to courts where documentation and indemnity decide outcomes (Vadde 2024). Rubber dam, accurate records, and standard-of-care adherence are simultaneously clinical safety and medico-legal defence.

9. **Write settlement agreements for content, not ritual.** In Korean precedents only 26.5% of covenant-not-to-sue settlements actually barred a later claim — enforceability turns on what the agreement *says* (scope, waived claims, consideration), and goodwill free re-treatment is not credited against compensation (Kwon 2017). For surgical dentistry, the recurring liability grounds are concrete and pre-emptable: culture and treat infection promptly, refer when indicated, document infection control, and obtain explicit preoperative consent covering infection risk — a missing infection-consent is independently actionable (Park 2016). At the macro level, East Asian malpractice claims mostly settle and judged cases lean physician-favorable (Taniguchi 2023, JP 75.8%), but that tilt does not dilute the case-level grounds.

10. **Treat consent as the upstream shutoff valve for this whole pipeline.** The cheapest complaint to handle is the one the consent process prevented. Otero 2022 puts the dental-specific magnitude on it: roughly **90% of legal claims against dentists trace to defects in transmitting information**, and to date more practitioners have been convicted for defective informed consent (Informed Consent, IC) than for malpractice — defective IC generates civil liability for physical *and* moral damage even where no malpractice occurred. Two practical consequences. First, consent is a *process*, not the signed form: a generic document, or one whose header does not match the procedure actually performed, is legally invalid, and a repeat procedure (a second implant, say) needs a fresh consent because the risks have changed. Second, readability is the failure point — all nine official dental consent documents Otero analysed scored INFLESZ 41.24–52.18, every one below the 55-point general-public accessibility threshold, so a signature is no evidence the patient read or understood anything. Courts treat elective/satisfactive dentistry (whitening, much orthodontics, aesthetics) most strictly: **the less necessary the intervention, the greater the disclosure required** — which is precisely where dental complaint and litigation volume concentrates.

## Gaps & Future Research
- **No dental-specific HCAT validation.** Classification evidence is hospital/nursing-derived; transferability to private dental complaints is interpretive [미검증]. A dental HCAT adaptation is an open opportunity.
- **No controlled CODE outcome data.** Elias 2025 is descriptive; effectiveness vs usual training is untested [미검증].
- **Korean context now partial.** Korean court-precedent evidence on settlement-agreement validity and SSI liability is now in (Kwon 2017, Park 2016), and Japanese national trends add an East Asian macro frame (Taniguchi 2023) — but all three are plastic-surgery / cross-specialty, applied to dentistry by analogy of shared civil-law structure [미검증]. Korean *dental*-specific complaint epidemiology and consumer-dispute pathways remain uncovered — see [[practice-management/ha-2023-dentist-issues-constitutional-court-decisions]] for adjacent Korean legal material.
- **Sentiment analytics are language-bound — but only the sentiment layer is.** Zheng 2025 is a three-layer stack, not a single "emotional-language model": the Boson sentiment lexicon and the KANN-DBSCAN keyword clustering are Chinese-trained and a Korean complaint-text equivalent would need separate development, whereas the DISC behaviour-typing layer that carried the independent effect (aOR 3.06) is language-independent. An earlier revision of this page collapsed the three layers into "sentiment analytics" and thereby wrote off the transferable part.
- **Causal evidence on the response side is thin, not absent.** Li 2024 and most response-axis studies are pre/post or case studies. Zheng 2025 is the exception and the field's strongest design — a randomised controlled intervention showing a response protocol moving satisfaction, compensation, and escalation rates — but it is **single-site, Chinese, and non-dental**, and it randomised a bundled intervention, so the DISC component is separated from the analytics components only by regression adjustment, not by design. The real gap is therefore multi-site and dental replication, not the absence of randomised evidence. ## Evidence Update — Written Informed Consent and Litigation Outcomes in Cosmetic Procedures (Grillo 2023)

Grillo 2023 (retrospective 10-year litigation analysis, Brazil 2012–2022; 992 facial cosmetic surgery cases, 122 meeting inclusion criteria) provides litigation-level data on the protective value of written informed consent (WIC). Appropriate WIC was associated with **favorable defendant outcomes**, significantly reducing both litigation losses and financial damage. Mean financial damage was $59,536 (range $75–$500,000). Female patients were significantly more prone to initiating litigation, and the number of legal disputes increased over the decade — consistent with rising patient aesthetic expectations and awareness. For dental cosmetic procedures (whitening, veneers, implant aesthetics), this forensic data reinforces the complaint management framework in this overview: rigorous WIC documentation is not just an ethical obligation but a quantifiable litigation-risk reduction tool.

Otero 2022 supplies the dental-specific half of this axis that Grillo alone could not. Grillo's cohort is facial *cosmetic surgery*, so its transfer to dentistry is by analogy; Otero analyses dental consent documents directly and gives the magnitude — approximately **90% of legal claims against dentists relate to defects in information transmission**, with more practitioners convicted for defective informed consent than for malpractice. The two papers point the same way but do different work: Grillo shows that appropriate written consent *changes litigation outcomes*, Otero shows *why dental consent typically fails* — it is treated as a document rather than a process, and the documents themselves are unreadable (all 9 dental forms below the INFLESZ 55 accessibility threshold). Read together, the operational instruction is not "get a signature" but "run a procedure-specific, plain-language disclosure conversation and record that you ran it."
