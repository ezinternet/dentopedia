## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: 투명교정(CAT)과 고정장치(FA)의 효능 비교(모체 1절)와 가속 보조법(9절)을 분리한 하위 종합. 절 번호는 모체의 axis 번호를 유지한다. [확인]
> - 단순 비발치 부정교합: RCT만 모은 SR+MA(Baneshi 2024, 21편, n=970)에서 ABO-OGS·Little 불규칙 지수·PAR 차이는 없었고, 플라크·치은 지표와 6개월 삶의 질은 CAT가 우위다. [확인]
> - 경계: 18편 umbrella(Yassir 2022)는 CAT가 경중등도에는 효과적이나 중증과 특정 이동(torque·압하·회전)에서 열세이고 재발이 많다고 정리한다. [확인]
> - 이동 유형별 정확도: 성인 전치부에서 회전·torque는 FA가 우위다(Ubuzima 2025). 상악 절치 torque은 가장 정확도가 낮고(49.1–51.5%), 압하는 역전되기도 한다(Gonçalves 2023). [확인]
> - 실사용 전치부 정확도: 전향이 아닌 후향 40명 480개 치아에서 전체 평균 50.3%였고, 압입이 23.1%로 가장 낮았다(Saif 2026). 구치 근심원심 이동은 별도 SR이 제한적이라고 본다(Garcia-Marin 2025). [확인]
> - 회전: SR(Benedetti 2025, 12편)에서 전체 약 65%, 구치 74–85%로 가장 예측 가능하고, 견치는 36–58%로 가장 어렵다. 부착물의 회전 이득은 일관되지 않다. [확인]
> - 발치 증례: Roller Coaster Effect(Zhuo 2026), 하악 곡선 평탄화 실패(Tang 2025, 1.64배 미달), 뿌리 이동 과소(Wang 2025), 구치 근심경사 −6.08°·후퇴 부족 −1.93 mm(Abu-Arqub 2025)가 누적된다. SR+MA(Vicioni-Marques 2025, 14편)도 발치 공간 폐쇄에서 예측-실제 불일치를 확인했다(GRADE very low). [확인]
> - 측절치 원심 경사: 발치 CAT에서 부착물 사용과 견치 우선 후퇴가 기본 설계 선택으로 제시된다(Yan 2025). [확인]
> - 개방교합·깊은 물림: CAT의 개방교합 교정은 절치 압출에 의존하고 구치 압입이 없어(Charoenrat 2025), FATADs보다 overbite 증가가 1.64 mm 적다. 깊은 물림 정확도는 33–48.88%(Husain 2025), 하악 절치 압입은 0.4–1.5 mm·예측성 35–65%다(Fracchia 2025). [확인]
> - 가속과 착용 주기: 진동·레이저 같은 물리적 보조는 일관된 이득이 없고 착용 주기가 기기보다 중요하다(Sirri 2026, RCT 7편). 14일 착용이 후방 압입·원심 경사에서 더 정확했다(Monisha 2024). [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 발치 증례의 CAT는 치아별·이동별 과교정을 계획에 넣어야 하며, 정확도 수치는 보장치가 아니라 상담용 기준치다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the efficacy-versus-fixed-appliance section and the accelerated-orthodontics section of the parent clear-aligner synthesis.
Clear aligners match fixed appliances on occlusal outcome for simple non-extraction malocclusions, but are weaker for severe cases and for torque, rotation and intrusion-type movements, and extraction cases carry compounding penalties.
Plan movement-specific overcorrection for extraction cases, and treat the reported accuracy figures as counseling benchmarks rather than guarantees.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 투명교정 종합의 효능 대 고정장치 절과 가속 보조 절을 담는다.
단순 비발치 부정교합에서는 치열 결과가 고정장치와 같지만, 중증 증례와 torque·회전·압입형 이동에서는 열세이고, 발치 증례에서는 penalty가 누적된다.
발치 증례는 이동별 과교정을 계획에 넣고, 보고된 정확도 수치는 보장치가 아니라 상담 기준치로 쓴다.

### 1. Efficacy vs fixed appliances (the core equivalence + its boundary)

The quantitative anchor is [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/baneshi-2024-clear-aligners-malocclusion-sr-ma]] — an RCT-only SR+MA (21 RCTs, n=970) finding **no significant CAT-vs-FA difference in occlusal outcome** (ABO-OGS, Little Irregularity Index, PAR) for simple non-extraction malocclusions, with CAT favored on plaque/gingival/bleeding indices and 6-month quality of life. The umbrella review [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yassir-2022-cat-vs-fat-overview-systematic-reviews]] (18 SRs) states the same direction narratively while drawing the boundary: CAT is **effective for mild-to-moderate malocclusion but inferior for severe cases and specific movements (torque, extrusion, rotation)**, with greater relapse. Treatment duration in mild-to-moderate crowding is comparable ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/alhamwi-2024-clear-aligner-vs-fixed-treatment-duration-crowding-sr]], GRADE low). The boundary is sharpened by two movement-specific reviews: adult anterior tooth movement ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ubuzima-2025-clear-aligner-fixed-anterior-teeth-movement-adults-sr]], 12 studies) found vertical movement inconsistent and **FA superior for rotation and torque control**; complex/extraction cases ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/jaber-2023-clear-aligners-complex-extraction-vs-fixed-sr]], 6 trials, n=283) reached equivalent ABO-OGS/PAR endpoints but FA achieved better buccolingual inclination and occlusal contacts in less time, with notable predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies for CAT in extraction cases. A movement-type-specific SR focused purely on maxillary incisors ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/goncalves-2023-invisalign-upper-incisor-accuracy-sr]], 5 studies, 612 tooth movements, post-2010 SmartTrack era) quantifies exactly which movements drive that FA advantage: **torque was consistently the least accurate movement** (49.1–51.5%), intrusion sometimes reversed into extrusion, while extrusion (up to 142.4%) and horizontal translation (67.6–78.9%) were comparatively reliable — attachments/Power Ridge improve but do not resolve the torque/intrusion weak points, and no RCT evidence exists.

A retrospective study provides a complementary real-world benchmark for those accuracy figures: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/saif-2026-clear-aligner-anterior-3d-accuracy]] (40 adults, 480 anterior teeth, Invisalign non-extraction, first aligner set without refinement; 10-day change, mean 27 aligners) measured predicted-vs-achieved accuracy across all four movement types simultaneously in the anterior arch, finding an **overall mean accuracy of 50.3%** (upper 47.6%, lower 53.0%). By movement type the ranking is: intrusion 23.1% (worst — upper incisors), followed by mesiodistal rotation (63.32% upper mesial) and mesiodistal tipping (64.85% upper distal), with buccolingual tipping most reliable (lingual 75.52% lower); all four showed significant predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies (p<0.05 for all). The 50.3% overall figure aligns tightly with Lei 2026's ~50% expression-rate synthesis across movement types and isolates intrusion as the floor — confirming ClinCheck's systematic overestimation of achievable anterior tooth movement across all planes. The molar analogue of these anterior accuracy data is addressed by a dedicated SR on **mesiodistal molar movement** ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/garcia-marin-2025-clear-aligner-molar-mesiodistal-sr]], PROSPERO CRD42022357639; 919 screened → 6 included; ROBINS-I; GRADE; search through September 2024): without auxiliaries, predictability of upper and lower molar mesiodistal movement via CA is limited and highly variable. Read alongside saif-2026 (anterior) and the Gonçalves SR (maxillary incisors), the full-arch accuracy hierarchy becomes: buccolingual tipping (anterior) most reliable → mesiodistal rotation/tipping → intrusion (least reliable) → molar mesiodistal movement requiring auxiliary support for clinically useful predictability. A critical baseline consideration for upper second molar accuracy targets: a 3D digital cast study ([[orthodontics/goracci-2021-maxillary-second-molar-angulation-normal-occlusion]], n=41 untreated adolescents with normal occlusion) found that fully-erupted maxillary second molars in natural occlusion show **significantly more distal crown tip** (−18.9° vs Andrews' +0.4°, P<0.0001) and **more lingual inclination** (−10.6° vs −8.0°, P=0.0118) than Andrews' historical values — the reference encoded in most preadjusted bracket and aligner prescription tables. If ClinCheck's U7 tip target derives from Andrews' nearly zero mesial tip, the planned-vs-achieved discrepancy for second molar tipping in distalization cases may partly reflect a prescription target that is nearly 19° off in direction, not only aligner force delivery failure.

Rotation — the movement Ubuzima flagged narratively and Gonçalves did not isolate — now has its own tooth-type breakdown. A dedicated SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/benedetti-2025-clear-aligner-rotation-accuracy-sr]], 12 studies: 1 RCT + 11 non-randomized, adults, 3D digital superimposition of predicted-vs-achieved measured **before refinement**) puts overall rotational accuracy at **~65% (range 36–85%)** and, more usefully, stratifies it by tooth: **molars are the most predictable (74–85%)**, then premolars (54–83%) and incisors (35.1–90%), with **canines the problem tooth (36–58%, ~4.5° residual error)**. That ranking is worth reading against intuition — the canine's near-circular root cross-section gives an aligner little purchase to deliver a couple, which is the same crown-versus-root force-transfer limit Cao 2025's FEA synthesis describes mechanistically in axis 4, expressed as a per-tooth number. Two negative findings matter as much as the ranking: **11 of the 12 studies used attachments, yet the evidence for their rotational benefit remains inconsistent** — converging directly with Muthuswamy Pandian 2025's optimized-vs-conventional derotation null in axis 4 — and IPR's contribution is unproven (the single study isolating it, Kravitz, found 43% accuracy with IPR, not statistically significant). The one actionable lever the review does identify is **staging**: most included studies used 2° per aligner, and reducing to <1.5° per aligner is suggested by the literature to improve accuracy. Certainty is low-to-moderate (the single RCT rated moderate; **no non-randomized study rated low risk of bias**), so ~65% is a planning and patient-counseling benchmark — and a concrete one for axis 11's expectation-calibration problem — rather than a validated guideline figure.

Efficacy also depends on a factor orthogonal to malocclusion type: **wear protocol (activation schedule)**. A dedicated SR ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/monisha-2024-efficacy-of-clear-aligner]], 6 studies, n=449, GRADE very low) found expedited 7-/10-day tray-wear schedules perform comparably to the conventional 14-day schedule for anterior tooth movement, but the single low-RoB RCT directly testing it found 14-day wear significantly more accurate for **posterior intrusion, distal tipping, and buccal torque** — the same movement categories flagged as CAT-weak in axis 1 above — with no pain penalty for shortened wear. The authors propose a hybrid protocol (short wear for simple movements, 14-day for complex ones) rather than a uniform expedited schedule.

**Deep bite** — the vertical counterpart to open bite — now has its own SR-level evidence: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/husain-2025-deep-bite-clear-aligner-sr]] (18 studies, n not pooled) found CAT opens deep bite by 0.4–3.8 mm with treatment accuracy of 33–48.88%, effective for dentoalveolar correction (posterior extrusion, incisor intrusion) but without meaningful skeletal overbite change — mirroring the dentoalveolar-ceiling pattern charoenrat identified for open bite. Lower-incisor intrusion specifically — the key movement in curve-of-Spee leveling — was quantified by [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/fracchia-2025-lower-incisor-intrusion-clear-aligner-sr]] (8 studies): 0.4–1.5 mm achieved, 35–65% predictability, GRADE moderate-low, confirming the intrusion accuracy ceiling as a hard limit on deep-bite correction beyond dentoalveolar rebound. Clinically: CAT deep-bite correction is predictable enough for dentoalveolar presentations but should not be planned as the primary mechanism for skeletal overbite in adult growth-complete patients.

The downstream consequence of the movement-accuracy ceilings documented above is **treatment refinement**. A systematic review characterizing what drives refinement need: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/nogueira-matos-2026-refinement-factors-clear-aligner-sr]] (20 studies, SWiM methodology) found refinement demand is multifactorial — driven by case complexity, extraction treatment, IPR use, initial spacing, sequence length, and movement types (rotation/intrusion/vertical/transverse the worst predictors, directly consistent with the accuracy data throughout this synthesis). Remote monitoring reduces appointment frequency but does not reduce the need for refinement itself, and AI/ML prediction of refinement need remains preliminary. The practical implication: refinement should be budgeted in treatment planning as a structural feature of complex CAT cases rather than treated as a corrective exception.

Vertical correction — specifically anterior open bite — shows a distinctive aligner mechanism: an SR+MA ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/charoenrat-2025-clear-aligner-anterior-open-bite-molar-intrusion-sr-ma]], 10 studies, no RCTs) found CAT closes open bite predominantly via **incisor extrusion** (U1 +0.87 mm, L1 +1.06 mm) with **no significant molar intrusion**, whereas fixed appliances with temporary anchorage devices (FATADs) achieve significantly greater total overbite gain (+1.64 mm more) primarily through **molar intrusion** (U6 +1.88 mm) — the same anchorage/torque-transmission constraint seen in axis 2's molar-distalization anchorage loss recurring in the vertical dimension; CAT-corrected open bites were stable in retention (6 months–>1 year), though certainty is low-to-very-low with no RCTs available. Predictability itself varies by product line: a retrospective analysis of the reduced-movement **Invisalign Lite** variant ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/meade-2026-invisalign-lite-efficacy-retrospective]], n=122, abstract-only) found overjet and mandibular incisor inclination tracked plan well, but overbite correction accuracy worsened as initial overbite increased (proportional bias) and maxillary incisor inclination under-achieved plan by −2.905° — a maxilla-mandible torque-accuracy asymmetry consistent with axis 4's design-dependence thesis.

Extraction-based CAT carries a specific, now-named posterior complication: the **"Roller Coaster Effect"** (mid-arch posterior open bite from premature anterior/posterior contact during space closure). A retrospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhuo-2026-the-roller-coaster-effect]], 65 patients post four-premolar extraction) operationalized it via posterior functional cusp distance (FCD): the first molar showed the largest increase (mesiobuccal +1.64±1.39 mm, p<0.001), with **mild-crowding cases paradoxically at higher risk** than moderate-to-severe crowding, and a smaller ANB angle plus greater upper incisor proclination as independent risk factors. The narrative review [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/lei-2026-advancements-in-biomechanics-and]] supplies the mechanistic name and explanation (posterior anchorage/torque loss during space closure), alongside two sibling CAT-specific phenomena — the **Watermelon Seed Effect** (uneven force from asymmetric crown coverage) and the **Pendulum Effect** (anterior lingual tipping/extrusion reacting to posterior retraction) — and reports an overall CAT movement "expression rate" averaging only ~50%, ranging from 58–75% for molar distalization (most reliable) down to 13–52.9% for vertical movement (least reliable, upper incisor intrusion worst). A related extraction-specific biomechanical penalty affects the mandibular curve of Spee (CoS): a retrospective comparison ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/tang-2025-evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-clear]], n=60, 30 extraction/30 non-extraction) found extraction patients undercorrected CoS 1.64× more than predicted by ClinCheck software (prediction gap −1.38 vs −0.84 mm, p<0.001), attributed to reduced aligner stiffness/anchorage over the longer inter-support span after first-premolar extraction and current attachment designs (G5/G6) not being optimized for combined sagittal-vertical control — reinforcing that extraction cases carry compounding biomechanical penalties (Roller Coaster Effect + CoS leveling failure) beyond simple space closure. A companion 3D CBCT-superimposition study on the maxillary arch ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/wang-2025-clear-aligner-premolar-extraction-3d-tooth-movement]], 47 patients, 542 teeth, four-premolar extraction) extends this from crown-level to **root-level** discrepancy: root movement was consistently underachieved versus crown movement (maximum gap at the U1 root, 5.49±2.30 mm anteroposterior), anterior teeth extruded despite a designed intrusion, and U3 crowns reached only 71.5% of designed distal movement — meaning ClinCheck planning for extraction CAT needs systematic root/torque overcorrection, not just crown-level target adjustment. A split-mouth RCT testing one candidate mitigation ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/wonghinkong-2026-canine-retraction-palatal-power-arm-rct]], 18 adults, in-house aligners) found a **palatal power arm** during premolar-extraction space closure did not change linear canine-distalization accuracy but significantly reduced canine rotation (mean diff 5.22°) and distal crown tipping — an angular-control auxiliary, not an anchorage fix (both sides still showed anchorage loss), and IHCA-specific pending replication in commercial systems.

The question of whether aligners can reliably close **extraction space** itself — at the whole-case level — now has an SR+MA: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/vicioni-marques-2025-space-closure-extraction-clear-aligner-sr]] (14 studies, 510 patients; search through November 2024; 4 low-bias, 10 high-bias; GRADE very low to low) meta-analyzed predicted-vs-actual tooth movement for extraction-space closure cases. Consistent with Wang 2025 (crown vs root underachievement) and Tang 2025 (CoS leveling failure), the pooled finding confirms significant predicted-vs-achieved discrepancies for the extraction arch as a whole. The GRADE very-low certainty means these results are directionally concordant but insufficiently precise for a specific numeric target; the clinical message is that extraction CAT should plan systematic overcorrection across all movement types, and that the three individually documented biomechanical penalties (Roller Coaster Effect, CoS leveling failure, root-movement underachievement) are now joined by an SR+MA-level confirmation of global space-closure underprediction.

Movement-level detail within extraction-space closure is now available from a dedicated SR+MA: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/abu-arqub-2025-clear-aligner-extraction-sr-ma]] (20 studies, 536 patients) quantified the most clinically consequential intraoral consequence of incomplete space closure — **mesial molar tipping at −6.08°** (pooled) and **under-retraction of −1.93 mm** — while also confirming inferior root-control compared to fixed appliances. These numbers directly operationalize the "systematic overcorrection" principle above: the specific movements that demand overcorrection are not uniform (Wang 2025's root-crown gap peaks at U1, abu-arqub's tipping peaks at the molar), suggesting that overcorrection planning for extraction CAT must be tooth-position-specific rather than a global percentage adjustment.

A further extraction-specific angular risk — independent of the Roller Coaster Effect and CoS leveling failure — is **maxillary lateral incisor mesiodistal tipping** during space closure. A retrospective CBCT study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yan-2025-mesiodistal-angulation-maxillary-lateral-incisor-clear-aligner]], 27 patients, 54 lateral incisors, four-premolar extraction) identified significant distal inclination at the lateral incisor (p<0.001) and isolated three independently modifiable design factors: **attachment presence** reduced distal tipping (88.4% vs 86.9% accuracy, p<0.001); **canine-first retraction** sequence significantly preserved angulation vs en masse (p<0.001); **horizontal movement distance** was dose-dependent (>4 mm: p=0.008; >7 mm: p=0.01); crown-to-root ratio had no effect. The lateral incisor is biomechanically vulnerable in extraction CAT — small blunt crown limits aligner seating, simultaneous mesiodistal and labiopalatal movement demands compound, and AlBalaa reported actual CAT precision for lateral incisor movement at only 51.19%. ABO OGS requires apex deviation ≤1 mm post-treatment, making angulation control clinically consequential. Practical design response: specify **attachment + canine-first retraction** as default choices for any extraction case with lateral incisor involvement.

### 9. Accelerated orthodontics — adjuncts do not help

Physical adjunctive interventions (vibration devices, low-level laser therapy) marketed to accelerate movement / improve tracking / reduce pain do **not** produce consistent benefit in CAT — no improvement in Little Irregularity Index, only transient day-1–3 pain reduction, vibration helping tracking only under a 7-day change cadence, GRADE low to very low ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/sirri-2026-physical-adjunctive-interventions-clear-aligner-rct-sr]], 7 RCTs, n=266). The change cadence matters more than the device.
