## Three-line Summary

The ubiquitous 47°C/1-min bone necrosis threshold originates from Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983 — a rabbit tibia heated-chamber in vivo study (n=15), not a drilling study — and represents the lower bound of detectable tissue reactions (hyperemia onset), not a single validated necrosis threshold: Kniha 2020 SR (25 animal studies, 455 animals) confirmed the evidence range spans **47–55°C/1 min** with no single clear value. [확인: PMC full text reviewed this session, doi 10.3390/ma13163461]

Eriksson 1984's direct human in vivo drilling measurements reveal the species-translation gap: cortical bone thickness drives peak temperature linearly — rabbit 1.5 mm → 40°C, dog 3.5 mm → 56°C, human femur 6.5 mm → 89°C (same drill parameters), yet **actual human mandible implant drilling peaked at 33.8°C** far below threshold, showing that standard irrigated implant protocol keeps temperature in a clinically safe zone. [확인: pubmed-abstract source, PMID 6524329]

Bone quality modulates the threshold independently: at 50°C/1 min cancellous bone BIC decreased significantly while cortical bone BIC was unchanged (Kniha SR) — meaning maxillary (cancellous-dominant) implant sites carry higher thermal risk than mandibular (cortical-dominant) ones at the same heat input, and 47°C/1 min is a conservative guideline that is well-supported by cross-specialty validation (Timon 2019 orthopedic: ~50°C). [확인: PMC full text, doi 10.3390/ma13163461 and doi 10.7759/cureus.5226]

## 세줄요약

무수히 인용되는 47°C/1분 골괴사 임계값은 Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983 — 토끼 경골 가열 챔버 in vivo (n=15), 드릴링 연구가 아님 — 에서 유래했으며, 단일 괴사 임계값이 아니라 조직반응(충혈, hyperemia) 시작의 **하한선**이다; Kniha 2020 SR (동물 생체 내 25편, 455마리)은 범위가 **47–55°C/1분**이며 단일 명확한 값이 없음을 확인했다. [확인]

Eriksson 1984의 인간 대상 직접 측정은 종(種) 번역 갭을 드러낸다: 피질골 두께가 최고온도를 선형 구동 (토끼 1.5mm→40°C, 개 3.5mm→56°C, 사람 대퇴골 6.5mm→89°C, 동일 드릴 조건), 그러나 **인간 하악 임플란트 드릴링의 실제 최고온도 33.8°C**로 임계값 훨씬 하회 — 올바른 관주 프로토콜이 임상 안전 구간을 보장함을 보여준다. [확인]

골 질이 임계값을 독립적으로 조절: 50°C/1분에서 해면골 (Cancellous Bone) 골-임플란트 접촉률 (Bone-to-Implant Contact, BIC) 유의하게 감소, 피질골 (Cortical Bone) 불변 (Kniha SR) — 상악(해면골 우세)이 하악(피질골 우세)보다 열손상 위험 높음; 47°C/1분은 교차 전문과 검증(Timon 2019 정형외과: ~50°C)을 통과한 보수적 하한 기준이다. [확인]

## Thesis

The 47°C/1-min threshold functions well as a clinical safety anchor precisely because it is conservative — but it is frequently misunderstood as a single validated sharp boundary rather than the lower end of an evidence range, and it is derived almost entirely from animal models in which the bone geometry differs substantially from human jaw bone. This overview synthesizes four held papers to answer: *what is the actual evidence quality behind the threshold, what is the species-translation gap, and what does this mean for clinical implant drilling safety?*

The short answer: the threshold is a sensible and well-supported conservative lower bound; the concern for practical implant drilling is overblown because proper protocol keeps actual bone temperatures 10–15°C below it; the threshold becomes clinically relevant primarily when protocol fails (no irrigation, blunt drills, cortical bone accumulating heat in deep osteotomies).

## 1. Origin of the 47°C/1-min Threshold

The threshold traces to Eriksson & Albrektsson 1983 (rabbit tibia, heated chamber applied directly to bone for 1 min at specified temperatures). That study was **not a drilling study** — it used a fixed-temperature chamber to study bone metabolism at controlled temperatures, not rotating instrumentation. The findings: 44°C/1 min caused no damage, 47°C/1 min produced the first histologic reactions (hyperemia onset), and damage increased progressively with temperature and duration.

The design choice — a fixed-temperature chamber rather than a drilling model — means the threshold identifies when any sustained heat source of 47°C causes tissue reactions, but it says nothing about whether implant drilling protocols actually reach or sustain that temperature. The subsequent Eriksson 1984 paper (PMID 6524329) addresses exactly that gap with direct in vivo measurements during drilling.

**47°C/1 min was always a lower bound, not a threshold:** it is the temperature/time at which the first detectable tissue reactions (hyperemia) begin, not the confirmed point of irreversible necrosis. Rouiller (1953) placed the threshold at 55°C/1 min; Lundskog (1972) at 50°C/30 s. All three values derived from animal models. Reviewing 25 animal in vivo studies, Kniha 2020 SR found no single consistent threshold and concluded the evidence range spans **47–55°C for 1 min**. [확인: PMC full text, PMID 32781597]

## 2. What Eriksson 1984 Actually Found: The Species-Translation Gap

Eriksson 1984 (PMID 6524329) is the companion paper that measured bone temperature during actual drilling — in vivo, in patients and in animals, with thermocouples at standardized positions. This is the missing link between the 47°C threshold and practical drilling.

**Key finding: cortical bone thickness is the primary driver of peak drilling temperature.**

| Species | Cortical thickness (mm) | Peak temp (°C) | Protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rabbit tibia | 1.5 | 40 | Same drill, same parameters |
| Dog mandible | 3.5 | 56 | Same |
| Human femur | 6.5 | 89 | Same |
| **Human mandible** | **~1–2** | **33.8** | Standard implant protocol |

The human mandible drilling peaked at 33.8°C with standard clinical protocol including irrigation. This is **13.2°C below the 47°C lower bound** and well below the 55°C upper end of the evidence range. [확인: pubmed-abstract source, PMID 6524329]

**The implication**: the high temperatures seen in animal models (dog mandible 56°C, human femur 89°C) result from thick cortical bone accumulating heat during drilling. These are not predictions for mandibular implant drilling — they confirm that bone geometry determines heat generation, and the human jaw, being thinner cortically than a dog's, generates proportionally less heat in a standard osteotomy.

This also explains why animal model temperatures cannot be directly extrapolated to human jaw drilling: rabbit tibias and dog mandibles have different cortical thicknesses, and even human femur data (used in orthopedic research) does not predict human jaw temperature.

## 3. Kniha 2020 SR: The Evidence Range is 47–55°C

The most rigorous synthesis of the threshold evidence (Kniha 2020 SR, Materials, PROSPERO CRD42019141867) reviewed 25 in vivo animal studies (455 animals: 224 rats, 117 rabbits, 70 emus, 41 sheep, 10 dogs) and was unable to perform meta-analysis due to high clinical heterogeneity. [확인: PMC full text, PMID 32781597]

Key findings:

| Temperature | Duration | Bone effect |
|---|---|---|
| 44°C | 1 min | No damage |
| 47°C | 1 min | First reactions (hyperemia) — Eriksson lower bound |
| 48°C | 1 min | Osteocyte apoptosis in calvarial study |
| 48–50°C | 1 min | Likely heat boundary (most studies) |
| 50°C | 30 s | Threshold per Lundskog 1972 |
| 55°C | 1 min | Threshold per Rouiller 1953 |
| 180–190°C | seconds | No necrosis (Arnoldi 2012) — time dominates |

**The bone-quality finding is clinically critical**: at 50°C/1 min:
- Cancellous bone: BIC significantly decreased, infrabony pockets significantly increased
- Cortical bone: BIC unchanged, pocket depth unchanged

This means the same thermal event damages cancellous and cortical bone differently. At 60°C/1 min both tissue types were affected, but cancellous sites still showed larger effects. The mandible (cortical-dominant) tolerates more heat than the maxillary premolar/molar area (cancellous-dominant).

**Review conclusion**: "No clear threshold value for bone necrosis is available according to the current literature. The values ranged between 47 °C and 55 °C for 1 min." [확인]

## 4. Kniha 2020 Pilot: Time-Compressed Thresholds

The Kniha 2020 pilot study (Clin Oral Investig, PMID 32500401) tested porcine cadaver jaws at shorter exposure times: 49–56°C/10 s and 1–5°C/30 s, in Camlog 3.8 mm osteotomy sites. [확인: PMC full text, PMID 32500401]

**51°C for 10 seconds** caused significant bone matrix degeneration (p<0.01). This is consistent with the temperature×time reciprocal relationship: shorter duration requires higher temperature for the same tissue effect. The 47°C/1-min threshold does not stand alone — there is likely a threshold curve, with equivalent damage achievable at higher temperature/shorter duration.

**Critical limitation**: cadaveric model (no blood flow). In vivo, blood flow dissipates heat faster, meaning the actual thresholds in living tissue are likely higher than 51°C/10s. This was the pilot for the subsequent Kniha 2023 rat in vivo study.

**Osteocyte condition vs matrix degeneration**: at 10 s exposure, osteocyte count scoring was not significantly affected even at 56°C, while matrix degeneration was. Matrix degeneration is the more sensitive endpoint at short exposures — osteocyte scoring may underestimate damage in brief high-temperature events.

## 5. Cross-specialty Validation: Timon 2019

Timon & Keady 2019 (Cureus, orthopedic perspective, Cochrane/PubMed/ScienceDirect through Dec 2017) independently placed the critical threshold at **~50°C** — slightly higher than the dental 47°C, adding cross-specialty validation that the range is roughly 47–50°C. Both reviews reach the same two conclusions: no single threshold, and irrigation is the primary mitigation. [확인: wiki page reviewed this session]

The difference in anchor value (47°C dental vs 50°C orthopedic) reflects differences in underlying studies used; the two values are compatible with the 47–55°C range from Kniha SR, with the dental community using the more conservative lower bound.

## 6. Synthesis: What Does This Mean for Clinical Implant Drilling?

**The threshold question and the drilling safety question are distinct.** The threshold literature tells us when heat causes tissue damage. The drilling temperature literature (Eriksson 1984, and the broader category covered in the companion overview) tells us what temperatures implant drilling actually generates. These two literatures must be combined to assess clinical risk.

| Question | Answer from held papers |
|---|---|
| What is the threshold? | 47–55°C/1 min (range, Kniha SR; dental lower bound 47°C, orthopedic ~50°C) |
| Is 47°C a sharp line? | No — it is hyperemia onset (lower bound), not confirmed necrosis threshold |
| What does drilling actually generate? | Human mandible: 33.8°C with standard protocol (Eriksson 1984) |
| Safety margin | 33.8°C vs 47°C lower bound = ~13°C margin |
| When is the threshold relevant? | When protocol fails: no irrigation, blunt drills, thick cortical sites |
| Does bone type matter? | Yes: cancellous > cortical susceptibility at same temperature |
| Is 47°C valid for clinical use? | Yes — conservative, cross-specialty validated, appropriate as clinical upper limit |

**The TikTok claim that "drilling reaches 47°C and kills bone" deserves context**: in a properly irrigated, properly sequenced implant osteotomy, peak temperatures in the human mandible are well below 47°C (Eriksson 1984 direct measurement). The threshold becomes relevant when the protocol degrades — the companion overview ranks the most important mitigations.

## 7. What We Do Not Know (Gaps)

- No human jaw in vivo histologic data with direct correlation between drilling temperature and osseointegration failure. All threshold data comes from animal models.
- Species-translation gap: rabbit, rat, dog, sheep, emu thresholds are not directly transferable to human jaws.
- The bone quality moderator (cancellous vs cortical) is well-established in animal models but not prospectively studied in human maxillary vs mandibular implant survival rates attributable specifically to thermal damage.
- Time-temperature reciprocal curve in human jaw bone is not empirically characterized — 47°C/1 min is known; shorter/higher combinations are extrapolated.
- Cold thermo-explantation thresholds (5°C/30s in cadaveric model, Kniha pilot) have not been validated in vivo.
