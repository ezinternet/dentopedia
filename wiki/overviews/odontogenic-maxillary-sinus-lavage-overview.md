## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 상악동 세척(Maxillary Sinus Lavage/Irrigation)은 감염원 제거(Source Control)를 *대체*하지 않는다. 보유한 모든 세척 프로토콜은 원인치·임플란트·감염 이식재·이물·낭종을 같은 회차에 제거하고, 세척은 그 위에 얹는 **배액 경로**다. 경쟁 상대는 감염원 제거가 아니라 내시경 부비동수술(Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, FESS)·칼드웰-뤽(Caldwell-Luc, CL) 같은 다른 배액 방식이다.
> - **경로 7종**: 발치와(Kim 2025, n=7)·측방 소창(Jaiswal 2024, n=21)·견치와 개창+튜브 배액(Hong 2017, n=1)·측방벽 소파(Kim 2010, n=2, 초록만)·하비도 개창(Inferior Meatal Antrostomy, IMA)+카테터(Gang 2021, RCT n=40)·임플란트 내부 포트(Nahlieli 2018 n=11 / 2020 n=31, 둘 다 초록만, 동일 그룹 → n 합산 금지)·**근관 경유(Iikubo 2002, n=1)** — 발치 없이 원인치 근관으로 식염수 15 mL 1회 주입, 근관치료+항생제 6주 불응 증례에서 2일 내 증상 소실·6개월 CT 완전 소실(단 동시 치료·무대조라 효과 분리 불가).
> - **발치와 세척(Kim 2025)이 유일하게 단계별로 기술된 외래 프로토콜**: 국소마취 하 원인치·임플란트 제거 → **19G 바늘**로 발치와 통해 상악동 의도적 천공 → 배양 → 생리식염수 세척(**직립 자세 + 턱 밑 농반 + "아" 발성**으로 흡인 예방) → 경험적 항생제 후 배양 기반 전환 → **3일–2주 간격** 반복. 평균 **2.7±1.3회**(중앙값 3, IQR 2–4)로 해소, 구강상악동루(Oroantral Fistula, OAF) 0건.
> - **자연공 개존 bedside 확인 + 중단 규칙**: 발치와로 넣은 식염수가 **코로 나오면** 자연공(natural ostium) 개존으로 해석(전례). **2–3회 세척에도 흐름·배액 호전 없으면** 비치성 원인 탐색 + 이비인후과(ENT) 협진. 재발 시에도 ENT.
> - **측방 소창(Jaiswal 2024)은 "세척 단독"이 아니다**: 항생제 1–2주 무반응 후, 치조정 **≥10 mm 위 3-mm 라운드 버** 창 → 염증 점막·이물 제거 → 생리식염수 또는 요오드 용액 1회 세척 → 지혈 패킹. 전신마취 16/21. CBCT 혼탁 감소 16/21(76.1%), 증상 호전 17/21(80.9%) — 그러나 대조군 없고 점막·이물 제거와 효과 분리 불가, 본문 수치 불일치 있음.
> - **유일한 대조 비교(Gang 2021)는 FESS가 아니라 CL 대비, 그것도 낭종 상황**: 대형 치성낭종 적출 후 IMA로 경막외 카테터 삽입, **생리식염수 100 mL를 20-mL 주사기로 하루 2회, 머리 숙여 코로 배출, 2주 후 카테터 제거** vs CL 점막 박리+패킹. 술후 1일 시각통증척도(VAS) 2.50 vs 4.15, 2일 발열 1/20 vs 8/20, 중증부종 2/20 vs 6/20; 치유·8–12주 CT 소실은 양군 동일. 무작위화·배정은폐·눈가림 미기술.
> - **적응증은 논문마다 다르다**: Kim 2025는 급성(≤4주)·아급성(4–12주)만, Hong 2017·Kim 2010은 급성 술후 상악동염, Nahlieli는 **만성 재발성** 비부비동염(Chronic Rhinosinusitis, CRS, 치성/비치성 구분 없음), Gang은 낭종 동반 상악동염. "세척은 비만성용"은 클러스터 결론이 아니라 Kim 2025의 입장.
> - **합병증·위험 메모**: 발치와 세척은 의도적 구강상악동 교통을 만들지만 반복 주사기 세척으로 OAF 0(유치 배액관은 OAF 위험 때문에 회피). 임플란트·이식재 관련 증례는 **잔존 골이식 입자 때문에 혼탁이 남거나 증가**(Jaiswal 3/21 증가). 카테터 세척은 전례 비루·후비루(대개 2주 내 소실). OAF가 있으면 감염 조절(급성: 약물+세척) 후 폐쇄(Kwon 2020 — 단, 그 세척 단계는 Hong 2017 인용이라 독립 근거 아님).
> - **근거 장부**: RCT 1(보고 부실, 낭종 집단) + 후향 증례군 2(전문) + 초록만 비대조 2(동일 그룹) + 증례보고 5(측방벽 소파 1편 초록만, 근관 경유 1편 포함). **세척 vs FESS, 세척 vs 항생제 단독, 발치와 세척의 장기 재발률 비교는 하나도 없다.** Albu 2025의 "임플란트 관련 ~79% ESS 필요"와 Kim 2025가 인용한 "19명 중 79%"는 같은 Kim n=19 코호트라 독립 근거가 아니다.
> - **미기술 공백(Not specified)**: 회당 세척량(Gang 100 mL만 기술)·압력·주입 장치·요오드 농도·바늘 진입 위치/각도·세척액 온도·해당 증례군의 항생제 기간·패킹 제거 시점·추적 기간(Kim 2025·Nahlieli)·카테터 합병증 — 위키는 이 값을 채우지 않는다.
> - **임상 takeaway(보유 근거로부터의 추론)**: 가장 지지되는 영역은 "급성·아급성 + 어차피 원인치/임플란트를 빼서 발치와 접근 가능 + 세척액이 코로 나옴"이고, 2–3회 무반응이면 ENT로 넘긴다. 이식재/임플란트 관련은 입자 제거가 관건. 만성·자연공 폐쇄·타 부비동 파급은 세척 근거 밖 → FESS ± 구강내 병용(Nam 2014) 경로. 상악동 혼탁 환자에 세척과 동시 임플란트 식립(Nahlieli·Park)은 술전 만성 부비동염이 측방 거상술 후 감염·임플란트 상실의 최강 예측인자(p=0.007, Kozuma 2017)라는 근거와 긴장 관계다.
> - **범위 경계**: 비강 생리식염수 세척(nasal saline irrigation, Chong 2016·Tait 2018·Gnanasekaran 2024)은 일반 만성 비부비동염·FESS 후 집단의 **경비(transnasal)** 세척이라 상악동 내 세척(antral lavage)과 다르며, 치성 부비동염에서의 효과는 연구된 바 없다(Craig 2022).

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 22 papers (8 lavage-route papers, 4 papers in which irrigation sits inside another drainage or fistula operation, 7 framework/risk papers, 3 nasal-irrigation papers held only to mark the scope boundary) on maxillary sinus lavage/irrigation for odontogenic or dental-procedure-related sinusitis: every held lavage protocol removes the dental source in the same episode, so lavage is a drainage route layered on source control and competes with FESS or Caldwell-Luc, not with source control itself.

Seven routes are described with very uneven detail — including trans-root-canal irrigation without extraction (Iikubo 2002, n=1): extraction socket (Kim 2025, n=7, 19-gauge needle, saline every 3 days to 2 weeks, 2.7±1.3 sessions, no oroantral fistula), small lateral window (Jaiswal 2024, n=21, one intraoperative saline/iodine irrigation with debridement and packing, opacity down in 16/21), canine-fossa antrostomy with tube drain (Hong 2017, n=1, 4 sessions 4 days apart), lateral-wall curettage (Kim 2010, n=2, abstract-only), inferior meatal antrostomy with catheter (Gang 2021, RCT n=40, 100 mL saline twice daily for 2 weeks after cyst enucleation) and an implant with an internal port (Nahlieli 2018/2020, abstract-only, same group, never summed).

The only controlled comparison is Gang 2021 against Caldwell-Luc in a cyst population (less early pain, fever and swelling, equal cure; randomization undescribed); no held paper compares lavage with FESS or antibiotics alone, irrigant volume, pressure and iodine concentration are mostly unreported, and the best-supported niche is acute/subacute disease with socket access, a nasal-outflow patency check and a 2–3-session stop rule before ENT referral.

## 세줄요약

상악동 세척(Maxillary Sinus Lavage/Irrigation) 22편 종합(세척 경로 8편 + 다른 배액·누공 수술 안의 세척 4편 + 틀·위험 논문 7편 + 범위 경계용 비강세척 3편): 보유한 모든 세척 프로토콜은 같은 회차에 치성 감염원을 제거하므로, 세척은 감염원 제거의 대체가 아니라 그 위에 얹는 배액 경로이며 경쟁 상대는 내시경 부비동수술(FESS)·칼드웰-뤽(Caldwell-Luc)이다.

경로 7종(근관 경유 Iikubo 2002 n=1 포함)의 기술 수준은 크게 다르다 — 발치와(Kim 2025, n=7, 19G 바늘, 3일–2주 간격, 2.7±1.3회, OAF 0)·측방 소창(Jaiswal 2024, n=21, 점막·이물 제거와 함께 술중 1회 식염수/요오드 세척 + 패킹, 혼탁 감소 16/21)·견치와 개창+튜브(Hong 2017, n=1, 4일 간격 4회)·측방벽 소파(Kim 2010, n=2, 초록만)·하비도 개창 카테터(Gang 2021, RCT n=40, 낭종적출 후 100 mL 하루 2회 2주)·임플란트 내부 포트(Nahlieli 2018/2020, 초록만, 동일 그룹 → 합산 금지).

유일한 대조 비교는 낭종 집단에서 칼드웰-뤽과 비교한 Gang 2021(조기 통증·발열·부종↓, 치유 동일, 무작위화 미기술)뿐이고 세척 vs FESS·항생제 단독 비교는 없으며 세척량·압력·요오드 농도는 대부분 미기술 — 가장 지지되는 영역은 "급성·아급성 + 발치와 접근 + 코로 세척액 유출(자연공 개존) + 2–3회 무반응 시 ENT 의뢰"다.

## Thesis

Lavage is a **drainage technique, not a cure for the source**. This page is the dedicated home for the topic that [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]] summarises in its "Lavage / Irrigation Axis"; the parent overview's thesis (odontogenic maxillary sinusitis, OMS, resolves when the dental source is treated, with sinus drainage as the adjunct) is unchanged here. What this page adds is the route-by-route, step-level detail and an explicit ledger of what the held papers do **not** say.

Three propositions organise the evidence:

1. **Source control comes first in every held protocol.** Kim 2025 removes the tooth or implant at the same visit as the first irrigation; Jaiswal 2024 removes inflamed membrane and foreign bodies and closes the dental cause; Hong 2017 explants the implant and evacuates infected graft; Gang 2021 enucleates the cyst; Nahlieli 2018/2020 drain pus through the implant channel. No held paper tests lavage *instead of* removing the cause.
2. **The routes differ mainly in access, anaesthesia burden and schedule, and almost nowhere in tested outcome.** Only one route (inferior meatal catheter, Gang 2021) has a concurrent comparator, and that comparator is Caldwell-Luc after cyst surgery, not FESS.
3. **Ostial patency is the hinge.** The socket-lavage logic depends on a patent natural ostium (Kim 2025 reads saline exiting the nose as the bedside check), which is also the ENT decision variable ([[sinus-lift/lateral/kim-2019-maxillary-implants-otolaryngologist-perspective]]). Where the ostium is obstructed or disease spreads beyond the maxillary sinus, the held evidence points to FESS ± intraoral surgery (Nam 2014), and lavage has no comparative data.

## Scope — what "lavage" means on this page

- **In scope (antral lavage):** saline or other fluid introduced *into the maxillary sinus* through an oral or antral route (socket, bony window, antrostomy, catheter, implant port), repeated or intraoperative, as part of managing sinusitis of odontogenic, iatrogenic or dental-cyst origin.
- **Adjacent, read for protocol detail only:** irrigation performed *inside* another drainage operation — intentional membrane incision during a lateral-window lift (Park 2024), mucosa-preserving Caldwell-Luc with a nasoantral window (Ohyama 2022), FESS combined with intraoral implant removal (Nam 2014), and cavity irrigation at ESS to prevent antrolith formation (Shenoy 2013). The oroantral-fistula algorithm of Kwon 2020 is included because it places irrigation before fistula closure.
- **Out of scope but held (boundary marker):** transnasal saline irrigation for chronic rhinosinusitis or after FESS — [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/chong-2016-saline-irrigation-chronic-rhinosinusitis]] (Cochrane review, Chong 2016), [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/tait-2018-budesonide-saline-irrigation-chronic-rhinosinusitis]] (RCT, 80 enrolled, 61 analysed, Tait 2018) and [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/gnanasekaran-2024-nasal-irrigation-postoperative-fess]] (SR+MA of 14 studies, Gnanasekaran 2024). These are general CRS or post-FESS populations irrigated through the nose. Craig 2022 states that no studies have analysed whether odontogenic sinusitis patients benefit from topical saline irrigation ([[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/craig-2022-odontogenic-sinusitis-state-art]]), so their results should not be transferred to antral lavage or to OMS.

### 1. Extraction-socket lavage (Kim 2025)

Source: [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2025-treatment-of-non-chronic-odontogenic]] (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, 2019–2023; n=7, 5 male / 2 female, mean age 52.6±16.6 years). This is the only route with a full chairside step sequence.

1. **Work-up.** CBCT (with initial radiographs, CT or Waters' view in some) identifies the causative tooth or implant; unilateral disease only; teeth removed when judged non-restorable after endodontic or periodontal therapy.
2. **Removal + intentional perforation, same visit, local anaesthesia.** Extract the tooth or remove the implant, then perforate the sinus floor through the socket with a **19-gauge needle** so the sinus drains into the mouth. A large pus discharge followed in the illustrated case.
3. **Culture and sensitivity** at the perforation visit.
4. **Saline irrigation** through the drainage route, patient **upright**, **pus pan under the chin**, patient saying **"Ah"** — the authors' stated reason is aspiration-pneumonia risk, because discharge can pass out of the nose into the throat.
5. **Antibiotics.** Empiric antibiotic immediately after extraction, switched to culture-guided therapy. Illustrated case (tooth #26; *Viridans streptococcus*, *Candida albicans*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, penicillin-resistant): cephalexin 1 g bid × 5 days, then ciprofloxacin 500 mg bid × 7 days. One case (Case 2) resolved with lavage and no specific antibiotic, a single observation.
6. **Repeat** every **3 days to 2 weeks**, set by patient cooperation and discharge volume (illustrated case: every 2 weeks, pus falling markedly).
7. **Patency check.** Saline injected via the socket emerged from the nose in all 7 patients; the authors take this as evidence of a patent ostium. They could not assess ostial function before disease onset (stated limitation).
8. **Stop and escalate.** Stop when pus, symptoms and radiographic signs resolve. If no irrigation flow or no drainage improvement after **2–3 irrigations**, look for non-odontogenic causes and involve otolaryngology; recurrence also prompts ENT consultation.
9. **Design choice.** A sutured indwelling oral drainage tube was avoided because of oroantral-fistula risk; repeated syringe irrigation produced no fistula.

Outcome: 2.7±1.3 sessions (median 3, IQR 2–4); no OAF; planned ENT surgery cancelled in the illustrated case. Follow-up duration is not stated.

### 2. Small-lateral-window irrigation (Jaiswal 2024)

Source: [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/jaiswal-2024-sinus-irrigation-as-an-adjunctive]] (Pusan National University Dental Hospital, 2016–2022; n=21; 16 male; mean age 53.71±15.27 years; OAF present in 7).

1. **Selection.** All patients received antibiotics for 1 week; if improving, another week; if not improving, surgery to remove the odontogenic cause and irrigate.
2. **Access.** Vestibular or intrasulcular incision with vertical releasing cuts; window made with a **3-mm round bur at least 10 mm above the alveolar crest** (instead of the conventional thinnest area below the infraorbital foramen) to avoid limiting later implant surgery or grafting.
3. **Debridement + irrigation.** Remove inflamed membrane and foreign bodies, irrigate with **saline or iodine solution** (allocation not stated), **pack for hemostasis**, prescribe antibiotics afterwards. Where a dental cause was removed, primary closure was done (Discussion).
4. **Anaesthesia.** General with nasotracheal intubation in 16, monitored care in 2, local in 3.

Outcome: opaque volume (CBCT, ImageJ) decreased in 16/21 (76.1%), unchanged in 2, increased in 3; mean reduction 40.15% (unit undefined); symptoms improved in 17/21 (80.9%), none worse. Radiographic decrease by cause: extraction 5/5, implant 5/7 (2 increased), sinus graft 5/7 (1 increased); etiology vs improvement p=0.29. The text describes one irrigation at surgery, not serial lavage, and the effect of irrigation cannot be separated from membrane and foreign-body removal. Internal inconsistencies: implant subgroup 3+3+1 in prose vs 4+2+1 in Table 2.

Citation-chain caution: Jaiswal's Discussion describes Gang 2021 as Caldwell-Luc "followed by sinus irrigation", which does not match Gang's two-arm design (irrigation arm vs Caldwell-Luc arm). It also cites a two-patient lateral-irrigation report with CBCT opacity decreasing at 2 weeks; this is consistent with Kim 2010 (n=2, post-implant) but the reference list is not in our held text, and Kim 2010's own abstract reports no CBCT data. Do not import either secondary statement as data.

### 3. Canine-fossa antrostomy with tube drain (Hong 2017)

Source: [[sinus-lift/lateral/hong-2017-novel-approach-to-the-maxillary]] (n=1, 47 years). Acute right maxillary sinusitis 10 days after crestal osteotome lift, graft and implant at another clinic (foul odour, sinus tenderness, headache, nasal pus, Waters' view haziness).

1. Explant the infected implant.
2. Lateral antrostomy through the **canine fossa** (thinnest part of the anterior wall); aspirate pus with a syringe for culture (alpha-hemolytic *Streptococcus viridans*).
3. Insert a suction-irrigation tip and lightly irrigate with saline to remove remaining infected graft.
4. Suture a **tube drain** to the gingiva; place a **flexible silastic suction tip in the ipsilateral nostril** toward the ostiomeatal complex so saline does not flow out of the nose and mouth; irrigate copiously through the drain.
5. **Four sessions 4 days apart**; remove the drain on the last day.
6. Amoxicillin 500 mg, pseudoephedrine 60 mg, carbocisteine 750 mg and NSAID, three times daily for **21 days**.

Outcome: headache relieved immediately after graft/implant removal; no pus after four sessions; CT at 3.5 months read as recovery. The authors call it a blind technique requiring accurate sinus anatomy, and their claimed advantages over Caldwell-Luc/ESS (fewer complications, less bleeding, shorter time, lower cost) are asserted, not measured.

### 4. Lateral-wall curettage with regular irrigation (Kim 2010)

Source: [[sinus-lift/lateral/kim-2010-diagnosis-and-treatment-of-maxillary]] (n=2, abstract-only). Curettage through the lateral sinus wall, antibiotics and sinus irrigation improved post-implant sinusitis (abstract: "after 2 weeks of the operation"); the authors recommend a direct lateral-wall approach, regular in-hospital irrigation and antibiotics for *acute* maxillary sinusitis. No step-level detail is held.

### 5. Inferior meatal antrostomy + catheter irrigation (Gang 2021)

Source: [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/gang-2021-comparing-the-efficacy-of-sinus]] (Fujian, 2012–2018; 20 vs 20).

1. **Pre-op.** Root-treat non-vital involved teeth first. Intravenous-inhalation (general) anaesthesia.
2. **Cyst surgery.** Gingival-fold or intrasulcular incision with releasing cuts; enucleate the cyst with the adherent sinus membrane that cannot be separated; keep the remaining sinus mucosa.
3. **Drainage route.** Inferior meatal antrostomy with an antrostomy knife at the thinnest bony point of the cyst wall on CT.
4. **Catheter.** Pass an **epidural catheter** through the antrostomy into the cyst cavity; suture it near the base of the nose; close the oral incision with absorbable sutures.
5. **Antibiotic.** Cefazolin 2000 mg IV twice daily × 3 days; sinus precautions for 2 weeks.
6. **Irrigation.** **100 mL normal saline via a 20-mL syringe, twice a day**, head lowered so fluid leaves through the nose; **remove the catheter at 2 weeks**.
7. **Expected sequelae.** Rhinorrhea with postnasal drip in all patients, usually gone within 2 weeks; swelling falls from day 3 and resolves within 1 week.

Comparator arm: Caldwell-Luc with stripping of diseased membrane and packing. Results: day-1 VAS 2.50 (1.43) vs 4.15 (2.78); day-2 temperature 38.0–39.0 °C in 1/20 vs 8/20; severe facial swelling 2/20 vs 6/20 (all P<0.05 per authors, exact P not reported); no nasal obstruction or hemorrhage in either arm; all cured with CT clearance at 8–12 weeks; no cyst recurrence, nasal obstruction or paresthesia to 36 months (pooled statement). The catheter lies in the cyst cavity, so this is postoperative cavity irrigation after cyst surgery, not lavage for odontogenic sinusitis without a cyst.

### 6. Trans-implant internal-port lavage (Nahlieli 2018 / 2020)

Sources: [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/nahlieli-2018-transimplant-antral-lavage-as-minimally]] (n=11, mean age 68.1 years) and [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/nahlieli-2020-trans-implant-treatment-of-chronic]] (n=31), both abstract-only, same group.

- **2018:** once the implant is fully seated, puncture the sinus membrane through the implant channel; after pus drainage, irrigate with normal saline and inject **100 mg hydrocortisone**. 3 patients via existing implants, 8 combined with implant placement. 9/11 complete relief of most symptoms at day 30; endoscopy showed no active disease; all implants osseointegrated.
- **2020:** drain pus through the punctured floor, then **three lavage sessions**, **four CBCT scans** and **four transnasal endoscopies**, with joint implant/sinus evaluation at **days 30 and 60**. 28 of 31 were referred for sinus elevation and had simultaneous implantation; 3 used an existing implant. 28/31 (90%) relief by day 30; 27 implants in prosthetic use; 1 failure (poor bone quality); no recurrence reported over an unstated follow-up.
- **Overlap warning:** patient overlap between the two series is not stated. Never add 11 + 31.

### 7. Irrigation inside another drainage operation (adjacent evidence)

- **Intentional membrane incision during a lateral-window lift — Park 2024** ([[sinus-lift/lateral/park-2024-surgical-drainage-simultaneous-sinus-floor]], n=2 male smokers, chronic maxillary sinusitis with complete opacification, 2-week amoxicillin/clavulanate failure, FESS refused). Window ~1.5 × 2.0 cm; long horizontal incision with a **#15 blade in the upper third** of the exposed membrane; suction drainage in every direction; **saline irrigation until no inflammatory exudate or residue was seen**; then floor elevation, xenograft with a Prichard elevator covering the perforation, and simultaneous implants. Post-op ciprofloxacin 500 mg + naproxen 275 mg for 2 weeks; no nose-blowing. One year: sinus clearance, 100% implant survival, no postoperative sinusitis; transient nasal bleeding, facial swelling and hematoma reported.
- **Mucosa-preserving Caldwell-Luc with a nasoantral window — Ohyama 2022** ([[sinus-lift/lateral/ohyama-2022-novel-management-severe-odontogenic-maxillary]], n=1, OMS spreading to the frontal sinus). Canine-fossa access, apicoectomy + retrograde filling of #25/#26, **saline irrigation**, thickened mucosa preserved, large inferior-meatus window; tetracycline gauze packed through the window and **removed at 1 week**; clarithromycin for 1 month. Recovery and patent window at 2 years. The authors argue that ostium-based ESS drainage is anatomically mismatched for OMS because pus pools below the ostium — the same inferior-meatus route Gang 2021 used for its catheter.
- **FESS + intraoral surgery — Nam 2014** ([[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]], n=1). Implant protruding 5 mm into the sinus, obstructed ostium and ethmoid opacification after 4 weeks of failed medication. FESS (middle turbinate excision, uncinate removal, ostium enlargement, pus and graft evacuation, **saline irrigation**, mucosa preserved) plus intraoral implant removal and OAF closure; nasal packing 1 week; CT recovery at 10 months.
- **Cavity irrigation to clear residual fragments — Shenoy 2013** ([[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/shenoy-2013-maxillary-antrolith-recurrent-sinusitis-case]], n=1). Bone chips left by a Caldwell-Luc operation decades earlier became the nidus of a 2×1 cm antrolith with recurrent sinusitis and OAF; the authors recommend thorough cavity irrigation at ESS as prevention.

### 8. The oroantral fistula as a drainage route (Kwon 2020)

[[oral-surgery/kwon-2020-closure-oroantral-fistula-local]]: if sinusitis accompanies an OAF, infection control precedes closure because the fistula tract itself acts as a drainage route and closing it first may exacerbate the infection. Acute sinusitis: amoxicillin/clavulanate 1 g/125 mg three times daily for 10–14 days, nasal decongestants and NSAIDs, with routine sinus irrigation described as helpful; chronic sinusitis: ESS or Caldwell-Luc before closure. The irrigation statement cites Hong 2017 (Kwon reference 29), so it is not independent support for lavage.

### 9. Trans-root-canal irrigation without extraction (Iikubo 2002)

[[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/iikubo-2002-nonsurgical-treatment-for-odontogenic-maxillary]] (case report, n=1, 25-year-old woman, Tohoku University Dental Hospital) is the only held route that keeps the causal tooth. A left maxillary second molar with apical periodontitis had caused acute OMS that persisted through 6 weeks of root-canal treatment plus antibiotics. One session of saline irrigation through the root canal (15 mL total as 3 × 5 mL via a 23-gauge needle, followed by 15 mL of air) abolished cheek pain, nasal obstruction and retrorhinorrhoea within 2 days; CT at 1 week showed near-disappearance of mucosal thickening and marked improvement of ostiomeatal-unit obstruction, with complete resolution at 6 months and no recurrence or side effect at 12 months. Limits: n=1, concurrent antibiotics and endodontic therapy, no comparator, so effect cannot be separated from delayed resolution of the source treatment. It fits proposition 1 of the Thesis — the source was already under treatment — and, like socket lavage, presumes a canal that communicates with the sinus. Read against Kim 2025 (my judgment): trans-canal irrigation is the tooth-preserving analogue of socket lavage for a restorable tooth, but it rests on one case and has no stop rule or schedule.

## Indications and Exclusions as Reported (not validated)

| Criterion | What the held papers report |
|---|---|
| **Acuity** | Kim 2025: acute (≤4 weeks) or subacute (4–12 weeks) only. Hong 2017 and Kim 2010: acute postoperative sinusitis. Jaiswal 2024: no duration criterion; selection was failure of 1–2 weeks of antibiotics, and the conclusion is phrased for acute iatrogenic OMS. Nahlieli 2018/2020: chronic (recurrent) rhinosinusitis. Gang 2021: sinusitis with a large cyst. Park 2024: chronic. **Acuity is not a shared criterion across the cluster.** |

## Comparators — what is and is not controlled

- **One concurrent comparison, against Caldwell-Luc, in cyst surgery** (Gang 2021). Lower early morbidity fits the Caldwell-Luc morbidity profile in [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/kim-2019-definition-management-odontogenic-maxillary]] (postoperative maxillary cyst, paresthesia, wall sclerosis). Randomization, allocation concealment and blinding are not described; mucosal or mucociliary recovery was not measured; antrostomy patency was not reported (the authors cite 82% closure of 367 inferior meatal antrostomies at 3 months from other literature).
- **No held paper compares lavage with FESS/ESS.** Kim 2025's argument that socket lavage "may spare FESS" rests on FESS-need rates quoted from other studies (48% of 43 patients; 79% of 19 post-implant patients; Wang et al. 33% FESS only + 33% FESS plus dental surgery). [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/albu-2025-treatment-landscape-odontogenic-sinusitis]] reports ~79% ESS need in implant-related ODS from "Kim n=19" — the same cohort, so the two pages do not confirm each other. Albu's ~97% one-stage dental + ESS success and Kim 2025's 7/7 resolution are compatible only if lavage candidates are a selected subgroup (acute/subacute, patent ostium, source already removed). That is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated finding.
- **No held paper compares lavage with antibiotics alone.** Kim 2025's Case 2 (resolution with lavage and no specific antibiotic) is one observation.
- **No head-to-head between routes.** Socket vs window vs canine fossa vs catheter vs implant port is untested; differences in anaesthesia (local for socket; general in 16/21 window cases and in Gang) are descriptive.
- **Consensus position.** As held, [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/lin-2024-odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-expert-consensus]] places drainage via ESS in its algorithm; our page records no lavage route in it. Lavage therefore sits outside the consensus algorithm, supported by uncontrolled series.
- **Drainage philosophy is itself contested.** Ohyama 2022 (n=1) favours an inferior-meatus window over ostium-based ESS; the ostium-centred algorithms (Lin 2024; Kim 2019 ENT) put ESS at the drainage step. No held paper compares them.

## Complications and Risk Notes

- **Oroantral fistula.** Socket lavage deliberately creates an oroantral communication with a 19-gauge needle; with repeated syringe irrigation (no indwelling drain) no OAF occurred in 7 patients (Kim 2025). Jaiswal 2024 had pre-existing OAF in 7/21 (all 5 extraction cases arose from an oroantral fistula) and closed the dental site primarily. Kwon 2020 warns that closing an OAF before infection control can worsen sinusitis.
- **Aspiration and fluid escape.** Kim 2025 positions the patient upright with a pus pan and "Ah" phonation for aspiration risk; Hong 2017 places a suction tip in the nostril to keep saline from escaping through the nose and mouth; Gang 2021 has the patient lower the head so fluid exits the nose. These positioning instructions differ by route and none was compared.
- **Persistent or rising opacity with retained bone particles.** In Jaiswal 2024, opacity increased in 3/21 (2 implant-related, 1 graft-related); the authors attribute this to bone particles that irrigation did not wash out. Hong 2017 and Park 2024 explicitly evacuate graft/residue before or during irrigation, and Shenoy 2013 shows retained bone chips acting as an antrolith nidus decades later. Reasoning from these: in graft- or implant-related sinusitis, mechanical evacuation of particulate material seems to matter more than the irrigant; this has not been tested.
- **Catheter-related.** Gang 2021: rhinorrhea and postnasal drip in all irrigation patients, usually resolving within 2 weeks; no nasal obstruction or hemorrhage. Catheter displacement, blockage or infection and antrostomy closure are not reported.
- **Implants placed into a diseased sinus.** Nahlieli 2020 placed implants simultaneously in 28 of 31 CRS patients (1 failure); Park 2024 placed implants simultaneously in 2 chronic cases. In a lateral-augmentation cohort, pre-operative chronic sinusitis was the strongest predictor of both postoperative infection and implant loss (p=0.007 each; 8/121 sinuses, 6.6%) ([[sinus-lift/lateral/kozuma-2017-chronic-sinusitis-sinus-augmentation-infection]]). The lavage series are too small and too thinly reported to resolve this tension.
- **Other reported events.** Jaiswal 2024: one implant-related patient with postoperative facial pain. Park 2024: transient nasal bleeding, facial swelling and hematoma. Hong 2017: blind technique dependent on anatomical knowledge.
- **Anaesthesia burden.** The window route was done under general anaesthesia in 16/21 (Jaiswal); Gang, Ohyama and Nam used general anaesthesia. Socket lavage (Kim 2025) is the only route reported entirely under local anaesthesia.

```
        │       expect less consistent CBCT clearance when bone particles remain
        │
        ├─ Large odontogenic cyst filling the sinus?
        │     → enucleation + inferior meatal catheter irrigation is the only route with a
        │       concurrent comparator: less early morbidity than Caldwell-Luc, equal cure (Gang 2021)
        │
        ├─ OAF present?  → control infection (acute: drugs ± irrigation) before closure (Kwon 2020)
        │
        └─ Chronic disease, obstructed ostium, ethmoid/frontal spread?
              → outside the comparative lavage evidence: FESS ± intraoral surgery (Nam 2014;
                Lin 2024; Kim 2019 ENT) or mucosa-preserving inferior-meatus window (Ohyama 2022, n=1)
```

1. **Lavage without source removal has no held support.** Use it only as the drainage step of a source-control episode.
2. **The socket route fits when extraction or explantation is already indicated**, because access is created by the treatment itself under local anaesthesia; the nasal-outflow check and the 2–3-session stop rule are the only operational safeguards held.
3. **Graft- and implant-related sinusitis is a particle problem as much as an infection problem** (Jaiswal's rising-opacity cases; Hong's and Park's evacuation steps; Shenoy's antrolith nidus).
4. **Do not use the trans-implant CRS series to justify implant placement into an infected sinus**: they are abstract-only, etiology-unspecified, and sit against Kozuma 2017's predictor data.
5. **Do not transfer nasal-irrigation trials to antral lavage or OMS** (scope boundary above; Craig 2022).

## Open Questions

- Does socket lavage reduce the need for FESS in acute/subacute OMS, or does it simply select patients who would have resolved with source removal alone? No held paper has a comparator.
- Is the nasal-outflow sign a reliable proxy for ostial patency? It was positive in all 7 Kim 2025 patients, which gives no information on how it behaves when the ostium is partly obstructed.
- Does irrigant choice (saline vs iodine vs steroid additive) matter, or is the mechanical clearance of pus and particles the active ingredient? Unaddressed.
- What irrigation volume, pressure and interval are needed? Only Gang 2021 reports a volume, in a cyst cavity.
- Does lavage plus simultaneous implant placement in a chronically diseased sinus carry the infection/implant-loss risk Kozuma 2017 associates with pre-operative chronic sinusitis? The Nahlieli and Park series are too small and thinly reported to say.
- How often does an inferior meatal antrostomy stay open after catheter irrigation, and does it matter? Gang 2021 did not report it.
