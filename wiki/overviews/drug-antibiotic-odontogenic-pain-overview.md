## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 원칙 하나: 치통 = 감염이 아니다.** 증상성 비가역적 치수염 (Symptomatic Irreversible Pulpitis, SIP), 치수괴사 + 국소농양 (Localized Acute Apical Abscess, LAAA), 치관주위염 (Pericoronitis) — 모두 항생제가 치료가 아니다. 1차 처치는 항상 **결정적 보존 치과치료 (Definitive Conservative Dental Treatment, DCDT): 발수·근관치료·절개배농.** [확인]
> - **ADA 2019 CPG (Lockhart + Tampi)**: SIP±근단치주염 (Symptomatic Apical Periodontitis, SAP) → 항생제 **강한 비권고 (낮은 확실성)**. 치수괴사 + 국소농양 → 항생제 **조건부 비권고 (매우 낮은 확실성)**. 즉시 DCDT 불가 시에만 **지연처방 (delayed prescription)** 옵션: amoxicillin 500 mg 1일 3회. [확인]
> - **근관치료 후 예방 항생제**: Shamszadeh 2020 SR+MA (8 RCT, n=690) — 통증 5개 시점 모두 SMD 유의하지 않음 (전부 p>0.05). 근관치료 후 항생제 예방 투여는 근거 없음. [확인]
> - **유럽 근관치료 항생제 지침 (Segura-Egea 2017, ESE 공식)**: SIP·치수괴사·급성 근단치주염·국소 급성 근단농양 → 항생제 **NO**. 발열(≥38°C)·개구장애 (Trismus)·림프절병증·광범위 봉와직염 동반 시에만 **YES**. 유럽 치과의사 40–86%가 금기 상황에서 처방 중. [확인]
> - **급성 치성감염 경험적 항생제 (Teoh 2021, SR, n=8편)**: 채택된 거의 모든 요법이 임상 성공; **배농 시 무항생제 = 광범위 항생제 동일 결과**. 1차 = 배농·감염원 제거; 항생제는 전신 침범 시에만 보조. 광범위 1차 처방 불필요 — 좁은 스펙트럼 (amoxicillin)으로 충분. [확인]
> - **치관주위염 (Pericoronitis)**: Schmidt 2021 SR (11편) — 경증·중등도는 국소 세척·변연제거가 1차; 항생제는 심부공간 감염·전신 증상 동반 시만. 그러나 **치과의사의 ~75%, 환자 코호트의 >50%가 항생제 처방** — 치과 항생제 과처방 1–4위 진단. 주 원인: 진단 불확실성 (47.3%)·시간 압박 (30%). [확인]
> - **소아 치의학 (Goel 2020)**: 전 지구 항생제 소비량 2000–2015년 65% 증가; 치과의사는 전체 처방의 7–11%. 소아 조기 노출 시 장내 미생물 불균형 (Dysbiosis)·비만·아목시실린으로 인한 법랑질 발달 결손·칸디다 중복감염. 소아에서도 적응증은 성인과 동일: 전신 침범 시에만. [확인]
> - **실제 진료 격차 (Law 2026, PBRN 전향, n=1,723)**: 비수술적 근관치료 후 환자 5명 중 1명(19%)이 항생제 수령 — 가이드라인 적응증을 크게 초과. 소수 인종·저학력 환자가 더 많이 받음 (임상 필요도 차이 아닌 형평성 격차). [확인]
> - **임플란트 예방 항생제 재검토 (Momand 2024, SR+MA, 7 RCT, n=1,859)**: 위약대조·이중맹검 RCT만 포함 → 조기 실패 유의 감소 없음 (RR 0.66, CI 0.30–1.47; **NNT=143**); GRADE 중간. 발치 후 즉시 식립에만 효과 가능성. 건강한 환자의 통상 임플란트 수술에 routine 예방 항생제 불필요. [확인]
> - **즉시 적용 4룰**: ① 치수염·국소 근단농양 → 항생제 처방 전에 반드시 DCDT 실시 ② 치관주위염 → 국소 세척부터; 항생제는 발열·개구장애 동반 시만 ③ 소아 처방 시 체중 기반 용량 + 최단 기간 ④ 통상 임플란트 수술 → 술전 단일 2g로 충분하되 "통상" 건강 환자에서 생략도 증거 근거 있음 (NNT=143).

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 9 papers (ADA CPG, 3 SR+MA, 2 SR, 3 narrative reviews) on antibiotic use for dental pain, pulpal/periapical conditions, and odontogenic infections: the unifying principle is that pain is not infection — systemic antibiotics are contraindicated for symptomatic irreversible pulpitis, pulp necrosis with localized abscess, and uncomplicated pericoronitis; definitive conservative dental treatment (DCDT: pulpotomy/pulpectomy/RCT/I&D) is invariably first-line, with antibiotics justified only when infection spreads beyond the periodontium with systemic signs (fever ≥38°C, trismus, lymphadenopathy, cellulitis).

Prophylactic antibiotics do not reduce post-endodontic pain or swelling at any time point (Shamszadeh 2020 SR+MA, 8 RCTs, n=690; all SMDs non-significant), and a US PBRN prospective study (Law 2026, n=1,723) found 1-in-5 post-RCT patients received antibiotics anyway — with sociodemographic equity gaps showing minority patients prescribed more, not because of greater clinical need; European surveys document 40–86% inappropriate prescribing for endodontic conditions (Segura-Egea 2017), and pericoronitis drives inappropriate prescribing for ~75% of dentists (Schmidt 2021 SR, 11 studies).

When genuine odontogenic infection warrants antibiotics, narrow-spectrum (amoxicillin) is equally effective as broad-spectrum with drainage established (Teoh 2021 SR, no-antibiotic = broad-spectrum in one study), and routine implant prophylaxis produces NNT=143 in high-quality RCT-only analysis (Momand 2024), making it unjustifiable in uncomplicated healthy-patient surgery; drug selection, pediatric dosing principles (Goel 2020 "4 D's"), and the delayed-prescription option for access-limited settings are covered.

## 세줄요약

9편 (ADA CPG·SR+MA 3·SR 2·narrative 3) 종합: 통합 원칙은 **통증 ≠ 감염** — 증상성 비가역적 치수염 (SIP)·치수괴사 + 국소농양·비복잡 치관주위염에 전신 항생제 금기; 결정적 보존 치과치료 (DCDT: 발수·근관치료·근관형성·절개배농)가 불변의 1차 처치; 항생제는 감염이 치주를 넘어 전신 징후 (발열 ≥38°C·개구장애·림프절병증·봉와직염)를 동반할 때만 정당화.

예방 항생제는 근관치료 후 통증·부종을 어느 시점에서도 유의미하게 감소시키지 않으며 (Shamszadeh 2020 SR+MA, 8 RCT, n=690; 전 시점 SMD 비유의), 미국 PBRN 전향 연구 (Law 2026, n=1,723)에서 5명 중 1명이 근관치료 후 항생제를 받았고 — 소수 인종·저학력 환자에게 과처방되는 형평성 격차 존재; 유럽 치과의사의 40–86%가 근관 치료 상태에서 부적절 처방 (Segura-Egea 2017), 치관주위염에서 ~75%가 항생제 처방 (Schmidt 2021 SR, 11편).

실제 감염에 항생제가 필요한 경우에도 배농 확립 시 좁은 스펙트럼 = 광범위 항생제 동일 결과 (Teoh 2021 SR); 임플란트 통상 예방 항생제는 고품질 위약대조 RCT만 분석 시 NNT=143 (Momand 2024 SR+MA, 7 RCT, n=1,859) — 건강 환자 통상 수술에 생략 정당화; 소아 처방은 4 D's 원칙으로 최소화.

## Summary

This overview synthesises the evidence for antibiotic use in settings where dentists most commonly over-prescribe: **dental pain of pulpal/periapical origin, odontogenic infections, pericoronitis, post-endodontic symptoms, and routine implant surgery.** The companion overview [[drug-antibiotic-stewardship-overview]] covers prophylaxis for systemic conditions (IE, joint replacement), extractive procedures, periodontics, sinus-lift, and stewardship interventions. Together they cover the full range of dental antibiotic indications.

The central finding across all 9 papers is consistent: **the most common dental pain scenarios do not require antibiotics — they require dental treatment.** Dentists understand this in principle (91% worry about antibiotic resistance; 93% want updated guidelines; Segura-Egea, Schmidt) yet prescribe inappropriately in 20–86% of situations. The stewardship gap is not knowledge-based but behavioural: diagnostic uncertainty, time pressure, patient expectations, and equity dynamics all drive prescribing beyond the evidence base.

---

## 1. The Core Principle — Pain ≠ Infection

Inflammatory dental pain is driven by chemical mediators (prostaglandins, cytokines, neuropeptides) released by pulpal and periapical tissue under stress — **not by bacterial systemic spread requiring antibiotic treatment.** Antibiotics cannot reach avascular pulp tissue, cannot reduce inflammatory mediators, and cannot resolve the mechanical cause (necrotic tissue, stagnation space, pericoronal flap). This is why the same conclusion emerges independently from the ADA CPG (Lockhart 2019), the ESE review (Segura-Egea 2017), and acute dentoalveolar infection evidence (Teoh 2021): antibiotic use is limited to conditions where infection has already escaped dental boundaries.

**The clinical test for antibiotic justification**: Is there systemic involvement (fever ≥38°C, trismus, lymphadenopathy, spreading cellulitis, difficulty swallowing or breathing)? If no → DCDT first. If yes → DCDT + amoxicillin adjunct, or hospital escalation.

## 2. Pulpal and Periapical Conditions — ADA 2019 Framework

### 2-1. Decision Table by Condition

[확인] ADA 2019 CPG (Lockhart 2019) + companion SR+MA (Tampi 2019); immunocompetent adults ≥18 years:

| Condition | Abbreviation | Antibiotic? | Recommendation strength | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis ± SAP | SIP±SAP | **NO** — refer for DCDT | Strong | Low |
| Pulp necrosis + symptomatic AP | PN-SAP | **NO preferred**; delayed Rx if DCDT unavailable | Conditional | Very low |
| Pulp necrosis + localized acute apical abscess | PN-LAAA | **NO preferred**; delayed Rx if DCDT unavailable | Conditional | Very low |
| Any of above + systemic signs | — | DCDT + amoxicillin; escalate if needed | Good Practice | — |
| Any of above, DCDT immediately available | — | No antibiotic needed | Good Practice | — |

**Delayed prescription option**: When DCDT cannot be arranged immediately (access, after-hours), offer **amoxicillin 500 mg TID** as a delayed prescription — patient fills only if symptoms worsen before the dental appointment. Evidence: patient satisfaction equivalent between delayed and immediate prescribing (91% vs 86%, moderate certainty); antibiotic harms avoided when DCDT succeeds.

**Harm context (Tampi 2019)**: ~50 million dental antibiotic prescriptions/year in the US, 30–85% potentially inappropriate; ~23,000 US deaths/year from antibiotic-resistant infections; 14% of community-associated *C. difficile* preceded by antibiotic use. Only 3 penicillin RCTs underlie the evidence base — none with amoxicillin, clindamycin, or azithromycin.

### 2-2. Evidence Base (Tampi 2019 SR+MA)

[확인] Companion SR+MA to the ADA CPG; updated 2 prior Cochrane reviews; 3 RCTs + 8 observational reports:

| Outcome (condition) | Effect estimate | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Pain at 24–72h (SIP, no DCDT) | MD ≈ 0 (analgesic use p=0.84–0.19) | Very low |
| Pain at 24h (PN-SAP/LAAA + DCDT) | MD 0.09 (CI −0.37–0.55, p=0.69) | Very low |
| Pain at 48h | MD 0.39 (CI −0.13–0.91, p=0.14) | Very low |
| Pain at 72h | MD 0.12 (CI −0.32–0.56, p=0.59) | Very low |
| Intraoral swelling | Null pattern throughout | Very low |

No RCT evidence exists for amoxicillin (only penicillin tested); all 3 RCTs carry high risk of bias. Population-level harms (resistance, CDI, anaphylaxis) are moderate-certainty and large.

## 3. Post-Endodontic Symptoms — No Prophylactic Role

[확인] Shamszadeh 2020 SR+MA (JOR, PMID 32681652); pulpal necrosis adults; 8 RCTs (n=690) for pain, 4 RCTs (n=149) for swelling:

| Outcome | Time points | Pooled SMD range | All significant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-operative pain | 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 h | −0.08 to 0.18 | **None** (all p>0.05) |
| Post-operative swelling | 24, 48, 72 h | −0.23 to 0.29 | **None** (all p>0.05) |

**Clinical implication**: A patient with pulpal necrosis undergoing root canal treatment does not benefit from prophylactic antibiotics either pre- or post-operatively. The dominant post-procedural symptom drivers are mechanical (file debris extrusion, incomplete debridement, flare-up) — not microbial proliferation amenable to systemic antibiotics.

## 4. Acute Odontogenic Infections — Drainage First, Narrow-Spectrum Second

### 4-1. Indication Matrix (Segura-Egea 2017, ESE official review)

[확인] Narrative review synthesising 60+ studies + European prescribing surveys across 7 countries:

| Condition | Systemic signs | Antibiotics? |
|---|---|---|
| Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis | None | **NO** |
| Pulp necrosis | None | **NO** |
| Acute apical periodontitis | Pain on biting; widened PDL space | **NO** |
| Chronic apical abscess | Sinus tract; periapical radiolucency | **NO** |
| Acute apical abscess (localized, no fever) | Fluctuant swelling | **NO** — drainage |
| Acute apical abscess + medically compromised | Localized + impaired immunity | **YES** |
| Acute apical abscess + systemic involvement | Fever ≥38°C; trismus; lymphadenopathy | **YES** |
| Progressive cellulitis (<24 h rapid onset) | Spreading soft-tissue involvement | **YES** |
| Persistent/refractory infection | Osteomyelitis; chronic exudation | **YES** |

European prescribing reality at time of review: **40–86% of dentists prescribing antibiotics in contraindicated conditions** across UK, Turkey, Belgium, Lithuania, Spain, Croatia.

### 4-2. Empirical Drug Selection for Genuine Infections

[확인] Teoh 2021 SR (8 studies) + Segura-Egea 2017:

- **Primary intervention**: drainage and source removal (pulpectomy/extraction/incision and drainage) — in one study, no-antibiotic with drainage = broad-spectrum antibiotic outcomes.
- **When antibiotics justified**: narrow-spectrum first; **amoxicillin** (or amoxicillin/clavulanate for anaerobic coverage) is the empirical choice; virtually all regimens studied achieved clinical success regardless of spectrum.
- **Hierarchy**: amoxicillin → amoxicillin-clavulanate (add if anaerobic involvement suspected) → clindamycin (penicillin allergy) — note: clindamycin carries highest fatality risk among dental antibiotics (2.9/million, mostly *C. difficile*; Thornhill 2019 in [[drug-antibiotic-stewardship-overview]]).
- **Broad-spectrum 1st-line not justified**: all narrow-spectrum regimens performed equivalently; antibiotic-free management is justified when drainage is achievable in healthy patients.

## 5. Pericoronitis — Local Treatment First, Antibiotics Only for Spread

[확인] Schmidt 2021 dual-part review + SR (11 studies, 2000–2021):

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Mild/moderate pericoronitis | Local irrigation + debridement; NSAIDs; **no antibiotics** |
| 1st episode, not severe | Operculectomy ± gingivoplasty; no immediate extraction |
| 2nd or subsequent episode | Extraction; do not delay once decided |
| Infection spread / fever / trismus / dysphagia | Metronidazole + amoxicillin; culture if possible; consider hospital |
| Penicillin allergy | Erythromycin |
| Floor-of-mouth swelling / breathing difficulty | Immediate hospital transfer — Ludwig's angina risk |

**Prescribing reality** (Schmidt Part B, 11 studies):
- ~75% of surveyed dentists prescribed antibiotics for pericoronitis
- Patient cohorts: >50% of pericoronitis patients received antibiotics
- Pericoronitis ranked **1st or 2nd** among all diagnoses for antibiotic use in patient cohort studies
- Dentist self-awareness: 91% worried about resistance; 47.7% felt inadequately trained; 93% wanted practical guideline updates
- Primary drivers of over-prescribing: diagnostic uncertainty (47.3%), time pressure (30%), treatment-delay decisions (72.5%)

aPDT (antimicrobial photodynamic therapy) is a promising adjunct with no resistance risk, but current evidence is insufficient for routine recommendation.

## 6. Pediatric Dentistry — Same Principles, Additional Harms

[확인] Goel 2020 narrative review (2000–2019 literature):

**Indications** are identical to adult practice — systemic spread/fever/cellulitis/contaminated wounds justify antibiotics; irreversible pulpitis/necrotic pulp/localized abscess do not. The additional concern in children is **adverse developmental and metabolic harm from early-life antibiotic exposure**:

| Adverse effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Intestinal dysbiosis | Altered microbiota with long-term metabolic consequences |
| Obesity risk | Microbiota disruption → adiposity |
| Developmental enamel defects | Amoxicillin exposure linked to permanent first molars + maxillary central incisors |
| Candida superinfection | Secondary fungal overgrowth |
| Allergy / asthma risk | Increased risk in antibiotic-treated children |
| GI disturbances | Diarrhea more frequent with TID than BID regimens |

**Global scale**: dentists account for 7–11% of all antibiotic prescriptions; ~1/3 of outpatient prescriptions unnecessary; at current growth rates, global consumption will reach 128 billion DDDs by 2030 (+202% vs 2000).

**"4 D's" framework** (Joseph & Rodvold, cited in Goel 2020):
1. Right **Drug** — appropriate spectrum
2. Right **Dose** — weight-based pediatric dosing
3. **De-escalation** — narrow to pathogen-directed once culture available
4. Right **Duration** — shortest effective course

## 7. Real-World Prescribing Gap — Post-RCT Antibiotics (Law 2026)

[확인] Law 2026 prospective PBRN (PREDICT project); 153 US dentists; 1,723 patients; non-surgical RCT:

**1 in 5 patients (19%)** received systemic antibiotics after non-surgical root canal treatment — substantially exceeding the narrow guideline indications (systemic signs or documented medical risk factors only).

**Predictors of higher antibiotic prescribing**:

| Factor | Direction | OR | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apical periodontitis (present) | ↑ | 1.56 | .004 |
| Tenderness to palpation | ↑ | 1.40 | .046 |
| Dentist age ≥45 yr | ↑ (vs <45) | ref | — |
| Dentist age <45 yr | ↓ | 0.47 | .02 |
| Non-Hispanic White ethnicity | ↓ | 0.40 | .01 |
| Large/academic practice | ↓ | 0.25 | .007 |
| Patient: minority background | ↑ | sig | <.05 |
| Patient: lower education | ↑ | sig | <.05 |

**Equity gap**: minority and lower-education patients received more antibiotics even in the restricted subgroup of vital pulp + no swelling — a scenario where guidelines clearly contraindicate antibiotics. This confirms that sociodemographic dynamics, not clinical severity, drive part of the over-prescribing.

## 8. Implant Prophylaxis Revisited — NNT=143 (Momand 2024)

[확인] Momand 2024 SR+MA (BMC Oral Health; PROSPERO CRD42021292610); restricted to **placebo-controlled, double-blinded, low/moderate-RoB RCTs**; 7 trials, 1,859 patients, 3,014 implants:

| Analysis | RR (95% CI) | Risk difference | NNT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall (7 RCTs) | 0.66 (0.30–1.47) | −0.007 | **143 (29–∞)** |
| Amoxicillin only (6 RCTs) | 0.60 (0.27–1.31) | −0.011 | — |
| Excluding immediate-socket (5 RCTs) | 1.10 (0.35–3.45) | +0.002 | — |

GRADE certainty: **moderate**. The subgroup excluding immediate post-extraction implants reverses the direction (RR 1.10) — any residual benefit is localised to immediate post-extraction placement, not routine uncomplicated surgery.

**Tension with Torof 2023 and Esposito 2013** (in [[drug-antibiotic-stewardship-overview]]): Earlier SR+MAs recommended preoperative single amoxicillin 2g; Momand 2024 exposes this as artefact of including high-RoB, non-blinded trials. The "NNT=143 with CI to infinity" means routine prophylaxis treats very many healthy patients to prevent one early failure — not justifiable against antibiotic-resistance risk. Immediate post-extraction implants may still warrant prophylaxis given higher failure risk in those subgroups.
