> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **LAST(국소마취제 전신독성)** 는 치과 진료실 심정지의 주요 원인; 침묵의 위험 — 발현 즉시 인지 못 하면 5–15분 내 심정지로 진행
> - **발현 순서**: 구강주위 마비·금속맛·이명 → 흥분·경련 → CNS 억제 → 심부정맥·저혈압 → **심정지** (뷰피바카인 (Bupivacaine)은 표준 심폐소생술에 저항적)
> - **에피네프린 = 흡수경로 안전 장치**: 에피네프린 (Epinephrine)이 주사 부위 혈관을 수축시켜 Cmax를 ~0.7–1.2 µg/mL로 제한 (독성역치 ~5 µg/mL의 5배 여유); **3% 메피바카인 (Mepivacaine) plain = 에피네프린 없음 + 최고 농도 → 흡수 가장 빠름 → 소아 LAST 고위험**; 단, 혈관내 직접 주입은 이 방어선을 무력화 — 에피네프린은 흡수경로 독성만 막음
> - **ILE 구제**: 20% 리피드 에멀전 (Lipid Emulsion) 1.5 mL/kg 볼루스 + 0.25 mL/kg/min 지속주입; 소아 21례 SR에서 95.2% 완전회복
> - **왜 뷰피바카인이 가장 위험한가**: log P 3.41(최고 지질친화성) + 95% 단백결합 → Na+ 채널 결합 후 서서히 해리("fast-in, slow-out") → 표준 ACLS 실패 → ILE 필수
> - **덴탈 대비 갭**: 2026년 서베이(n=369) — 0.5%만 ILE 투여 세부지식, 14.1%만 기관 보유 확인; 인식 ≠ 대비
> - **소아 고위험**: <1세 43% 차지(α1-산성 당단백↓·미성숙 CYP450·저체중); 3% 메피바카인 (Mepivacaine) plain = 최고 농도 + 혈관수축제 없음 → 빠른 흡수
> - **메트헤모글로빈혈증 (Methemoglobinemia)**: 프릴로카인(Prilocaine) → o-톨루이딘 → MetHb; 소아·임신에서 리도케인(Lidocaine) 우선
> - **전신질환 환자**: 심혈관 = 메피바카인(plain); 임신 = 리도케인; 간/신장 = 아티카인 (Articaine)(혈장 에스테라제 85–90%); 갑상선 = 에피네프린 금기
> - **치과 용량의 안전 여유 — 약동학 4편 추가(2026-10-28)**: 구강 내 2% lidocaine/epi 4앰플(144 mg)의 최고 혈중농도는 경증 독성 역치 6 µg/mL보다 훨씬 낮았다(Moore 2008, n=16, 유일한 치과 앰플 단위 약동학). 노인 서혜부 탈장에서 7 mg/kg 침윤의 최고 농도는 0.23–0.90 mg/L로 역치 5 mg/L의 1/5 미만이었다(Karatassas 1993, n=14). 피하 팽창 마취에서는 28 mg/kg도 역치를 넘을 위험이 1/2000 미만이었다(Klein 2016). [확인]
> - **해석 주의**: 안전 여유는 혈관 외 주사일 때만 성립한다. 혈관 내 오주입은 이 여유를 통째로 건너뛴다(Moore 2008). Klein의 피하 저장소 약동학(Tmax 약 13시간)은 치과 주사와 다르므로 치과 최대 용량을 올리는 근거로 쓰지 않는다. [확인]
> - **ASRA 3차 권고(Neal 2018)**: 지질 유제 기전 3가지(lipid sink, 직접 강심, post-conditioning), 지연 발현 증가, 작은 체구·근감소증이라는 새 위험 인자, 병원 밖·비마취과 발생 증가. [확인]
> - **에피네프린의 전신 효과(Paladugu 2026, RCT n=110)**: 발치 후 수축기 혈압 +12.69%, 혈당 +14.63%, 맥박 +15.84%로 단순 lidocaine보다 모두 높았다(p<0.001). 나이가 많을수록 혈압 반응이 컸다. 고혈압·당뇨·심혈관 환자에서 모니터링 근거다. [확인]
> - **핵심 임상 행동**: 체중 기반 용량 계산 필수 + ILE 킷 구비 + 전구증상 조기 인지 교육

## Three-line Summary

This synthesis integrates 8 papers (2010–2026) on local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST), intravenous lipid emulsion (ILE) rescue, methemoglobinemia, and LA selection for medically compromised patients into a unified clinical framework for dental practice; the central tension is that LAST is a preventable-yet-potentially-fatal complication for which a highly effective rescue exists (ILE, 95.2% recovery in pediatric SR), yet as of 2026, only 0.5% of dentists have detailed ILE knowledge and only 14.1% have it institutionally available.

LAST risk is determined by the pharmacokinetic profile of the agent: bupivacaine (log P 3.41, 95% protein binding) is the most dangerous because its Na+ channel binding dissociates slowly — making cardiac arrest refractory to standard ACLS — while lidocaine (log P 2.44, 65% binding) produces more reversible toxicity; pediatric patients are disproportionately vulnerable through reduced α1-acid glycoprotein, immature CYP450, and low body weight; and methemoglobinemia from prilocaine represents a separate but related toxicity pathway, particularly in infants.

Preventing LAST requires weight-based dose calculation (only 40.1% of dentists do this per Bakır 2026), careful aspiration, and appropriate agent selection for the patient's systemic status; treating LAST requires prompt ILE administration — within 30 minutes of onset — and the clinical gap between what dentists know and what they can actually do is the most actionable finding from this surveillance.

## 1. LAST: The Toxicity Cascade

### 1.1 Mechanism
Local anesthetics block voltage-gated Na+ channels — the same mechanism that produces anesthesia produces systemic toxicity when plasma concentrations rise beyond the therapeutic threshold.

**Routes to systemic toxicity**:
1. Inadvertent intravascular injection (most dangerous — immediate peak concentration)
2. Absorption from highly vascular injection sites (e.g., IANB — up to 15.3% positive aspiration rate)
3. Relative overdose (mg/kg exceeds MRD, especially in children/low body weight)
4. Impaired elimination (hepatic disease, renal insufficiency)

### 1.2 Clinical Progression

```
EARLY (CNS excitation)                     LATE (cardiac)
perioral numbness                           Na+ channel blockade
metallic taste            →    seizures  →  ventricular arrhythmia
tinnitus / dizziness                        hypotension → cardiac arrest
agitation, confusion
```

**Time to cardiac arrest**: can be <5 minutes with intravascular injection; up to 15–30 minutes with slower absorption from tissue.

### 1.3 Role of Epinephrine — Why the Vasoconstrictor Is a Safety Mechanism

Epinephrine (에피네프린, Epinephrine) in the carpule works as a local vasoconstrictor: it narrows blood vessels at the injection site, slowing the rate at which lidocaine diffuses into systemic circulation. The result is a substantially lower Cmax and a wider safety margin.

| Formulation | Absorption speed | Peak Cmax (IANB, 1 carpule) | Safety margin to CNS threshold (~5 µg/mL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2% lidocaine **with** 1:100,000 epi | Slow | ~0.7–1.2 µg/mL | **~5× margin** |
| 2% lidocaine **plain** (no epi) | Fast | Higher | Narrower |
| 3% mepivacaine **plain** (no epi) | Fastest (high concentration + no vasoconstrictor) | Highest per volume | **Smallest margin** |

**Practical implications**:

1. **Epinephrine (에피네프린) does three things simultaneously**: reduces Cmax (safety), prolongs duration (clinical benefit), and reduces operative bleeding.

2. **Mepivacaine 3% plain = highest LAST risk combination** in routine dental use: highest amide concentration + no vasoconstrictor = fastest absorption → least safe absolute dose per carpule. For a 15 kg child: MRD = 99 mg → only 1.8 carpules (54 mg each). Extractions often require >2 carpules → easy MRD breach. (See §3.2.)

3. **Intravascular injection bypasses this protection entirely** — the drug reaches cardiac tissue within seconds regardless of vasoconstrictor. Epinephrine protects against *absorption-route* LAST; it provides no protection against inadvertent intravascular injection.

4. **When to use plain solutions**: patients with severe uncontrolled cardiovascular disease or hyperthyroid state may require epi-free formulations (plain mepivacaine or prilocaine) — accepting a narrower safety margin in exchange for avoiding adrenergic cardiovascular stress. The trade-off should be explicit.

### 1.4 Why Bupivacaine Is in a Different Risk Category

| Property | Bupivacaine | Lidocaine | Clinical implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| log P (lipophilicity) | **3.41** | 2.44 | Higher cardiac membrane penetration |
| Protein binding | **95%** | 65% | Slow dissociation from Na+ channels |
| Na+ channel kinetics | Fast-in, **slow-out** | Fast-in, fast-out | Cardiac arrest refractory to ACLS |
| ILE sequestration | Very efficient | Moderate | ILE essential for bupivacaine LAST |
| CC:CNS ratio | ~7 | ~7 | Similar ratio, but lower absolute dose |

Standard ACLS with epinephrine fails bupivacaine cardiac arrest because epinephrine's α/β-adrenergic effects cannot overcome slow Na+ channel re-opening. ILE's lipid sink directly competes with bupivacaine for cardiac membrane binding.

## 2. Intravenous Lipid Emulsion: The Rescue

### 2.1 Mechanism (Three Overlapping Pathways)

**Lipid sink** (primary): IV lipid droplets create a separate plasma compartment; bupivacaine partitions into lipid phase (Nernst partition by log P), reducing free drug at myocardial membranes.

**Lipid shuttle**: lipid particles carry drug from target organs to metabolizing organs (liver, muscle), accelerating redistribution.

**Cardiotonic effects**: ILE provides direct myocardial support — volume expansion, NO inhibition, mitochondrial ATP support, GSK-3β anti-apoptosis, partial Na+ channel reversal.

### 2.2 Protocol (ASRA 2020)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Agent | **20% lipid emulsion** (Intralipid or equivalent) |
| Bolus | **1.5 mL/kg** IV over 1 minute |
| Infusion | **0.25 mL/kg/min** × 30–60 min |
| Re-bolus if collapse persists | Repeat bolus × 1–2 |
| Maximum total | **10–12 mL/kg** |
| Do NOT substitute | Propofol (10% lipid, cardiotoxic agent — dangerous) |

### 2.3 Evidence

| Population | Evidence | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Pediatric LAST (Lee 2024) | SR, 21 cases | 95.2% full recovery; all within 30 min of onset |
| Animal models (Nendumba 2025) | 22 RCTs, n=242 | OR 0.26 (95% CI: 0.16–0.44) mortality vs control |
| Human LAST | Case reports/series | No powered RCT; consistent rescue success |
| Non-LAST toxicity | Human RCTs (tramadol, clozapine, organophosphate) | RCT-level evidence |

### 2.4 ILE Efficacy by Lipophilicity

| Agent | log P | ILE efficacy |
|---|---|---|
| Levobupivacaine | 3.6 | Highest |
| Bupivacaine | 3.41 | Very high |
| Ropivacaine | 2.9 | High |
| Lidocaine | 2.44 | Moderate |
| Mepivacaine | 1.95 | Lower |

## 3. Pediatric LAST: Disproportionate Vulnerability

### 3.1 Why Children Are at Highest Risk

| Factor | Mechanism | Age correction |
|---|---|---|
| Reduced α1-acid glycoprotein (AAG) | Higher free (unbound) LA fraction | Approaches adult levels by ~12 months |
| Immature CYP450 | Slower amide LA metabolism → longer half-life | Matures by 6–12 months |
| Low body weight | Small absolute MRD → little margin for error | Weight-based calculation critical |
| HbF (fetal hemoglobin) | More susceptible to MetHb formation | Replaced by HbA by ~6 months |

### 3.2 Agent-Specific Pediatric Risks

**Mepivacaine 3% plain**: highest concentration LA in dental tray + no vasoconstrictor (faster absorption) = greatest LAST risk per volume. For a 15 kg child: MRD = 99 mg → only 1.8 cartridges (54 mg each). Most extractions require >2 cartridges → **easy MRD breach**.

**Articaine 4%**: highest concentration amide; FDA approval limited to ≥4 years; off-label use in younger children requires strict weight-based calculation.

**Bupivacaine**: not appropriate for routine pediatric dental anesthesia; post-op pain management use requires extreme caution.

### 3.3 Pediatric LAST Data (Lee 2024 SR)

- 21 cases (2006–2022): 43% aged <1 year; 66.7% bupivacaine
- 95.2% full recovery with ILE within 30 min
- 1 death: bupivacaine + valproic acid-induced carnitine deficiency → amplified mitochondrial failure
- **Critical lesson**: patients on valproic acid → screen for carnitine deficiency before bupivacaine regional block

## 4. Methemoglobinemia: The Other LA Toxicity

### 4.1 Mechanism

**Prilocaine → o-toluidine** (hepatic metabolism) → oxidizes Fe²⁺ hemoglobin → Fe³⁺ methemoglobin (MetHb) → functional anemia

MetHb threshold: symptomatic at >25%; life-threatening at >50–70%

### 4.2 Risk Stratification

| Patient | Risk level | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Age <6 months | High (low MetHb reductase + HbF) | Lidocaine only |
| EMLA + prilocaine injection | High (additive MetHb load) | Lidocaine for both topical and injection |
| G6PD deficiency | High (NADPH pathway impaired) | Lidocaine only |
| Anemia (Hb <10 g/dL) | Moderate | Avoid prilocaine |
| Pregnancy | High (fetal MetHb via o-toluidine) | Lidocaine (FDA Cat B) |
| Healthy adult | Low | Standard dose prilocaine acceptable |

### 4.3 Evidence

Gutenberg 2013 RCT: prilocaine produced ~2× higher MetHb vs lidocaine in pediatric dental patients — the direct RCT evidence supporting lidocaine preference.

**Diagnosis clue**: cyanosis not responding to O₂ in a child after LA administration → suspect MetHb; SpO₂ reads falsely ~85% regardless of true MetHb (co-oximetry required).

**Treatment**: methylene blue 1–2 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes (contraindicated in G6PD deficiency).

## 5. LA Selection for Medically Compromised Patients

| Condition | Recommended LA | Key reasoning | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular disease | Plain mepivacaine; lidocaine ≤0.04 mg epi | Minimize adrenergic stress | High-dose epi; bupivacaine |
| Hyperthyroid | Plain mepivacaine or prilocaine | Avoid epi → thyrotoxic crisis risk | Any epi formulation |
| Diabetes | Lidocaine 2% + 1:100,000 epi (≤2 carpules) | Stress minimization | — |
| Hepatic impairment | Articaine (85–90% plasma esterase) | Minimal hepatic dependence | Bupivacaine; high-dose amides |
| Renal impairment | Articaine; reduced lidocaine | Avoid accumulation | — |
| Pregnancy | **Lidocaine** (FDA Cat B) | Safety profile established | Prilocaine + felypressin (uterotonic + fetal MetHb) |
| Elderly | Articaine; reduced lidocaine | Impaired hepatic/renal clearance | Bupivacaine (prolonged half-life) |
| Amide allergy | Chloroprocaine (ester) | Plasma cholinesterase hydrolysis | All amides |
| Asthma/COPD | Plain lidocaine; plain mepivacaine | Avoid metabisulfite (preservative in epi) | Prilocaine in hypoxic patients |

**Articaine's plasma metabolism advantage**: 85–90% hydrolyzed by plasma esterases (not hepatic CYP450) → preferred when liver function impaired; also ideal for elderly patients with polypharmacy-reduced CYP450.

**Epinephrine limit in cardiovascular disease**: ≤0.04 mg total = ~2 standard carpules of 1:100,000 formulation.

## 6. The Preparedness Gap (Bakır 2026 Survey Context)

The Bakır 2026 cross-sectional survey (n=369 Turkish dentists, 2025) quantified the gap between LAST awareness and actionable preparedness:

| Metric | % |
|---|---|
| Basic LAST knowledge | 45.8% |
| Weight-based dose calculation | **40.1%** |
| Institutional ILE availability confirmed | **14.1%** |
| ILE detailed dosing knowledge | **0.5%** |
| Emergency response plan prepared | 3.3% |

**The core paradox**: ILE achieves 95.2% recovery when available and used promptly (Lee 2024), yet only 14.1% of institutions have it available and only 0.5% of dentists know how to use it.

**Translating to clinical action**:
1. Stock 20% lipid emulsion (Intralipid) in the dental emergency kit
2. Post the ASRA LAST checklist in the procedure room
3. Calculate maximum dose by weight before every pediatric procedure
4. Brief the team on LAST prodromal symptoms and ILE protocol

## 7. Evidence Map

| Topic | Best evidence | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| ILE mechanism | Nendumba 2025 (animal RCT meta-analysis) | Human RCTs lack for LAST specifically |
| Pediatric ILE outcome | Lee 2024 SR (21 cases) | Small case series; no RCT |
| Methemoglobin | Gutenberg 2013 RCT | Pediatric single-site |
| Medically compromised LA | Gazal 2026 (narrative review, 35 papers) | Heterogeneous; no large RCTs |
| Dental preparedness | Bakır 2026 (cross-sectional survey) | Single country; self-report bias |
| Dose calculation | Saraghi 2015 (narrative review) | Abstract-only; indirect evidence |
| LA pharmacology | Moore 2010 (narrative review) | 16 years old; foundational not outcome-based |

| Dental-dose PK safety margin | Moore 2008 (phase 1 PK, intraoral carpules); Karatassas 1993 (elderly 7 mg/kg); Klein 2016 (tumescent) | Small n; Klein/Karatassas are non-dental routes |
| LAST guideline | Neal 2018 (ASRA 3rd advisory) | Superseded by 2023 4th advisory, not held |
| Epinephrine systemic load | Paladugu 2026 (RCT, n=110) | Single centre; short-term vitals only |

**Pharmacokinetic safety margin (added 2026-10-28).** [[local-anesthesia/moore-2008-lidocaine-epinephrine-pk-phentolamine-reversal]] is the only held dental, carpule-level PK study: four carpules of 2% lidocaine with epinephrine (144 mg) gave peak plasma levels well under the 6 µg/mL mild-toxicity threshold, and phentolamine reversal raised Cmax only slightly. [[local-anesthesia/karatassas-1993-lignocaine-safety-hernia-elderly]] supports the 7 mg/kg infiltration limit in elderly patients (peak 0.23–0.90 mg/L vs a 5 mg/L threshold). [[local-anesthesia/klein-2016-tumescent-lidocaine-estimated-maximal-safe-dosages]] shows much higher subcutaneous tumescent doses (28 mg/kg) stay below threshold, but its slow depot kinetics (Tmax ~13 h) do not apply to dental injection. The practical reading: with correct technique, dental doses are far from toxic plasma levels, so **LAST in dentistry is mainly a problem of intravascular injection, small body size and cumulative dosing in children**, which is why aspiration, weight-based calculation (§3) and ILE stocking (§2) remain the levers. [[local-anesthesia/neal-2018-asra-last-practice-advisory-executive-summary]] adds the 2018 ASRA view: delayed presentations, sarcopenia and small size as risk factors, and rising events outside hospitals. [[local-anesthesia/paladugu-2026-lidocaine-adrenaline-systemic-effects-extraction]] quantifies the epinephrine side (§1.3): systolic BP +12.69%, glucose +14.63% and pulse +15.84% versus plain lidocaine after extraction, larger with age — supporting the plain-agent recommendation for cardiovascular patients in §8.

**Evidence gap**: no adequately powered RCT of ILE for human LAST exists; ethical constraints make this unlikely. The evidence base rests on animal RCTs, case series, and mechanistic studies. The 95.2% pediatric rescue rate (Lee 2024) is the strongest human outcome signal.

## 8. Summary: What Changes in Practice

1. **ILE must be stocked** — not optional; the single most impactful preparedness step
2. **Weight-based dose calculation** for every pediatric patient — even straightforward procedures
3. **Lidocaine first-line for children, pregnant patients, and G6PD-deficient patients** — prilocaine's methemoglobinemia risk is documented
4. **Articaine for hepatic/renal impairment and elderly** — plasma metabolism reduces systemic burden
5. **Plain mepivacaine for cardiovascular/thyroid patients** — eliminate epinephrine-associated adrenergic stress
6. **Bupivacaine caution in pediatrics and cardiovascular disease** — reserve for post-op pain management with strict dosing
