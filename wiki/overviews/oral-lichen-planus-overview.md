## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 구강편평태선 (Oral Lichen Planus, OLP) 5편을 **진단·치료·전신연관 3축**으로 종합. 결론 미리 — 국소 스테로이드 (Topical Corticosteroid) 가 근거상 1차이나 **근거 강도는 낮음~매우 낮음 (low–very-low certainty)**, 완치는 목표가 아니며 (만성·재발), OLP는 숨은 전신질환의 **파수꾼 (sentinel)**. **악성전환율 종합은 이 페이지가 아니라 `oral-medicine/opmd`** 에 있고 여기서는 교차참조만 한다.
> - 진단: WHO 2003 대 Van der Meij 2003 진단기준; 6개 임상 아형 (그물형 reticular · 구진형 papular · 판형 plaque · 미란형 erosive · 위축형 atrophic · 수포형 bullous); **미란형·위축형이 가장 증상이 심하고 위험이 높음**. 병리 = CD8+ 세포독성 T세포 (CD8+ Cytotoxic T-cell) 의 기저각질세포 손상 (경계부 피부염, interface dermatitis), 위컴 선조 (Wickham striae) · 쾨브너 현상 (Koebner phenomenon).
> - 최고 근거 — Lodi 2020 코크란 SR+MA (35 RCT, 1474명): 국소 스테로이드가 위약 대비 **통증 해소 RR 1.91 (95% CI 1.08–3.36, 낮은 근거)**; 임상적 병변 해소는 불확실 (RR 6.00, 95% CI 0.76–47.58, 매우 낮은 근거); **타크롤리무스 (Tacrolimus) 가 클로베타솔 (Clobetasol) 보다 통증 해소에 나을 수 있음** (매우 낮은 근거). 요지 — 1차 지위는 확립, 그러나 모든 결과가 low~very-low.
> - 비용 축 — Sandhu 2022 PRISMA SR: 국소 스테로이드는 **낮은 비용 대비 양호한 효능 → 가치 (value) 기준으로도 1차**; 타크롤리무스는 효과적이나 고가; 연구 간 비용 보고 이질성으로 정량적 비용-효과비 (cost-effectiveness ratio) 산출은 불가.
> - 치료 사다리 (서술 리뷰, Srivastava 2022 · Patel 2023): 국소 스테로이드 (clobetasol) → 불응 시 국소 칼시뉴린 억제제 (Calcineurin Inhibitor, tacrolimus) → 전신 면역억제. **무증상 그물형은 약물 불필요·관찰**. 완치는 드묾 → 목표는 증상 조절과 장기 관리.
> - 전신 파수꾼 — Warnakulasuriya 2025 (J Dent Res, 약 20개 질환 메타분석 종합): 정서장애가 가장 강함 — **우울 (Depression) OR 6.15 · 불안 (Anxiety) OR 3.51 · 스트레스 OR 3.64** (유병률도 최고, 불안 54.8%); **셀리악병 (Celiac Disease) OR 18.44** (광신뢰구간) · **C형 간염 (Hepatitis C) OR 4.09** · 간경변 OR 5.58 · 하시모토 갑상선염 (Hashimoto's Thyroiditis) OR 2.23 · 당뇨 (Diabetes) OR 1.64.
> - 임상 함의 (sentinel): 다수 동반질환이 **무증상**이고 일부는 암 위험인자 (HCV → 구강암 위험 RR 3.67, 갑상선·간). 실행층 = **인식 + 증상 유발 검사 + 다학제 의뢰**이지 일괄 선별검사가 아니다 (인과방향 대부분 미확립, 루틴 스크리닝 근거 아직 부족).
> - 약물 caveat: OLP 치료용 코르티코스테로이드가 **혈당·혈압을 악화** → 당뇨·고혈압 환자에서 특히 혈당·혈압 모니터링과 협진.
> - 악성전환 경계: OLP는 구강잠재악성질환 (Oral Potentially Malignant Disorder, OPMD) 으로 분류되나 (Warnakulasuriya 2025 인용 1.14–2.28%), **전환율 종합 근거** — 고품질 연구만 0.5–1.4% (Gonzalez-Moles 2021), 엄격 WHO 기준 <1% (Kaur 2022), 진단 오분류가 과거 ~5% 추정을 부풀림 — **는 `oral-medicine/opmd` 하위카테고리에 있으므로 교차참조**. 미란형·위축형이 고위험이라는 점만 여기서 진단·추적 강도로 연결.
> - 종합 결론: 국소 스테로이드 1차 (근거는 낮으나 실용적·저비용), 완치가 아닌 조절, **아형이 치료 강도·추적 일정을 가름**, OLP 진단 시 전신 병력청취를 파수꾼으로 활용. 근거 spine = 코크란 SR 1편 + PRISMA SR 1편 + 서술 리뷰 3편 → 치료 방향은 있으나 certainty는 낮다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 5 wiki papers on oral lichen planus (OLP) organized across three axes — diagnosis/subtyping, treatment (ordered by evidence hierarchy), and systemic-disease association — while explicitly bounding malignant-transformation risk out to the `oral-medicine/opmd` subcategory and only cross-referencing it here.

The best treatment evidence (Lodi 2020 Cochrane SR+MA, 35 RCTs/1474 patients) shows topical corticosteroids resolve pain better than placebo (RR 1.91, 95% CI 1.08–3.36, low certainty) with inconclusive clinical resolution (RR 6.00, very low) and a very-low-certainty signal that tacrolimus beats clobetasol; a cost SR (Sandhu 2022) confirms topical corticosteroids as the value first-line (favorable efficacy at low cost; tacrolimus effective but costlier; cost reporting too heterogeneous for cost-effectiveness ratios); narrative reviews (Srivastava 2022, Patel 2023) supply the six subtypes, the CD8+ T-cell interface pathology, and the topical-corticosteroid → tacrolimus → systemic-immunosuppression ladder; and a critical review (Warnakulasuriya 2025) reframes OLP as a sentinel for ~20 systemic comorbidities — depression OR 6.15, celiac OR 18.44, HCV OR 4.09, Hashimoto OR 2.23, diabetes OR 1.64.

Clinically: topical corticosteroids are first-line but all treatment evidence is low-to-very-low certainty; OLP is chronic and not curable, so the goal is symptom control with subtype-calibrated follow-up (erosive/atrophic get the most attention); treat OLP as a marker for hidden systemic disease using symptom-triggered work-up and referral rather than blanket screening; monitor glycemia/blood pressure when prescribing corticosteroids in diabetic/hypertensive patients; and defer transformation-rate decisions to the opmd pages.

## Summary

Oral lichen planus (OLP, 구강편평태선) is a chronic, T-cell-mediated inflammatory disease of the oral mucosa affecting roughly 1% of the population, and it is the kind of condition a clinician meets far more often than the literature settles: it relapses, it rarely resolves, and its "textbook" first-line therapy rests on surprisingly thin evidence. This overview asks the practical question the wiki's five OLP papers can actually answer together: **how do you diagnose and subtype OLP, what does the evidence really support for treating it, and what does an OLP diagnosis tell you about the rest of the patient — while deliberately leaving the malignant-transformation-rate question to the `oral-medicine/opmd` pages that own it?** The five papers converge cleanly on treatment direction and on a newer "systemic sentinel" reframing, while staying honest that the treatment certainty is low.

---

## Diagnosis and subtyping — get the subtype right, because it drives everything downstream

The two narrative reviews anchor the diagnostic picture. **Srivastava 2022** compares the two competing diagnostic criteria sets in use — **WHO 2003** and **Van der Meij 2003** — and lays out the **six clinical subtypes: reticular, papular, plaque, erosive, atrophic, and bullous.** The clinically load-bearing distinction is that **erosive and atrophic OLP are the most symptomatic subtypes and carry the highest malignant risk**, whereas reticular OLP is often asymptomatic. That single axis — symptomatic (erosive/atrophic) vs asymptomatic (reticular) — is what later determines whether the patient needs a drug at all and how closely they are followed.

**Patel 2023** supplies the shared immunopathogenesis, situating oral LP within lichen planus across all sites (skin, oral mucosa, nails, scalp): **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells damage basal keratinocytes**, producing the interface (lichenoid) inflammation, with **Wickham striae, the Koebner phenomenon, and interface dermatitis** as hallmarks. Both reviews agree on the clinical consequence: **oral LP is chronic and relapsing and cannot be cured** — long-term management, not resolution, is the realistic frame.

Diagnostic rigor is not cosmetic here. Histopathologic confirmation is what separates *true* OLP from lichenoid mimics, and that same rigor is what corrects the historically inflated risk estimates discussed in the transformation-boundary section below.

## Treatment — read by evidence hierarchy, the direction is firm but the certainty is low

The distinctive value of synthesizing these five papers is that they span the full evidence hierarchy for the *same* first-line answer, so you can see both the recommendation and exactly how much confidence it deserves.

### Top of the hierarchy — Lodi 2020, the Cochrane SR+MA
- **35 RCTs, 1474 participants** (risk of bias: 7 low, 11 unclear, 17 high), updating the 2011 Cochrane review and isolating corticosteroid evidence.
- **Topical corticosteroids vs placebo — pain resolution RR 1.91 (95% CI 1.08–3.36), low certainty.** A real but modest benefit.
- **Clinical lesion resolution RR 6.00 (95% CI 0.76–47.58) — very low certainty, inconclusive.** The confidence interval crosses 1 and is enormous.
- **Adverse effects RR 1.48 — very low certainty.**
- Head-to-head, **a very-low-certainty signal favors tacrolimus over clobetasol for pain resolution.**
- The honest bottom line the review itself states: corticosteroids are the **pragmatic first line**, but the field lacks the high-quality RCTs needed to establish superiority of any specific agent — **all of it is low to very low certainty.**

### Adding the cost axis — Sandhu 2022, the PRISMA SR
- Among the few reviews to weigh **efficacy alongside direct treatment cost** across topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors, and systemic agents.
- **Topical corticosteroids deliver favorable efficacy at low cost → they remain first-line on value grounds, not just efficacy grounds.**
- **Tacrolimus is effective but more expensive.**
- **Inconsistent cost reporting across studies prevents formal cost-effectiveness modeling** — cost is surfaced as an under-reported decision variable rather than quantified into ratios.
- This is the paper that keeps "first-line = topical corticosteroid" standing even once price enters the decision.

### The practical ladder — Srivastava 2022 & Patel 2023 (narrative)
Both narrative reviews articulate the same stepwise ladder that the SR evidence supports in direction:
1. **Topical corticosteroids (e.g., clobetasol)** — first-line for symptomatic disease.
2. **Topical tacrolimus (calcineurin inhibitor)** — for refractory disease.
3. **Systemic immunosuppression** — for disease that fails topical control.
Asymptomatic reticular OLP generally needs **no drug** — risk-factor control and monitoring suffice. And because **complete cure is rare**, the entire ladder is framed as long-term symptom control, not a cure pathway.

**Agreement across the hierarchy:** every paper that addresses treatment lands on topical corticosteroids first and tacrolimus second. **The disagreement is not about direction but about confidence** — the Cochrane review's low/very-low GRADE ratings are the honest ceiling on how strongly any of this can be asserted.

## OLP as a systemic-disease sentinel — the newest and most clinically expansive lens

**Warnakulasuriya 2025** (a critical review in *J Dent Res*, drawing almost entirely on meta-analyses from the prior three years) reframes OLP from a local mucosal problem into **a marker of substantial systemic disease burden**, tabulating pooled prevalence and odds ratios for ~20 conditions. The significant associations, roughly by strength:

| Comorbidity | Pooled prevalence | OR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Celiac disease | 8.66% | **18.44** (2.40–141.43) |
| Depression | 31.19% | **6.15** (2.72–13.89) |
| Liver cirrhosis | 4.27% | **5.58** (1.83–16.96) |
| Steatohepatitis | 7.06% | 5.71 (0.97–33.60), borderline |
| Hepatitis C | 7.14% | **4.09** (2.77–6.03) |
| Stress | 41.10% | **3.64** (1.48–8.94) |
| Anxiety | 54.76% | **3.51** (2.10–5.85) |
| Hashimoto's thyroiditis | 8.60% | **2.23** (1.76–2.82) |
| Hyperthyroidism | 2.84% | 2.11 (1.22–3.63) |
| Hypothyroidism | 8.14% | 1.65 (1.08–2.53) |
| Diabetes mellitus | 9.41% | **1.64** (1.34–2.00) |
| Hepatitis B | 3.90% | 1.62 (1.01–2.40) |
| Hypertension | 24.17% | 1.28 (1.01–1.63), weak |

Associations reported as non-significant/weak include fibromyalgia, inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, Sjögren's, lupus, psoriasis, vitiligo, and hepatocellular carcinoma itself (OR 0.94). The **emotional-disorder cluster is both the strongest by prevalence (anxiety 54.8%, stress 41.1%, depression 31.2%) and mechanistically framed as emotional→OLP** via a stress-driven proinflammatory state. Two threads matter most for the dentist:

- **Many of these comorbidities are silent** (subclinical thyroid disease, asymptomatic hepatitis B/C, steatohepatitis, undiagnosed celiac disease), and **several act as cancer risk factors** — notably HCV, which increases oral cancer risk in OLP (RR 3.67) and whose direct-acting antivirals both reduce HCC risk (50–80%) and improve OLP.
- **A pharmacologic caveat closes the loop with the treatment section: the corticosteroids used to treat OLP worsen glycemic control and blood pressure**, making glycemia/BP monitoring and interdisciplinary co-management important precisely in the diabetic and hypertensive OLP patients this table flags.

The review is careful about what this licenses: **causal direction is unproven for most links and routine screening is not yet evidence-justified.** The actionable layer is **awareness, symptom-triggered work-up, and referral — not blanket testing.** (It also notes Grinspan's syndrome — the classic OLP+diabetes+hypertension triad — is now in question, since no meta-analysis confirms concurrent DM+hypertension prevalence and the hypertension signal is weak.)

## The malignant-transformation boundary (deferred to `oral-medicine/opmd`)

OLP is classified as an oral potentially malignant disorder (OPMD), and Warnakulasuriya 2025 cites a transformation rate of **1.14%–2.28%**. But the **synthesis of transformation risk deliberately lives in the `oral-medicine/opmd` subcategory, not here** — this page's job is diagnosis, treatment, and systemic association. For context only: the opmd pages restrict analysis to histopathologically confirmed OLP and report a **true rate of 0.5–1.4%** ([[oral-medicine/opmd/gonzalez-moles-2021-lichen-planus-malignant-transformation-sr]], 23 high-quality studies, median time to transformation ~5 years), **~1% under strict criteria** with 6-month surveillance for erosive/atrophic disease ([[oral-medicine/opmd/chiang-2021-lichen-planus-malignant-transformation-review]]), and **<1%** once dysplastic lichenoid mimics are excluded ([[oral-medicine/opmd/kaur-2022-oral-lichen-planus-malignant-disorder-appraisal]], which challenges the OPMD label itself). Warnakulasuriya's 1.14–2.28% sits slightly above these strict-criteria estimates; the reconciliation is diagnostic misclassification, and that reconciliation is worked out in opmd. **What carries over to *this* page is only the diagnostic/follow-up consequence: erosive and atrophic subtypes are higher-risk and warrant closer surveillance — see the opmd pages for the transformation-rate evidence.**

## Synthesis & Clinical Bottom Line

| Question | Answer | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| First-line treatment? | Topical corticosteroids (e.g., clobetasol) | Cochrane SR+MA (Lodi 2020), low certainty |
| How strong is the treatment evidence? | Modest pain benefit over placebo (RR 1.91); clinical resolution inconclusive; all outcomes low–very-low certainty | Cochrane SR+MA |
| Does cost change the first choice? | No — topical corticosteroids are also the value choice; tacrolimus effective but costlier | cost SR (Sandhu 2022) |
| What for refractory disease? | Topical calcineurin inhibitor (tacrolimus), then systemic immunosuppression | narrative reviews (Srivastava 2022, Patel 2023) |
| Can OLP be cured? | No — chronic/relapsing; goal is long-term symptom control | narrative reviews |
| Which subtype needs the most attention? | Erosive/atrophic — most symptomatic, highest risk | narrative + SR |
| Is OLP just a local disease? | No — sentinel for systemic comorbidity (depression OR 6.15, celiac OR 18.44, HCV OR 4.09, Hashimoto OR 2.23, diabetes OR 1.64) | critical review (Warnakulasuriya 2025) |
| Screen every OLP patient for all of these? | No — awareness + symptom-triggered work-up + referral; routine screening not yet justified | critical review |
| Drug-interaction caveat? | Corticosteroids worsen glycemia/BP — monitor in diabetic/hypertensive patients | critical review |
| Malignant transformation? | OLP is an OPMD (erosive/atrophic higher-risk); the transformation-rate synthesis lives in `oral-medicine/opmd` | cross-reference |

**Decision rule:**
1. **Diagnose with histopathologic confirmation** to separate true OLP from lichenoid mimics — the same rigor that corrects inflated downstream risk estimates — then **classify the subtype.**
2. **Reticular/asymptomatic OLP → risk-factor control + monitoring, no drug needed.**
3. **Symptomatic (erosive/atrophic) → topical corticosteroid (e.g., clobetasol) first-line** — pragmatic and low-cost, while disclosing that the supporting evidence is low certainty.
4. **Refractory → topical tacrolimus** (effective but costlier), then **systemic immunosuppression**; set expectations for **control, not cure.**
5. **At diagnosis, take a systemic history using OLP as a sentinel** — be alert (by symptoms) to depression/anxiety/stress, thyroid disease, celiac disease, hepatitis B/C, and diabetes, and **refer rather than blanket-test.**
6. **When prescribing corticosteroids, monitor glycemia and blood pressure** in diabetic/hypertensive patients, and co-manage interdisciplinarily.
7. **Calibrate malignant-transformation surveillance to subtype** (erosive/atrophic higher) — and consult the opmd pages/overview for the transformation-rate evidence itself.

**Evidence spine:** one Cochrane SR+MA + one PRISMA cost SR + three narrative/critical reviews. **Treatment direction is firm and consistent across all five, but the treatment certainty is low-to-very-low** — the most defensible reading is a well-worn first-line choice on weak formal evidence, paired with a strong (and newer) case for reading OLP as a window onto systemic health.
