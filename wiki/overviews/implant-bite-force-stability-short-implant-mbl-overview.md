## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 저작력 (Bite Force, BF)과 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ)는 정량적 양의 관계 — BF가 높을수록 ISQ가 높으며, 이는 기능적 하중이 골-임플란트 계면을 강화한다는 기계적 자극 이론과 일치한다.
> - **BF-ISQ 상관 (Mohammed 2026)**: n=80명·235개 즉시 로딩 임플란트, 18개월; Pearson 상관 + 혼합효과 회귀 모두 유의한 양의 관계 확인. 구치부 ISQ > 전치부, 남성 > 여성 (구치부 BF 및 ISQ 모두 p<0.05).
> - **숏 임플란트 변연골 (Lee 2009)**: n=40개 단근 임플란트 <10 mm, 1년; 표준 임플란트 대비 변연골 소실 유의차 없음(p>0.05); 치관-임플란트 비율 (Crown-to-Implant ratio, C:I) ≤1 vs >1 모두 NS — 높은 C:I 자체가 1년 내 변연골 흡수를 가속하지 않음.
> - **두 논문의 재구성**: 임플란트 결과 예측에 있어 "임플란트 길이"보다 "기능적 하중의 안정성(ISQ 궤적)"이 더 직접적인 지표. 하중이 예측 가능하고 안정적이면, 단기 임플란트도 변연골 관점에서 표준 임플란트와 동등하다.
> - **성별·위치 조절**: 여성 전치부는 BF 낮고 ISQ 낮음 → 즉시 로딩 계획 시 추가 안전 여유 필요. 구치부(교근력 직접 작용)는 BF↑·ISQ↑ 패턴으로 오히려 유리.
> - **임상 전환점**: 문제는 "숏이냐 장축이냐"가 아니라 "하중 안정성이 확보되는가" — ISQ 모니터링이 임플란트 길이 선택보다 더 직접적인 예후 신호.

## Three-line Summary

Two-paper synthesis: prospective BF–ISQ cohort (Mohammed 2026, n=80, 235 implants, 18 months) and retrospective short implant MBL study (Lee 2009, n=40 short implants <10 mm, 1 year).

BF and ISQ are significantly positively correlated across all timepoints, locations, and sex groups; short implants show no greater marginal bone loss than standard implants at 1 year regardless of C:I ratio.

Functional load stability — tracked as ISQ trajectory — is a more direct predictor of implant outcome than absolute implant length; elevated C:I inherent to short implants does not independently accelerate early marginal bone resorption.

## 세줄요약

두 논문 종합: 저작력 (BF)–ISQ 전향 코호트 (Mohammed 2026, n=80, 235개, 18개월)와 단근 임플란트 변연골 소실 후향 연구 (Lee 2009, n=40개 <10 mm, 1년).

BF-ISQ는 모든 시점·부위·성별에서 유의한 양의 상관; 단근 임플란트는 C:I 비율 무관하게 1년 변연골 소실이 표준 임플란트 대비 유의한 차이 없음.

기능적 하중 안정성 (ISQ 궤적)이 임플란트 길이보다 더 직접적인 예후 지표 — 높은 C:I 그 자체는 초기 변연골 흡수를 가속하지 않음.

## Summary

This overview synthesizes two studies that together reframe how functional loading relates to implant outcomes. Mohammed et al. (2026) demonstrated a quantifiable positive relationship between bite force (BF) and implant stability (ISQ) in a prospective 18-month cohort of immediately loaded implants, while Lee et al. (2009) showed that short implants — which inherently carry elevated crown-to-implant (C:I) ratios — do not exhibit accelerated marginal bone loss in their first year of function.

The shared message is that **stable functional loading** is the operative variable, not implant length per se. When bite force is adequate and the implant achieves reliable osseointegration (rising ISQ trajectory), the bone-implant interface consolidates predictably regardless of whether the implant is short or standard-length. Elevated C:I ratio, a theoretical biomechanical concern with short implants, did not translate into measurable marginal bone harm at 1 year.

This synthesis complements the existing ISQ overview ([[overviews/implants-isq-stability-ladder]]) by adding bite force as a patient-level modulator of ISQ trajectory, and extends the short implant overview ([[overviews/implant-length-selection-why-not-always-short]]) by addressing the early marginal bone behavior of short implants under real-world functional loading.

## Axis 1 — Bite Force ↔ ISQ Correlation (Mohammed 2026)

### Study Design

Mohammed et al. (2026) enrolled 80 patients (ages 25–40) divided into four equal groups (n=20 each): anterior male, posterior male, anterior female, posterior female. A total of 235 Straumann® and Medintika® implants (diameters 3.3–4.0 mm; lengths 9–11 mm) received immediate loading. Bite force was quantified with the Loadstar™ force sensor (maximum bite force, Newtons, first molar region) and ISQ was measured with the Osstell ISQ Mentor (RFA). Three measurement timepoints: crown placement day (Visit 1), 6 months (Visit 2), and 18 months (Visit 3).

### Key Findings

| Variable | Direction | Statistical Test | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| BF → ISQ (overall) | Positive | Pearson correlation + mixed-effects regression | p < 0.05 |
| Posterior vs. anterior ISQ | Posterior higher | Two-way ANOVA | p < 0.05 |
| Male vs. female posterior BF | Male higher | Two-way/three-way ANOVA | p < 0.05 |
| Male vs. female posterior ISQ | Male higher | Two-way/three-way ANOVA | p < 0.05 |
| BF–ISQ dynamics over 18 months | Strengthens with time | Longitudinal mixed-effects | p < 0.05 |

### Mechanistic Interpretation

The positive BF–ISQ relationship is consistent with **Wolff's law / mechano-adaptation**: controlled functional load (within physiological range) stimulates peri-implant cortical bone remodeling and maturation, translating into higher RFA-measured stiffness at the bone-implant interface. The posterior jaw benefits from direct masseter/temporalis force transmission, explaining the persistent posterior ISQ advantage.

The dynamic nature of the correlation — correlation coefficient evolves from Visit 1 to Visit 3 — suggests that osseointegration quality progressively mediates the force–stability coupling. Early (Visit 1) ISQ may reflect primarily primary mechanical stability; by Visit 3 it predominantly reflects biological osseointegration quality shaped by functional load history.

### Sex and Location Modifiers

- **Males**: Higher masseter muscle mass → higher posterior BF → higher posterior ISQ. This is a biomechanical advantage for osseointegration but requires no clinical adjustment when loading is immediate (implants still achieve adequate ISQ).
- **Females, anterior region**: Lower BF, lower ISQ at all timepoints. This combination warrants careful immediate-loading decision-making — lower BF means less mechanical stimulation and the anterior region already has less cortical support. A more conservative loading protocol or extended healing observation period is appropriate.

## Axis 2 — Short Implant C:I Ratio and Marginal Bone Loss (Lee 2009)

### Study Design

Lee et al. (2009) retrospectively selected 40 single-unit short implant cases (<10 mm, Brånemark machined-surface system) from 389 short implants placed at Yonsei University's dental hospital from 2003 onward. Marginal bone loss (MBL) was measured radiographically at 1 year using the UTHSCSA ImageTool (calibrated to 0.1 mm) and compared against standard implant controls (≥10 mm). The C:I ratio (crown length from occlusal surface to bone crest ÷ intrabony implant length) was calculated for each case and patients were stratified into C:I ≤1 vs. C:I >1 subgroups.

### Key Findings

| Comparison | Result | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| Short (<10 mm) vs. standard (≥10 mm) MBL at 1 year | Not significantly different | p > 0.05 |
| C:I ≤1 vs. C:I >1 subgroup MBL difference | Not significantly different | NS (all groups) |
| Bone apposition (negative MBL values) | Observed in both groups | — |

### The C:I Concern — Theory vs. Data

The theoretical biomechanical argument against short implants is that a high C:I ratio creates an unfavorable lever arm: greater off-axis forces at the bone crest, amplifying bending moments and potentially accelerating crestal bone resorption. Lee (2009) directly tested this — and found no 1-year support for the concern. Neither the short-vs-standard comparison nor the within-short C:I ≤1 vs. >1 stratification reached significance.

**Important caveats**: This was machined-surface Brånemark implants (pre-roughened-surface era), single-unit restorations only (no splinting-effect analysis), and follow-up was limited to 1 year. Modern roughened-surface short implants show even more favorable MBL profiles in later studies (see barausse-2024-4mm-short-implants-posterior-atrophic-8year for 8-year data). The finding should be read as: *in the first year under standard single-unit loading, elevated C:I does not independently accelerate MBL* — not as a statement about decade-long behavior under high parafunctional load.

### Clinical Relevance

When bone height is limited and a short implant is indicated, the 1-year MBL data do not support refusing a short implant on the grounds of elevated C:I alone. The decision frame should shift from "will high C:I hurt the bone?" (1-year data: no) to "is functional load stable and predictable?" — which brings the analysis back to ISQ trajectory (Axis 1).

## Integration Table — Loading, Stability, and Bone Preservation

| Clinical scenario | BF level | Expected ISQ | MBL risk at 1 year | Decision signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Posterior, male, high BF | High | High (consistently > anterior) | Low (short or standard: NS difference) | Proceed; ISQ trajectory favorable |
| Posterior, female, moderate BF | Moderate | Moderate | Low | Proceed; monitor ISQ at 6 months |
| Anterior, female, lower BF | Lower | Lower | Low (no MBL excess shown) | Caution on immediate loading; consider delayed protocol if ISQ < threshold |
| Short implant, C:I > 1 | Any | Any | No excess vs. standard at 1 year (Lee 2009) | C:I alone not a contraindication; evaluate residual bone height, primary stability ISQ |
| Short implant, low primary ISQ | Low | Low | Uncertain — under-studied | Delay loading; re-measure; supplement with healing protocol |

**Key integration principle**: ISQ trajectory under functional load is the unifying metric. BF positively drives ISQ (Mohammed 2026); short implants achieve ISQ-equivalent outcomes at 1 year despite high C:I (Lee 2009). The clinical question is therefore: *can this implant achieve and maintain adequate ISQ?* — not *is this implant long enough?*
