> - **수축 계획**: fAPF 적용 시 KG 수축률 평균 42.43%(Han 2021) — 목표 KMW에 1.7× 여유 확보 필요.
> - **골증대술(Ridge Augmentation, RA) 동시 시행**: fAPF KG 증가량은 RA 유무와 무관(Han 2021, n=203 임플란트, GEE p>0.05) — RA 환자에서도 식립 시 fAPF 결정 주저 불필요.
> - **심미 전치부 복잡 케이스**: APF+결합조직이식술(Connective Tissue Graft, CTG) 선시행 → GBR → 임플란트 식립 → 출현윤곽(Emergence Profile, EP) 30% 감량 임시치관 10개월 조정 순서(Kim 2015). 연조직 선처리가 이후 GBR·임플란트 성공의 기반.
> - **타이밍 분기**: 식립 시(Bruschi PTF, Han fAPF) vs 2차 수술 시(Wang MRARF) vs 유지관리 중 — 모든 시점에서 효과적; 2차 수술이 생물학적·실용적 선호 창.
> - **콜라겐 매트릭스(Collagen Matrix, CM)**: FGG/SCTG 대비 KMW 열등하지만 수술시간·이환율 유의 낮음(Thoma 2014, 2 RCTs). 두께/심미 목표엔 SCTG 우선.
> - **결정 기준 요약**: (1) 이식 불가/거부 → PTF 또는 MRARF/FFS (2) 대면적 KM 결핍(하악) → APF+FGG (3) 두께+폭 동시 필요(2차 수술) → MRARF 또는 SCTG (4) 전치부 심미 + 경조직 결핍 → soft-tissue-first staged protocol (5) 이환율 최소화 우선 → CM 또는 MRARF/FFS

## Three-line Summary

Surgical technique selection for peri-implant keratinized mucosa (KM) augmentation depends on the clinical goal (width gain vs. volume/aesthetics), timing (implant placement vs. second-stage vs. maintenance), site (mandible posterior vs. maxillary anterior esthetic zone), and patient preference for donor morbidity. APF/vestibuloplasty + FGG or SCTG remains the gold-standard technique (KMW gain 1.4–3.3 mm; Thoma 2014 SR/EAO consensus), while graft-free alternatives — partial thickness flap (PTF; Bruschi 2014), MRARF (Wang 2024), and flap folding suture (FFS; Jung 2021 pilot RCT) — reduce donor morbidity at the cost of less evidence. Anticipated KG shrinkage of ~42% (Han 2021) must factor into flap design; concurrent ridge augmentation does not impair fAPF effectiveness.

## 세줄요약

임플란트주위 각화점막(KM) 증대 술식 선택은 목표(폭 vs. 두께/심미), 타이밍(식립 시·2차 수술·유지관리), 부위(하악 구치 vs. 전치부 심미), 공여부 이환율 수용 여부에 따라 분기된다. APF/전정성형술+FGG 또는 SCTG가 표준(KMW 1.4–3.3 mm 증가, Thoma 2014 SR), 이식재 없는 대안(PTF·MRARF·FFS)은 이환율 없으나 근거 수준 낮음; 이종 콜라겐 매트릭스(CM)는 이환율에서 유리, KMW 효과 열등. fAPF 후 KG 수축률 42.43%(Han 2021) 계획 필요; 골증대술 동시 시행이 KG 증가를 저해하지 않음(Han 2021, GEE).

## Summary

### Scope and Relationship to Other Overviews

This overview synthesizes the **technique dimension** of peri-implant soft tissue management — specifically which surgical approach to use and when. The rationale for KM augmentation (why ≥2 mm KMW matters, risk factors, outcome evidence) is addressed in [[overviews/keratinized-mucosa-peri-implant-health-overview]]. The evidence base for specific graft materials (FGG vs. SCTG vs. XCM vs. ADM) is most completely covered by [[implants/soft-tissue/tavelli-2021-soft-tissue-phenotype-modification-peri-implant-nma]] and [[implants/soft-tissue/rios-osorio-2025-xcm-vs-ctg-fgg-implant-soft-tissue-sr-ma]].

This synthesis draws on seven previously unsynthesized papers: four technique papers (Bruschi 2014 PTF, Han 2021 fAPF, Jung 2021 FFS pilot RCT, Wang 2024 MRARF) and three broader evidence sources (Thoma 2014 SR, De Greef 2023 mesh-FGG case report, Kim 2015 staged esthetic zone case).

### I. Evidence Foundation: What the SR Tells Us (Thoma 2014)

Thoma et al. (2014), published in the EAO 2014 Consensus Supplement, conducted a systematic review of 9 KM augmentation studies and 11 soft tissue volume studies. Meta-analysis was not feasible due to heterogeneity.

**Key findings:**

| Technique | KMW Gain | Statistical Result | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| APF/V + FGG or SCTG | 1.4–3.3 mm | p < 0.05 vs controls | Gold standard for KMW |
| APF/V + Collagen Matrix (CM) | Less than FGG/SCTG | Less morbidity, less surgery time (p<0.05, 2 RCTs) | Patient-comfort alternative |
| SCTG (soft tissue volume) | Best documented | Superior papilla fill, marginal mucosal levels at immediate implant sites | Aesthetic goal |
| APF/V alone | Minimal | Reference/control | Insufficient without graft |

**Clinical implication**: APF/V is the necessary scaffold for KMW gain; the graft material determines the magnitude. FGG/SCTG maximize width; CM trades efficacy for morbidity reduction.

---

### II. Graft-Free Flap Techniques: Three Options

#### A. Partial Thickness Flap (PTF) — Bruschi 2014

**Concept**: At implant placement, residual keratinized tissue is raised as a partial-thickness flap from the palatal/lingual aspect and repositioned apically. The palatal/lingual donor site heals by secondary intention. No graft material.

**Evidence**: Prospective 4-year, n=131 implants (85 patients), mandible 78.6%.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| KMW at 1 year | 7.26 ± 2.01 mm |
| KMW at 4 years | 7.37 ± 2.12 mm |
| KMW gain stability | Δ+0.11 mm (1y→4y) — stable |
| KM gain frequency | 100% of cases |
| Implant survival (4y) | 87.79% |

**Advantages**: Single-stage with implant placement; 4-year stability; no donor site.

**Limitations**: Requires sufficient residual keratinized tissue on palatal/lingual aspect; case-series design with no control group; single-center.

**Best indication**: Implant placement in sites with adequate palatal/lingual keratinized tissue where the patient declines or cannot tolerate a separate graft procedure.

---

#### B. Flap Folding Suture (FFS) — Jung 2021

**Concept**: After a paramarginal incision and full-thickness buccal flap elevation, the flap is folded horizontally under the healing abutment and fixed with a single-knot suture — no de-epithelialization, no graft material.

**Evidence**: Pilot RCT, n=15 patients (18 implants), Dankook University.

| Outcome | FFS (n=9) | Interrupted Suture (n=9) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft tissue volume at 3 months (median mm³) | 14.8 [IQR 9.4–19.2] | 8.7 [8.1–11.4] | 0.262 (NS) |
| Time effect (volume loss) | Significant | Significant | <0.001 |

**Novel measurement**: First 3D volumetric analysis using intraoral scanner (IOS) Boolean subtraction between sequential scans. Provides a reproducible perioperative soft tissue volume tracking method.

**Advantages**: No biomaterial cost; no donor morbidity; marginally increased surgical complexity only; blood clot stabilization promotes re-epithelialization.

**Limitations**: Pilot RCT — underpowered (P=0.262); 3-month follow-up; trend not statistically confirmed. Larger RCTs required.

**Best indication**: Cases where volume preservation (thickness) is the goal at implant placement, patient declines graft, and moderate-sized paramarginal flap is available.

---

#### C. Modified Roll Envelope with APF (MRARF) — Wang 2024

**Concept**: At second-stage implant surgery, a modified roll envelope flap is combined with an apically repositioned flap — the existing peri-implant tissue is rolled buccally to augment volume and widen the keratinized band simultaneously, without any graft.

**Evidence**: Case series, n=4 patients (July 2021–February 2022), Zhejiang University.

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Healing | Uneventful, all 4 cases |
| KMW | Increased, all 4 cases |
| STT | Increased, all 4 cases |
| Buccal contour | Corrected, all 4 cases |
| Color/texture | Harmonious with adjacent teeth |

*Quantitative mm-level data not available from abstract; full text required.*

**Triple simultaneous correction**: Buccal contour concavity + KMW deficit + soft tissue thinness — in one operation at second-stage surgery.

**Advantages**: Second-stage timing is practical and biologically favorable; no donor site; esthetic color/texture integration.

**Limitations**: n=4 case series; no control; no quantitative KMW data from abstract; single center.

**Best indication**: Second-stage uncovering in sites with buccal contour concavity AND inadequate KMW AND thin tissue, where the patient declines graft or the donor site is unavailable.

---

### III. Modified FGG Technique: Mesh-FGG (De Greef 2023)

**Concept**: Conventional FGG is scored in a lattice/mesh pattern (analogous to burn surgery split-thickness skin mesh grafts) before suturing to the recipient bed. The scoring physically expands the graft's surface area, allowing a smaller palatal harvest to cover a larger recipient area.

**Evidence**: Case series, n=2, posterior and anterior mandible.

**Advantages over conventional FGG**: Smaller palatal harvest → reduced donor site morbidity; good color integration (less demarcation than conventional FGG per authors' observation).

**Disadvantages**: n=2 only; 4-month follow-up; expansion ratio not quantified; no RCT comparison vs. conventional FGG.

**Best indication**: Large edentulous ridge with minimal KM and shallow vestibule where a conventional FGG would require a prohibitively large palatal harvest; combined with APF for vestibuloplasty.

---

### IV. APF + CTG: Staged Soft-Tissue-First Protocol (Kim 2015)

Kim 2015 presents a single case report of maxillary anterior esthetic zone management in a young patient (19y, #21, prior failed implant, thin scalloped biotype, scarring, low frenum). The key contribution is a **soft-tissue-first staging rationale** — performing APF + CTG (envelope technique) before GBR and implant placement:

**Staging sequence:**
1. Soft tissue surgery: frenectomy + APF + CTG → 4 months maturation
2. GBR (titanium-reinforced Gore-tex + Tutoplast allograft)
3. Implant placement (Astra 3.5×13 mm, 3 mm sub-CEJ)
4. Provisional: screw-retained crown at 30%-reduced emergence profile, expanded biweekly × 10 months
5. Final impression via customized impression coping (VPS + pattern resin from provisional abutment)

**Rationale for soft-tissue-first**: Advancing a GBR flap over unprepared scar-laden tissue compromises KMW and healing. APF+CTG first establishes a healthy keratinized tissue bed so GBR flap closure is tension-free over adequate tissue.

**Best indication**: Anterior esthetic zone with ≥2 simultaneous deficiencies (hard + soft tissue + scar/anatomic complication). Not a first-line approach for routine cases.

---

### V. APF Quantitative Benchmarks (Han 2021)

Han 2021 prospectively studied 79 patients (203 implants) receiving full-thickness APF (fAPF) at implant placement, with and without concurrent ridge augmentation.

**Shrinkage planning data:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| KG gain, no RA (Group A) | 1.92 ± 1.67 mm |
| KG gain, with RA (Group B) | 1.48 ± 1.36 mm |
| Difference A vs B | Not significant (GEE p>0.05) |
| Total KG shrinkage ratio | **42.43%** |
| Baseline KG difference (A vs B) | 1.68 mm vs 0.82 mm (p<0.001) |

**Clinical planning rule**: Anticipate ~42% tissue contraction after fAPF. To achieve a 3 mm final KMW, plan for ~5.1 mm of initial flap displacement. Ridge augmentation does not impair this outcome.

### VI. Technique Selection Matrix

| Scenario | First-choice | Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large KMW deficit, mandible posterior, donor acceptable | APF + FGG | APF + SCTG | Gold standard; Thoma 2014 SR |
| KMW deficit + graft refused or morbidity concern | PTF (at placement) | MRARF (at 2nd stage) | PTF: Bruschi 2014 (4y, 100% gain); MRARF: Wang 2024 (n=4) |
| Volume (thickness) goal + no graft | FFS (at placement) | MRARF (at 2nd stage) | FFS: pilot RCT trend; MRARF: case series |
| KMW + buccal concavity + thinness, 2nd stage timing | MRARF | APF + CTG | MRARF corrects all 3 simultaneously |
| Large area, donor morbidity concern, FGG needed | Mesh-FGG + APF | Standard FGG + APF | Mesh-FGG: n=2; expansion ratio unquantified |
| Anterior esthetic zone, ≥2 deficiencies (hard+soft+scar) | Staged: APF+CTG → GBR → implant | — | Kim 2015; not for routine cases |
| KMW goal + patient comfort priority, graft not ideal | APF + CM | APF + FGG | CM: less morbidity, less KMW gain (Thoma 2014, 2 RCTs) |
| Concurrent RA + KMW deficit | fAPF (at placement) | — | RA does not impair fAPF efficacy (Han 2021, GEE) |

---

### VII. Timing Considerations

| Timing | Applicable Techniques | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| At implant placement | PTF (Bruschi), fAPF (Han), FFS (Jung) | Single-stage efficiency; requires anticipation at planning |
| At second-stage surgery | MRARF (Wang), APF+FGG (Bassetti SR) | Biologically and practically preferred window |
| During supportive periodontal care | APF+FGG | Effective and durable (Roccuzzo 2025, 20y cohort) |
| Before GBR (staged) | APF+CTG first (Kim 2015) | Esthetic zone with scar/biotype complication only |

| Paper | Design | n | Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thoma 2014 | Systematic Review (EAO consensus) | 9+11 studies | SR (no MA possible) |
| Han 2021 | Prospective comparative study | 79 pts, 203 implants | Prospective |
| Jung 2021 | Pilot RCT | 15 pts, 18 implants | RCT (underpowered) |
| Bruschi 2014 | Prospective 4-year | 85 pts, 131 implants | Prospective |
| Wang 2024 | Case series | 4 pts | Case series |
| De Greef 2023 | Case series | 2 pts | Case series |

**Evidence hierarchy for technique selection**: SR (Thoma 2014) → Prospective studies (Han 2021, Bruschi 2014) → Pilot RCT (Jung 2021, underpowered) → Case series (Wang 2024, De Greef 2023) → Case report (Kim 2015).
