> - **구강 내 스캔 한계**: 동적 점막(연구개, 혀 아래, 구전정 sulcus)에서의 경계부 인상(Border Seal) 재현이 여전히 불충분 — 전통 border-molding이 이 부위에서는 여전히 우세.

## Three-line Summary

Two 2025 publications — a 27-author expert consensus (Feng) and an SR+MA of 5 cost studies (n=184, Muehlemann) — converge on the same answer: digital complete dentures are equivalent to conventional in outcomes that matter (cost, satisfaction, OHRQoL, session count), while saving 58–233 minutes of clinical chair time.

No statistically significant difference was found in any cost metric (lab MD −239.77 USD, clinical MD +74.39 USD, total MD −357.76 USD, all p>0.05); operator experience — not workflow modality — was the dominant driver of cost variability (p<0.0001); and patient satisfaction/OHRQoL did not differ significantly between digital and conventional dentures.

The remaining clinical differentiators are intraoral-scanning limitations at movable-mucosa borders (still favoring conventional border molding), a proven chair-time advantage for digital workflows, and the yet-uncaptured capital cost of digital equipment in any published study.

## 세줄요약

2025년 두 편의 연구(27인 합의문 Feng + SR+MA 5편 Muehlemann, n=184)가 동일한 결론으로 수렴: 디지털 총의치는 임상적으로 중요한 결과(비용·만족도·OHRQoL·내원횟수)에서 전통 의치와 동등하되, 임상시간은 58–233분 절감된다.

4개 비용 지표(기공비·임상비·총비용·내원횟수) 모두 통계적으로 유의한 차이 없고, 비용 변동의 주결정 인자는 워크플로우 종류가 아닌 술자 숙련도(p<0.0001)이며, 환자 만족도·OHRQoL도 디지털 대 전통 간 유의차 없다.

남은 임상적 변별 인자는 동적 점막 경계부에서의 구강 내 스캔 한계, 확인된 임상시간 단축 효과, 그리고 어떤 연구에서도 아직 포함되지 않은 장비 초기 투자비이다.

## Summary

Two landmark 2025 publications close the two largest evidence gaps in digital complete denture (DCD) adoption: **clinical workflow standardization** and **cost-effectiveness**. Together they reframe the digital-vs-conventional debate — not as a question of superiority, but as a question of equivalence plus efficiency.

**Feng et al. 2025** (expert consensus, International Journal of Oral Science, PMID 40739137) is the first consensus-level standardization of the DCD workflow, produced by a 27-author Chinese multi-institutional prosthodontics panel. It synthesizes evidence from 39+ studies into structured recommendations spanning case selection, digital data acquisition (intraoral and facial scanning), custom tray design, jaw-relation recording, CAD tooth arrangement, additive vs subtractive manufacturing, and follow-up protocols. The panel's evidence synthesis confirms: 58–233 min clinical chair-time savings, milled denture retention up to 74 N (comparable to conventional border-molded maxillary retention), equivalent patient satisfaction and OHRQoL between digital and conventional, and better manufacturing precision with subtractive (milled) than additive (3D-printed) methods.

**Muehlemann et al. 2025** (SR+MA, Journal of Prosthodontics, PMID 41368723, PROSPERO CRD42024607329) is the first meta-analysis — rather than a descriptive cost-analysis — of cost-efficiency between digital and conventional removable complete denture (RCD) workflows. Five studies (n=184 patients, 202 RCDs) were pooled using a PRISMA 2020/Cochrane-compliant methodology with formal economic harmonization (inflation-adjustment + purchasing-power-parity standardization to 2025 international USD). All four pooled outcomes were non-significant: laboratory cost (MD −239.77 USD, p=0.106), clinical cost (MD +74.39 USD, p=0.451), total cost (MD −357.76 USD, p=0.258), and number of sessions (MD −1.47, p=0.351). Crucially, operator experience (dental students vs prosthodontic specialists) was identified as the dominant significant driver of cost variability (clinical cost p<0.0001; sessions p=0.0001), not workflow modality. No included study captured digital equipment capital investment in its cost accounting.

The convergence of these two evidence streams — a clinical-workflow consensus and a cost-outcome meta-analysis — establishes a coherent clinical picture: **digital complete dentures are equivalent in outcomes, faster in delivery, and variable in cost depending on who uses them**.

## Axis 1 — Workflow Standardization (Feng 2025)

### Case Selection

The consensus defines clear indications and contraindications for digital workflows. Intraoral scanning is appropriate when the patient has adequate mouth opening, manageable gag reflex, and limited soft-tissue undercuts. Patients with severe gag reflex, markedly limited mouth opening, or extensive movable-mucosa displacement remain better served by conventional border-molded impression techniques.

### Digital Data Acquisition

Two scanning pathways are recommended:

- **Intraoral scanning (IOS)**: acceptable accuracy (deviations within 0.2 mm of conventional impressions; <0.9 mm vs gypsum models), but a 39-study SR confirmed that traditional border molding remains predominant for denture base fabrication because direct IOS underperforms in movable-mucosa regions — soft palate, sublingual area, and vestibular sulcus — that are critical to border seal and retention.
- **Facial scanning**: full-smiling and postural positions, facebow-fork registration, STL/OBJ/PLY export; used to capture facial profile for esthetic tooth arrangement and to construct a personalized virtual articulator reference.

The consensus does not declare one jaw-relation-recording device (facebow, gothic arch tracer, jaw-motion tracker) superior within digital workflows — this is explicitly flagged as an evidence gap requiring future comparative studies.

### Manufacturing: Subtractive vs Additive

| Parameter | Milled (CNC, subtractive) | 3D-Printed (SLA/DLP, additive) |
|---|---|---|
| Retention force | Up to 74 N; comparable to conventional | Lower than conventional and milled |
| Dimensional precision | Consistently superior (lower SD) | Good fit accuracy but less consistent |
| Esthetics | Material uniformity high | Less uniform; some patients rate as less esthetic |
| Polymerization shrinkage | Minimal (pre-polymerized PMMA disc) | Higher (layer-by-layer curing) |
| Recommendation | Preferred for retention-critical cases | Appropriate for preliminary/custom-tray work |

### Chair-Time and Visit Savings

Digital workflows save **58–233 minutes** of cumulative clinical chair time versus conventional fabrication (synthesis across multiple underlying studies cited in the consensus). Fabrication time is approximately one-third of conventional methods, and the number of required patient visits is reduced.

### Outcome Equivalence (Satisfaction, OHRQoL)

Meta-analysis cited by the panel found **no significant difference** in patient satisfaction or OHRQoL between digital and conventional complete dentures, nor among conventional, milled, and 3D-printed subtypes. Complication rates (pain, tenderness) were also similar across manufacturing methods.

### Priority Future Directions

The panel's highest-priority gaps: (1) validated jaw-relation-recording device comparisons within digital workflows; (2) AI/AR integration into CAD/CAM workflows to improve IOS accuracy in challenging soft-tissue areas and to enable individualized esthetic customization.

## Axis 2 — Cost Equivalence (Muehlemann 2025)

### What Was Measured

Four economic outcomes were pooled across 5 studies (n=184 patients/202 RCDs; countries: Switzerland, USA, Japan, Italy; 2019–2025):

| Outcome | Mean Difference (2025 USD) | 95% CI | p-value | I² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laboratory cost | −239.77 | −714.4 to +234.8 | 0.106 (NS) | 77.7% |
| Clinical cost | +74.39 | −120.0 to +268.7 | 0.451 (NS) | 97.8% |
| Total cost | −357.76 | −1340.1 to +624.6 | 0.258 (NS) | 73.8% |
| Sessions | −1.47 | −6.7 to +3.8 | 0.351 (NS) | 95.1% |

Negative MD = digital cheaper; positive MD = digital more expensive. None reached significance.

### The Operator-Experience Driver

The dominant signal in the meta-analysis was not workflow type but **operator experience**: clinical cost and session count differed significantly between studies using dental students versus prosthodontic specialists (p<0.0001 and p=0.0001, respectively). Experienced clinicians showed a non-significant trend toward fewer sessions and lower clinical costs with digital workflows — suggesting the efficiency advantage of DCD may be real but only manifests once operators have passed a competency threshold.

### The Missing Capital Cost

No included study incorporated the capital cost of digital equipment (CAD/CAM milling machine, 3D printer, IOS unit, software licenses) into its cost accounting. This is not a minor omission: initial equipment investment is frequently cited by clinicians as the primary barrier to DCD adoption. Any claimed cost equivalence or advantage must be re-evaluated once equipment depreciation is included.

### Prior Literature Reconciliation

This meta-analysis contradicts the conclusion of Jafarpour 2024 (an earlier SR+MA that reported CAD/CAM RCDs as significantly cheaper). Muehlemann et al. attribute the discrepancy to Jafarpour's cost-analysis-only methodology — which did not adjust for patient outcomes or apply rigorous currency harmonization. Once those adjustments are applied, the cost difference disappears.

## Integration with the Broader Digital Denture Evidence Base

The existing synthesis ([[overviews/complete-denture-digital-overdenture-overview]]) covers 24 papers on CAD/CAM mechanics, retention, implant-supported overdenture decisions, and attachment systems. That overview established the structural picture: milled CAD/CAM retention is superior to conventional (p=0.015 in Srinivasan 2021 SR+MA), digital workflows cut chair time 205–233 minutes, and patient satisfaction/OHRQoL are equivalent across fabrication methods.

The two papers synthesized here **extend** that foundation in two previously uncovered directions:

1. **Standardization gap closed**: Feng 2025 translates the aggregated mechanical/outcome evidence into a consensus-level clinical protocol — the first of its kind at this scope. It defines decision rules for IOS vs facial scanning, gives detailed custom-tray and jaw-relation-transfer specifications, and provides special-scenario protocols (RPD-to-CD transition, re-fabrication from existing denture).

2. **Cost gap closed**: Muehlemann 2025 answers the question the prior overview could not — "is digital actually cheaper?" The answer: not significantly, and the variability is largely explained by who performs the procedure, not which technology is used.

Together, the three pillars of the complete-denture digital evidence base are now covered:
- **Mechanical/retention outcomes** → Srinivasan 2021, Khorasani 2024, Avelino 2024 (in the prior overview)
- **Clinical workflow protocol** → Feng 2025 (this synthesis)
- **Cost-effectiveness** → Muehlemann 2025 (this synthesis)
