## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 임플란트주위 조직 안정성을 좌우하는 것은 지대주의 **표면 처리**도 아니고 **분리 횟수**도 아니라, 보철물이 점막을 미는 **형태 — 즉 출현윤곽 (Emergence Profile)의 윤곽과 각도**다. 네 개의 축 중 "형태" 축만 강하고 일관된 임상 효과를 보인다.
> - 4개 하위 축: (a) 지대주 **표면** 처리(Canullo 2020) — 효과 없음, (b) **원-어버트먼트-원-타임 (One-Abutment-One-Time, OAOT)** vs 반복 분리(Ríos-Santos 2020) — 골 이득 없음, (c) **맞춤형 vs 기성 치유지대주**(Ruhstorfer 2024) — 동등 이상·불리함 없음, (d) **출현윤곽 윤곽**(Siegenthaler 2022·Wang 2022) — 형태가 점막퇴축 (Mucosal Recession)을 직접 결정.
> - 가장 강한 신호(전치부): 볼록(convex) 출현윤곽은 오목(concave) 대비 12개월 점막퇴축 위험이 **약 13배** (64.3% vs 14.3%, OR 12.69, 95% CI 1.89–88.48, p=.01; Siegenthaler 2022). 협측 점막이 baseline에서 얇으면 더 불안정.
> - 일관된 신호(구치부): 점막 폭/높이비 (Width/Height ratio, W/H) 기반 협측 윤곽은 치유지대주 윤곽 복사 대비 퇴축을 0.13 vs 0.63 mm로 줄임(p=.006); 초기 **출현각도 (Emergence Angle) ~32°가 40°보다** 우수(p=.025), 단 치조정상부 점막 ≥2 mm일 때(Wang 2022).
> - 표면 축은 무효: 변형 티타늄 표면은 단기 플라크지수·탐침시출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BoP)·탐침깊이 (Probing Depth, PD)에서 대조군과 차이 없음(P=0.091/0.099/0.488), 장기는 상반(Canullo 2020 SR+MA).
> - 분리 축 — 초기 단독 RCT는 무효, 두 SR+MA는 유의 이득: Ríos-Santos 2020(단일 RCT, 231개 임플란트)에서는 원-어버트먼트-원-타임(OAOT, One-Abutment-One-Time) vs 반복 분리 MBL 유의차 없음(0.36 vs 0.48 mm, NS). 그러나 최근 **두 SR+MA가 방향을 뒤집었다** — Abayov 2026(7편 RCT, 645개, 플랫폼 스위칭(PS, Platform Switching)+원뿔 연결(Conical Connection) 제한): MD −0.23 mm(95% CI −0.44 to −0.02, **유의**); Nunes 2025(11편 RCT, 699개, 연결 방식 불문): OAOT 유의한 골 보존 확인. 현재 위치: OAOT는 PS+원뿔 임플란트에서 소폭이지만 일관된 골 이득을 보이며(MD ~0.23 mm), 단 **지대주 높이 ≥2 mm** 레버는 여전히 더 크고 독립적이다.
> - 맞춤형 치유지대주는 형태 축을 *치유 단계에서* 미리 빚는 도구: 연·경조직·심미 동등 이상, 보철 장착 시 압박 성형 통증 감소(생존율 100%, 6–36개월; Ruhstorfer 2024). 즉시식립·심미부·큰 구치 발치와에 특히 유용.
> - 형태를 최종 보철로 *전달*하는 축(e): 디지털 인상 시 임시보철을 빼고 연조직을 직접 스캔하면 즉시 ~200–500 μm, 20분 내 최대 ~1 mm 붕괴 → 간접 스캔(제거한 임시보철 구외 스캔·중첩)이 출현윤곽을 가장 충실히 재현해 전통 인상에 근접(Mikulás 2025 SR, 24편). 빚은 윤곽은 *조직*이 아니라 *임시보철*을 스캔해 옮길 것.
> - 모순처럼 보이는 두 결과의 화해: "형태가 중요하다"(c·d 축)와 "표면·분리는 무의미"(a·b 축)는 충돌이 아니라 **레버의 서열** — 임상 노력은 표면 코팅·OAOT 프로토콜이 아니라 윤곽 설계에 투입해야 한다.
> - 공통 조절 변수 = **연조직 두께/높이**. 얇은 점막에서 볼록 윤곽이 가장 위험(Siegenthaler), 두꺼운(≥2 mm) 점막에서만 W/H 설계가 작동(Wang) — 출현윤곽 효과는 [[overviews/keratinized-mucosa-peri-implant-health-overview]]의 연조직 축과 분리할 수 없다.
> - **출현윤곽(Emergence Profile) SR+MA 신규 근거 (Atieh 2023 · Liu 2025)**: Atieh 2023 SR+MA (4편, 임플란트 912개, 최대 10.9년): 출현각도(EA, Emergence Angle) >30° vs ≤30° — 전체 변연골 소실(MBL, Marginal Bone Loss) 유의차 없음(MD 0.80mm, p=0.09), 단 **볼록 윤곽은 임플란트주위염(Peri-implantitis) 위험비(RR, Risk Ratio) 2.32**(57.8% vs 21.3%, p=0.02) — 각도보다 **윤곽 형태(Profile Shape)**가 주위염 위험의 더 강한 예측인자. Liu 2025 SR+MA(7편 RCT, 임플란트 283개): 전체 MBL 차이 미달(p=0.082)이나 **EA >30° 서브그룹에서 MBL 유의 증가**(MD +0.190mm, p<0.001); 볼록/넓은 어버트먼트가 치조정골 재형성(CBR, Crestal Bone Remodeling) +0.560mm 증가(p=0.001) — 각도와 윤곽이 독립적으로 골에 영향.
> - 출현**각도 (Emergence Angle, EA)** 임계값의 전임상 인과 확립: 개 모델 RCT에서 EA가 넓을수록 MBL이 용량반응적으로 증가(20/40/60/80° → 24주 MBL 0.07/0.11/0.23/0.38 mm; 80°가 20°의 약 4배, p<0.001), ≥60°에서 접합상피 (Junctional Epithelium) 연속성 붕괴·플라크 100% — **각도 <40° 유지** 근거(Strauss 2024). 별개 split-mouth 개 모델은 60° vs 30°에서 결합조직 부착 단축(~0.7 vs ~1.1 mm)·연조직 봉쇄 (Soft Tissue Seal) 실패를 확인했고 **splinted/중간 임플란트**가 더 취약(Lee 2025).
> - 각도는 **조건부** 위험 인자다: 7.4년 추적 후향연구(임플란트 192개)에서 출현각 ≥30°는 **지대주 높이 (Trans-mucosal Abutment Height, TmAH) <2 mm일 때만** 유의한 MBL을 일으켰고(짧은+넓은 그룹 MBL +0.48 mm·임플란트주위염 ~4배, OR 4.19), TmAH 1 mm 증가마다 MBL 발생 odds 37% 감소(OR 0.63, p=0.003) — Ríos-Santos의 "높이 ≥2 mm" 레버를 각도 차원으로 확장(Misch 2025). 즉 **짧은 지대주 위의 넓은 각도**가 가장 위험.
> - 17편 scoping review(Prpic 2026)가 증거 기반을 통합: **EA <30°**는 MBL 감소와 *그럴듯하게* 연관되나 일관성 부족으로 불확실(RCT 대기), **오목 (concave) 윤곽**은 *일관되게* 연조직 안정·중안면 퇴축 감소와 연관(2 RCT) — 각도와 윤곽이 상호작용해 위험은 "넓은 각 + 볼록 윤곽 + 짧은 지대주"가 겹칠 때 의미를 가진다.
> - 취리히 내러티브 리뷰(Pirc 2026)가 같은 임계값(각도 >30° 위험, **티타늄 베이스 (Ti-base) 지대주 높이 ≥2 mm** 완충)을 독립적으로 재확인하면서 **점막 터널 (Mucosal Tunnel) 깊이 >3 mm**(임플란트 과매식 시)를 새 위험구간으로, **표면조도 <0.2 μm Ra** 연마를 새 목표치로 추가 — 그리고 n=1 임상 증례로 볼록/짧은 지대주 크라운을 오목/높은 지대주 크라운으로 교체해 **4년 된 골결손을 4개월 만에 방사선학적으로 되돌린** 사례를 제시, 형태·높이 레버가 예방뿐 아니라 **치료(reversal)**에도 쓰일 수 있음을 보여준다.
> - **7년 장기 RCT 보강 (Muñoz 2025)**: 1.5 mm 매식 임플란트에 1 mm vs 3 mm 지대주를 비교한 가장 긴 추적 RCT(n=63). 3 mm군이 근심·원심 MBL 모두 유의하게 낮음(p<0.001). 임플란트주위염은 1 mm군 18.2% vs 3 mm군 3.2% — 수치 차이는 5.7배이나 탈락률 ~43%로 검정력 부족(p=0.158). **높이 3 mm의 장기 MBL 보호 효과는 확인; 주위염 예방 효과는 현재 불확실.**
> - **형태 × 높이 교호작용 RCT (Seijas Naya 2025)**: 오목(Slim/concave) vs 직선(straight) 지대주 24개월 이중맹검 RCT(n=68). 오목 군이 MBL 유의하게 낮음(p=0.006–0.026). **결정적 발견**: 직선형에서는 지대주 높이↑ → MBL 증가(p<0.01)이지만, **오목형에서는 높이↑ → MBL 감소(역방향, p<0.01)**. 즉 "높이가 클수록 유리하다"는 원칙은 **오목 지대주를 선택했을 때만 성립** — 직선 지대주에서 높이를 키우면 오히려 불리할 수 있음.
> - 임상 takeaway: ① 전치부·협측은 **오목/평탄(under-contoured) 윤곽**·낮은 출현각도(~32° 목표) — 볼록은 잉여 연조직을 의도적으로 밀어낼 때만 ② **지대주 높이 ≥2 mm, 가능하면 3 mm** 우선(골 보존; 단 오목 지대주 선택 시에만 높이 증가 효과가 보호 방향으로 작동) ③ 표면 처리·OAOT는 골/연조직 결정 인자가 아니므로 다른 이유(워크플로·심미)로 선택 ④ 맞춤형 치유지대주로 윤곽을 조기 형성 ⑤ 모든 형태 결정은 baseline 연조직 두께에 종속.
> - **Ra 0.2 µm 임계값의 원출처(Bollen 1997, 2026-10-28 추가)**: Pirc 2026이 제시한 "표면조도 <0.2 µm Ra" 연마 목표는 Bollen 1997 서술 리뷰에서 나온 값이다. 이 아래로는 세균 부착이 더 줄지 않고, 위로는 조도에 비례해 치태가 늘어난다. 구강 내 경조직 일반에 대한 1997년 초록 근거라 임플란트 지대주에 대한 직접 검증은 아니다. [확인]
> - **치은 정점 (Gingival Zenith) 축 신설 (2026-11-21, 14편)**: 출현윤곽 설계의 *목표점*이 생겼다. 자연치 기준값 — 중절치 정점은 치축보다 평균 1 mm 원심, 측절치 0.4 mm, 견치는 97.5%가 중앙; 측절치 정점은 중절치–견치 정점선보다 ~1 mm 치관측(Chu 2009). SR+MA(Martins 2024)도 원심 편위 비율 중절치 96%·측절치 84%·견치 43%, 측절치 82%가 정점선보다 치관측으로 같은 방향 — 단 GRADE 매우 낮음.
> - **정점을 움직이는 레버 = 임계 윤곽 (Critical Contour)**: 과풍융은 치은연을 대체로 근단으로, 저풍융은 반대로 이동(Su 2010, 개념 원출처). 하임계 윤곽 (Subcritical Contour)은 연조직 부피·순측 윤곽을 담당. 실행 술식은 임시수복물 동적 압박 술식 (Dynamic Compression Technique, Wittneben 2013) — 모두 전문가 의견·술식 수준.
> - **"볼록은 나쁘다"의 조건화 (Chu 2019)**: 구개측·절단측 식립이면 치은연을 받치려 협측 임계 윤곽을 *볼록*하게, 협측 식립이면 *평탄/오목*하게, 하임계부는 저풍융. Siegenthaler 2022의 볼록 퇴축 OR 12.69를 뒤집는 게 아니라 "볼록은 구개측 임플란트용 도구"로 범위를 좁힌다.
> - **식립 위치가 윤곽의 전제**: 인접치 정점 연결선 기준 임플란트 순면까지 수평 2–3 mm, 플랫폼까지 수직 3–4 mm에서 치경부 형태 양호, 수직 ≤1 mm 불량(Park 2024, 왁스업 주관평가). 협측·얕은 식립은 넓은 출현각·볼록 윤곽을 강제 — 각도·형태 축의 상류 원인.
> - **정점 아래 골 (Osseous Zenith)**: 발치 6개월 수직 2.1 ± 1.7 mm·수평 1.8 ± 0.8 mm 변위, 중간 높이 순측골 <0.7 mm면 >3 mm 변위(Carranza 2016, n=20) — 위 수직 3–4 mm 창을 graft 없이 확보할 수 있는지를 결정.
> - **연결부 (Connection) 축 신설 — 골엔 영향, 정점엔 불확실**: 5년 RCT(Cooper 2021, 141명)에서 원뿔 연결 MBL −0.16 vs 평면 −0.92 vs PS −0.81 mm(P<.0005)지만 점막 정점 변화는 전 군 미미(P>.60). 1년 RCT(Farronato 2020)는 5° 원뿔이 45° 대비 협측 정점 점막 높이 3.32 vs 2.70 mm로 컸으나 군집 미보정·구치부 84%로 가설 수준. PS 심미 SR(Benli 2026)은 정점 개선을 보고하나 메타분석 없음.
> - **형태 레버 명제에 대한 반대 근거 (Calazans Neto 2026, RCT 10편 SR+MA) — 전문 재평가 후 무게 하향**: 좁은(오목·곡선) vs 직선 지대주 합성에서 MBL SMD −0.25 (−0.55~0.06, p=0.11, I²=38%; 초록의 0.08·p=0.66과 불일치), PD·PES 각 2편 무차이(p=0.80). 볼록 vs 오목은 **합성하지 않았고**(Koutouzis 2편은 서술만), MBL 포함 연구 인용 오류·Patil 3편 중복 가능성·표 복사 오류·GRADE 불일치가 있다. 판단: "오목 vs 선형/원통 지대주는 소규모 RCT에서 차이를 못 찾았다" 정도로만 읽고, **"볼록 회피"는 이 리뷰와 무관하게 유지**되고, "오목 vs 선형" 우열은 이 리뷰가 아니라 Seijas Naya 2025(오목 우위)·Barwacz(무차이) 사이에서 여전히 미결이다.
> - **자연치 정점 형태와 조직 두께는 별개 변수**: 정점 각도는 여성에서 작고 연령·치은 두께와 무상관(Kolte 2019); 좌우 소폭 비대칭 보고(Pawar 2011, 내부 수치 불일치) vs 대칭(Martins 2024).
> - **정점 축 임상 takeaway**: ① 인접 정점선 기준 수평 2–3 mm·수직 3–4 mm로 식립 ② 임계 윤곽으로 정점을 중절치 원심·견치 중앙에 배치 ③ 볼록 임계 윤곽은 구개측 식립에서 치은연 지지가 필요할 때만 ④ 하임계는 저풍융 ⑤ 정점 지지골(중간 높이 순측골)을 CBCT로 사전 평가.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 31 papers across seven abutment/restoration levers — emergence-profile shape/angle, abutment surface, disconnection policy, customized healing abutments, digital profile transfer, and (added 2026-11-21) the gingival/mucosal zenith target and the implant–abutment connection — spanning SR+MA, RCT, cohort, cross-sectional, preclinical, and narrative/technique evidence.

Emergence-profile shape and angle remain the strongest levers (convex recession OR 12.69, Siegenthaler 2022; convex peri-implantitis RR 2.32, Atieh 2023; EA >30° MBL +0.19 mm, Liu 2025; ~4× MBL at 80° vs 20° in dogs), height ≥2 mm buffers the angle penalty (Misch 2025), while an RCT-only SR+MA found no MBL/PD/PES difference between concave/curved and linear/cylindrical abutments — imprecise, internally inconsistent, and with no pooled convex-vs-concave estimate (Calazans Neto 2026, full text); natural-tooth zenith targets (central ~1 mm distal, canine centred, lateral ~1 mm coronal; Chu 2009, Martins 2024) and zenith-referenced placement (2–3 mm horizontal, 3–4 mm vertical; Park 2024) define where the contour should put the margin, while connection design changes bone (conical −0.16 vs ~−0.8/−0.9 mm at 5 y) but not zenith position (Cooper 2021).

Place the implant inside the zenith-referenced window, use the critical contour to set the zenith (convex only to support the margin over a palatal implant; Chu 2019), undercontour the subcritical zone, keep the angle narrow and the abutment tall, condition on soft-tissue thickness, and transfer the profile by scanning the provisional.

## Summary

> Update 2026-11-21: sub-axis (f) (gingival/mucosal zenith — target, contour lever, placement, osseous zenith), sub-axis (g) (implant–abutment connection), and an RCT-only counter-evidence SR+MA (Calazans Neto 2026) under (d) were added; the paper counts in the paragraph below describe the original synthesis.

A peri-implant restoration interfaces with the soft and hard tissues through four distinct, separable abutment-related levers: the abutment's **surface**, how often it is **disconnected/reconnected**, whether the healing abutment is **customized** to anatomy, and the **shape/angle of the emergence profile** the prosthesis presents to the mucosa. The wiki holds ten papers — three RCTs, two SRs, one scoping review, one SR+MA, one retrospective cohort, and two preclinical animal studies — that each interrogate one or more of these levers. Read together they sort the levers into a clear hierarchy: **shape and angle govern the tissue; surface and disconnection do not.**

**The synthesizable thesis.** When you spend clinical effort to protect the peri-implant mucosal margin, spend it on the **contour and emergence angle** of the transmucosal profile, not on the abutment's surface coating or on a one-abutment-one-time protocol. The two "shape" papers produce the largest, most consistent effects in the set; the "surface" and "disconnection" papers are essentially null on tissue outcomes. The fifth paper (customized healing abutments) is the bridge — it is the practical tool for delivering the favorable shape early, during healing, at no measured cost to biology or esthetics.

### Sub-axis (a) — Abutment surface modification: no reliable lever

Canullo 2020 (SR+MA, 10 studies; 6 pooled — 4 RCT/2 CCT, 118 patients, 182 implants) tested whether **titanium healing-abutment surface modifications** (machined vs anodized/laser/other) change peri-implant soft-tissue behavior. Short-term, modified surfaces showed **no significant difference** vs controls in plaque index (P=0.091), bleeding on probing (P=0.099), or probing depth (P=0.488), with no significant moderators; the 4 long-term (5–6 y) studies were **contradictory** by technique. Surface treatment is therefore not a dependable lever on peri-implant inflammation — choose abutments on other grounds.

### Sub-axis (b) — Disconnection policy (one-abutment-one-time): single RCT was null, but two SR+MAs now confirm modest benefit in PS+conical implants

Ríos-Santos 2020 (20-center, double-blind RCT; 147 patients, 231 platform-switched subcrestal implants) compared the **one-abutment-one-time (OAOT)** protocol (definitive abutment placed at surgery, never removed) against conventional repeated healing-abutment disconnection (up to 3×). At 12 months, marginal bone loss did **not** differ significantly (OAOT 0.36 ± 0.79 mm vs control 0.48 ± 0.71 mm), nor did ISQ (p=0.731) or plaque/bleeding indices. The one significant marginal-bone factor was **abutment height**: 2 mm abutments lost less bone than 1 mm (p=0.002).

**Updated evidence — two SR+MAs overturn the "no bone benefit" conclusion for PS+conical implants.** Abayov 2026 (SR+MA, 7 RCTs, 645 implants, 2020–2024 studies; restricted to platform-switched + conical-connection implants) found a pooled MBL difference of **MD −0.23 mm (95% CI −0.44 to −0.02)** in favor of OAOT — the first SR+MA to demonstrate a statistically significant OAOT bone benefit. Heterogeneity was substantial (I²=77%), and probing depth was NS, but implant survival exceeded 94% in both groups. Calatrava 2024, the strongest single RCT in that analysis (80 implants, 40 patients; PS conical connection), showed MBL −0.13 vs −0.61 mm at 6 months (p=0.008, Δ=0.48 mm), with keratinized tissue and probing depth also significantly better in the OAOT group.

Nunes 2025 (SR+MA, 11 RCTs, 699 implants; broader inclusion — all connection types) independently confirmed that OAOT significantly reduces marginal bone loss vs repeated disconnection.

**Current synthesis.** OAOT in **PS+conical-connection** implants now has SR+MA-level evidence for a modest but consistent MBL benefit (MD ~0.23 mm). The earlier null result of Ríos-Santos 2020 likely reflects the mixed connection types and implant designs in that trial — the benefit may be connection-type-conditional. The dominant bone lever remains **abutment height ≥2 mm**, which carries a larger, more consistent effect; OAOT adds a smaller, conditional increment on top of height. Because OAOT still carries no measured penalty in either trial type, it is a defensible choice whenever workflow or esthetics favor it, and a rational choice in PS+conical implants when MBL minimization is the goal.

**Muñoz 2025** (7-year RCT, 37 patients, 63 implants — 32 in 1-mm group, 31 in 3-mm group; all implants placed 1.5 mm subcrestally with a definitive abutment connected at surgery) is the longest follow-up RCT on abutment height. At 7 years, the 3-mm group showed significantly smaller marginal bone level changes than the 1-mm group at both mesial (−0.27 ± 1.32 mm vs −0.42 ± 0.50 mm, p<0.001) and distal (−0.33 ± 1.39 mm vs −0.35 ± 0.38 mm, p<0.001) sites. Peri-implant mucositis was numerically higher in the 3-mm group (74.2% vs 54.6%), while peri-implantitis was substantially lower (3.2% vs 18.2%) — a 5.7-fold difference that did not reach statistical significance (p=0.158) due to a ~43% dropout rate. This study extends the short-term abutment-height evidence (Ríos-Santos 2020) across 7 years: **the bone-preserving advantage of a taller abutment is durable long-term**, though the peri-implantitis prevention effect remains statistically unresolved.

### Sub-axis (c) — Customized vs conventional healing abutments: equal-or-better, no downside

Ruhstorfer 2024 (SR; 5 studies — 2 RCT/2 prospective/1 retrospective, 190 implants, 100% survival, 6–36 mo, all low RoB) compared **customized (anatomically shaped) vs conventional stock titanium** healing abutments. With the healing method held constant, customized abutments showed a **trend to equal-or-better** soft tissue (Amato: less keratinized-tissue volumetric loss, p<0.001; Perez: better papilla, p<0.001), hard tissue (Crespi: greater alveolar-width gain +2.5 vs +1.0 mm, p<0.001), esthetics (Chokaree: better PES, p=0.022), and **less insertion pain** (Chokaree, p=0.003) — with **no measured disadvantage**. The mechanism is exactly the shape lever: customized abutments contour the emergence profile during the healing phase, sparing the patient stepwise compressive shaping at delivery. Evidence is short-term and thin (5 studies, no meta-analysis), so this is a defensible preference, not a mandate.

### Sub-axis (d) — Emergence-profile shape & angle: the dominant lever

Two human RCTs converge on shape as the decisive driver:

- **Siegenthaler 2022** (anterior, 3-arm RCT, n=47; convex vs concave provisional vs healing-abutment control): emergence-profile **shape**, not the mere presence of a provisional, governed recession. At 12 months, mid-facial recession occurred in **64.3% convex vs 14.3% concave** (control 31.4%); convex carried **~13× adjusted odds** vs concave (OR 12.69, 95% CI 1.89–88.48, p=.01). Esthetics (PES/WES) did not differ across arms, and a conditioning provisional added ~2.5× visits and ~869 CHF — so the provisional's value lies in the shape it imparts, not in itself. Convex sites also trended thinner at baseline, suggesting soft-tissue thickness as the underlying modifier.
- **Wang 2022** (mandibular molars, supracrestal mucosa ≥2 mm, RCT, n=21): a **W/H-ratio-derived** buccal profile cut 12-month recession to **0.13 vs 0.63 mm** vs copying the healing-abutment contour (p=.006), with a lower initial **emergence angle (31.4° vs 40.0°, p=.025)**. A flatter, lower-angle, mucosa-derived contour preserves the margin — provided supracrestal tissue is ≥2 mm.

Both point the same way: a less-convex, lower-angle, anatomy-derived emergence profile preserves the mucosal margin, and the benefit is gated by soft-tissue thickness.

**SR+MA-level evidence for profile shape and angle thresholds.** Two SR+MAs now pool the observational and RCT evidence on emergence angle and profile:

- **Atieh 2023** (SR+MA, 4 studies, 912 implants, 397 participants, 3.8–10.9 yr follow-up) compared EA >30° vs ≤30° on MBL and peri-implantitis. Overall MBL between the two angle groups was **not** significantly different (MD 0.80 mm; 95% CI −0.13 to 1.72; p=0.09) — the angle threshold alone is a weak MBL predictor. However, in platform-matched (non-platform-switched) implants, ≤30° showed significantly less MBL, and crucially, **convex profile** was associated with a **2.3-fold peri-implantitis rate** (57.8% vs 21.3%; RR 2.32, 95% CI 1.12–4.82; p=0.02). This is the first SR+MA to pool human observational data on profile shape and peri-implantitis: profile *shape* (convex vs concave/straight) is the more clinically decisive predictor than the angular number.

- **Liu 2025** (SR+MA of RCTs only, 7 RCTs, 283 implants) compared convex/wide vs concave/flat abutments. Overall MBL difference was NS (MD 0.083 mm, p=0.082), but the **EA >30° subgroup** showed a significant MBL increase (MD +0.190 mm; p<0.001). Separately, convex/wide abutments generated significantly more **crestal bone remodeling (CBR)** than concave/flat (MD +0.560 mm; p=0.001) — a distinct outcome (bone *above* the implant platform) that angle alone doesn't capture. The take: EA >30° is the key MBL threshold; convex geometry independently drives CBR regardless of angle.

Atieh 2023 and Liu 2025 converge: **the profile's convexity is a peri-implantitis risk independent of angle, and EA >30° is where the MBL signal appears in platform-matched and RCT subgroups.** The Wang 2022 RCT ~32° recommendation and the Prpic 2026 scoping review <30° rule both now have SR+MA support.

**Preclinical causal confirmation of the angle threshold.** The clinical RCTs above could correlate angle with outcome but not prove causation. Two controlled dog-model studies supply the histological mechanism and a clean dose-response, both pointing to a narrow angle:

- **Strauss 2024** (preclinical RCT, 6 mongrel dogs, 48 bone-level sites, four customized abutments at **20°/40°/60°/80°**, 24 wk; radiograph + micro-CT + undecalcified histology + synchrotron): marginal bone loss rose **monotonically with angle** (24-wk MBL 0.07 / 0.11 / 0.23 / 0.38 mm — **~4× at 80° vs 20°**, angle effect and time×angle interaction both p<0.001), with plaque climbing to 100% at 80°. Histologically, 20°/40° kept a **continuous, intact junctional epithelium**, whereas **≥60° disrupted junctional-epithelium continuity** and disorganized supracrestal connective tissue, with the inflammatory infiltrate creeping closer to the bone crest (1.69→1.00 mm, fitting Waerhaug's ~1 mm plaque radius). Importantly, GEE adjusted for baseline soft-tissue thickness, which had **no** significant influence here (p=0.915) — isolating angle as the driver. Conclusion: keep the restorative angle **as narrow as clinically possible (<40°)**, now resting on causal rather than cross-sectional evidence.
- **Lee 2025** (preclinical split-mouth, 5 beagle dogs, 30 equicrestal implants in three-implant **splints** per hemimandible, **30° narrow vs 60° wide** custom abutments, 6-mo function; histomorphometry + polarized-light collagen): the wide angle produced **greater crestal bone remodeling** (24-wk T-splint 1.4 vs 0.57 mm), larger infiltrated connective tissue, a **shorter connective-tissue attachment (~0.7 vs ~1.1 mm)**, longer junctional epithelium, and reduced peri-implant epithelial collagen — a **failed soft-tissue seal** (biologic width inadequate under WE, adequate under NE). Its distinctive contribution is the **splinted-restoration** context: harm concentrated at the splinted/middle positions (collagen dropping 85.5%→54.1%→33.8% toward the middle implant), flagging splinted and middle implants as higher-risk for the wide-angle seal failure.

**The angle is conditional, not independent — abutment height gates it.** **Misch 2025** (retrospective radiographic cohort, 192 bone-level implants / 119 patients, mean 7.4-yr follow-up) cross-classified trans-mucosal abutment height (TmAH, cutoff 2 mm) × restoration emergence angle (REA, cutoff 30°) into four groups. REA ≥30° drove significant MBL **only when TmAH was <2 mm**: the Short/Wide group had the greatest bone loss (+0.48 mm vs Long/Narrow, p<0.001) and **~4× the peri-implantitis** (OR 4.19 vs Long/Wide, p=0.013) plus 9 of 10 failed sites, whereas **Long/Wide was statistically indistinguishable from Long/Narrow** (+0.08 mm, p=0.444). Abutment height was the dominant protective lever — each 1 mm of TmAH cut the odds of any MBL by 37% (OR 0.63, p=0.003), while REA alone was non-significant (OR 0.99). This directly extends the abutment-height result of Ríos-Santos 2020 (sub-axis b) into the angle dimension: **a wide emergence angle is dangerous mainly when stacked on a short abutment**, and a tall (≥2 mm) abutment largely neutralizes the angle penalty.

**Evidence-base consolidation.** **Prpic 2026** (PRISMA-ScR scoping review, 17 studies — 10 on angle, 7 on profile, incl. 2 RCTs + 1 SR+MA) draws the same two threads together as design rules: (1) an **emergence angle <30°** is *plausibly* protective against MBL and peri-implantitis but the cohort evidence is inconsistent (several found no association) — the authors call it **inconclusive pending RCTs**; (2) a **concave emergence profile** is *consistently* associated with soft-tissue margin stability and reduced mid-facial recession (anchored by the Siegenthaler and Endres RCTs). Crucially Prpic 2026 formalizes the **interaction**: the angle's biological risk becomes meaningful mainly when it **exceeds 30° AND the profile is convex AND the abutment is short**, while a concave profile, a stable abutment interface, and TmAH >2 mm each buffer a larger angle — exactly the conditional picture Misch 2025 quantified.

**Clinical/conceptual bridge to peri-implantitis management.** **Pirc 2026** (narrative review, University of Zurich, plus a clinical case) does not add new quantitative evidence but performs the field-consolidating move this axis needed: it unifies four overlapping nomenclatures for the transmucosal zone (Supracrestal Tissue Height/STH, Implant Supracrestal Complex/ISC, Supraplatform Complex, and the critical/subcritical Esthetic Biological Contour/EBC) into one biological map, and it reframes emergence contour/angle explicitly as a **treatment lever for existing peri-implantitis**, not only a prevention lever. It restates the same **>30° restorative-angle risk zone** as Misch 2025/Prpic 2026 (citing the same RCT evidence base) and the same **≥2 mm titanium-base abutment height** buffer, so it independently confirms rather than revises the angle/height thresholds already established in this axis. Its distinct contributions: (1) a **mucosal-tunnel depth >3 mm** (from over-deep implant placement) is proposed as a separate danger zone — driving elevated plaque and delayed inflammation resolution even in compliant patients, adding an implant-position-linked risk factor alongside angle and height; (2) a **surface-roughness target of <0.2 μm Ra** for CAD/CAM-polished restorative surfaces, extending the "surface" sub-axis (a) — but note this is a polish-finish claim about the *prosthetic* restorative surface, not the abutment-material surface treatments Canullo 2020 tested null on, so it does not contradict sub-axis (a); (3) a documented **n=1 clinical case** in which a 4-year angular bone defect resolved radiographically at 4 months after replacing a convex, short-abutment lithium-disilicate crown with a concave, tall-abutment monolithic-zirconia crown — the first case-level illustration in this axis that the shape/height levers can be used to *reverse*, not just *prevent*, peri-implant bone loss. As a narrative review with no systematic search or GRADE assessment and an n=1 case, it cannot upgrade the evidence weight of the <30°/≥2 mm thresholds, but it strengthens their face validity by tying them to a treatment outcome.

**Seijas Naya 2025** (double-blind RCT, 21 patients, 68 implants; Galimplant IPX hexagonal internal connection, 4 mm diameter, 10 mm length; straight vs concave/Slim antirotational abutments at 2 or 3 mm height; one-abutment-one-time, screw-retained; 24 months) is the first RCT to directly cross-classify **abutment shape × abutment height** on MBL. Concave abutments showed significantly less MBL than straight abutments across all radiographic measurements at 24 months (p=0.006–0.026), with the early benefit (significant at 8 weeks and 6 months) sustained. The study's most distinctive finding is a **height × shape interaction** (p<0.01): in the straight-abutment group, greater height was *positively* associated with MBL (i.e., taller straight abutments fared worse), whereas in the concave group, greater height *mitigated* bone loss (i.e., taller concave abutments fared better). This means the generally accepted rule that "taller abutments protect bone" applies only when the abutment is concave — **a taller straight abutment may not confer the expected protective benefit and may even be counter-productive.** The proposed mechanism is that a concave design creates additional supracrestal space for soft-tissue accommodation; a straight/convex design at greater height may instead increase the compressive tissue load. This finding extends Misch 2025 by adding a *shape* dimension to the height axis and provides the first controlled human evidence for the concave-abutment bone-preservation advantage.

**Counter-evidence — an RCT-only SR+MA reports no shape effect (added 2026-11-21; re-appraised from full text the same day).** **Calazans Neto 2026** (PRISMA 2020 SR+MA restricted to RCTs; PROSPERO CRD420251034218; 10 RCTs 2011–2024, 3–60 months; CC BY full text) pooled only **narrowed (concave/curved) vs straight (linear/cylindrical)** abutments: MBL 5 RCTs/282 implants **SMD −0.25 (95% CI −0.55 to 0.06; p=0.11; I²=38%)**, PD 2 RCTs SMD −0.05 (p=0.80), PES 2 RCTs SMD −0.04 (p=0.80). The abstract reports different MBL and PES figures (SMD 0.08, p=0.66; −0.12, p=0.51). The full text also shows that the **two convex-vs-concave trials (Koutouzis 2019/2023) were not pooled** — they are narrative, with no PI/BoP/PD/MBL difference, a soft-tissue-profile difference at several time points (p≤0.02), and one early concave implant failure each. Further problems: the MBL study citation includes a non-trial reference, two of three probably overlapping Patil reports appear in one pooling despite a stated one-report-per-population rule, a Table 4 row is duplicated from another study, and the GRADE text conflicts with the RoB figure. Narrative signals point both ways — wide > cylindrical MBL (Bernabeu-Mira 2023, p<0.001), concave > cylindrical soft-tissue histology (Camacho-Alonso 2025, p<0.001), single concave fractures/failures. **This page's reading:** the review shows only that small RCTs have not detected an MBL/PD/PES difference between concave/curved and linear/cylindrical abutments, with imprecise estimates. It is not evidence of equivalence, it says nothing pooled about convex vs concave, and it does not address crown emergence angle. The shape thesis therefore stands as before; the first-pass note that this review made "concave beats linear" uncertain is withdrawn — that question remains open on Seijas Naya 2025 (for) and Barwacz (null), not on this review.

### Sub-axis (e) — Capturing/transferring the shape: indirect digital scanning beats direct

A favorable emergence profile is only clinically useful if it survives the transfer from provisional to definitive restoration. Mikulás 2025 (SR, 24 studies — 1 pilot RCT, 2 cross-sectional, 4 cross-over, 12 case reports, 5 dental techniques; mostly maxillary esthetic zone) compared three digital impression workflows against the conventional elastomeric method (CM) for recording the submucosal emergence profile. The verdict: **indirect scanning** — an extraoral scan of the removed implant-supported provisional restoration (ISPR), superimposed onto the in-situ scan — most faithfully captures the EP and approaches CM, whereas **direct scanning** (intraoral scan immediately after ISPR removal) suffers significant, rapid soft-tissue collapse once mucosal support is lost: ~200–500 μm immediately, up to ~1 mm by 20 minutes (Li, Galibourg, Joda). The pilot RCT (Ling) found direct-scan labial EP deviation 1.87 mm vs indirect 0.75 mm, with comparable pink esthetic scores. This is the **delivery/transfer lever for the shape axis** — the same logic as Ruhstorfer's customized healing abutments (sub-axis c), one step downstream: having sculpted the contour with the provisional, scan the *provisional*, not the collapsing tissue, to carry that contour to the final crown. Caveats: planned meta-analysis was not interpretable (high heterogeneity); most studies moderate-to-high RoB; no agreed clinically acceptable EP-discrepancy threshold.

### Sub-axis (f) — The gingival/mucosal zenith: the target the contour aims at (added 2026-11-21)

Sub-axes (c)–(e) describe *how* to shape the transmucosal profile; they never said *where the margin should end up*. The zenith — the most apical point of the facial gingival/mucosal margin — supplies that target, and a small cluster of papers now connects it to implant position, contour, and the bone underneath.

**Natural-tooth reference values.** Chu 2009 (20 healthy young adults, 240 sites, cast calipers; abstract-only) gives the canonical numbers: central-incisor zenith **~1 mm distal** to the crown's bisecting midline (all centrals distal), laterals **~0.4 mm**, canines centred in **97.5%**; the lateral-incisor zenith sits **~1 mm coronal** to the line joining central and canine zeniths. Martins 2024 (SR+MA of cross-sectional studies; abstract-only) reproduces the gradient — distal zenith in **96% / 84% / 43%** of centrals / laterals / canines, lateral zenith coronal to the zenith line in **82%**, contralateral symmetry — but at **very low GRADE certainty**. Two small studies add texture rather than rules: Pawar 2011 (n=35) reports a canine-apical gingival line (GLA <90°) with a ~2° directional left–right asymmetry, but its lateral-incisor proportion is internally inconsistent (17% vs 75.7%); Kolte 2019 (n=160; abstract-only) finds zenith angle smaller in women, unaffected by age, and **uncorrelated with gingival thickness** (thickness ~1.1 mm F vs ~2.5 mm M) — i.e., zenith shape and tissue phenotype are separate design inputs.

**The restorative lever that moves the zenith: the critical contour.** Su 2010 (concept origin; abstract-only) splits the restoration's transmucosal part into a **critical contour** (just below the margin) and a **subcritical contour** (toward the platform) and states the working rule: **overcontouring generally moves the gingival margin apically, undercontouring the opposite**; either zone can change margin level, papillae, architecture, labial profile, and color. González-Martín 2020 restates the model for immediate and delayed provisionals, naming the zenith among the outcomes the zones control. Chu 2019 turns it into a **position-conditional decision rule**: a palatally or incisally placed implant often needs a **convex facial critical contour** to support the margin; a facially placed implant gets a **flat or concave** contour; the subcritical zone may be **undercontoured** to thicken and stabilize the cuff. Wittneben 2013 describes the chairside execution (dynamic compression technique with the provisional), listing zenith localization as an explicit end-point. All four are expert opinion/technique papers — they define vocabulary and procedure, not effect sizes.

**This refines, not reverses, the shape thesis.** Chu 2019's "convex when the implant is palatal" sits alongside Siegenthaler 2022's convex OR 12.69 for recession: convexity is a tool for supporting the margin over a palatal implant, not a default, and thin tissue (Siegenthaler) still argues against it.

**Placement is upstream of contour.** Park 2024 (58 wax-ups on healed-site single implants, 10 raters; abstract-only) referenced implant position to a wire across the adjacent teeth's zeniths: **horizontal 2.0–3.0 mm** (zenith line → implant facial surface) and **vertical 3.0–4.0 mm** (zenith line → platform) gave good cervical crown form; vertical **≤1.0 mm** was poor, and out-of-window positions were flagged as grafting indications. In this page's reading, a facial or shallow implant forces an abrupt transition from platform to margin — the wide angle and convex buccal contour that sub-axis (d) associates with bone loss and recession — so the zenith-referenced window is the placement-side way to stay out of that corner (an inference; Park measured esthetic form, not tissue outcomes).

**The bone under the zenith.** Carranza 2016 (n=20, CT at <24 h and 6 months after atraumatic extraction with primary closure; abstract-only) followed the **osseous zenith** (crest apical to the gingival zenith): vertical displacement **2.1 ± 1.7 mm** (range 0.2–7.5) and horizontal **1.8 ± 0.8 mm**; crestal facial width did not predict displacement, but **mid-level facial width <0.7 mm predicted >3 mm**. This determines whether Park's 3–4 mm vertical window is reachable without grafting.

### Sub-axis (g) — Implant–abutment connection: changes bone, not clearly the zenith (added 2026-11-21)

**Cooper 2021** (multicenter RCT, 141 subjects, anterior maxillary single implants, healed ridges, immediate provisionalization, 5 years; abstract-only) compared conical, flat-to-flat, and platform-switched interfaces. MBL change was **−0.16 ± 0.45 (conical) vs −0.92 ± 0.70 (flat-to-flat) vs −0.81 ± 1.06 mm (PS)**, P<.0005, but **mucosal zenith change was minimal in every group (P>.60)**, with similar papillae and BoP 16–19%. Attrition was heavy (141 → 88 evaluable). **Farronato 2020** (RCT, 104 patients / 188 PS implants, 12-month provisional; PMC full text) found the 5° conical connection supported taller and thicker buccal-zenith mucosa than a 45° internal hex (MH 3.32 vs 2.70 mm; MT 4.37 vs 3.93 mm; MT/MH associated with group, p=0.047), but tested groups with Pearson correlation on implant-level data without clustering adjustment, was 84% posterior, and compared whole implant systems. **Benli 2026** (SR, 24 in vivo studies, no meta-analysis; abstract-only) reports PS improving PES, papillae, and zenith change. Read together: connection design is a real **bone** lever (conical advantage consistent with sub-axis (b)'s PS+conical OAOT data), but the **zenith** effect is unresolved — the longest, three-arm RCT shows none.

### Reconciliation — why the axes diverge

- **Shape > surface/disconnection is a hierarchy, not a contradiction.** The two shape RCTs deliver large, significant, same-direction effects (recession 13× higher convex; recession halved at lower angle), while the surface SR+MA and the OAOT RCT are flatly null on tissue outcomes. There is no genuine conflict — the levers simply differ in magnitude, and clinical attention should track the magnitude.
- **The OAOT null does not erase a real height effect.** Ríos-Santos found disconnection count irrelevant but abutment **height** strongly relevant (p=0.002), echoing the general crestal-bone literature. So "abutment choice matters for bone" survives as *height*, not as *disconnection avoidance*. Misch 2025 then **fuses the height and angle axes**: a wide emergence angle is harmful chiefly *on a short abutment*, and a tall (≥2 mm) abutment buffers the angle penalty — so the two formerly separate "bone levers" (height, angle) are one interacting system, not independent additive risks.
- **The angle threshold is now causal, not merely correlational.** The early angle data were cross-sectional (confounded by case mix); Strauss 2024 and Lee 2025 are randomized/controlled animal experiments that reproduce the angle→MBL→seal-failure chain under controlled conditions, with a clean dose-response (Strauss: 20→80° monotonic, ~4× MBL) and a histological mechanism (junctional-epithelium breakdown ≥60°, shortened connective-tissue attachment). This upgrades the "<40° / <30°" advice from expert opinion to mechanistically grounded recommendation, and Prpic 2026 packages it as a design rule — though Prpic also flags that the *clinical* <30° threshold remains formally inconclusive pending human RCTs.
- **Splinting and implant position modulate the angle risk.** Lee 2025's split-mouth design shows the wide-angle seal failure is worst at **splinted and middle** implants (collagen and attachment degrade toward the center of a splint), adding a positional caveat absent from the single-unit RCTs — relevant when designing multi-unit emergence profiles.
- **Soft-tissue thickness is the shared moderator.** Siegenthaler's convex sites trended thinner at baseline (plausible recession mechanism); Wang's W/H design only applies with ≥2 mm supracrestal mucosa. The shape lever is conditional on adequate soft tissue — connecting this page to the keratinized-mucosa / dehiscence axes below.
- **Customization is the delivery vehicle, not a fifth independent effect.** Ruhstorfer's customized-abutment advantage *is* the shape lever, applied earlier in the timeline — it pre-forms the emergence profile during healing rather than at the definitive stage (Wang/Siegenthaler).
- **Abutment height and shape are not independent — they interact.** Seijas Naya 2025 shows that the height axis works in *opposite* directions depending on abutment shape: taller concave abutment → less MBL; taller straight abutment → more MBL (interaction p<0.01). This resolves an apparent inconsistency in the literature and means the three variables (height, angle, shape) form a single interacting system rather than independent additive risks. The practical corollary: select a concave abutment *before* increasing height, or a height increase may backfire.
- **The long-term durability of the height advantage is now supported.** Muñoz 2025 (7 years, n=63) confirms that the MBL benefit of a taller abutment (1 mm vs 3 mm) seen at 12 months persists across 7 years (p<0.001). The peri-implantitis prevention signal (3.2% vs 18.2%) is numerically large but statistically unresolved due to ~43% dropout — a longer, adequately powered RCT is needed.
- **Evidence caveats.** Wang and Canullo are abstract-only/short-term; Ruhstorfer is qualitative (no MA, 5 small studies); Siegenthaler's absolute recession magnitudes are near probe error; Muñoz 2025 is underpowered for peri-implantitis (~43% dropout); Seijas Naya 2025 has no control arm and small n. The *direction* (favor concave/low-angle/tall design, customize early, ignore surface/disconnection) is consistent; precise effect sizes are not fully nailed down.

- **The zenith gives the shape axis a target, and position comes before contour (added 2026-11-21).** Sub-axes (c)–(e) tune the contour; sub-axis (f) says where it should place the margin (natural-tooth zenith values) and shows that the contour can only do so when the implant sits inside a zenith-referenced window (Park 2024) over enough mid-level facial bone (Carranza 2016). The convex-contour exception (Chu 2019) is a position-driven exception, consistent with — not contrary to — Siegenthaler's recession data.
- **Calazans Neto 2026 does not move the shape thesis (full-text re-appraisal).** Its pooled comparisons are concave/curved vs linear/cylindrical only, imprecise (MBL CI −0.55 to +0.06 SMD), and internally inconsistent (abstract vs results, study citations, a duplicated table row, GRADE). It contains no pooled convex-vs-concave estimate, so neither "avoid convex" (Siegenthaler RCT, Atieh SR+MA) nor the concave-over-straight bone signal (Seijas Naya 2025) is contradicted by it; it adds that no detectable difference emerged among the gentler profiles in small trials.
- **Connection changes bone but not demonstrably the zenith.** Cooper 2021's 5-year three-arm RCT separates the two: conical interfaces lost far less bone, yet the zenith barely moved in any arm. Farronato 2020's 1-year zenith-dimension difference is methodologically weak, and Benli 2026's PS-esthetic SR has no pooled estimate. Bone and mucosal-margin outcomes should not be assumed to move together.
- **Zenith evidence is low-tier.** The natural-tooth values come from small cross-sectional samples (SR+MA at very-low GRADE); the contour lever and conditioning technique are expert opinion; Park 2024 used subjective wax-up grading; five of the fourteen added papers are abstract-only. Use the zenith values as design defaults, not thresholds.

### Clinical decision

**Emergence contour & angle (the primary lever).**
- Anterior/esthetic zone: favor a **concave (or flat/under-contoured)** emergence profile over convex; reserve convex only to deliberately push apically excess overgrown soft tissue (Siegenthaler 2022; concave consistency confirmed across the scoping review, Prpic 2026).
- Posterior with ≥2 mm supracrestal mucosa: design the buccal profile from the **W/H ratio** and keep the initial **emergence angle near ~32°** rather than 40°; do not simply copy the healing-abutment contour (Wang 2022).
- **Keep the emergence angle narrow — target <30°, and <40° at minimum.** Controlled dog-model evidence shows a dose-dependent rise in bone loss with angle (~4× MBL at 80° vs 20°) and junctional-epithelium breakdown once the angle reaches ≥60° (Strauss 2024); a separate splinted-implant model confirms the seal failure at 60° vs 30° (Lee 2025). For **multi-unit / splinted** restorations, be especially conservative with the angle at the middle/splinted units, which are the most vulnerable to seal failure (Lee 2025).
- **The angle is conditional on abutment height** — a wide (≥30°) angle does most of its damage on a short (<2 mm) abutment; if a wider angle is anatomically unavoidable, pair it with a **taller (≥2 mm) trans-mucosal abutment**, which largely neutralizes the penalty (Misch 2025).
- Gate every shape decision on **baseline soft-tissue thickness** — thin tissue amplifies convex-profile recession.

**Abutment disconnection policy.**
- For **platform-switched + conical-connection implants**: OAOT is now supported by two SR+MAs (Abayov 2026: MD −0.23 mm, 7 RCTs; Nunes 2025: 11 RCTs) — choose OAOT for both workflow/esthetic convenience *and* a modest bone-preservation benefit. The single earlier null RCT (Ríos-Santos 2020) likely reflects a mixed implant-connection population where the benefit is diluted. For other connection types, OAOT remains a neutral choice (no penalty), and the earlier null result may still apply.
- The earlier recommendation — "choose OAOT for workflow/esthetic reasons, not bone" — is now updated to: **choose OAOT for both reasons in PS+conical systems; it is neutral-to-beneficial in all other systems.**
- Prioritize **transmucosal abutment height ≥2 mm (ideally 3 mm)** for crestal bone — this is the real bone lever (Ríos-Santos 2020, Misch 2025, Muñoz 2025 7-year RCT). However, the height benefit is **conditional on abutment shape**: increasing height is protective only when the abutment is concave; in a straight abutment, greater height may worsen MBL (Seijas Naya 2025 interaction p<0.01). **Choose a concave abutment first, then maximize height.**

**Healing-abutment choice.**
- Prefer a **customized (anatomically shaped) healing abutment** when you want the emergence profile contoured during healing — especially immediate placement in esthetic sites or large posterior sockets — to reduce delivery-time compressive pain and streamline the (digital) workflow; there is no measured biological/esthetic downside (Ruhstorfer 2024).
- Do **not** rely on **abutment surface modification** to control plaque, bleeding, or pocket depth — it is not a reliable lever (Canullo 2020).

**Zenith target, placement, and contour sequencing (added 2026-11-21).**
- **Plan from the zenith.** Default targets: central-incisor zenith ~1 mm distal to the crown midline, lateral ~0.4 mm, canine centred; lateral zenith ~1 mm coronal to the central–canine line (Chu 2009; Martins 2024, very-low certainty). Match the contralateral tooth when it is healthy.
- **Place inside the zenith-referenced window.** Measured from a line across the adjacent zeniths: implant facial surface 2–3 mm palatal, platform 3–4 mm apical; avoid ≤1 mm depth (Park 2024). Before extraction, read the **mid-level** facial bone on CBCT — <0.7 mm predicts >3 mm loss of the bone under the zenith (Carranza 2016) and argues for grafting or a staged approach.
- **Set the zenith with the critical contour; free the subcritical.** Convex facial critical contour only when a palatal/incisal implant leaves the margin unsupported; flat/concave for a facial implant; undercontour the subcritical zone (Chu 2019; Su 2010). Condition the margin with the provisional before final impression (Wittneben 2013), then transfer by scanning the provisional (Mikulás 2025).
- **Connection choice is a bone decision.** Prefer conical/PS-conical interfaces for crestal bone (Cooper 2021), but do not expect the connection to fix a zenith problem — contour and position do that.

**Capturing the profile for the definitive crown.**
- When transferring a provisional-sculpted emergence profile digitally, **scan the removed provisional (indirect method)** and superimpose it, rather than scanning the soft tissue directly after provisional removal — direct scanning loses the submucosal contour to rapid collapse (~200–500 μm immediately, up to ~1 mm within 20 min) (Mikulás 2025). If a direct scan is unavoidable, capture it within seconds. Conventional elastomeric impression with a customized coping remains the gold-standard comparator.
