## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: "진통제 알러지"·"아스피린 알러지"는 **하나의 진단이 아니라 표현형(phenotype) 질문**이다. 표현형이 "다른 NSAID·선택적 사이클로옥시게나제-2 억제제(Cyclooxygenase-2 inhibitor, COX-2 억제제)·아세트아미노펜(Acetaminophen) 중 무엇이 안전한가"를 결정한다. 3편(WAO 2025 합의성명 1·서술적 고찰 1·체계적 고찰 1)을 치과 진통제 결정으로 번역.
> - **가장 큰 갈림길 — 교차반응형(cross-reactive) vs 약물특이형(drug-specific)**: ① **화학적으로 무관한 NSAID 2종 이상**에 반응했거나 천식·비용종·만성 두드러기가 깔려 있으면 → 교차반응형(N-ERD·NECD·NIUAA). 기전은 COX-1 억제 → 류코트리엔 션팅(leukotriene shunting), **면역글로불린 E(Immunoglobulin E, IgE) 아님**. **모든 강력 COX-1 억제제(ibuprofen·naproxen·aspirin·diclofenac·ketorolac)가 금기.** ② **딱 한 가지 NSAID(또는 같은 화학군)**에만 반응하고 나머지는 잘 견디면 → 약물특이형(SNIUAA IgE / SNIDHR T세포). **그 약과 같은 화학군만 회피**, 다른 계열 NSAID는 사용 가능 (Romano 2025 `[확인]`).
> - **교차반응형에서 안전한 선택지 (정량)** `[확인]`: 선택적 COX-2 억제제 유발검사 양성률 전체 **3.3%**(106/3,218), 호흡기 반응군은 **0.13%**(1/753) — 전부 경증, 에피네프린 필요 0건 (Li 2019). 멜록시캄(meloxicam)은 **4.7%**(847건)로 더 높아 코xib보다 열등. N-ERD에서 고용량 파라세타몰(Paracetamol ≥1 g)은 **~19–22%**가 반응 (Blanca-Lopez 2019 ~19%, Romano 2025 인용 22.4%).
> - **잘 조절되는 천식 + 단독 호흡기 반응** → 셀레콕시브(Celecoxib) **무감독 투여 가능**. **두드러기·혈관부종·아나필락시스 병력** → 감독하 투여 또는 2단계 점증 경구유발검사(1/10–1/4 용량 → 전량, 각 1–2시간 관찰). **멜록시캄 전엔 알레르기 평가.** **중증·조절불량 천식**은 셀레콕시브 첫 투여도 주의(브리검 AERD 센터: 조절불량 천식에서 FEV1 21–64% 저하 증례) (Li 2019 `[확인]`).
> - **치과 1차 처방에 미치는 대가**: 위키의 표준 1차 [[drug-analgesics-postop-pain-overview|치과 급성통증 처방]]은 **ibuprofen 400 mg + APAP 1000 mg 병용(치료필요수 Number Needed to Treat, NNT ≈ 1.5)**. 교차반응형 환자는 이 조합을 통째로 잃고 → **APAP 단독(NNT ≈ 3.5)** 또는 **셀레콕시브 200 mg**으로 내려간다. 약물특이형(비-culprit)은 대가 없음 — ibuprofen 또는 naproxen 그대로 사용.
> - **⚠️ COX-2 이중구속(double-bind)**: 자매 오버뷰 [[nsaid-osseointegration-impairment-overview]]는 **신선 임플란트 직후 선택적 COX-2 억제제 회피**를 권고. 따라서 **NSAID 과민 + 임플란트 식립** 환자에서 셀레콕시브는 과민반응 축에선 정답이지만 골유착 축에선 오답 → **교집합은 아세트아미노펜**.
> - **라벨은 대개 틀렸다** `[확인]`: 전자의무기록상 NSAID "알러지" 성인 외래환자의 **20% 미만**만 실제 과민반응에 부합 (Li 2019). NSAID가 베타락탐을 제치고 **과민성 약물반응(Hypersensitivity Drug Reaction, HDR) 원인 1위** — 4,000건+ 유발검사 확진 코호트에서 **47.2%가 다중 NSAID 과민**, 항생제는 18% (Blanca-Lopez 2019). 과잉 회피는 환자를 오피오이드·저효능 진통으로 내몬다.
> - **진단 확정은 경구 아스피린 유발검사(Aspirin/ASA challenge)** `[확인]`: 교차반응형에 **검증된 체외/체내 검사 없음** — 약물유발검사(Drug Provocation Test, DPT)가 유일(반복적·일관된 다약제 병력이면 병력만으로 분류 가능). 피부검사는 피린계(pyrazolone)와 지연형 반응에만 유용. 베타차단제는 유발검사 전 금기 (Romano 2025, Blanca-Lopez 2019).
> - **약물특이형 화학군 지도** `[확인]`: 아릴프로피온산(ibuprofen·ketoprofen·flurbiprofen·naproxen), 아세트산(diclofenac·aceclofenac), 옥시캄(piroxicam·meloxicam·tenoxicam), 피린계(metamizole·propyphenazone). culprit + **같은 군 전체** 회피가 보수적 기본값 — 단 일부 선택적 프로피온산 반응자는 다른 프로피온산을 견딤(예: naproxen 반응자가 ibuprofen 내약) → 유발검사로 확인 (Blanca-Lopez 2019).
> - **NEFA/NIFA (음식 공동인자형)**: NSAID가 특정 음식·운동과 겹칠 때만 반응 → NSAID 자체는 **culprit 음식과 4시간 이상 분리**하면 안전 (Romano 2025 `[확인]`).
> - **범위 밖 — 혼동 금지**: 진성 국소마취제(Local Anesthetic, LA) 알러지는 별개이고 극히 드물다(대부분 에피네프린·미주신경성 실신·독성·첨가물[metabisulfite·methylparaben] — [[local-anesthesia/bina-2018-true-allergy-amide-local-anesthetics-case]]). 페니실린·항생제 알러지도 별개 경로. NSAID 골유착 우려도 다른 축(과민반응 아님).
> - **치과 실무 정리**: ① 병력 3문항(어떤 약·어떤 반응·몇 번·언제 / 천식·비용종·만성두드러기 유무 / 한 약인가 여러 약인가) → ② 교차반응형이면 APAP ≤1 g 저용량 시작 또는 (검증된) 셀레콕시브, 강력 COX-1 억제제 전면 회피 → ③ 약물특이형이면 비-culprit NSAID 그대로, 위키 표준 처방 유지 → ④ 분류 불가면 알레르기내과 의뢰 + 잠정적으로 APAP ≤1 g → ⑤ 확정 표현형과 안전 대체약을 **약물알레르기 여권(drug allergy passport)**에 문서화. 오피오이드는 ADA 2024 지침대로 불충분 시에만·최소.

## Three-line Summary

A reported "NSAID allergy" or "aspirin allergy" is not one diagnosis — it is a phenotype question, and the phenotype (established from three history questions and, when unclear, an oral aspirin challenge) decides whether another NSAID, a selective COX-2 inhibitor, or only acetaminophen is safe for dental analgesia.

The pivotal split is cross-reactive (reactions to ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs, or reactions in a patient with underlying asthma/nasal polyps/chronic urticaria — a COX-1-inhibition, non-IgE mechanism that bars every strong COX-1 inhibitor) versus drug-specific (one NSAID or one chemical group only, an IgE- or T-cell-mediated mechanism that spares unrelated NSAIDs); for the cross-reactive group the quantified safe options are low-dose acetaminophen (≤1 g; ~19–22% of N-ERD still react) and a selective COX-2 inhibitor (celecoxib/etoricoxib, ~3.3% cross-reaction overall, 0.13% in respiratory reactors, none severe), while meloxicam (~4.7%) is inferior.

For dentistry this means the cross-reactive patient loses the wiki's first-line ibuprofen 400 mg + acetaminophen 1000 mg combination (NNT ≈ 1.5) and drops to acetaminophen alone (NNT ≈ 3.5) or challenge-verified celecoxib — which is simultaneously the agent a sibling overview says to avoid around fresh implants, making acetaminophen the intersection; the drug-specific patient loses nothing but the culprit drug and its chemical group.

## 세줄요약

보고된 "NSAID 알러지"·"아스피린 알러지"는 하나의 진단이 아니라 표현형(phenotype) 질문이다 — 병력 3문항과, 애매하면 경구 아스피린 유발검사로 표현형을 정하고, 그 표현형이 다른 NSAID·선택적 COX-2 억제제·아세트아미노펜 중 무엇이 치과 진통에 안전한지를 결정한다.

결정적 갈림길은 교차반응형(화학적으로 무관한 NSAID 2종 이상 반응, 또는 천식·비용종·만성두드러기 환자의 반응 — COX-1 억제·비IgE 기전으로 모든 강력 COX-1 억제제 금기)과 약물특이형(한 가지 NSAID 또는 한 화학군만 반응 — IgE·T세포 기전으로 무관한 NSAID는 안전); 교차반응형의 정량적 안전 선택지는 저용량 아세트아미노펜(≤1 g; N-ERD의 ~19–22%는 여전히 반응)과 선택적 COX-2 억제제(셀레콕시브·에토리콕시브, 전체 교차반응 ~3.3%, 호흡기 반응군 0.13%, 중증 0건)이며 멜록시캄(~4.7%)은 열등하다.

치과에서는 교차반응형 환자가 위키 1차 처방인 ibuprofen 400 mg + 아세트아미노펜 1000 mg 병용(NNT ≈ 1.5)을 통째로 잃고 아세트아미노펜 단독(NNT ≈ 3.5) 또는 검증된 셀레콕시브로 내려가며 — 셀레콕시브는 자매 오버뷰가 신선 임플란트 주변에서 회피하라는 약이기도 해서 아세트아미노펜이 교집합이 된다; 약물특이형 환자는 culprit 약과 그 화학군만 잃는다.

## The Core Distinction: Cross-Reactive vs Drug-Specific

Everything downstream hangs on one question: **did the patient react to more than one chemically unrelated NSAID, or to just one?**

| | Cross-reactive (cross-intolerant, CI) | Drug-specific (selective responder, SR) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger pattern** | ≥2 chemically unrelated NSAIDs, **or** reaction in a patient with underlying asthma / chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps / chronic spontaneous urticaria | One NSAID, or several from the **same chemical group**, with proven tolerance of unrelated NSAIDs |
| **Mechanism** | Pharmacologic COX-1 inhibition → prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) falls → leukotriene shunting → mast-cell/eosinophil mediator release. **Not IgE.** (Becker 2010 calls this "pseudoallergy") | Immunologic — IgE-mediated (SNIUAA, mostly pyrazolones) or T-cell-mediated (SNIDHR, delayed). Independent of COX-1 potency |
| **Phenotypes** | N-ERD, NECD, NIUAA (+ mixed variants) | SNIUAA (immediate), SNIDHR (delayed) |
| **Are other NSAIDs safe?** | **No** — every strong COX-1 inhibitor is barred | **Yes** — only the culprit drug + its chemical group must be avoided |
| **Relative frequency** | CI : SR ≈ 3 : 1 in adult populations; children skew even more toward CI (Blanca-Lopez 2019) `[확인]` | ~15–47% of confirmed immediate NSAID hypersensitivity, depending on series (Romano 2025) `[확인]` |

`[확인]` NSAIDs are now the **single most common cause of hypersensitivity drug reactions**, having surpassed beta-lactams; in a series of >4,000 provocation-confirmed cases, 47.2% were multiple-NSAID hypersensitivity vs 18% antibiotics (Blanca-Lopez 2019). Ibuprofen is the "universal culprit"; aspirin's share has declined with its falling cardiovascular use.

## Phenotype Map (WAO 2025)

`[확인]` From [[drug/analgesics/romano-2025-nsaid-hypersensitivity-classification-diagnosis-wao]]:

| Phenotype | Recognise it by | Mechanism | What is safe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N-ERD** (NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease; = AERD / aspirin-induced asthma) | Asthma + chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps; nasal congestion / bronchospasm 30 min–3 h after any COX-1 inhibitor. ~7.2% of adult asthmatics, ~14.9% of severe asthmatics | COX-1 inhibition, leukotriene overproduction | Selective COX-2 (almost always tolerated on challenge); low-dose acetaminophen; **high-dose acetaminophen ≥1 g reacts in ~22%** |
| **Mixed N-ERD** | N-ERD patient who also gets urticaria/angioedema (i.e. an anaphylactic reaction) | Same | Same, but challenge-verify if anaphylaxis history |
| **NECD** (NSAID-exacerbated cutaneous disease) | Known chronic spontaneous urticaria that flares within hours of an unrelated NSAID; up to 30% of chronic urticaria patients | COX-1 inhibition | Weak COX-1 inhibitors (acetaminophen), preferential/selective COX-2 — **challenge-verify before prescribing**; tolerance returns during urticaria remission |
| **Mixed NECD** | NECD patient who also gets respiratory symptoms | Same | Same |
| **NIUAA** (NSAID-induced urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis; new umbrella term replacing NIUA) | Urticaria/angioedema ± a second organ system, to ≥2 unrelated NSAIDs, **no** underlying chronic urticaria. Most common category at all ages (>40% adults); up to 60% atopic (dust mite) | COX-1 inhibition | As NECD — **challenge-verify the alternative** |
| **SNIUAA** (single-NSAID urticaria/angioedema/anaphylaxis) | Reaction to one NSAID / one chemical group only, unrelated NSAIDs tolerated; most often pyrazolones (metamizole) or propionic acids | IgE-mediated (proven mainly for pyrazolones); pyrazolone sensitivity may wane over years | **All chemically unrelated NSAIDs** — avoid only culprit + same group |
| **SNIDHR** (single-NSAID-induced delayed hypersensitivity) | Fixed drug eruption, maculopapular exanthem, contact/photocontact dermatitis 24–48 h after one NSAID; rarely SJS/TEN, DRESS | T-cell-mediated | Chemically unrelated NSAIDs; avoid culprit + group |
| **NEFA / NIFA** (NSAID-exacerbated / -induced food allergy) | Reaction only when NSAID is taken near a specific food (± exercise, alcohol); Pru p 3 / ω-5 gliadin sensitisation | NSAID increases gut allergen absorption | The NSAID itself — **if separated from the culprit food by ≥4 h** |

## Mechanism: Why COX-1 Inhibition Drives Cross-Reactivity

`[확인]` In the cross-reactive phenotypes, blocking **constitutive COX-1** lowers protective PGE2. PGE2 normally brakes 5-lipoxygenase; with the brake off, arachidonic acid is shunted to cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC4/D4/E4), which contract bronchial smooth muscle, raise vascular permeability, and recruit inflammatory cells. Any drug that inhibits COX-1 strongly enough — aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, ketorolac, and **high-dose acetaminophen** — triggers the same cascade, which is why the reaction is *cross*-reactive and not drug-specific (Romano 2025; Becker 2010 frames it as "leukotriene-shunt pseudoallergy"). Selective COX-2 inhibitors spare COX-1, so they "almost always" pass observed challenges in N-ERD (Romano 2025) `[확인]`.

Drug-specific reactions (SNIUAA/SNIDHR) are the opposite: an immune response to one molecule's structure, **independent of how strongly it inhibits COX** — so a patient can react to ibuprofen and tolerate aspirin, which would be impossible in a cross-reactive patient.

## The Dental Decision Path

**Step 1 — three history questions** (Blanca-Lopez 2019; Romano 2025) `[확인]`:
1. *Which* drug(s), *what* reaction (respiratory / cutaneous / anaphylaxis / delayed rash), *how many* separate episodes, *how long* ago (>5 y makes history unreliable)?
2. Underlying **asthma / nasal polyps / chronic rhinosinusitis** (→ N-ERD) or **chronic urticaria** (→ NECD)?
3. **One** NSAID, or **several chemically unrelated** ones?

**Step 2 — classify and choose:**

| History | Phenotype | Dental analgesic |
|---|---|---|
| ≥2 unrelated NSAIDs, **or** asthma/polyps/urticaria + any NSAID reaction | Cross-reactive (N-ERD / NECD / NIUAA) | **Avoid all strong COX-1 inhibitors** (ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin, diclofenac, ketorolac). Use **acetaminophen ≤1 g single dose, start low** (weak COX-1 — but ~19–22% of N-ERD still react at high dose), **or a selective COX-2 inhibitor** (celecoxib 200 mg, etoricoxib). Challenge-verify the COX-2 agent for NECD/NIUAA and for N-ERD with anaphylaxis history; **avoid meloxicam/nimesulide** (higher cross-reaction). Opioid step-up per ADA 2024 only if inadequate |
| One NSAID (or one chemical group), tolerates others | Drug-specific (SNIUAA / SNIDHR) | Avoid **culprit + its chemical group only**. Any unrelated NSAID is fine — including the wiki's first-line ibuprofen or naproxen if that group is not the culprit. If ibuprofen is the culprit, the conservative default is to avoid all arylpropionic acids (ketoprofen, flurbiprofen, naproxen) and use diclofenac, etoricoxib, or acetaminophen — though some selective propionic-acid reactors do tolerate other propionic drugs, so a supervised challenge can recover options (Blanca-Lopez 2019) |
| Reaction only alongside a specific food (± exercise) | NEFA / NIFA | The NSAID is safe if taken **≥4 h apart from the culprit food** |
| History cannot be resolved | Unclassified | Refer to allergy for an **oral aspirin provocation test** (gold standard; no validated in-vitro test for cross-reactive types). Interim: acetaminophen ≤1 g, or a supervised low-dose COX-2 |

**Step 3 — document.** Record the confirmed phenotype and the specific safe alternatives in a written **drug allergy passport** (Blanca-Lopez 2019) `[확인]` so the next prescriber does not re-avoid everything.

### When can celecoxib be given without a supervised challenge?

`[확인]` Li 2019's risk-stratification tool for non-allergists:

- **Isolated respiratory reaction history + well-controlled asthma** → celecoxib may be prescribed **without** supervised challenge (respiratory-reactor cross-reaction rate 0.13%).
- **Urticaria / angioedema / anaphylaxis history** → **supervised administration** or a **2-step graded oral challenge** (1/10–1/4 dose, then full dose, 1 h observation after each).
- **Severe or poorly controlled asthma** → delay first celecoxib dose / give under supervision (rare bronchospasm case reports, FEV1 drop 21–64%, one requiring epinephrine).
- **Before meloxicam** in any NSAID-hypersensitive patient → formal allergy evaluation (4.7% cross-reaction).

## Quantitative Safety of the Alternatives

`[확인]` Pooled challenge data (Li 2019 systematic search of 62 blinded placebo-controlled studies, 3,218 challenges; echoed in Romano 2025):

| Alternative agent | Reaction rate on challenge in NSAID-hypersensitive patients | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Selective COX-2 inhibitors (overall) | **3.29%** (106 / 3,218) — all cutaneous or non-severe; **0 requiring emergency care or epinephrine**; 1 laryngeal edema | Most who react to one coxib tolerate another |
| Celecoxib | 3.5% (40 / 1,140); post-2019 N-ERD trials ~4.1% | Only coxib marketed in many countries incl. USA |
| Etoricoxib | 5.4% (32 / 595) | Higher COX-2 selectivity than celecoxib |
| Parecoxib | 0% (0 / 116) | Parenteral |
| **Coxibs in respiratory-reaction subgroup** | **0.13%** (1 / 753) | The safest scenario |
| Meloxicam (preferential COX-2) | **4.72%** (40 / 847); post-2019 N-ERD ~14.3% | Inferior to coxibs — allergy eval first |
| Nimesulide (preferential COX-2) | ~8–16% in cross-hypersensitive patients | Not available in many countries |
| **Acetaminophen ≥1 g** (weak COX-1 inhibitor) | **~19%** cross-reactive adults (Blanca-Lopez); **22.4%** (11 / 49) N-ERD, 9.4% (3 / 32) NIUAA (Romano) | Low doses (≤500 mg, ~15 mg/kg in children) generally tolerated — **start low** |

`[근거낮음]` [[drug/analgesics/beaudart-2025-celecoxib-safety-musculoskeletal-sr-ma|Beaudart 2025 umbrella review]] adds that celecoxib's gastrointestinal safety is clearly better than nonselective NSAIDs (ulcer RR 0.22, moderate certainty) and its cardiovascular/renal signals are non-inferior — but that is musculoskeletal (non-dental) population data, and 14/16 included reviews were AMSTAR-2 critically low.

## Mapping to the Wiki's First-Line Dental Regimen

`[확인]` The wiki's standard for acute dental pain ([[overviews/drug-analgesics-postop-pain-overview]], [[drug/analgesics/moore-2015-single-dose-oral-analgesics-cochrane-overview]], [[drug/analgesics/carrasco-labra-2024-pharmacologic-management-acute-dental-pain-guideline]]):

| Regimen | NNT (≥50% pain relief, single dose) | Available to a cross-reactive NSAID-allergic patient? |
|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen 400 mg + acetaminophen 1000 mg | **≈ 1.5** | **No** — ibuprofen barred |
| Ibuprofen 400 mg alone | ≈ 2.5 | **No** |
| Acetaminophen 1000 mg alone | ≈ 3.5 | **Marginal** — this *is* a high dose; ~1 in 5 N-ERD patients react. Prefer ≤500–650 mg and titrate |
| Celecoxib 200 mg | (not a dental single-dose NNT in the wiki; Becker 2010 lists 200 mg qd–bid as an analgesic dose) | **Yes**, challenge-verified as above |
| Alternate ibuprofen / acetaminophen q4h | rescue-analgesia need 15% vs 53% concurrent (Gaballah 2025) | **No** (ibuprofen component) |
| Opioid (APAP + hydrocodone/oxycodone) as ADA step-up | — | Yes, but reserved: [[drug/analgesics/feldman-2024-nonopioid-opioid-third-molar-rct|opioids are not superior]] to non-opioid for third-molar pain; ADA 2024 = lowest dose, rarely >3 days |

**Bottom line for the cross-reactive patient:** the realistic dental plan is **acetaminophen (low-dose, titrated) as the base**, **challenge-verified celecoxib** when more anti-inflammatory effect is needed, and an opioid only as the ADA step-up. The drug-specific patient keeps the full first-line ladder minus one drug group.

## The COX-2 Double-Bind and the Aspirin Crossover

`[미검증]` Two adjacent wiki axes constrain the celecoxib recommendation:

- **Osseointegration** — [[overviews/nsaid-osseointegration-impairment-overview]] concludes that *selective* COX-2 inhibitors are the NSAID subgroup most likely to impair early bone formation, and advises avoiding them around freshly placed implants. So for an **NSAID-hypersensitive implant patient**, celecoxib is the right answer on the hypersensitivity axis and the wrong one on the osseointegration axis → **acetaminophen is the intersection**; if a COX-2 agent is genuinely needed, keep it to the shortest possible course and weigh the bone-quality risk factors.
- **Aspirin antiplatelet** — [[overviews/nsaid-aspirin-antiplatelet-interaction-overview]] already flags celecoxib as **not** interfering with low-dose aspirin's cardioprotection (unlike ibuprofen/naproxen). So for a **cardiac patient on low-dose aspirin who is NSAID-hypersensitive**, celecoxib is consistent on both axes — provided the aspirin hypersensitivity itself has been phenotyped (a true aspirin-triggered N-ERD patient on cardiac aspirin has usually been desensitised; see Blanca-Lopez 2019 on ASA desensitisation, 40.5 mg → 325 mg over 1–3 days).

## What This Overview Does NOT Cover

- **True local anaesthetic allergy** — a separate and *far rarer* entity. `[확인]` [[local-anesthesia/bina-2018-true-allergy-amide-local-anesthetics-case]]: the overwhelming majority of "LA allergy" labels are reactions to epinephrine, vasovagal syncope, overdose toxicity, or additives (metabisulfite in every epinephrine-containing cartridge; historically methylparaben) — not IgE allergy to the amide itself. Work it up separately; do not fold it into an "all painkillers" avoidance.
- **Penicillin / antibiotic allergy** — separate mechanism and separate delabeling pathway ([[drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]]).
- **NSAID effects on osseointegration and on bleeding** — different axes (bone healing, platelet COX-1), covered in [[overviews/nsaid-osseointegration-impairment-overview]] and [[overviews/nsaid-aspirin-antiplatelet-interaction-overview]]. Cross-referenced above only where they constrain the analgesic choice.
- **Paediatric-specific challenge protocols** — most European paediatric allergists avoid ASA challenges (Reye syndrome concern) and classify from history (Romano 2025).
