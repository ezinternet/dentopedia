## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 thesis**: Sa 1–2 µm 최적 범위는 임의적 경험치가 아닌 분자 생물학적 필연 — 이 범위에서 골아세포 (Osteoblast) 부착·증식 신호와 간엽줄기세포 (MSC, Mesenchymal Stem Cell) 모집 축이 동시에 최적화된다.
> - **표면처리 3분류 (Koshy 2015)**: 첨가형 (TPS, HA 코팅) / 삭감형 (SLA, 산에칭, 양극산화) / 재질변형 (레이저, 이온주입) — 이 분류법이 임상-기전 연결의 공통 언어.
> - **거칠기 4등급 (Ballo 2011, Albrektsson-Wennerberg)**: Smooth < 0.5 µm → 최소 (Minimally) rough 0.5–1.0 µm → 중간 (Moderately) rough 1.0–2.0 µm → Rough > 2.0 µm. 최적은 중간 거칠기 대역.
> - **CXCR4/SDF-1α 축**: 양극산화 (Anodized) 표면은 machined 대비 12시간에 CXCR4 발현 11배 상향 → 케모카인 (Chemokine) SDF-1α가 MSC를 골-임플란트 계면으로 유도 → 더 빠른 골형성. 이것이 나노구조화·산화 표면이 매끄러운 표면보다 우월한 분자적 이유.
> - **염증 프로파일 분기**: Machined 표면 → TNF-α·IL-1β·MCP-1 과발현(섬유소 반응 우세) / Anodized 표면 → CXCR4 우세(MSC 모집 경로 활성화). 거칠기가 다르면 염증-재생 경로 자체가 갈린다.
> - **HA 코팅 딜레마**: 초기 골-임플란트 접촉률 (BIC) 우수(첨가형 최고) but 장기 박리 (Delamination) 위험 — 기전은 명확하지만 장기 임상 신뢰도 낮음.
> - **임상 연계**: 이 분자 기전 프레임은 [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]] (15편, SLA·CA·UV 광기능화 임상 결과 매트릭스)의 WHY를 설명 — 거기서 보인 UV 광기능화·친수성 효과도 생물학적 노화(탄화수소 축적) 역전을 통해 같은 CXCR4/초기 단백흡착 경로로 귀결.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of two foundational narrative reviews (Koshy 2015; Ballo 2011) providing the mechanistic basis for the Sa 1–2 µm clinical optimum: surface modification taxonomy (additive / subtractive / material-modification) and the four-grade roughness continuum (smooth < 0.5 µm → moderately rough 1.0–2.0 µm optimal → rough > 2.0 µm) explain why SLA at Sa ~1.5–2 µm is the gold standard.

The molecular pivot point is the CXCR4/SDF-1α axis: anodically oxidized surfaces upregulate CXCR4 11-fold at 12 h vs machined surfaces, shifting the early inflammatory profile from TNF-α/IL-1β-dominated fibrinous response toward MSC recruitment and accelerated bone formation — the mechanistic WHY behind nanostructured/oxidized surface superiority.

HA coatings provide the highest early BIC via additive topology but carry delamination risk that undercuts long-term outcomes; nanotopography (< 1 µm) governs protein adsorption and fibronectin conformation independently of micro-roughness, establishing hierarchical multi-scale optimization as the theoretical ideal.

## Summary

The clinical observation that Sa 1–2 µm is optimal for osseointegration has been documented across dozens of bench, animal, and human studies. What has been less clearly articulated is *why* this range works at the molecular level. This overview synthesizes Koshy (2015) and Ballo (2011) to answer that question, and provides the mechanistic foundation for the clinical data summarized in [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]].

**The central insight**: surface topography does not merely provide mechanical interlocking. It actively steers the early host immune response — determining whether the peri-implant healing environment is dominated by pro-inflammatory cytokines (fibrinous, scar-prone) or by MSC-recruiting chemokines (bone-forming). The Sa 1–2 µm range is optimal because it activates the MSC recruitment pathway without triggering excessive inflammatory cascades associated with surfaces rougher than 2.0 µm.

## Axis 1 — Surface Treatment Taxonomy

Koshy (2015) organizes all implant surface modification into three mechanistic classes. This taxonomy provides the common language for understanding how different clinical products achieve their surface effects.

### Class A — Additive Methods

Material is added to the titanium surface to increase total surface area and create macrotopography.

| Method | Roughness | Mechanism | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Titanium Plasma Spray (TPS) | Ra ~7 µm | Molten Ti particles fused to surface; macropores 10–20 µm | Excessive roughness → peri-implantitis risk |
| Hydroxyapatite (HA) coating | Variable | Ca/P-enriched surface; early BIC enhancement via chemical bioactivity | Delamination at bone–coating interface (long-term) |
| Calcium phosphate (CaP) | Variable | Soluble; releases Ca²⁺/PO₄³⁻ locally | Variable resorption rate; thin coatings dissolve before osseointegration |
| Magnetron sputter coating | < 1 µm | Nanothin HA; avoids delamination of thick coatings | Limited clinical data |

HA coatings deserve particular attention: they provide the highest early BIC among additive methods because the calcium phosphate chemistry directly promotes osteoblast attachment and mineralization. The fundamental problem is mechanical — the HA–titanium interface is bonded by sintering or plasma-spray under high temperature, and differential thermal expansion/contraction cycles in vivo lead to micro-crack propagation and eventual delamination. A delaminated HA coating fragment in the peri-implant space is a foreign body that triggers osteoclastic resorption, which explains why long-term HA survival data consistently underperform HA's excellent early BIC metrics.

### Class B — Subtractive Methods

Material is removed to create microtopography. These are the most clinically dominant class.

| Method | Roughness | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Grit blasting (Al₂O₃, TiO₂) | Sa 0.5–2.0 µm | Mechanical abrasion; peak at Sa ~1.5 µm for removal torque |
| Acid etching (H₂SO₄/HCl) | < 1 µm | Creates micropits 0.5–3 µm; removes surface oxide contaminants |
| SLA (combined) | Sa ~1.5–2 µm | Sequential grit-blast then acid-etch; micro + submicro dual scale |
| Anodization | 0.5–10 µm pores | Electrochemical TiO₂ thickening; incorporates Ca/P; fundamentally changes surface chemistry |

SLA is the dominant clinical surface because the sequential process creates a dual-scale topography: large pits (from blasting, ~10–20 µm) nested with smaller micropits (from acid etching, 1–3 µm). This hierarchical structure maximizes cell contact area across both the cell body (10–20 µm scale) and cell foot processes/filopodia (1–3 µm scale).

Anodization warrants special attention as it is the only subtractive method that also fundamentally alters surface chemistry: it grows a thick crystalline TiO₂ layer (up to 1000 nm, vs native 2–10 nm amorphous TiO₂) with incorporated Ca and P ions. This chemistry change — not just topography — is the substrate for the CXCR4/SDF-1α molecular findings described in Axis 2.

### Class C — Material Modification

Neither material is added nor removed; the surface is altered by energy input or ion exchange.

| Method | Mechanism | Status (2015) |
|---|---|---|
| Electron beam | Surface melting/recrystallization; creates defined microstructure | Research stage |
| Laser treatment | Precise micro/nano ablation with programmable pattern; can create hierarchical topography | Emerging clinical; no RCT at time of writing |
| Ion implantation | Nitrogen, calcium, or fluoride ions embedded into surface; alters chemical composition to depth of ~100 nm | Preclinical / research stage |

### Roughness Scale Framework (Wennerberg & Albrektsson)

All three classes operate across three roughness scales that govern different biological processes:

| Scale | Range | Biological function |
|---|---|---|
| Macrotopography | > 10 µm | Primary mechanical interlocking; thread design (V-thread, buttress, reverse buttress) |
| Microtopography | 1–10 µm | Secondary osseointegration; cell adhesion, BIC enhancement — the clinically optimized zone |
| Nanotopography | < 1 µm | Governs protein adsorption, fibronectin conformation, early osteoblast and platelet signaling |

The optimal surface (Sa 1–2 µm) is the middle tier — microtopography. But nanotopography is not optional: without nanosurface features, protein adsorption (the molecular first step in osseointegration) is suboptimal regardless of microscale roughness. This is why modern clinical surfaces (SLActive, OsseoSpeed, Nanotite) layer nanofeatures onto an SLA-like microstructure.

## Axis 2 — Molecular Mechanisms: Why Sa 1–2 µm Is a Biological Optimum

### The Osseointegration Cascade at the Molecular Level

Osseointegration unfolds as a cascade of overlapping phases, each dependent on signals generated at the implant surface:

1. **Protein adsorption** (seconds to minutes): Fibronectin, vitronectin, albumin, and fibrinogen adsorb onto the TiO₂ surface. The conformation of adsorbed fibronectin — extended vs denatured — determines whether osteoblasts will recognize the RGD binding domains. Nanotopography (< 1 µm features) keeps fibronectin in the extended conformation; flat surfaces denature it.

2. **Platelet activation** (minutes to hours): Platelets adhere, release TGF-β1, PDGF, and VEGF, initiating the provisional matrix. Surface roughness at Sa 1–2 µm maximizes platelet spread area without inducing aggregation-exhaustion seen on ultra-rough surfaces.

3. **Acute inflammatory response** (hours to 3 days): Neutrophils and macrophages arrive. Surface chemistry determines whether the macrophage polarizes toward M1 (pro-inflammatory, TNF-α/IL-1β-high, scar-promoting) or M2 (anti-inflammatory, IL-10/TGF-β-high, bone-forming permissive) phenotype.

4. **MSC recruitment** (12 h to 5 days): Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) from the bone marrow and periosteum are recruited to the healing site. This step is the critical branch point where modern oxidized surfaces dramatically outperform machined surfaces.

5. **Woven bone formation** (1–4 weeks): MSCs differentiate into osteoblasts; woven bone forms in the clot scaffold adjacent to the implant.

6. **Lamellar bone maturation** (4 weeks to 3+ months): Woven bone remodels to lamellar bone via osteoclast–osteoblast coupling.

### CXCR4/SDF-1α Axis — The Key Molecular Finding

Ballo et al. (2011), drawing on the molecular interface biology work of Omar et al. (2010, 2011), provide the clearest mechanistic data distinguishing anodized from machined surfaces.

Using rat tibia implantation with qPCR and immunohistochemistry at multiple time points (3 h, 12 h, 1 day, 3 days, 6 days):

**Machined surface molecular profile:**
- TNF-α elevated at 3 h and 1 day
- IL-1β elevated across 1–6 days
- MCP-1 (monocyte chemotactic protein) elevated
- Histology: predominantly fibrinous material at the interface; pro-inflammatory M1 macrophage phenotype
- Interpretation: prolonged classical inflammatory response → slower transition to bone formation

**Anodically oxidized (TiUnite-type) surface molecular profile:**
- TNF-α and IL-1β: lower relative to machined
- **CXCR4: 11-fold higher at 12 h post-implantation vs machined**
- Interpretation: CXCR4 is the receptor for stromal cell-derived factor-1α (SDF-1α). SDF-1α is constitutively expressed in bone marrow and up-regulated by hypoxia/injury at the healing site. High CXCR4 expression means the peri-implant cells are primed to respond to the SDF-1α gradient — MSCs expressing CXCR4 migrate toward the SDF-1α source at the implant surface, arrive earlier, and differentiate into osteoblasts faster.

**Clinical implication**: This is the molecular explanation for why oxidized/nanostructured surfaces consistently show earlier bone formation, higher BIC at early time points (4–8 weeks), and faster secondary stability development compared to machined surfaces — even when the two surfaces have similar Sa values. The topography effect and the chemistry effect are inseparable with current methods (as Ballo 2011 explicitly acknowledges), but the CXCR4 data shows that the TiO₂ chemistry change from anodization is at least partly responsible, beyond roughness alone.

### Roughness Optimum: Why Sa 1–2 µm, Not Rougher?

The four-grade Albrektsson-Wennerberg roughness classification defines the optimum as moderately rough (Sa 1.0–2.0 µm). The biological logic for both boundaries:

**Lower boundary (Sa < 1.0 µm → smooth/minimally rough):**
- Cell body spans ~10–20 µm; Sa < 1 µm presents a surface that is effectively flat at the cellular scale
- Osteoblast focal adhesion formation is impaired: integrin clustering requires micro-scale topographical cues
- Fibronectin adsorbs but in less favorable conformation on smooth surfaces
- BIC and removal torque consistently lower for machined (Sa 0.3–1.0 µm) vs SLA surfaces
- *Exception*: the transmucosal collar benefits from smooth surfaces because low roughness minimizes plaque accumulation in the supracrestal soft tissue zone

**Upper boundary (Sa > 2.0 µm → rough/very rough):**
- TPS (Ra ~7 µm) shows high early BIC but also higher peri-implantitis rates in long-term studies
- Mechanism: deep inter-thread spaces and peaks > 2 µm create niches inaccessible to host immune cells but accessible to bacterial biofilm
- Additionally, extremely rough surfaces trap inflammatory cells in recesses, sustaining local inflammatory cytokines beyond the healing phase
- The net result: initial osseointegration may be equivalent or better, but long-term marginal bone loss risk is elevated

**Sa 1–2 µm as the biological sweet spot:**
- Feature size matches osteoblast filopodium diameter (0.1–0.5 µm) and cell body scale (10–20 µm) simultaneously when hierarchical nano + micro texture is present
- Sufficient surface area enhancement to maximize protein adsorption and cell contact
- Pore/pit dimensions permeable to immune cell surveillance (neutrophil diameter ~10–12 µm can traverse 1–2 µm features as surface irregularities, not as entrapment sites)
- CXCR4/SDF-1α signaling optimized: the oxidized TiO₂ chemistry native to acid-etched/anodized surfaces in this range provides the chemokine receptor upregulation documented by Ballo 2011

### Confounding: Roughness and Chemistry Are Not Separable

A critical methodological caveat (Ballo 2011): changing surface roughness simultaneously changes surface chemistry. Sandblasting introduces Al₂O₃ residues; acid etching alters the TiO₂ stoichiometry and removes surface hydrocarbons; anodization grows crystalline TiO₂. It is therefore not possible with current experimental methods to attribute a bone-response difference entirely to topography vs chemistry. The CXCR4 finding is likely both a chemistry effect (crystalline vs amorphous TiO₂) and a topography effect (micro-pore scale of anodized surface).

This confounding is why the later generation of experiments (photofunctionalization studies, covered in [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]]) became important: UV treatment modifies chemistry (removes hydrocarbons, restores electropositivity) with minimal topography change, allowing partial deconvolution of the two variables.

### What the Molecular Layer Does Not Explain

The molecular mechanism page has limits that the clinical overview must supply:

1. **Quantitative thresholds for loading decisions**: CXCR4 upregulation timing (12 h) is a molecular event, not a clinical ISQ value. The ISQ ≥ 65 loading threshold comes from clinical cohort studies, not from molecular biology.
2. **Peri-implantitis prevention**: The upper Sa boundary (> 2.0 µm → peri-implantitis risk) is supported by clinical epidemiology (Czumbel 2019) more than by molecular biology.
3. **Surface aging effects**: Hydrocarbon contamination kinetics are captured in [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]] but not in Ballo 2011 (2011 predates the Att/Ogawa photofunctionalization evidence).
4. **Hydrophilic surface variants**: SLActive and CA surfaces were not fully characterized in either source paper; their enhanced early stability is detailed in [[overviews/implant-surface-comparison]].
