## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **논지**: 치과 의인성 하치조신경(Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN)·설신경(Lingual Nerve, LN) 손상은 임플란트·사랑니 발치·하악 전달마취·악교정 수술 후 발생하며, 관리의 핵심은 **시간 기반(time-based) 의사결정**이다 — 동일한 손상이라도 개입 시점에 따라 완전 회복 vs 영구 장애가 갈린다.
> - **유병률(Prevalence)**: 임플란트 후 신경병증 1주 12% → 3개월 이상 5% (측방이동 없는 군); 측방이동군 1주 90% → 42% — 약 8배 차이(Reus 2024, SR). 사랑니 발치 후 IAN 손상률 0.4–5.5% (일시적 8–12%); 임플란트 후 일시적 >30% (Kammerer 2024).
> - **분류**: Seddon/Sunderland 분류 — 신경실조(Neuropraxia, 일시적·완전 회복), 축삭 손상(Axonotmesis, 서서히 회복), 신경절단(Neurotmesis, 외과적 수복 필요). 임플란트 관련 원인: 기계적(직접 접촉·압박)·열적·화학적, 수술 후 혈종·감염·부종 (Juodzbalys 2011).
> - **예방의 핵심**: 하악관 상단으로부터 방사선학적 ≥2 mm 안전 거리; CBCT로 정확한 해부학 파악 — 임플란트 식립 후 즉시 새 감각이상 발생 시 즉시 파노라마/CT로 확인 (Juodzbalys 2011, Kammerer 2024).
> - **황금 창(36-hour window)**: 임플란트 식립 후 IAN 접촉/침범 의심 시 **36시간 이내 제거**가 임상 회복의 분기점 — 18 h·36 h 제거: 거의 완전 회복 vs 2일·4일 제거: 영구 완전 마취(Khawaja 2009, 4례).
> - **36시간 창의 SR 근거(Shavit 2014, 체계적문헌고찰 6편·n=157)**: 36시간 이내 조기 진단 47%, 36–48시간 8%, 6개월 초과 지연 진단 43% — 지연 진단군은 보고된 전례에서 회복 없음. 손상 후 **48시간 이내 치료 시 완전 회복이 일관되게 관찰**(단 신경절단(Neurotmesis)은 시기와 무관하게 회복 실패 가능). 진단의 64%가 주관적 신경감각검사였고, 객관적 검사(교근억제반사(Masseter Inhibitory Reflex, MIR) — 특이도 99%·민감도 51%)는 포함된 6편 중 **0편**에서 사용 → 43%의 지연은 치료 실패가 아니라 **발견 실패**다.
> - **36시간 규칙의 경계조건(Baskaran 2024, 증례보고 n=1)**: 임플란트 첨단이 하악관에 **접촉만** 한(신경내 침습 없음) 65세 남성에서 **2년 경과 후** 제거했음에도 6주 내 완전 회복(3주 50% → 4주 70% → 6주 100%, 작열감 VAS 6→0). 임플란트주위염(Peri-implantitis, 탐침깊이 5 mm)에 의한 환상 골소실이 골유착된 임플란트의 제거를 용이하게 했다. 즉 결정 변수는 경과 시간만이 아니라 **손상 유형(접촉·압박 vs 신경내 침습·절단)**일 수 있다 — 다만 n=1이며, 36시간 원칙을 미루는 근거로 삼아서는 안 된다(장기 경과 증례를 "치료 불가"로 단정하지 않는 근거일 뿐).
> - **제3의 원인 경로 — 마취 차단술 관련 지각이상(Ahmad 2018, 서술 리뷰)**: 임플란트·발치 외에 하치조신경 전달마취(Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block) 자체가 독립적 원인 경로다. 설신경(Lingual Nerve, LN) 신경다발 수 7–39개(평균 20) vs IAN 3–14개(평균 7.2) — 신경다발 수 변이가 해부학적 위험인자로 제시됨; 하악 전달마취 후 신경병증의 **89%가 설신경**(모의 주사 관통률 4.5%, 설측 치조정 직접 접촉 62%, 주사 중 "전기충격" 감각 31/134 = 23.1%). 아티카인(Articaine) 4%가 1999–2008년 지각이상 보고의 59%(연구 간 33–71%)를 차지했으나 **동물·세포 연구에서는 신경독성 우위가 확인되지 않음 — 미해결 논쟁(gray zone)이지 정설이 아니다.** 대부분 수일–수개월 내 회복, 6개월 초과 지속은 드묾.
> - **지연발현형(delayed-onset) 지각이상(Doh 2018, 증례보고 n=1)**: 하악 제3대구치 발치 **2주 후**에 발현한 IAN 지각이상 — 술중·즉시 발현이 아니다(솔방향검사 7/10, 침자극 5/10 vs 대조 10/10, 2점식별 15 mm vs 12 mm; CBCT상 하악관 상방 피질골 불연속, 혈병 부분 소실). 제안 기전은 직접 기계적·열적 손상이 아니라 **혈병 붕괴에 따른 헤모글로빈 유리 + 국소 염증**(건성치조와(Alveolar Osteitis) 유사 과정). 소염제·스테로이드 투여 후 신경실조(Neuropraxia) 경과대로 발현 6주에 완전 회복. 임상 함의: **퇴원 시 정상 신경감각검사가 이후 손상 발현을 배제하지 않는다**; 영상에서 지속적 기계적 원인이 없다면 Axis 3의 긴급 감압 논리가 아니라 수주 단위 신경실조 예후로 다룬다.
> - **약물 관리(Pharmacology)**: ① 덱사메타손(Dexamethasone): 수술 중·수술 직후 국소/전신 투여가 뉴로파직 통증·염증 억제 — **조기 투여만 유효**, 확립된 신경병증에는 효과 없음(Han 2010, 동물). ② 비타민 B12/메틸코발라민(Methylcobalamin): COX 억제·BDNF 증가·수초재생 → 신경병증 통증 감소 및 회복 촉진, keykha SR에서 paresthesia RCT 효과 확인(Buesing 2019, review; Zhang 2020, animal). ③ 은행잎(Ginkgo biloba, EGb): 용량 의존적 말초신경재생 촉진(쥐 모델; 200 > 100 > 50 mg/kg/d), 감각 재생 거리·전도 속도·근육량 모두 개선(Lin 2007, animal) — 인간 임상 검증 필요.
> - **광생물조절(PBM, Photobiomodulation)**: 808 nm, 9 J/site, 격일 15회 + 메코발라민 병용 → 30일째 이점식별(TPD) −2.6–3.2 mm 개선 vs 위약 −0.8 mm; SWME 1등급 이상 개선 80–85% vs 25%(Ma 2026, RCT, n=60). SR 분석(Keykha 2024, 7 RCT): PBM·비타민 B12·코르티코스테로이드 모두 회복 촉진 — 표준은 없으나 PBM이 가장 일관적.
> - **전임상 재생 프런티어(Axis 4D, 아직 임상 아님)**: ① 치수줄기세포(Dental Pulp Stem Cell, DPSC) 세포치료 — DPSC 탑재 도관이 원숭이 12개월 모델에서 수초·CMAP 회복을 자가이식에 근접하게 개선; 기전은 터널링 나노튜브(TNT)를 통한 **기능성 미토콘드리아 전달**로 슈반세포 파이롭토시스 구제(mtDNA 결핍 DPSC는 전달해도 구제 실패 → 미토콘드리아가 기능적이어야 함), TNFα 전처리로 효과 증강. 세포원은 **발치한 사랑니**(자가·저면역원성)(Zheng 2025). ② 감각자극 재활 — 삼차신경 감각 입력이 안면 운동회복에 필수이며, 단순 도수 감각자극이 병적 다중신경지배를 절반(53%→22%)으로 줄임(Rink-Notzon 2022, 랫드·안면신경). 둘 다 **동물 근거뿐** — 연구 방향이지 프로토콜 아님.
> - **시간 기반 프로토콜(Misch 2010)**: ① 수술 중: 국소 덱사메타손 4 mg/mL ± 임플란트 제거(신경 절단 확인 시 즉시 의뢰). ② 1주 이내: 경구 덱사메타손 테이퍼 + 이부프로펜 600–800 mg TID×3주 + 조기 PBM·B12 개시. ③ 12주 이내 개선 없음: 전문의 의뢰 ± 수술적 감압/재건.
> - **한계**: 치료 관련 RCT가 단 1편(Reus 2024 SR) — 모든 권고는 소규모 RCT·증례·동물 연구 수준; 표준 프로토콜 미확립; 동물→인간 용량 외삽 미검증.
> - **임상 결론**: 예방(≥2 mm 안전거리·CBCT) → 조기 발견(술 중·술 후 감각 검사) → 36시간 내 임플란트 제거(직접 손상 의심 시) → 덱사메타손 조기 투여 → PBM + 메틸코발라민 × 4–8주 → 12주 무반응 시 신경외과 의뢰. 이 **시간 축 원칙**이 현존하는 가장 강력한 임상 레버다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 18 papers (3 SR, 1 RCT, 5 narrative reviews, 3 case reports/series, 1 retrospective case-control, 5 animal/preclinical studies) on iatrogenic IAN/LN injury from dental implants, third-molar extraction, mandibular block injection, and orthognathic surgery — covering epidemiology, Seddon/Sunderland classification, the three etiologic pathways (implant, extraction, anesthetic block), time-critical management (the 36-hour window and its boundary conditions), pharmacological/physical treatments (dexamethasone, vitamin B12/methylcobalamin, PBM, ginkgo biloba), and the preclinical regeneration frontier (DPSC mitochondrial-transfer cell therapy, sensory-stimulation-driven recovery).

Implant-related neuropathy occurs in 12% at 1 week resolving to 5% at 3 months (SR, n=503–662); implant removal within 36 hours is the single most critical intervention for direct IAN contact (SR of 6 studies, n=157: 47% diagnosed <36 h, 43% diagnosed >6 months with no recovery, full recovery consistent when treated <48 h); PBM and vitamin B12 each independently accelerate sensory recovery in the established-injury phase (SR: 7 RCTs; RCT: n=60, TPD −2.6 mm vs −0.8 mm sham); dexamethasone is effective only when administered early.

No universal treatment guideline exists; all therapies rest on small RCTs with high risk of bias; the strongest clinical levers are prevention (≥2 mm radiographic safety margin, CBCT guidance) and the time-based protocol: intraoperative topical dexamethasone → early implant removal within 36 h if suspected contact → oral corticosteroids + PBM + methylcobalamin × 4–12 weeks → specialist referral at 12 weeks if no improvement.

## 세줄요약

18편 종합(SR 3, RCT 1, 서술 리뷰 5, 증례보고/증례군 3, 후향적 환자-대조 1, 동물/전임상 5): 임플란트·사랑니·하악 전달마취·악교정 수술 후 하치조신경(IAN)/설신경(LN) 손상의 역학·Seddon/Sunderland 분류·3대 원인 경로(임플란트·발치·마취 차단술)·시간 기반 관리(36시간 창과 그 경계조건)·약물(덱사메타손·비타민 B12·메틸코발라민·은행잎)·광생물조절(PBM)·전임상 재생 프런티어(치수줄기세포 미토콘드리아 전달 세포치료·감각자극 유도 회복) 종합.

표준 치료 지침 없음; 예방(≥2 mm 안전거리·CBCT)과 시간 기반 프로토콜(수술 중 국소 덱사 → 36시간 내 임플란트 제거 → 경구 스테로이드·PBM·메틸코발라민×4–12주 → 12주 무반응 시 신경외과 의뢰)이 현재 근거상 최강 임상 레버.

## Axis 1 — Epidemiology & Classification

### 1A. Prevalence

**Reus 2024** (SR, 38 studies) provides the most current quantitative picture for implant-related IAN neuropathy:

| Group | At 1 week | At ≥3 months |
|---|---|---|
| Without nerve lateralization | 12% | 5% |
| With nerve lateralization | 90% | 42% |

**Kammerer 2024** (scoping review) adds the third-molar perspective:
- IAN injury: 0.4–5.5% of lower third molar removals (transient up to 8–12%)
- Implant-related: >30% transient; permanent 0.01–0.09%
- Lingual nerve: 0–2% (2-week), most resolve within 3 months

**Clinical implication**: most transient neuropathy resolves spontaneously. The goal of management is to prevent transient from becoming permanent.

### 1B. Seddon/Sunderland Classification

**Juodzbalys 2011** (narrative review, 136 sources) established the foundational classification framework:

| Seddon Grade | Sunderland | Description | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neuropraxia** | I | Focal demyelination; axon intact | Full recovery, weeks–months |
| **Axonotmesis** | II–III | Axon disrupted; endoneurium intact | Gradual recovery, months |
| **Neurotmesis** | IV–V | Complete severance or fibrosis | Surgical repair required |

**Mechanism categories** (Juodzbalys 2011):
- *Intraoperative*: mechanical (direct contact, compression, stretching), thermal (drilling heat, laser), chemical (irrigant, local anesthetic intoxication)
- *Postoperative*: thermal necrosis (polymerizing methylmethacrylate), infection, hematoma pressure

#### Delayed-onset paresthesia — a distinct presentation pattern

The rest of this synthesis frames paresthesia as intraoperative or immediately postoperative. **Doh 2018** (case report, n=1) documents the pattern that framing misses: a 53-year-old woman whose IAN paresthesia began **2 weeks after** surgical removal of a lower third molar, with no intraoperative complication and the IAN never visualized during surgery.

| Finding at onset (day 14) | Affected side | Control side |
|---|---|---|
| Brush direction test | 7/10 | 10/10 |
| Pin prick test | 5/10 | 10/10 |
| Two-point discrimination | 15 mm | 12 mm |

- Socket showed **partial loss of the blood clot** without swelling, redness, or lymphadenopathy; postoperative CBCT showed discontinuity of the upper cortical layer of the mandibular canal.
- **Proposed mechanism**: hemoglobin release from clot breakdown plus local inflammation — an alveolar-osteitis-like process — rather than direct mechanical or thermal injury. The authors explicitly distinguish this from the previously proposed mechanisms of organized-hematoma compression or retained bone fragments.
- Treated with anti-inflammatory drugs and steroids; pain resolved at 1 week, paresthesia completely at 6 weeks post-onset. Classified as Seddon **neuropraxia**, consistent with the expected 6–8-week recovery window.

> **Clinical implication**: a normal neurosensory test at discharge (Axis 2, prevention step 5) does not exclude injury appearing days to weeks later. Delayed-onset numbness after extraction is a recognized pattern with an inflammatory rather than mechanical cause, and — absent a persistent mechanical cause on imaging — carries a neuropraxia (weeks-timeline) prognosis rather than triggering the urgent-decompression logic of Axis 3. n=1; the mechanism is a hypothesis, not demonstrated.

### 1C. Anesthetic-Block-Related Paresthesia (the third etiologic pathway)

Beyond implant placement and third-molar surgery, the **inferior alveolar nerve block injection itself** is a distinct causal pathway. **Ahmad 2018** (narrative review synthesizing cadaveric anatomy studies, retrospective paresthesia registries, and injury-classification literature) supplies the anatomical and mechanistic layer underneath the incidence data in 1A.

**Anatomical risk — fascicle-count variability** (Pogrel et al. 2003, at the lingula, as reported in Ahmad 2018):

| Nerve | Fascicle count | Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Lingual nerve | 7–39 | 20 |
| Inferior alveolar nerve | 3–14 | 7.2 |

Ahmad lists fascicle-count variability as a *potential* anatomical risk factor alongside nerve position; the review does not establish a threshold or direction of effect. Further anatomical variability compounds the picture: accessory foramina, variant nerve branches, multiple mandibular canals, and lingual-nerve position (direct contact with the lingual crest in 62%; Kiesselbach & Chamberlain 1984).

**Lingual-nerve dominance after mandibular block**:
- The lingual nerve accounts for **89%** of neuropathies following mandibular blocks (vs. the IAN)
- Simulated injections penetrated the lingual nerve in **4.5%** of attempts (Morris et al. 2010)
- "Electric shock" sensation during injection was reported in **31/134 (23.1%)**

**The Articaine controversy — unresolved, treat as a gray zone**:
- Retrospective voluntary-reporting data: Articaine 4% accounted for **59%** of paresthesia reports 1999–2008 (Gaffen & Haas 2009), with a **33–71%** range across studies; prilocaine 3–4% was the second most commonly implicated agent.
- **But** animal and cell-culture studies do **not** confirm higher neurotoxicity for articaine.
- Registry data carry reporting bias and denominator uncertainty (agent market share is not controlled for).

> **Do not repeat "articaine causes paresthesia" as settled fact.** The honest reading is a signal in human retrospective registries that laboratory work has not corroborated. Ahmad's own conclusion is explicitly multifactorial: anatomical variation + anesthetic neurotoxicity + mechanical needle trauma (including barbed needles). Supporting the chemical contribution: roughly half of IAN damage cases show no sign of needle trauma at all.

**Prognosis**: most block-related paresthesia resolves within days to months; persistence beyond 6 months is rare. Severity spectrum follows the same Seddon ladder as 1B — neurapraxia (hours–days) → axonotmesis (months) → neurotmesis (complete sensory loss).

## Axis 2 — Prevention

**Juodzbalys 2011** establishes the core prevention principles for implant-related IAN injury:

1. **Radiographic safety margin**: ≥2 mm from the superior wall of the mandibular canal
2. **CBCT guidance** for posterior mandible when canal is close to the crest or curved
3. **Drill-stop devices** calibrated to planned depth minus 2 mm
4. **Intraoperative monitoring**: if unusual pain, pressure, or bleeding during drilling → stop immediately
5. **Neurosensory testing**: test the chin/lip before patient discharge; any new hypoesthesia requires OPG/CT same day

**Kammerer 2024** adds: the IAN canal can loop anteriorly beyond the mental foramen — CBCT is the only way to detect this variant and avoid "safe" anterior placements that actually violate the canal.

## Axis 3 — The 36-Hour Window (Implant-Related Injury)

**Khawaja 2009** (4 cases, King's College Hospital) provides the clearest clinical evidence for time-critical implant removal:

| Case | Removal timing | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 18 hours | Near-complete recovery |
| 2 | 36 hours | Near-complete recovery |
| 3 | 2 days | Permanent complete anesthesia |
| 4 | 4 days | Permanent complete anesthesia |

**Shavit 2014** (SR, 6 studies, n=157, PROSPERO-registered) is the better-powered confirmation of the same window, upgrading the 36-hour rule from a 4-case series to systematic-review level — the strongest evidence the wiki holds on this axis:

| Diagnosis timing | Share of cases |
|---|---|
| Early (<36 h) | 47% |
| Critical window (36–48 h) | 8% |
| Very late (>6 months) | 43% |

- **Full recovery was consistently achieved when treatment occurred before 48 h post-injury** — with the stated exception of severe (neurotmesis) injury, which may fail to recover regardless of timing.
- Late diagnosis (>6 months) showed **no recovery in all reported cases**.
- Component series: Kubilius 2004 (n=68) — 38 mild and 20 moderate injuries treated at 48 h recovered fully within 1–4 weeks and 1–1.5 months respectively, while 3 severe cases treated at >1 week did not recover; Renton 2012 — full recovery at <24 h (2 patients) and <48 h (1 patient), no recovery from 72 h onward.
- **The diagnostic gap is the actionable finding**: subjective neurosensory testing was the dominant method (64% of cases), and objective tests — masseter inhibitory reflex (MIR: specificity 99%, sensitivity 51%), somatosensory evoked potentials — were used in **none** of the 6 studies. The 43% very-late group is therefore largely a *detection* failure rather than a treatment failure. Shavit also proposes phentolamine mesylate (anesthesia reversal) as a way to enable immediate postoperative sensory assessment.

**Misch 2010** (expert protocol, survey: 73% of dentists encountered IAN impairment) operationalizes this into a 4-period management flow:

| Period | Action |
|---|---|
| **Intraoperative** | Local dexamethasone 4 mg/mL at site; if nerve transection observed → immediate microsurgery referral |
| **Within 36 h** | OPG/CT to confirm canal violation; if implant in/through canal → remove within 36 h + oral dexamethasone taper |
| **1–12 weeks** | Oral dexamethasone + ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID × 3 weeks; add PBM + methylcobalamin |
| **≥12 weeks no improvement** | Microsurgery referral (decompression, neurolysis, or autograft) |

**Immediate referral criteria (Misch 2010)**:
- Intraoperative nerve transection (confirmed or suspected)
- Dysesthesia (burning/electrical pain) at 1 week — suggests Grade III+ damage
- Complete anesthesia at 1 week with no improvement

### Boundary condition — injury type may matter as much as elapsed time

**Baskaran 2024** (case report, n=1) is the one held paper that complicates a purely time-based reading of this axis. It should be read as a **subgroup nuance, not a refutation**:

- 65-year-old male, implant at tooth 46, presenting after **two years** of right lower-lip and mental-region paresthesia with occasional drooling, intermittent dull pain, and burning (VAS 6/10); complete loss of tactile and thermal sensation on testing.
- CBCT and orthopantomogram: the implant apex **resting on** the mandibular canal — surface contact, without evidence of intraneural invasion.
- Peri-implantitis with circumferential bone loss (probing depth 5 mm, bleeding on probing) made retrieval of the well-osseointegrated implant uncomplicated despite the 2-year interval.
- Recovery after retrieval: 50% at week 3 → 70% at week 4 → **100% at week 6**; intermittent pain gone within 2 days, burning VAS 6 → 0 at ~2 weeks, drooling resolved by week 6. Monitored 3 months to confirm.

> **How to hold this against the 36-hour rule.** The urgency of this axis stands as the default: Shavit's >6-month group recovered in *no* reported case, and Khawaja's 48 h and 96 h removals failed. Baskaran's case argues that the operative predictor may be **injury type — canal contact/compression versus intraneural invasion or transection — at least as much as raw time elapsed**: where the implant merely abuts the canal and the nerve was never invaded, decompression by retrieval can still restore full function years later. Two practical corollaries: (1) this is never a reason to wait — the 36-hour window remains the target, and no case series shows delay improving outcomes; (2) but do **not** write off a long-standing implant-contact paresthesia as untreatable. CBCT showing contact-without-invasion is grounds to offer retrieval even at two years. Evidence: n=1, no comparator.

## Axis 4 — Pharmacological Treatment

### 4A. Corticosteroids (Dexamethasone)

**Han 2010** (animal, rat IAN injury model) — critical timing finding:
- **Early dexamethasone** (25–50 mg/kg IP) produced **prolonged anti-allodynic effects**
- **Established neuropathic pain** showed **no response** to dexamethasone
- Bilateral (mirror-image) hypersensitivity observed → central sensitization confirmed

> **Clinical translation**: corticosteroids must be given in the acute/subacute phase (ideally within 24–72 h of injury). Once central sensitization is established, anti-inflammatory mechanisms are insufficient alone.

**Misch 2010** clinical protocol: dexamethasone 4 mg/mL locally at implant site (intraoperative) → oral dexamethasone taper starting same day if implant retained.

**Keykha 2024** (SR, 7 RCTs): prednisolone 3–6 weeks post-op accelerated sensory recovery in paresthesia patients — confirming clinical validity of the corticosteroid window.

### 4B. Vitamin B12 / Methylcobalamin

**Buesing 2019** (narrative review, PubMed) — mechanism and clinical evidence:

*Animal mechanisms*:
- Axon growth promotion and Schwann cell differentiation
- BDNF↑ (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)
- COX enzyme inhibition (reduces orofacial pain in formalin model Phase 2)
- TRPV1 modulation (reduces central sensitization)
- Synergy with NSAIDs and opioids

*Clinical RCTs*:
- Low back pain: IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 weeks → pain −87%
- Hydroxycobalamin 2 mg TID: neuropathic pain VAS −60%
- Herpetic neuralgia: ≥50% reduction in 60% of subjects

**Zhang 2020** (animal, 10 mm rat sciatic nerve gap):
- PLCL conduit + 15% methylcobalamin: best outcome — largest axon diameter, thickest myelin, Schwann cell proliferation, 21-day sustained release
- Supports local delivery concept (though not yet clinically validated for dental nerves)

**Keykha 2024** (SR): vitamin B12 showed efficacy comparable to PBM at 4 weeks for paresthesia recovery (direct comparison in ≥1 included RCT).

> **Clinical practice**: oral/IM methylcobalamin 0.5–1.5 mg/day × 4–8 weeks is a safe, low-cost adjunct; the Ma 2026 RCT used mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days as a background therapy for all arms.

### 4C. Ginkgo Biloba Extract (EGb)

**Lin 2007** (animal, rat sciatic nerve, 120 rats, 4 doses):
- EGb 50/100/200 mg/kg/day all improved vs saline: sensory regeneration distance, MNCV, CMAP, axon regeneration index, muscle mass
- Dose-response confirmed (SFI = functional index: non-significant)
- Proposed mechanism: antioxidant (flavonoids), vasodilatation (terpenoids), free-radical scavenging → better nerve bed perfusion

> **Clinical note**: dose extrapolation to humans is not validated. EGb 120–240 mg/day is the standard human dose for neuroprotection, but no dental-specific RCT exists. Consider as adjunct only when standard therapies insufficient.

### 4D. Experimental regeneration — cell therapy & sensory-stimulation rationale (전임상)

Two preclinical papers extend the conduit/Schwann-cell theme of Zhang 2020 beyond drug-loaded scaffolds — into cell therapy and into the sensory-input side of functional recovery. Both are **animal-only**; neither is a clinical option today. They belong here as the mechanistic frontier of the regeneration axis, not as treatment recommendations.

**Zheng 2025** (preclinical: 12-month macaque facial-nerve-transection n=12 + rat FNI crush n=16 + in-vitro Schwann-cell pyroptosis): dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) loaded into a nerve conduit improved long-term myelin/axon regeneration and CMAP recovery versus bare conduit in primates, approaching (not equaling) autograft. The **mechanism** is the contribution: DPSCs do not merely differentiate or act paracrine — they physically transfer functional mitochondria into pyroptosis-stressed Schwann cells through tunneling nanotubes (TNTs), rescuing mitochondrial-ROS overload; mtDNA-depleted (ρ⁰) DPSCs still transfer but fail to rescue, proving the *mitochondria themselves* must be functional. A feed-forward loop (pyroptotic-Schwann-cell TNFα → NF-κB in DPSCs → more biogenesis/TNTs) means TNFα-preconditioning DPSCs before transplant improves repair. **Dental relevance is the cell source**: DPSCs come from routinely-extracted third molars — an autologous, low-immunogenicity, chairside-accessible reservoir for future peripheral-nerve (facial, and by extension IAN/LN) regenerative strategies.

**Rink-Notzon 2022** (narrative review of the authors' Wistar-rat facial-nerve-transection program) — an *adjacent* finding that reframes what "rehabilitation" of an injured nerve means. Axotomized facial motoneurons are left "hyperexcitable but unable to discharge" when deprived of adequate trigeminal sensory input; excising the infraorbital (V2) sensory nerve worsened whisking recovery and *abolished* the benefit of manual stimulation, whereas simple manual/vibrissal sensory stimulation of intact trigeminal afferents restored perisomatic synaptic density and halved pathological polyinnervation (53%→22%). The proposed translational hypothesis: **massage-based sensory stimulation of trigeminal-innervated skin/mucosa could substitute for elaborate physiotherapy** after combined nerve injury. Caveats are large — this is facial-nerve *motor* recovery in rats (not IAN/LN sensory recovery), and human application is an explicit hypothesis, not evidence. It is included as the sensory-input rationale behind the wider "stimulate the recovering nerve" theme (of which PBM in Axis 5 is the one modality with human data).

> **Bottom line for 4D**: cell therapy (DPSC) and sensory-stimulation rehab are mechanistically promising and share the wiki's conduit/Schwann-cell lineage, but both rest entirely on animal models — they set research direction, not current protocol.

## Axis 5 — Photobiomodulation (PBM)

**Ma 2026** (RCT, n=60, IAN hypoesthesia 7 days–12 weeks post-third-molar extraction):

| Arm | TPD change | SWME ≥1 grade | VAS numbness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laser acupuncture (LA) | −3.2 mm | 85% | −60% |
| Direct PBM (IAN trajectory) | −2.6 mm | 80% | −48% |
| Sham | −0.8 mm | 25% | −12% |

- All arms received mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days
- 808 nm, 100 mW, 9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions
- Both active arms significantly outperformed sham (p<0.01)
- LA produced greater patient-reported numbness reduction — possibly additive central neuromodulation via acupoint

**Keykha 2024** (SR, 7 RCTs — implant/third molar/orthognathic/injection-related paresthesia):
- PBM, vitamin B12, and prednisolone each independently accelerated recovery
- PBM superior to PBM acupuncture in 1 included study
- At 4 weeks, PBM ≈ vitamin B12 (comparable outcomes)
- All 7 RCTs: small n, high risk of bias — **no meta-analysis possible**; no gold standard

**Kammerer 2024** also lists PBM as the most evidence-backed non-surgical physical modality for established trigeminal neuropathy in the published literature.

## Axis 6 — Surgical Management

**Juodzbalys 2011** (foundational): microsurgical repair indicated for Seddon Grade III (Neurotmesis, Sunderland IV–V) unresolved at 3 months:
- Options: neurolysis, internal neurolysis, end-to-end repair, interposed nerve graft (sural/great auricular)
- Success rate: better outcomes with early surgery (within 3 months of injury)

**Kammerer 2024** (2024 update): both decompression and nerve reconstruction effective when applied promptly; surgical outcomes worsen significantly if delayed beyond 6 months — emphasizing the value of early specialist triage.

## Integrated Clinical Protocol

```
PREVENTION
├── ≥2 mm radiographic safety margin from IAN canal
├── CBCT for high-risk cases (short ridge, anterior loop, curved canal)
└── Test neurosensory before patient leaves chair

INTRAOPERATIVE alert (paresthesia/bleeding/resistance change)
├── Stop drilling immediately
├── OPG / periapical radiograph
└── If violation confirmed → topical dexamethasone 4 mg/mL at site

WITHIN 36 HOURS (implant-related direct contact suspected)
├── Confirm position: OPG, CBCT
├── Violation confirmed → remove/reposition implant
├── Start oral dexamethasone taper + ibuprofen 600-800 mg TID × 3 wks
└── Start methylcobalamin 0.5 mg TID

WEEKS 1–12 (established paresthesia)
├── PBM: 808 nm, ~9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions
├── Continue methylcobalamin × 4–8 weeks
├── Consider prednisolone taper if not already given
└── Monitor with standardized neurosensory tests (LT, TPD, SWME)

≥12 WEEKS: no improvement
└── Refer: oral/maxillofacial surgery or neurosurgery
    (decompression / neurolysis / autograft — Grade III-V)
```

## Evidence Summary & Limitations

| Domain | Best evidence | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence | Reus 2024 SR (38 studies) | Moderate (SR, no MA) |
| Classification | Juodzbalys 2011 / Kammerer 2024 | Expert review |
| 36-h window | Shavit 2014 SR (6 studies, n=157) + Khawaja 2009 (4 cases) | Low (SR of case series/observational, no MA) |
| Delayed-retrieval exception (contact-only injury) | Baskaran 2024 (case report, n=1) | Very low (single case, no comparator) |
| Delayed-onset paresthesia | Doh 2018 (case report, n=1) | Very low (single case; mechanism hypothesized) |
| Block-injection anatomy (fascicle count, LN vulnerability) | Ahmad 2018 narrative review | Expert review (cadaveric source data) |
| Anesthetic neurotoxicity (Articaine) | Ahmad 2018 (Gaffen & Haas registry data) | Low (retrospective, contested — lab studies do not confirm) |
| Dexamethasone timing | Han 2010 (rat) | Preclinical only |
| Vitamin B12 | Buesing 2019 review + Keykha SR | Low-moderate |
| PBM | Ma 2026 RCT (n=60) + Keykha SR | Low (small RCTs, high RoB) |
| Ginkgo biloba | Lin 2007 (rat) | Preclinical only |
| Surgical repair | Kammerer 2024 review | Expert consensus |

**Key evidence gap**: only 1 RCT addresses management of implant-specific IAN injury (Reus 2024); the rest are generalized paresthesia treatments. Clinical guidelines urgently need prospective, powered RCTs.
