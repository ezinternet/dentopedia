## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **논지**: 치과 의인성 하치조신경(Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN)·설신경(Lingual Nerve, LN) 손상은 임플란트·사랑니 발치·악교정 수술 후 발생하며, 관리의 핵심은 **시간 기반(time-based) 의사결정**이다 — 동일한 손상이라도 개입 시점에 따라 완전 회복 vs 영구 장애가 갈린다.
> - **유병률(Prevalence)**: 임플란트 후 신경병증 1주 12% → 3개월 이상 5% (측방이동 없는 군); 측방이동군 1주 90% → 42% — 약 8배 차이(Reus 2024, SR). 사랑니 발치 후 IAN 손상률 0.4–5.5% (일시적 8–12%); 임플란트 후 일시적 >30% (Kammerer 2024).
> - **분류**: Seddon/Sunderland 분류 — 신경실조(Neuropraxia, 일시적·완전 회복), 축삭 손상(Axonotmesis, 서서히 회복), 신경절단(Neurotmesis, 외과적 수복 필요). 임플란트 관련 원인: 기계적(직접 접촉·압박)·열적·화학적, 수술 후 혈종·감염·부종 (Juodzbalys 2011).
> - **예방의 핵심**: 하악관 상단으로부터 방사선학적 ≥2 mm 안전 거리; CBCT로 정확한 해부학 파악 — 임플란트 식립 후 즉시 새 감각이상 발생 시 즉시 파노라마/CT로 확인 (Juodzbalys 2011, Kammerer 2024).
> - **황금 창(36-hour window)**: 임플란트 식립 후 IAN 접촉/침범 의심 시 **36시간 이내 제거**가 임상 회복의 분기점 — 18 h·36 h 제거: 거의 완전 회복 vs 2일·4일 제거: 영구 완전 마취(Khawaja 2009, 4례).
> - **약물 관리(Pharmacology)**: ① 덱사메타손(Dexamethasone): 수술 중·수술 직후 국소/전신 투여가 뉴로파직 통증·염증 억제 — **조기 투여만 유효**, 확립된 신경병증에는 효과 없음(Han 2010, 동물). ② 비타민 B12/메틸코발라민(Methylcobalamin): COX 억제·BDNF 증가·수초재생 → 신경병증 통증 감소 및 회복 촉진, keykha SR에서 paresthesia RCT 효과 확인(Buesing 2019, review; Zhang 2020, animal). ③ 은행잎(Ginkgo biloba, EGb): 용량 의존적 말초신경재생 촉진(쥐 모델; 200 > 100 > 50 mg/kg/d), 감각 재생 거리·전도 속도·근육량 모두 개선(Lin 2007, animal) — 인간 임상 검증 필요.
> - **광생물조절(PBM, Photobiomodulation)**: 808 nm, 9 J/site, 격일 15회 + 메코발라민 병용 → 30일째 이점식별(TPD) −2.6–3.2 mm 개선 vs 위약 −0.8 mm; SWME 1등급 이상 개선 80–85% vs 25%(Ma 2026, RCT, n=60). SR 분석(Keykha 2024, 7 RCT): PBM·비타민 B12·코르티코스테로이드 모두 회복 촉진 — 표준은 없으나 PBM이 가장 일관적.
> - **시간 기반 프로토콜(Misch 2010)**: ① 수술 중: 국소 덱사메타손 4 mg/mL ± 임플란트 제거(신경 절단 확인 시 즉시 의뢰). ② 1주 이내: 경구 덱사메타손 테이퍼 + 이부프로펜 600–800 mg TID×3주 + 조기 PBM·B12 개시. ③ 12주 이내 개선 없음: 전문의 의뢰 ± 수술적 감압/재건.
> - **한계**: 치료 관련 RCT가 단 1편(Reus 2024 SR) — 모든 권고는 소규모 RCT·증례·동물 연구 수준; 표준 프로토콜 미확립; 동물→인간 용량 외삽 미검증.
> - **임상 결론**: 예방(≥2 mm 안전거리·CBCT) → 조기 발견(술 중·술 후 감각 검사) → 36시간 내 임플란트 제거(직접 손상 의심 시) → 덱사메타손 조기 투여 → PBM + 메틸코발라민 × 4–8주 → 12주 무반응 시 신경외과 의뢰. 이 **시간 축 원칙**이 현존하는 가장 강력한 임상 레버다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 11 papers (2 SR, 1 RCT, 3 narrative reviews, 1 case-series, 3 animal studies) on iatrogenic IAN/LN injury from dental implants, third-molar extraction, and orthognathic surgery — covering epidemiology, Seddon/Sunderland classification, time-critical management (the 36-hour window), and pharmacological/physical treatments (dexamethasone, vitamin B12/methylcobalamin, PBM, ginkgo biloba).

Implant-related neuropathy occurs in 12% at 1 week resolving to 5% at 3 months (SR, n=503–662); implant removal within 36 hours is the single most critical intervention for direct IAN contact; PBM and vitamin B12 each independently accelerate sensory recovery in the established-injury phase (SR: 7 RCTs; RCT: n=60, TPD −2.6 mm vs −0.8 mm sham); dexamethasone is effective only when administered early.

No universal treatment guideline exists; all therapies rest on small RCTs with high risk of bias; the strongest clinical levers are prevention (≥2 mm radiographic safety margin, CBCT guidance) and the time-based protocol: intraoperative topical dexamethasone → early implant removal within 36 h if suspected contact → oral corticosteroids + PBM + methylcobalamin × 4–12 weeks → specialist referral at 12 weeks if no improvement.

## 세줄요약

11편 종합(SR 2, RCT 1, 서술 리뷰 3, 증례 1, 동물 3): 임플란트·사랑니·악교정 수술 후 하치조신경(IAN)/설신경(LN) 손상의 역학·Seddon/Sunderland 분류·시간 기반 관리(36시간 창)·약물(덱사메타손·비타민 B12·메틸코발라민·은행잎)·광생물조절(PBM) 종합.

임플란트 후 신경병증: 1주 12% → 3개월 5% (SR, n=503–662); 직접 IAN 접촉 시 36시간 이내 임플란트 제거가 단일 최대 회복 레버; PBM·비타민 B12 모두 독립적으로 감각 회복 촉진(SR: 7 RCT; RCT: n=60, TPD −2.6 mm vs 위약 −0.8 mm); 덱사메타손은 조기 투여 시만 유효.

표준 치료 지침 없음; 예방(≥2 mm 안전거리·CBCT)과 시간 기반 프로토콜(수술 중 국소 덱사 → 36시간 내 임플란트 제거 → 경구 스테로이드·PBM·메틸코발라민×4–12주 → 12주 무반응 시 신경외과 의뢰)이 현재 근거상 최강 임상 레버.

## Axis 1 — Epidemiology & Classification

### 1A. Prevalence

**Reus 2024** (SR, 38 studies) provides the most current quantitative picture for implant-related IAN neuropathy:

| Group | At 1 week | At ≥3 months |
|---|---|---|
| Without nerve lateralization | 12% | 5% |
| With nerve lateralization | 90% | 42% |

**Kammerer 2024** (scoping review) adds the third-molar perspective:
- IAN injury: 0.4–5.5% of lower third molar removals (transient up to 8–12%)
- Implant-related: >30% transient; permanent 0.01–0.09%
- Lingual nerve: 0–2% (2-week), most resolve within 3 months

**Clinical implication**: most transient neuropathy resolves spontaneously. The goal of management is to prevent transient from becoming permanent.

### 1B. Seddon/Sunderland Classification

**Juodzbalys 2011** (narrative review, 136 sources) established the foundational classification framework:

| Seddon Grade | Sunderland | Description | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neuropraxia** | I | Focal demyelination; axon intact | Full recovery, weeks–months |
| **Axonotmesis** | II–III | Axon disrupted; endoneurium intact | Gradual recovery, months |
| **Neurotmesis** | IV–V | Complete severance or fibrosis | Surgical repair required |

**Mechanism categories** (Juodzbalys 2011):
- *Intraoperative*: mechanical (direct contact, compression, stretching), thermal (drilling heat, laser), chemical (irrigant, local anesthetic intoxication)
- *Postoperative*: thermal necrosis (polymerizing methylmethacrylate), infection, hematoma pressure

## Axis 2 — Prevention

**Juodzbalys 2011** establishes the core prevention principles for implant-related IAN injury:

1. **Radiographic safety margin**: ≥2 mm from the superior wall of the mandibular canal
2. **CBCT guidance** for posterior mandible when canal is close to the crest or curved
3. **Drill-stop devices** calibrated to planned depth minus 2 mm
4. **Intraoperative monitoring**: if unusual pain, pressure, or bleeding during drilling → stop immediately
5. **Neurosensory testing**: test the chin/lip before patient discharge; any new hypoesthesia requires OPG/CT same day

**Kammerer 2024** adds: the IAN canal can loop anteriorly beyond the mental foramen — CBCT is the only way to detect this variant and avoid "safe" anterior placements that actually violate the canal.

---

## Axis 3 — The 36-Hour Window (Implant-Related Injury)

**Khawaja 2009** (4 cases, King's College Hospital) provides the clearest clinical evidence for time-critical implant removal:

| Case | Removal timing | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 18 hours | Near-complete recovery |
| 2 | 36 hours | Near-complete recovery |
| 3 | 2 days | Permanent complete anesthesia |
| 4 | 4 days | Permanent complete anesthesia |

**Misch 2010** (expert protocol, survey: 73% of dentists encountered IAN impairment) operationalizes this into a 4-period management flow:

| Period | Action |
|---|---|
| **Intraoperative** | Local dexamethasone 4 mg/mL at site; if nerve transection observed → immediate microsurgery referral |
| **Within 36 h** | OPG/CT to confirm canal violation; if implant in/through canal → remove within 36 h + oral dexamethasone taper |
| **1–12 weeks** | Oral dexamethasone + ibuprofen 600–800 mg TID × 3 weeks; add PBM + methylcobalamin |
| **≥12 weeks no improvement** | Microsurgery referral (decompression, neurolysis, or autograft) |

**Immediate referral criteria (Misch 2010)**:
- Intraoperative nerve transection (confirmed or suspected)
- Dysesthesia (burning/electrical pain) at 1 week — suggests Grade III+ damage
- Complete anesthesia at 1 week with no improvement

## Axis 4 — Pharmacological Treatment

### 4A. Corticosteroids (Dexamethasone)

**Han 2010** (animal, rat IAN injury model) — critical timing finding:
- **Early dexamethasone** (25–50 mg/kg IP) produced **prolonged anti-allodynic effects**
- **Established neuropathic pain** showed **no response** to dexamethasone
- Bilateral (mirror-image) hypersensitivity observed → central sensitization confirmed

> **Clinical translation**: corticosteroids must be given in the acute/subacute phase (ideally within 24–72 h of injury). Once central sensitization is established, anti-inflammatory mechanisms are insufficient alone.

**Misch 2010** clinical protocol: dexamethasone 4 mg/mL locally at implant site (intraoperative) → oral dexamethasone taper starting same day if implant retained.

**Keykha 2024** (SR, 7 RCTs): prednisolone 3–6 weeks post-op accelerated sensory recovery in paresthesia patients — confirming clinical validity of the corticosteroid window.

### 4B. Vitamin B12 / Methylcobalamin

**Buesing 2019** (narrative review, PubMed) — mechanism and clinical evidence:

*Animal mechanisms*:
- Axon growth promotion and Schwann cell differentiation
- BDNF↑ (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)
- COX enzyme inhibition (reduces orofacial pain in formalin model Phase 2)
- TRPV1 modulation (reduces central sensitization)
- Synergy with NSAIDs and opioids

*Clinical RCTs*:
- Low back pain: IM cyanocobalamin 1000 µg/day × 2 weeks → pain −87%
- Hydroxycobalamin 2 mg TID: neuropathic pain VAS −60%
- Herpetic neuralgia: ≥50% reduction in 60% of subjects

**Zhang 2020** (animal, 10 mm rat sciatic nerve gap):
- PLCL conduit + 15% methylcobalamin: best outcome — largest axon diameter, thickest myelin, Schwann cell proliferation, 21-day sustained release
- Supports local delivery concept (though not yet clinically validated for dental nerves)

**Keykha 2024** (SR): vitamin B12 showed efficacy comparable to PBM at 4 weeks for paresthesia recovery (direct comparison in ≥1 included RCT).

> **Clinical practice**: oral/IM methylcobalamin 0.5–1.5 mg/day × 4–8 weeks is a safe, low-cost adjunct; the Ma 2026 RCT used mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days as a background therapy for all arms.

### 4C. Ginkgo Biloba Extract (EGb)

**Lin 2007** (animal, rat sciatic nerve, 120 rats, 4 doses):
- EGb 50/100/200 mg/kg/day all improved vs saline: sensory regeneration distance, MNCV, CMAP, axon regeneration index, muscle mass
- Dose-response confirmed (SFI = functional index: non-significant)
- Proposed mechanism: antioxidant (flavonoids), vasodilatation (terpenoids), free-radical scavenging → better nerve bed perfusion

> **Clinical note**: dose extrapolation to humans is not validated. EGb 120–240 mg/day is the standard human dose for neuroprotection, but no dental-specific RCT exists. Consider as adjunct only when standard therapies insufficient.

## Axis 5 — Photobiomodulation (PBM)

**Ma 2026** (RCT, n=60, IAN hypoesthesia 7 days–12 weeks post-third-molar extraction):

| Arm | TPD change | SWME ≥1 grade | VAS numbness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laser acupuncture (LA) | −3.2 mm | 85% | −60% |
| Direct PBM (IAN trajectory) | −2.6 mm | 80% | −48% |
| Sham | −0.8 mm | 25% | −12% |

- All arms received mecobalamin 0.5 mg TID × 30 days
- 808 nm, 100 mW, 9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions
- Both active arms significantly outperformed sham (p<0.01)
- LA produced greater patient-reported numbness reduction — possibly additive central neuromodulation via acupoint

**Keykha 2024** (SR, 7 RCTs — implant/third molar/orthognathic/injection-related paresthesia):
- PBM, vitamin B12, and prednisolone each independently accelerated recovery
- PBM superior to PBM acupuncture in 1 included study
- At 4 weeks, PBM ≈ vitamin B12 (comparable outcomes)
- All 7 RCTs: small n, high risk of bias — **no meta-analysis possible**; no gold standard

**Kammerer 2024** also lists PBM as the most evidence-backed non-surgical physical modality for established trigeminal neuropathy in the published literature.

## Axis 6 — Surgical Management

**Juodzbalys 2011** (foundational): microsurgical repair indicated for Seddon Grade III (Neurotmesis, Sunderland IV–V) unresolved at 3 months:
- Options: neurolysis, internal neurolysis, end-to-end repair, interposed nerve graft (sural/great auricular)
- Success rate: better outcomes with early surgery (within 3 months of injury)

**Kammerer 2024** (2024 update): both decompression and nerve reconstruction effective when applied promptly; surgical outcomes worsen significantly if delayed beyond 6 months — emphasizing the value of early specialist triage.

## Integrated Clinical Protocol

```
PREVENTION
├── ≥2 mm radiographic safety margin from IAN canal
├── CBCT for high-risk cases (short ridge, anterior loop, curved canal)
└── Test neurosensory before patient leaves chair

INTRAOPERATIVE alert (paresthesia/bleeding/resistance change)
├── Stop drilling immediately
├── OPG / periapical radiograph
└── If violation confirmed → topical dexamethasone 4 mg/mL at site

WITHIN 36 HOURS (implant-related direct contact suspected)
├── Confirm position: OPG, CBCT
├── Violation confirmed → remove/reposition implant
├── Start oral dexamethasone taper + ibuprofen 600-800 mg TID × 3 wks
└── Start methylcobalamin 0.5 mg TID

WEEKS 1–12 (established paresthesia)
├── PBM: 808 nm, ~9 J/site, every other day × 15 sessions
├── Continue methylcobalamin × 4–8 weeks
├── Consider prednisolone taper if not already given
└── Monitor with standardized neurosensory tests (LT, TPD, SWME)

≥12 WEEKS: no improvement
└── Refer: oral/maxillofacial surgery or neurosurgery
    (decompression / neurolysis / autograft — Grade III-V)
```

## Evidence Summary & Limitations

| Domain | Best evidence | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Prevalence | Reus 2024 SR (38 studies) | Moderate (SR, no MA) |
| Classification | Juodzbalys 2011 / Kammerer 2024 | Expert review |
| 36-h window | Khawaja 2009 (4 cases) | Very low (case series) |
| Dexamethasone timing | Han 2010 (rat) | Preclinical only |
| Vitamin B12 | Buesing 2019 review + Keykha SR | Low-moderate |
| PBM | Ma 2026 RCT (n=60) + Keykha SR | Low (small RCTs, high RoB) |
| Ginkgo biloba | Lin 2007 (rat) | Preclinical only |
| Surgical repair | Kammerer 2024 review | Expert consensus |

**Key evidence gap**: only 1 RCT addresses management of implant-specific IAN injury (Reus 2024); the rest are generalized paresthesia treatments. Clinical guidelines urgently need prospective, powered RCTs.
