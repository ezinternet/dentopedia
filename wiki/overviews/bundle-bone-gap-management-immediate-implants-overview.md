> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **출처 성격**: 본 문서는 Periospot 블로그 글(Francisco Teixeira Barbosa, 2025-01-03 게시, 2026-11-17 확인)을 요약한 **외부 웹 자료 개요(external web overview)**이며, 본 위키에 인제스트된 1차 논문(`papers/`·`sources/`)을 직접 종합한 synthesis 페이지가 아니다. 임상 판단 시 인제스트된 근거 페이지와 교차 확인 필요.
> - **다발골(bundle bone, 치조골 고유판 alveolar bone proper / 방사선학적 치밀선 lamina dura)**: 치주인대(periodontal ligament, PDL) 샤피섬유(Sharpey’s fibers)가 부착되는 치조와 내벽의 얇은 골층(협측 약 0.2–0.4 mm 수준으로 소개)으로, **치아 의존적(tooth-dependent)** 조직이다. 발치 시 치주인대가 소실되면 협측 다발골의 주요 혈류가 차단되어 흡수가 불가피하다.
> - **용어 정리(동일 구조의 관점 차이)**: 조직학자(histologist)는 bundle bone, 방사선학자(radiologist)는 lamina dura, 해부학자(anatomist)는 alveolar bone proper로 부르며 — 동일 해부학적 구조를 다른 관점에서 지칭한다는 점을 강조.
> - **발치 후 변화(핵심 수치)**: 발치 후 12개월 내 협설 수평 폭의 약 **50% 감소**가 일어나며 대부분은 처음 3개월에 집중된다고 요약. 협측골이 더 얇고 다발골 비율이 높아 구개/설측보다 더 많이 흡수된다. 상악 전치부 협측골 두께 <1 mm 사례가 많고(본문에서는 91% 언급), 얇은 협측판의 40–60%가 bundle bone일 수 있다고 제시.
> - **즉시식립(immediate implant placement)과 골보존의 오해**: 임플란트는 보철적 지지체(prosthetic anchor)이며 치주인대·샤피섬유·고유수용감각(proprioception)을 재현하지 못한다. 즉시식립이 다발골 흡수를 막지 못한다는 점(Chen 2004, Araújo 2005/2006 계열 근거를 인용)을 강조하고, “골을 보존한다”가 아니라 “잔존 조직 유지/관리” 관점으로 기대치를 설정할 것을 권고.
> - **치조제 보존술(ridge preservation / socket preservation)**: 즉시식립을 하지 않는 경우 등에서 무처치 치유 대비 수평 흡수를 약 1–2 mm 줄이는 데 도움이 될 수 있다고 정리. 단, 즉시식립+동시 이식, 소켓실드(socket shield technique) 선택, 비용 제약 등에서는 예외가 있을 수 있음.
> - **연조직 이식(connective tissue graft, CTG)**: 심미부 즉시식립에서 CTG는 협측 윤곽 보상, 각화조직/조직 두께 강화, 임플란트·지대주 은폐 개선에 기여할 수 있어 선택이 아닌 표준 프로토콜로 고려할 것을 제안.
> - **협측 간극(jumping gap, buccal gap) 관리**: 임플란트를 구개측으로 위치시켜 협측판과 임플란트 표면 사이 약 **2 mm** 수평 간극을 확보하는 것을 권장. <0.4 mm의 매우 작은 간극은 자발 치유 가능성(critical size defect 개념)을 언급하나, 2 mm 협측 간극은 생체재료(biomaterial) 이식이 필요하다고 정리. 임플란트 직경·3차원 위치(CBCT 계획)가 핵심.
> - **소켓실드(socket shield technique, SST)**: 협측 치근편을 유지해 치주인대 부착과 혈류를 보존하려는 접근(Hürzeler 2010 등). 협측골·연조직 보존에 유리할 수 있으나 술식 민감도, 실드 이동/감염 위험, 장기 근거 한계 등 논쟁점이 있음을 함께 제시.
> - **의사결정 프레임(요지)**: CBCT로 협측골 두께·연조직 표현형(biotype) 평가 → 협측골 >1 mm·양호한 해부학이면 즉시식립 후보(2 mm 간극, 협측 이식, 심미부 CTG 고려) / 협측골 <1 mm·얇은 표현형이면 단계적 접근(발치와 보존 후 3–4개월 지연식립 ± GBR) / 온전한 협측 치근·병소 없음·술자 경험 등 충족 시 SST 고려.

## 1. Bundle Bone — Terminology & Biology

### 1.1 Definition (as presented)

- **Bundle bone** = thin bone lining the socket with Sharpey’s fiber insertions from the PDL; described as tooth-dependent tissue (also termed alveolar bone proper / cribriform plate).
- After extraction, the periodontal apparatus and its vascular contribution to bundle bone are lost, leading to inevitable resorption.

### 1.2 Same structure, different names

| Perspective | Term used | How seen | Feature emphasized |
|---|---|---|---|
| Histology (조직학) | Bundle bone (다발골) | Microscope | Sharpey’s fiber “bundle” insertions |
| Radiology (방사선학) | Lamina dura (치밀선) | X-ray/CBCT | Dense white line around root |
| Anatomy (해부학) | Alveolar bone proper (치조골 고유판) | Dissection/textbook | Thin socket-lining bone |

### 1.3 Alveolar bone layers & thickness context (article figures summarized)

The article distinguishes bundle bone, lamellar bone(층판골), trabecular bone(해면골), and cortical plate(피질골), noting buccal bone in the anterior maxilla is often very thin (<1 mm in most sites per cited clinical data figure) and bundle bone may constitute a large fraction of thin buccal plates.

### 1.4 Post-extraction resorption timeline (article summary)

- Day 0: PDL severed, blood supply interrupted
- Weeks 1–2: osteoclastic activity begins
- Weeks 4–8: peak resorption period
- Months 3–6: remodeling continues/slows
- Month 12: majority of dimensional change complete

## 2. The 12 Facts — Condensed Summary

1. **~50% buccal width reduction after extraction**: Major horizontal reduction within 12 months, mostly first 3 months; buccal resorbs more than palatal/lingual due to thin plate, higher bundle-bone proportion, less trabecular support. Measure buccal thickness (<1 mm common in anterior maxilla) and expect change.
2. **Loss of PDL = loss of blood supply**: PDL described as primary vascular source (~70% contribution stated) to bundle bone; extraction triggers resorption cascade (weeks to months). Biology can be managed, not fully prevented.
3. **Immediate implants do not prevent bundle-bone/buccal resorption**: Cites Chen 2004, Araújo 2005/2006; resorption occurs regardless of implant presence. Immediate placement offers other advantages but requires expectation setting.
4. **Implants do not preserve original alveolar bone volume**: No PDL, Sharpey’s fibers, proprioception, or PDL-mediated dynamic stimulation; implants help maintain what remains vs edentulous ridge but do not preserve pre-extraction volume.
5. **Ridge preservation is often preferable when not placing immediately**: Socket grafting ± membrane may reduce horizontal resorption by ~1–2 mm vs unassisted healing and improve future implant site; exceptions include planned immediate implant with simultaneous grafting, socket shield, or constraints.
6. **Connective tissue grafts (CTG) can enhance immediate-implant esthetic outcomes**: Adds soft-tissue volume/thickness/keratinization potential to compensate buccal contour loss; position to cover buccal gap and integrate with keratinized tissue (esthetic zone emphasis).
7. **Socket shield technique (SST) aims to preserve tooth-dependent tissues**: Retaining buccal root fragment/PDL may maintain bundle-bone blood supply (Hürzeler 2010 lineage); potential benefits for buccal bone/soft tissue/papilla with comparable survival in cited literature, but technique-sensitive with controversy (mobility/infection risk, evolving long-term data).
8. **The buccal “gap” — ~2 mm target**: Palatal/lingual implant positioning to create ~2 mm horizontal gap between implant and buccal plate allows clot/graft space and reduces direct pressure on thin buccal plate; too close risks resorption/thread exposure, too large may need more grafting/healing. CBCT planning recommended.
9. **3D implant position balances biology and restoration**: Mesio-distal centered, bucco-palatal palatal to socket axis (2 mm gap), apico-coronal ~3–4 mm apical to planned gingival margin, angulation following natural axis or slightly palatal; avoid sacrificing position for primary stability.
10. **Narrow implants when anatomy demands**: Match diameter to tooth/site (e.g., laterals, lower incisors, limited space/thin biotype); narrow designs (≈3.0–3.5 mm) can improve emergence-profile possibilities and conserve bone.
11. **Filling the gap — 0.4 mm threshold concept**: Very small gaps (<0.4 mm) may heal spontaneously (critical size defect idea), but the recommended ~2 mm buccal gap should be grafted; material choice (xenograft/allograft/autograft/combinations) case-dependent.
12. **Contact inhibition / gap-healing paradigm**: Strategic gaps with proper grafting/time can be favorable; understanding healing from socket walls vs direct implant contact informs why “pressing implant to thin buccal plate” is not always optimal.

## 3. Clinical Decision Framework (Article)

### Assessment
- CBCT analysis: buccal bone thickness
- Soft tissue: phenotype/biotype, keratinized tissue width
- Patient factors: expectations, healing capacity, compliance

### Decision points
- **Buccal bone >1 mm, favorable anatomy** → immediate implant candidate: plan 2 mm buccal gap, buccal grafting, consider CTG in esthetic zone.
- **Buccal bone <1 mm, thin biotype** → staged approach: ridge preservation at extraction, ~3–4 months healing, delayed implant ± GBR.
- **Socket shield candidate criteria (as listed)**: intact buccal root, no periapical pathology, restorable emergence profile, experienced surgeon.

## 4. FAQ / Key Takeaways (Selected)

- Bundle bone is tooth-dependent; PDL loss drives resorption.
- Approx. 50% horizontal and 2–4 mm vertical reduction within 12 months reported in article FAQ framing (majority early).
- Immediate implants do not prevent resorption but may aid treatment timing/soft-tissue management.
- Graft buccal gaps larger than ~0.4 mm; routinely manage the ~2 mm buccal gap in immediate protocols.
- SST may preserve bundle bone by retaining root/PDL in selected cases; ridge preservation minimizes but does not eliminate dimensional change.
