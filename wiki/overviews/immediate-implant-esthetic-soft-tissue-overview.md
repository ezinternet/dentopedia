> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **결론(bottom line)**: 전치부 즉시식립(Immediate Implant Placement, IIP)의 생존율은 모든 식립시점에서 >95%로 거의 동등하지만, **승패는 생존이 아니라 정중협측 퇴축(midfacial recession)·심미**에서 갈린다. 무선별 IIP는 >1 mm 퇴축 위험이 20–30%다 (Buser 2017).
> - **퇴축의 3대 동인**: ① 얇은 협측골벽(≤1 mm) ② 얇은 생체형(thin biotype) ③ 협측 임플란트 위치(buccal shoulder). 이 셋이 위험을 곱셈으로 키운다 (Chen 2009, Buser 2017).
> - **협측골벽 두께가 1차 게이트**: ≤1 mm 박벽형은 발치 후 8주에 수직골 중앙값 7.5 mm 소실(후벽형 1.1 mm의 ~3.5배) — 박벽형이면 IIP 자체를 재고하고 치조제보존술(Alveolar Ridge Preservation, ARP)+지연식립으로 (Chappuis 2013).
> - **임플란트 위치가 직접 동인**: 협측 숄더는 설측 대비 정중협측 퇴축이 ~3배(1.8 vs 0.6 mm). 구개측·심부 식립이 필수 — 안전구역은 안면 연조직연 4 mm 하방·협측 간극 >2 mm (Evans 2008, Cosyn 2026).
> - **팔라탈 포지셔닝의 중기 근거(Staas 2026)**: ≥2 mm 팔라탈 오프셋을 쓴 flapless 즉시식립·즉시임시치관(FIIPP) 프로토콜의 3년 다기관 전향적 CBCT 추적 — 협측 능선 두께·높이 보존, 생존율 양호. 단 비팔라탈 대조군이 없어 "이 정도 오프셋은 안전하다"는 근거지 "팔라탈이 더 낫다"는 비교 근거는 아님.
> - **왜 협측 천공 위험이 원래 내재하는가(Kan 2011)**: 상악 전치 치근의 대다수(Sagittal Root Position Class I)가 애초에 순측 피질골에 붙어있어, 발치와 궤적을 그대로 따르면 협측 천공·돌출 위험이 구조적으로 발생 — 팔라탈 시프트가 필요해지는 해부학적 근본 원인.
> - **순측골 두께 자체가 심미 상한선(Yang 2019)**: 위치를 잘 잡아도 순측골이 1–2 mm 미만이면 PES/WES가 뚜렷이 악화하는 변곡점이 존재 — 골소실이 이미 심한 케이스는 포지셔닝만으로 심미 결과의 천장 자체를 올리지 못함.
> - **식립시기(즉시 vs 조기 vs 지연)는 심미결과를 바꾸지 않는다**: 18 RCT SR+MA에서 식립 타이밍 간 정중협측 퇴축(Midfacial Recession)·PES·유두 지수(Papilla Index) 모두 1·2·10년에 유의차 없음. 부하 타이밍도 마찬가지. "더 기다리면 연조직이 좋아진다"는 통념은 현재 근거로 지지되지 않는다 (Francisco 2021 SR+MA).
> - **즉시 임시수복(immediate provisionalization)의 가치 — 선형지표만 보면 과소평가**: 생존율은 안 바꾸지만(~97% 동등) **연조직 윤곽·유두·emergence profile 보존**에 유리. 단, 선형 변연레벨 지표로는 치유지대주와 유의차 없을 수 있고(Chan 2019 RCT, n=40), 체적 분석으로 보면 즉시보철군이 협측 부피 손실을 유의하게 줄인다(11.9% vs 17.4%, P=0.04; Wang 2020). **선형으로 null ≠ 심미적으로 동등** — 측정 방법이 결론을 바꿀 수 있다.
> - **즉시보철의 유두 이득**: 기존 RCT에서 선형 변연레벨 차이가 없어도 유두 높이(Papillary Height, PH)에는 즉시보철이 유의한 이점(MD −0.519 mm, P=0.028; Kinaia 2017 SR+MA). 협측 생체형이 두꺼울수록 MFR도 유의하게 줄어든다(MD −0.478 mm, P<0.001).
> - **임시수복의 전제조건**: 충분한 일차안정성(보통 ≥35 N·cm 또는 임플란트안정지수 ISQ ≥70). 안정성이 부족하면 비-임플란트지지 대안(자연치관+섬유강화복합 FRC 스플린트)으로 PES 유지 가능(9.0 vs 6.3, p=0.005) (Wu 2026).
> - **결합조직이식(Connective Tissue Graft, CTG/SCTG)의 효과**: 정중협측 퇴축을 줄이고(~0.4–0.7 mm 보존; Seyssens 2021) 협측조직두께·PES를 높이며(상피하 SCTG: 두께 +0.74 mm, PES +0.79; Aldhohrah 2022), 1년 RCT에서 부피손실을 ~5배 감소(퇴축 −0.74 mm vs 골이식 −0.98, 맞춤치유지대주 단독 −1.54; Fettouh 2024). CTG의 이득은 협측 collapse 감소(2–5 mm 구간)에 집중되며, 중안모 치은연(Mid-facial Gingival Margin) 위치 자체는 CTG 유무 무관하게 유사하게 이동함 (Jiang 2020 RCT, 3D CBCT). 단 RCT-only 합성에서는 협측 치은레벨 차이가 비유의 경향(Torra-Moneny 2024) — CTG는 레벨을 '증가'보다 '유지'시킨다.
> - **CTG는 두께가 얇을 때 우선**: 네트워크 메타분석에서 연조직두께(Soft Tissue Thickness, STT)를 유의하게 늘린 것은 CTG뿐이나 대부분 재료간 차이는 비유의; 단층 이종 콜라겐 매트릭스가 PES 순위 1위 — 얇은 케이스만 CTG, 그 외엔 대체재로 충분 (Azadi 2025). DBBM+콜라겐(DBBM+C) 소켓이식도 즉시 임시수복과 병행 시 연조직 높이 및 협설측 골폭 유의 보존 (Girlanda 2019 RCT).
> - **협측 간극(buccal gap) 관리가 경조직의 핵심**: 간극 >2 mm는 Type 1A 생존을 +3.1 PP 올린다(근관감염 부재 +2.6 PP; Hamilton 2023). 간극은 이식재로 채우고, 열개(dehiscence) 소켓은 morphotype에 따라 재생전략을 단계화하되 Type III(열개+안면퇴축)와 인접치까지 확장된 결손은 **절대금기** (Cosyn 2026).
> - **즉시식립+즉시수복(IIPR) 장기 결과**: 17편 SR에서 성공률 97.96%, 생존율 98.25%(평균 31.2개월 추적), 중안모 점막변화 <0.95 mm — 두꺼운 생체형(전체 60.5%)·무피판(65.6%)·간극 이식(64.7%) 프로토콜에서 이 결과가 도출됨. 엄격한 환자 선별이 공통 전제 (Weigl 2016 SR).
> - **임상 takeaway**: IIP 심미성공 = 케이스선별(협측벽 intact·중간/두꺼운 생체형·간극 ≥2 mm·감염 없음) × 구개측 식립 × 간극 이식 × (얇으면) CTG × (안정성 충분하면) 즉시 임시수복. 비폐쇄 개방치유(open healing)도 선별된 전치부에서 일차봉합과 동등(정중협측 점막 SMD −0.26, 비유의; 근심유두는 오히려 유리; Zadikian 2026). **심미 결과를 선형 지표만으로 평가하면 즉시보철의 실제 이득을 놓친다** — 체적 분석이 더 민감한 측정 도구다 (Wang 2020).

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 22 papers on esthetic-zone immediate implant placement: survival is near-universal (>95%) at all placement timings, but Type 1 carries 20–30% risk of >1 mm midfacial recession when unselected (Buser 2017); the three multiplicative risk factors are thin buccal wall (≤1 mm → median 7.5 mm vertical bone loss at 8 weeks; Chappuis 2013), thin biotype, and buccal shoulder position (~3× recession vs palatal; Evans 2008).

The four controllable levers are palatal/deep 3-D positioning (safe zone ≥4 mm below facial margin, gap >2 mm; Cosyn 2026), buccal-gap grafting (>2 mm gap +3.1 PP survival; Hamilton 2023 SR+MA), CTG/SCTG for thin phenotypes (midfacial level −0.74 mm; volumetric loss 5× less vs customized healing abutment alone; Fettouh 2024 RCT), and immediate provisionalization when primary stability is adequate (~97% survival either way; Pitman 2022 SR+MA).

Open healing matches primary closure for midfacial mucosal level (SMD −0.26, NS) and favors the mesial papilla (Zadikian 2026 SR+MA, n=431); case selection sets the ceiling — a thin-walled or dehisced socket without regenerative correction is better deferred to ARP + early/late placement.

## 세줄요약

전치부 즉시식립 22편 종합: 생존율은 모든 시점에서 >95%로 보편적이나 Type 1은 무선별 시 정중협측 퇴축 >1 mm 위험 20–30%(Buser 2017); 3대 위험인자는 협측골 ≤1 mm(8주 수직골 소실 7.5 mm; Chappuis 2013)·얇은 생체형·협측 숄더 위치(퇴축 ~3배; Evans 2008).

4가지 조절 레버: 구개측·심부 식립(안전구역 ≥4 mm; Cosyn 2026), 협측간극 이식(>2 mm gap +3.1 PP 생존; Hamilton 2023 SR+MA), 얇은 표현형에 CTG(정중협측 변화 −0.74 mm·부피 손실 5배 감소 vs 맞춤 치유지대주 단독; Fettouh 2024 RCT), 안정성 충분 시 즉시 임시수복(생존율 동등, 연조직 윤곽·유두 보존; Pitman 2022 SR+MA).

개방 치유는 1차 봉합 대비 정중협측 점막레벨 동등(SMD −0.26, NS)하고 근심 유두에 유리(Zadikian 2026 SR+MA, n=431); 케이스 선별이 천장을 결정하고 — 협측골 결손·열개 소켓은 ARP + 조기/지연식립이 더 예측 가능한 기본값이다.

## Thesis

The esthetic-zone immediate implant is not a survival problem — it is a **soft-tissue stability problem**. Across timing-classification reviews (Buser 2017; Chen 2009), survival exceeds 95% for immediate (Type 1) just as for early (Type 2/3) and late (Type 4) placement, yet Type 1 carries a **20–30% risk of >1 mm midfacial mucosal recession** when patients and sites are unselected. The clinically meaningful outcome is therefore the **midfacial mucosal level (MML)**, the **papillae**, and the composite **Pink Esthetic Score (PES)** — not the implant-survival column.

Three risk factors recur in every analysis and behave **multiplicatively**, not additively:

1. **Buccal bone wall thickness** — the upstream gate. Chappuis 2013 quantified that a ≤1 mm thin-wall phenotype loses a median **7.5 mm of vertical bone** by 8 weeks post-extraction versus **1.1 mm** for thick walls (~3.5×), and most esthetic-zone patients are *already* thin-walled at the central buccal site. A wall that resorbs takes the overlying mucosa with it. Yang 2019 (prospective cohort) supplies the outcome-side confirmation: PES/WES deteriorates as labial bone thickness decreases, with a clinically meaningful inflection around the **1–2 mm labial-plate threshold** — meaning that once pre-existing labial bone loss has already crossed this line, no amount of 3-D repositioning fully restores the esthetic ceiling. Kan 2011's sagittal root position (SRP) classification explains *why* this thin-wall default is so common in the first place: the majority of maxillary anterior roots (Class I) sit directly against the labial cortex, so following the native root trajectory during immediate placement inherently risks labial fenestration/dehiscence — the structural reason a palatal shift is so often needed at all.
2. **Tissue biotype/phenotype** — thin biotype trends toward more recession (Evans 2008 found 1.0 vs 0.7 mm, NS; Buser 2017 lists it as a top-3 driver). Its main effect is to make wall loss visible and to limit the soft-tissue reserve a graft can build on.
3. **Implant 3-D position** — the one fully operator-controlled driver. Evans 2008 showed a **buccal shoulder yields ~3× the recession of a lingual shoulder (1.8 vs 0.6 mm, p<0.001)**. Cosyn 2026 formalizes this as a *safe zone* (≥4 mm below the facial soft-tissue margin vertically, buccal gap >2 mm horizontally) vs *danger* and *failure* zones. Staas 2026 (prospective multicentre CBCT, 3-yr follow-up) provides mid-term confirmation for a standardized **≥2 mm palatal offset** protocol in flapless immediate placement + provisionalization (FIIPP): buccal crest thickness and height were maintained at 3 years with survival in the expected high range — though the absence of a non-palatal comparator arm means this establishes that the offset is *safe and stable*, not that it is *superior* in a head-to-head sense. A caveat this synthesis must flag explicitly: the ≥2 mm figure is a **deliberate, protocol-driven offset applied to an otherwise adequate ridge** — it is not the same clinical situation as being *forced* substantially further palatal because pre-existing buccal/labial bone loss is already severe. None of the papers in this synthesis directly studies that rescue scenario (see Gaps below). Mechanistically, Wang 2022 (RCT, mandibular molars) helps explain why over-rotating palatal is not a free lunch: a steeper restorative emergence angle (40° vs ~32°) produced significantly more 12-month gingival recession (0.63 ± 0.38 mm vs 0.13 ± 0.32 mm, p=0.006) — pushing an implant deep palatal to escape a deficient buccal plate can itself force a steeper buccal emergence angle at the prosthetic stage, partially offsetting the positional benefit unless the emergence profile is deliberately designed (W/H-ratio-based) rather than left to default to the socket/healing-abutment contour.

A separate axis that the earlier evidence base left ambiguous is **placement timing itself** — does waiting longer before placing the implant produce better soft-tissue outcomes? Francisco 2021 (SR+MA, 18 RCTs) answers this cleanly at the esthetic-outcome level: no significant difference in midfacial recession, papilla level, PES, WES, or Papilla Index between immediate, early, and delayed placement at 1-, 2-, or 10-year follow-up, and loading timing is similarly irrelevant. Block 2009 (RCT, n=55, 2-yr follow-up) provides individual-trial-level corroboration: immediate placement preserved ~1 mm more facial gingival margin than delayed (4-month healing) placement (P<0.05), with equivalent hard-tissue outcomes — pointing the opposite direction from the intuition that more healing time helps. Weigl 2016 (SR, 17 studies, 626 IIPR implants) maps the broader outcomes picture: 97.96% success, 98.25% survival at mean 31.2-month follow-up, with mucosal change <0.95 mm regardless of biotype — but every study in the pool applied strict patient selection, which is the non-transferable condition.

Against this risk substrate, the literature converges on a small set of **controllable levers**:

- **Buccal-gap grafting** is the principal hard-tissue lever. A facial gap **>2 mm raised Type 1A survival by +3.1 percentage points** (Hamilton 2023) and is the space into which graft, blood clot, and biology fit; Pitman 2022 found jumping-gap management *more predictive of hard-tissue outcome than provisionalization itself*.
- **Connective tissue grafting (CTG/SCTG)** is the principal soft-tissue lever for thin phenotypes. Seyssens 2021 (SR+MA) found CTG preserves the midfacial level by ~0.4–0.7 mm; Aldhohrah 2022 found subepithelial CTG significantly raised buccal thickness (+0.74 mm), gingival level (+0.5 mm), and PES (+0.79); Fettouh 2024 (1-yr RCT) showed CTG cut midfacial recession to −0.74 mm and volumetric loss ~5-fold vs bone graft (−0.98) or customized healing abutment alone (−1.54). A critical qualification from Jiang 2020 (RCT, n=40, 3D CBCT superimposition): CTG significantly reduced buccal tissue collapse in the 2–5 mm apical zone, but **mid-facial gingival margin migration was identical** with and without CTG — decoupling "volume/contour preservation" from "margin-level stability" as two distinct outcomes that don't necessarily move together. This dissociation, detectable only with volumetric 3D analysis, explains the inconsistency between studies using 2D linear metrics. The socket-fill evidence extends further: Girlanda 2019 (RCT, n=22) showed DBBM+C socket grafting alongside immediate provisionalization preserved significantly greater soft-tissue height (mesiobuccal and distobuccal at 3 and 6 months, P<0.05) and buccolingual ridge width vs no graft — a biomaterial-specific confirmation that gap grafting with a collagen-bearing xenograft yields additive tissue benefit. The caveat: RCT-only pooling (Torra-Moneny 2024) found the buccal-level benefit non-significant (MD 0.09 mm, p=0.05) — **CTG maintains rather than increases** the level, and a network meta-analysis (Azadi 2025) found CTG uniquely raised soft-tissue thickness but most between-material differences were not significant, with a uni-layer xenogeneic collagen matrix ranking best for PES. Reserve CTG for thin tissue; substitutes suffice otherwise. Kinaia 2017 (SR+MA, 12 studies) provides the biotype-stratified quantification: thick biotype showed significantly less MFR (MD −0.478 mm) and better papillary height (MD −0.287 mm) vs thin biotype (both P<0.001) within the IIP group.
- **Immediate provisionalization** is the principal contour/papilla lever — and its esthetic benefit is **measurement-method dependent**. It does **not** change survival (~97% in both arms; Pitman 2022) and when assessed by conventional linear metrics (mucosal marginal level, papilla height measured as a point or linear dimension), a single-center RCT found no significant intergroup difference vs a healing abutment (Chan 2019, n=40, 12-month). However, when the same cohort was analyzed by 3D volumetric voxel-based model registration, the provisional crown group showed significantly less soft-tissue contour loss (11.9% vs 17.4%, P=0.04), concentrated at the mid-facial margin and 2–6 mm above the crest — the zone most visible clinically (Wang 2020). The implication: **a null result on a linear metric does not establish esthetic equivalence** — it may simply reflect the metric's limited sensitivity to volumetric contour change. Kinaia 2017 (SR+MA) adds that immediate provisionalization yields a significant papillary height advantage over conventional restoration (MD −0.519 mm, P=0.028) without changing midfacial recession, consistent with the idea that provisionalization's soft-tissue effect is papilla/contour-specific rather than margin-level-specific. The prerequisite is **primary stability** — when that is lacking, a non-implant-supported provisional (natural crown on an FRC splint) can still preserve PES (Wu 2026), and a guide-delivered indexless protocol can shape emergence profile with 0.37 mm 1-yr MBL and 1.41 mm buccal-contour gain (Nechita 2026). An **open-healing** seal (provisional/customized abutment + graft, no membrane, no primary closure) matches primary flap closure for MML (SMD −0.26, NS) and *favors* the mesial papilla (Zadikian 2026).

The synthesis: **case selection sets the ceiling; technique fills the room under it.** A site with an intact buccal wall, medium/thick biotype, and an achievable ≥2 mm gap can be driven to an excellent result with palatal positioning + gap graft + (thin → CTG) + (stable → immediate provisional). A thin-walled or dehisced site should either be regenerated by a morphotype-matched strategy (Cosyn 2026) or — the better-evidenced default for less-experienced operators — deferred to ARP + late placement.

## Clinical Decision Points

### 1. Case selection — is this socket an immediate-implant candidate at all? (set the ceiling)

Assess buccal wall + biotype + infection + gap **before** committing.

- **Intact buccal wall + medium/thick biotype + no active infection + achievable gap ≥2 mm** → IIP defensible (Hamilton 2023, Chen 2009).
- **Thin buccal wall (≤1 mm)** → expect ~7.5 mm vertical loss potential (Chappuis 2013); strongly consider **ARP + early/late placement (Type 2/3)** rather than forcing IIP — Type 2 is the more esthetically predictable default (Chen 2009).
- **Active endodontic/periodontal infection** → defer (−2.6 PP survival; Hamilton 2023).
- **Buccal dehiscence present** → classify by morphotype (Cosyn 2026). Type III (dehiscence + facial recession) and extended (interproximal-to-adjacent-tooth) defects are **absolute contraindications** → ARP + late placement.

### 2. 3-D position — where does the shoulder go? (the operator's dominant lever)

- Place the **shoulder palatally and deep**: safe zone is ≥4 mm below the facial soft-tissue margin and ≥2 mm buccal gap (Cosyn 2026). A buccal shoulder triples midfacial recession (Evans 2008), and a standardized ≥2 mm palatal offset holds up at 3 years (Staas 2026).
- Engage apical/palatal bone for primary stability; this also positions the gap on the buccal aspect where graft is needed.
- **Design the definitive emergence profile deliberately** rather than copying the socket/healing-abutment contour — a steeper buccal emergence angle (as a deep palatal position tends to produce) significantly worsens 12-month recession (Wang 2022).
- **Premolar sites**: a palatal/lingual shift changes the prosthetic axis more than it does for single-rooted anterior teeth. Plan the retention mode from the same CBCT (measure the ridge-angle discrepancy) rather than deciding chairside at delivery — a large angle can force cement retention where screw-retained/SCRP was intended (see [[overviews/implant-prosthesis-retention-screw-vs-cement-overview]]).

### 3. Buccal gap — graft it (the hard-tissue lever)

- A facial gap **>2 mm is favorable** (+3.1 PP survival; Hamilton 2023) and should be filled with a slow-resorbing graft (e.g., DBBM/xenograft) — gap management outweighs provisionalization for hard-tissue outcome (Pitman 2022).
- For dehisced sockets, match graft + barrier to morphotype-tiered regenerative potential (Cosyn 2026): modified trimodal (barrier omitted) for high-potential, four-pillar + barrier (± composite/autogenous bone) for moderate/low.

### 4. Soft-tissue graft — CTG or not? (the thin-phenotype lever)

- **Thin biotype / thin labial bone / high esthetic demand** → add CTG/SCTG. It preserves midfacial level (~0.4–0.7 mm; Seyssens 2021), raises thickness/PES (Aldhohrah 2022), and cuts volumetric loss ~5× (Fettouh 2024).
- **Adequate thickness** → CTG benefit is small/non-significant on level (Torra-Moneny 2024); a xenogeneic collagen matrix is a reasonable, lower-morbidity substitute (best PES rank; Azadi 2025).
- **Do not rely on a customized healing abutment alone** to maintain contour in thin sites (−1.54 mm midfacial change; Fettouh 2024).

### 5. Provisionalize or not? (the contour/papilla lever)

- **Primary stability adequate (≈≥35 N·cm / ISQ ≥70)** → place an immediate **non-occlusal** screw-retained provisional to shape emergence profile and support papillae (Kan 2003, Pitman 2022, Qin 2023). Survival is unchanged; the gain is soft-tissue contour.
- **Primary stability inadequate** → use a non-implant-supported provisional (natural crown + FRC splint; Wu 2026) or a customized healing abutment / open-healing seal — open healing matches primary closure for MML and favors the mesial papilla (Zadikian 2026).
- **Guided/digital workflow** → guide-delivered (even indexless) provisionals are feasible with favorable 1-yr tissue stability (Nechita 2026).
- **Always keep the provisional out of occlusion**; immediate functional occlusal loading is a separate, higher-risk decision (excluded from the soft-tissue evidence base; Zadikian 2026).

## Gaps & Future Research

- **Esthetic/functional success (not just survival) is under-reported.** Hamilton 2023 and the ITI consensus both flag that PES/contour outcomes are inadequately characterized in the primary literature; survival ceilings hide esthetic variance.
- **CTG level-maintenance is RCT-fragile.** Seyssens 2021 (RCT+NRS) found a benefit that Torra-Moneny 2024 (RCT-only) reduced to a non-significant trend — the true effect size and the threshold thickness at which CTG stops adding value are unresolved (Azadi 2025 shows most material differences are NS).
- **Substitute vs autograft.** Whether xenogeneic collagen matrices truly equal CTG for long-term level (not just PES) needs powered head-to-head RCTs.
- **Dehiscence framework is unvalidated.** Cosyn 2026's 11-morphotype → 3-strategy scheme is conceptual; it needs prospective CBCT/histology/outcome validation before it can be a standard.
- **Open healing evidence is shallow and short.** Zadikian 2026 is ~92% anterior, intact-wall, mostly ≤6-month follow-up — long-term and compromised-socket data are missing.
- **Provisionalization alternatives are exploratory.** Wu 2026 (n=20) and Nechita 2026 (n=20, single-arm, 95% female) are feasibility signals, not comparative proof.
- **Severe pre-existing bone loss forcing palatal placement *beyond* the studied protocol is not directly evidenced.** Every palatal-positioning paper here (Evans 2008, Cosyn 2026, Staas 2026) studies a *deliberate, modest* offset (≤2–4 mm) applied to an otherwise adequate ridge — none studies the rescue scenario where severe pre-existing labial/buccal bone loss forces a substantially deeper palatal shift. In that scenario, prognosis likely depends more on remaining labial bone thickness (Yang 2019) and final prosthetic emergence-angle design (Wang 2022) than on the position decision itself, but no paper in this synthesis tests the extreme-shift scenario head-to-head against staged augmentation (e.g., GBR then ideally-positioned implant) — a real decision this wiki cannot yet adjudicate.
- **Volumetric outcome reporting is rare and underused.** Chan 2019 and Wang 2020 (same cohort) demonstrate that linear metrics and volumetric metrics on identical patients can yield opposite conclusions. The broader evidence base relies almost entirely on 2D/linear measures, meaning clinically relevant contour-preservation benefits of interventions (provisionalization, CTG) may be systematically underestimated in pooled analyses. Future reviews should require volumetric outcome reporting as a pre-specified endpoint.
- **CTG decoupling is under-explored.** Jiang 2020 shows CTG reduces buccal tissue collapse but not mid-facial gingival margin migration — a distinction absent from most CTG trials. This means "volume" and "margin level" should be tracked as separate outcomes, not assumed to move together. No trial has prospectively powered on both endpoints simultaneously.
- **DBBM+collagen as a socket graft adjunct.** Girlanda 2019 is a single center, n=22, 6-month study — promising but insufficient for clinical adoption as a preferred material. Larger RCTs with ≥12-month follow-up and PES endpoints are lacking.
