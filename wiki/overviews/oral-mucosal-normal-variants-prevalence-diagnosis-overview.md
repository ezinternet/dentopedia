> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **핵심 명제**: 구강점막 정상 변이 병소(포다이스 그래뉼, 교흔/morsicatio, 마찰성 각화증, 담배쌈지각화증)는 흔하고(외래 환자 3.9–14%) 대부분 양성이지만, OLP·OPMD와 임상적으로 혼동되어 불필요한 조직검사·스테로이드 치료로 이어지는 주요 함정이 있다.
> - **유병률 스펙트럼**: 대규모 네팔 연구(n=16,572)에서 정상 변이 전체 13.96%; 포다이스 그래뉼 8.84%, 마찰성 각화증+교흔 우세. 인도 연구(n=144,056)에서 각화성 백색 병소 3.89% — 마찰성 각화증 71.65% > 담배쌈지각화증 27.38% > morsicatio 0.9%.
> - **성별·부위**: 3가지 각화성 병소 모두 남성 우세(p=0.01); 정상 변이의 80%가 협점막(buccal mucosa) 집중.
> - **최대 함정**: morsicatio ↔ OLP 혼동 — 2년 오진 증례(baklouti-2023); 더모스코피(구강경)로 Wickham striae 부재 확인 시 비침습적으로 감별 가능.
> - **감별 핵심**: 더모스코피에서 백황색 무구조 영역 + 느슨한 인설 + Wickham striae 없음 → morsicatio/마찰성 병소. 레이스 모양 Wickham striae → OLP 방향.
> - **처치 원칙**: 원인 제거 우선(불량 수복물·날카로운 치아 조정, 담배 습관 중단, 볼 물기 행동 교정) → 불응 시 조직검사. 정상 변이를 조직검사 없이 경과 관찰 후 해소 확인이 표준.
> - **포다이스 그래뉼** 유병률은 연구 간 1.2–82.8%로 극변동 — 단일 글로벌 기준치보다 병소 패턴 인식이 임상적으로 더 중요.
> - **골성 변이 축 추가 (2026-07-17, 점막 전용 → 점막+골성)**: 구강 융기(torus)·치조 외골증은 다른 오버뷰에 갈 곳이 없고, 무엇보다 **이 페이지의 두 명제가 뼈에서 그대로 반복**되어 함께 싣는다. ① 유병률 극변동 — Disha 2024(알바니아 파일럿 n=122) 융기 **48.4%**(하악 39%·치조 외골증 16%·구개 14%) vs 기존 보고 2.1–38%, 정점은 **18–29세**. 즉 가장 많이 인용되는 두 수치가 가장 크게 흔들린다(포다이스 1.2–82.8%, 융기 2.1–48.4%). ② **오진 함정의 구조 동일** — Ravi 2024(증례 1): 고령 무치악자의 설측 하악 골성 융기는 **MRONJ·골방사선괴사**로 읽히기 쉬운데, 감별한 것은 영상도 조직검사도 아닌 **비스포스포네이트·방사선 병력 청취**였다. Baklouti는 양성을 질환으로 읽어 2년 과잉치료, Ravi는 과잉검사 위험 — 양쪽 모두 **결정적 단계가 싸고 비침습적이며 바쁠 때 건너뛰기 쉬운 것**이다. 규칙 한 줄: *정상 변이는 조직검사가 아니라 조직검사 직전 단계에서 식별된다.*
> - **융기는 이갈이 근거가 아니다**: Disha 2024에서 성별·**이갈이 모두 무관**. 융기를 보고 파라펑션을 추정하거나 스플린트를 정당화하지 말 것. 처치도 보철 안착·궤양 반복·임플란트 계획이 요구할 때만 — 융기 자체는 적응증이 아니다. (단 n=122 단일기관 파일럿)
> - **임상 시사점**: 정상 변이 병소는 잠재악성질환(OPMD)이 아니므로 암 공포를 유발하지 않도록 환자 설명이 중요; 단, 담배쌈지각화증은 무연담배 노출의 OPMD 전환 위험 마커로 기억할 것.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 5 studies (total screening exposure n>160,000) on benign oral normal variants, **mucosal and bony** — Fordyce granules, linea alba/morsicatio, frictional keratosis, tobacco pouch keratosis, and oral tori/alveolar exostoses.

Prevalence: ~14% of dental outpatients have at least one mucosal normal variant (80% buccal, male-predominant), while oral tori ran to 48.4% in one Albanian pilot against 2.1–38% in prior reports — the two most-quoted figures (Fordyce 1.2–82.8%, tori 2.1–48.4%) are the two that vary most, so pattern recognition beats a memorized number; the clinically critical risk is a benign variant read as disease — morsicatio misdiagnosed as OLP and given topical steroids for 2 years (resolved by dermoscopy: absence of Wickham striae), and, in bone, an exostosis resembling MRONJ/osteoradionecrosis (resolved by drug/radiotherapy history alone).

Management is cause-elimination first and biopsy only if unresponsive; for bony variants there is no cause to eliminate — the history *is* the test, and treatment is warranted only when a prosthesis, ulceration, or implant plan requires it (tori showed **no bruxism association**); tobacco pouch keratosis is the one entity that warrants vigilance as a smokeless-tobacco exposure marker for OPMD risk.

## 세줄요약

5편 연구(총 스크리닝 n>160,000) — 포다이스 그래뉼, 교흔(linea alba/morsicatio), 마찰성 각화증(frictional keratosis), 담배쌈지각화증(tobacco pouch keratosis)에 더해 **골성 변이(구개·하악 융기, 치조 외골증)**까지 포함한 양성 구강 정상 변이 종합.

점막 변이 유병률 약 14%(치과 외래, 협점막 80%·남성 우세); 구강 융기는 알바니아 파일럿에서 48.4%로 기존 보고 2.1–38%를 크게 상회 — **가장 많이 인용되는 두 수치(포다이스 1.2–82.8%, 융기 2.1–48.4%)가 가장 크게 요동치므로 외국 유병률을 진료실로 들고 오지 말 것**; 임상적 함정은 양성 변이를 질환으로 읽는 것 — morsicatio의 OLP 오진(2년간 스테로이드; 더모스코피 Wickham striae 부재로 감별)과, 뼈에서는 외골증의 MRONJ/골방사선괴사 오인(**약물·방사선 병력 청취만으로 감별**).

처치 원칙: 점막은 원인 제거 우선·불응 시 조직검사; **골성 변이는 제거할 원인이 없으므로 병력 청취가 곧 검사**이고, 보철·궤양·임플란트 계획이 요구할 때만 절제(융기는 **이갈이와 무관** — 스플린트 근거로 쓰지 말 것); 담배쌈지각화증만 무연담배 노출의 OPMD 마커로 예외적 주의.

## Summary

Benign white keratotic and sebaceous normal variants of the oral mucosa are collectively common — present in roughly 14% of dental outpatients in large population-based records (Gupta 2023, Nepal, n=16,572; Nautiyal 2024, India, n=144,056 screened). Despite their benign nature, they generate clinical risk through misdiagnosis: the most dangerous confusion is **morsicatio (교흔) ↔ oral lichen planus (OLP)**, documented in a case where incorrect OLP treatment with topical steroids continued for 2 years (Baklouti 2023). Dermoscopy (mucoscopy) resolves this non-invasively.

**Scope note (2026-07-17):** this page originally covered only *mucosal* (soft-tissue) variants; it now also covers the **bony** normal variants — torus palatinus/mandibularis and alveolar exostoses. They are held together because they are one clinical unit and because the page's two central lessons **repeat in the bone**, from entirely separate literature: prevalence swings wildly between populations (Fordyce 1.2–82.8%; tori 2.1–48.4%), so pattern recognition beats a memorized number; and the real risk is a benign variant being mistaken for something serious (morsicatio → OLP → 2 years of steroids; exostosis → MRONJ/osteoradionecrosis), resolved by a non-invasive step rather than a biopsy. The file name still says "mucosal" for link stability.

This synthesis covers six entities across four axes: (1) prevalence and demography, (2) the misdiagnosis pitfall, (3) management algorithm, and (4) bony variants.

## Axis 1 — Prevalence and Demography

| Entity | Prevalence (context) | Key demographic |
|---|---|---|
| Fordyce granules (포다이스 그래뉼) | 8.84% overall (Nepal); ranges 1.2–82.8% globally | Peak in 11–20y; normal variant, no treatment needed |
| Linea alba / morsicatio | Subsumed under frictional keratosis; morsicatio 0.9% of keratotic whites (India) | Male predominant; often unaware of habit |
| Frictional keratosis (마찰성 각화증) | 71.65% of keratotic white lesions; 3.89% of all patients (India) | Male, mean age 26.3y |
| Tobacco pouch keratosis (담배쌈지각화증) | 27.38% of keratotic whites (India) | Male, mean age 44.5y; cumulative habit marker |
| **Oral tori (구개·하악 융기)** | **48.4% overall** (Albania pilot, n=122): torus mandibularis 39%, alveolar exostosis 16%, torus palatinus 14% — vs 2.1–38% in prior reports | Peak **18–29y** (54.2% of cases); **no gender association**; **no bruxism association** |

Across the three mucosal studies, **80% of normal variants were located on the buccal mucosa**, and all keratotic entities showed male predominance (chi-square p=0.01 in Nautiyal 2024). Global Fordyce granule prevalence varies by 60-fold across populations — pattern recognition is more reliable than a memorized prevalence number.

The bony variants repeat that lesson independently. [[oral-medicine/disha-2024-oral-tori-adult-albanian-population]] (cross-sectional pilot, n=122, Tirana) found **48.4% overall tori prevalence** — markedly above the 2.1–38% span of prior population reports — with the peak in the **18–29** age group. So the two most-quoted prevalence figures on this page (Fordyce, tori) are the two that vary most between populations: **a prevalence number from another country is not a prior you should carry to your chair.** Two of Disha's negative findings are worth more than the headline: **no association with gender** and, notably, **no association with bruxism** — the folk link between tori and clenching is not supported here, so a torus is not evidence of parafunction and should not be used to argue a patient into a splint. Caveats are real: single-centre, n=122, and incomplete vitamin D/calcium self-report, so 48.4% is a signal that populations differ, not a new reference value.

## Axis 2 — The OLP Misdiagnosis Pitfall

Morsicatio labiorum/buccarum is the entity most likely to be misdiagnosed as OLP, because both present as chronic white plaques on the buccal mucosa or lips. Baklouti 2023 reports a 2-year diagnostic error: the patient received topical steroids for "OLP" with no improvement before dermoscopy provided the correct diagnosis.

**Dermoscopy (mucoscopy) differentiates:**

| Feature | Morsicatio / frictional | OLP |
|---|---|---|
| Surface | Structureless whitish/yellowish areas | Reticular / lace pattern |
| Wickham striae | **ABSENT** ← key | **PRESENT** (pathognomonic) |
| Scale | Loose, easily removable | Adherent, not removable |
| Vessels | Dotted-vessel erosions possible | Linear/branching on background |

Key behavioral clue: patients with morsicatio are often **unaware of their habitual biting** — anxious temperament, particularly at night. Direct questioning about parafunctional habits should be routine.

Frictional keratosis from sharp tooth/restoration cusps can similarly be confused with early leukoplakia (OPMD). Management test: eliminate the irritant → if the lesion resolves within 2–4 weeks, it was frictional. Persistent lesions warrant biopsy.

**The same trap exists in bone, and it is the more dangerous one.** [[oral-medicine/ravi-2024-mandibular-bony-exostoses-or-hyperostosis]] (case report, 70-year-old edentulous male) describes a routine-checkup finding: a brownish-yellow, **bony-hard, non-tender, sessile** protuberance (~1×1.5 cm) at the lingual left mandibular second-molar region, with an orthopantomogram showing a radiopaque mass **blending with adjacent cortical bone**. Exposed hard tissue in the posterior lingual mandible of an elderly patient is exactly the presentation that reads as **medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ)** or **osteoradionecrosis** — and both of those carry an entirely different workup, prognosis, and conversation. What separated them here was not imaging and not histology: it was a **careful drug and radiotherapy history** (no bisphosphonate, no radiation), plus the clinical character of the lesion. Diagnosis was made on history and examination alone; no CBCT, no biopsy, no treatment.

Structurally this is the morsicatio↔OLP pitfall again, with the polarity of the error reversed and the stakes raised. Baklouti's patient was **over-treated** for two years because a benign variant was read as disease. Ravi's would be over-*investigated* — and, if the history were skipped, potentially worked up for osteonecrosis. In both, the discriminating step is **cheap, non-invasive, and easy to skip under time pressure**: dermoscopy for the mucosa, the drug/radiation history for the bone. Read the two together and the page's rule is one sentence: *the normal variant is identified by the step you take before the biopsy, not by the biopsy.* Honest limits on Ravi: n=1, and the authors themselves note the absence of confirmatory imaging or histopathology — it demonstrates that the history **can** settle the differential in a clear-cut presentation, not that imaging is dispensable when the presentation is not clear-cut.

## Axis 3 — Management Algorithm

```
White oral lesion observed
        │
        ├─ Obvious irritant (sharp tooth/restoration, biting habit, smokeless tobacco)?
        │   YES → eliminate cause + 2-4 week review
        │           ├─ Resolved → frictional/behavioral → no biopsy, reassure
        │           └─ Persistent → biopsy
        │
        └─ No clear irritant / Wickham-striae-like pattern?
            → dermoscopy first (if available)
                ├─ Wickham striae present → OLP workup
                └─ Structureless, no striae → morsicatio/frictional → habit counseling
```

**Tobacco pouch keratosis** is the one entity requiring heightened vigilance: it marks chronic smokeless-tobacco exposure, which is an OPMD risk factor. Unlike pure frictional keratosis, its resolution may be incomplete if the tobacco habit is entrenched, and it carries a non-negligible risk of progressing to erythroleukoplakia if the habit continues.

### Axis 4 — Bony variants: the parallel algorithm

Bony normal variants (torus palatinus, torus mandibularis, alveolar exostoses) need a different first question than white lesions, because the analogue of "eliminate the irritant" does not exist — you cannot make a torus resolve, and you should not try.

```
Bony-hard swelling observed
        │
        ├─ Is it bony-hard, non-tender, sessile, covered by intact mucosa,
        │  and radiopaque blending with adjacent cortex?
        │       │
        │       └─ YES → ask the question that actually decides it:
        │               ANY bisphosphonate / antiresorptive / head-neck radiotherapy history?
        │                   ├─ NO  → benign torus/exostosis → NO treatment, reassure, document
        │                   └─ YES → treat as MRONJ / osteoradionecrosis workup, refer
        │
        └─ Exposed bone, tenderness, suppuration, non-healing socket, mucosal breakdown?
                → NOT a normal variant → osteonecrosis workup regardless of history
```

**Treatment is removal only when something else requires it** — denture seating, prosthesis path of insertion, recurrent traumatic ulceration over a prominence, or interference with an implant plan. A torus is not an indication in itself. And per Axis 1, its presence says nothing about bruxism (Disha 2024: no association), so it is not a reason to raise parafunction with the patient.

## Clinical Takeaways

1. **Fordyce granules, linea alba, morsicatio, frictional keratosis** — inform patients these are benign. Avoid the word "lesion" if possible; say "normal variation."
2. **When OLP is suspected but unresponsive to topical steroids**, consider morsicatio in the differential and ask about biting habits — or use dermoscopy to look for Wickham striae.
3. **Biopsy is not routinely needed** for classic presentations; reserve it for lesions unresponsive to cause-elimination within 2–4 weeks.
4. **Tobacco pouch keratosis** in a smokeless-tobacco user is a behavioral marker; cessation counseling is primary management, with biopsy if white areas persist post-cessation.
5. **For a bony-hard swelling, the drug and radiotherapy history is the diagnostic test** (Ravi 2024). A benign exostosis and early MRONJ/osteoradionecrosis can look similar in the posterior lingual mandible of an older patient; asking about bisphosphonates/antiresorptives and head-neck radiation costs nothing and settles the clear-cut cases without imaging or biopsy. Exposed bone, tenderness, or suppuration takes it out of "normal variant" regardless of the history.
6. **A torus is not evidence of bruxism** (Disha 2024: no association) — do not use it to justify a splint. It also needs no treatment unless a prosthesis, ulceration, or implant plan requires it.
7. **Do not carry a prevalence number across borders.** The two most-quoted figures here vary most: Fordyce 1.2–82.8%, tori 2.1–48.4%. Recognize the pattern; do not calibrate suspicion to a statistic from another population.
