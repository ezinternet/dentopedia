## Three-line Summary

Hyperdense jaw lesions are not a uniform contraindication for implant placement: a narrative review of 19 cases (Taghsimi 2022) reports an 84.2% overall success rate, with condensing osteitis, idiopathic osteosclerosis, osteoid osteoma, odontoma, and cementoblastoma showing no major complications when treated with lesion-appropriate protocols. Cemento-osseous dysplasia (especially florid COD) carries meaningful failure risk through avascular dysplastic bone and drilling-induced overheating, while osteoblastoma is effectively contraindicated. For condensing osteitis specifically, two contrasting protocols exist: GBR + 6-month delayed implant (Alqahtani 2020) and single-visit immediate basal implant using the sclerotic CO bone as an osseofixation substrate (Hassan 2023).

## 세줄요약

과방사성 악골 병변은 임플란트 금기가 아니다 — 19증례 분석(Taghsimi 2022)에서 전체 성공률 84.2%이며, 치밀성 골수염·특발성 골경화증·골양골종·치아종·백악질모세포종은 적절한 프로토콜 사용 시 합병증 없이 성공 가능하다. 시멘토골성 이형성증(특히 광범위형)은 무혈관 이형성 조직과 드릴링 과열이 실패 기전으로 작용하여 내병소 식립이 고위험이고, 골모세포종은 전절제 필요로 금기다. 치밀성 골수염 부위 식립에는 두 프로토콜이 존재한다 — GBR+6개월 지연(Alqahtani 2020) vs 경화골을 피질골 고정 기질로 활용한 즉시 기저임플란트(Hassan 2023).

## 1. Background and Scope

Hyperdense (radiopaque) jaw lesions discovered incidentally on pre-implant imaging present a recurring clinical dilemma: can the planned implant site be used, and if so, what modifications are needed? These lesions range from reactive sclerosis (condensing osteitis, idiopathic osteosclerosis) to dysplastic tissue (cemento-osseous dysplasia) to true neoplasms (osteoid osteoma, osteoblastoma). Their density, vascularity, and biological behavior differ fundamentally, and conflating them leads to either unnecessary surgical delay or preventable failure.

This overview synthesizes the available evidence across four wiki papers — one narrative review, one broader systematic-style review, and two case reports — representing virtually all of the indexed English literature on this topic as of 2022–2023.

**Evidence base**: All available evidence is case reports/series; no RCTs exist. Conclusions must be interpreted accordingly.

## 2. Lesion Taxonomy and Risk Stratification

### 2.1 Safe for Implantation (with protocol adjustments)

| Lesion | Mechanism | Protocol adjustment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 치밀성 골수염 (Condensing Osteitis, CO) | Reactive sclerosis from chronic low-grade pulpal infection; becomes inert after extraction | Confirm lesion stability; choose GBR+delay or immediate basal approach based on anatomy | Alqahtani 2020, Hassan 2023 |
| 특발성 골경화증 (Idiopathic Osteosclerosis, IO) | Hyperdense bone island, no inflammatory source | Adjust implant length/angulation for small apical lesions; remove large coronal lesions first | Taghsimi 2022, Kim 2026 |
| 골양골종 (Osteoid Osteoma, OO) | Benign osteoblastic lesion; low recurrence | Standard placement with monitoring | Taghsimi 2022 (2 cases, stable 7 yr) |
| 치아종 (Odontoma) | Hamartoma; no malignant potential | Remove lesion; immediate or delayed placement both effective | Taghsimi 2022 (5 cases, all success) |
| 백악질모세포종 (Cementoblastoma) | Benign; requires tooth + lesion removal | Complete resection first; then immediate or delayed | Taghsimi 2022 (2 cases); Kim 2026 (success after resection) |

### 2.2 High-Risk (proceed with caution / specialist involvement)

| Lesion | Failure mechanism | Evidence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 시멘토골성 이형성증 (Cemento-Osseous Dysplasia, COD) — localized | Avascular dysplastic tissue, drilling heat, infection | Kim 2026: 100% survival for localized COD | Possible; minimize drilling heat; FCOD 3-stage protocol; antibiotic cover |
| 광범위 시멘토골성 이형성증 (Florid Cemento-Osseous Dysplasia, FCOD) | Worse vascularity; larger avascular zones | Kim 2026: 66.7% survival; Taghsimi 2022: all 3 failures in FCOD | High risk; intralesional placement contraindicated; peri-lesional if unavoidable |

### 2.3 Contraindicated

| Lesion | Reason |
|---|---|
| 골모세포종 (Osteoblastoma) | Aggressive growth, high recurrence; standard of care = en bloc resection; zero implant cases in literature |
| 과백악질증 잔존조직 부위 (Hypercementosis post-extraction) | Residual tissue in socket → failure; Kim 2026 |

## 3. Condensing Osteitis (CO): Two Protocols Compared

CO is the most clinically common of these lesions and the one with the most protocol documentation. The two indexed case reports describe opposite ends of the staging spectrum.

### 3.1 Protocol A: GBR + Delayed Implant (Alqahtani 2020)

- **Indication**: Large CO; anatomy allows GBR; patient accepts 6-month wait
- **Sequence**: Atraumatic extraction → immediate GBR at socket → 6-month healing → implant placement
- **Rationale**: Sclerotic bone is poorly vascularized; GBR reshapes socket biology toward normal trabecular bone before implant; reduces thermal necrosis risk
- **Outcome**: Successful osseointegration (case report; No. 45 mandibular premolar)
- **Limitation**: Details not available (J Oral Implantol, abstract only); follow-up duration not stated

### 3.2 Protocol B: Immediate Basal Implant (Hassan 2023)

- **Indication**: Large CO (13.82 × 9.91 mm) + buccal plate defect + low crestal bone density; patient demands single-visit rehabilitation
- **Mechanism**: KOC Plus basal implant (Simpladent, India) — bicortical apical screws engage the CO sclerotic bone as osseofixation substrate; conventional crestal osseointegration is bypassed
- **Sequence**: Same-visit extraction → immediate basal implant at 80 N/cm → immediate loading
- **Advantage over GBR protocol**: No 5–6 month delay; CO's high density is an asset rather than a liability for cortical fixation
- **Outcome**: No mobility at follow-up; fixed prosthesis functionally satisfactory
- **Limitation**: Follow-up duration not stated; no ISQ, marginal bone level, or radiographic bone loss data

### 3.3 Protocol Selection Heuristic

```
CO site planning:
├── Buccal plate intact + adequate crestal bone?
│   ├── YES → Protocol A (GBR + 6-month delay) or conventional delayed implant
│   └── NO (buccal plate defect / insufficient crestal bone)
│       ├── Patient accepts 5–6 month GBR wait → Protocol A
│       └── Patient requires immediate rehabilitation → Protocol B (basal implant)
│           └── Requires: adequate CO bone depth for bicortical engagement
```

*Note*: Protocol B evidence is limited to a single case. Conventional surgeons unfamiliar with basal implant systems should default to Protocol A or refer.

## 4. Thermal Risk in Sclerotic Bone

CO and IO bone approaches D1 cortical density. Dense bone is the highest-risk category for drilling-induced thermal osteonecrosis — the 47°C / 1-minute threshold is more easily exceeded in hyperdense sites with poor vascular dissipation.

Key rules for hyperdense jaw lesion implant osteotomy:
- **External saline irrigation mandatory** — skipping irrigation in D1-equivalent bone reliably exceeds 47°C (see [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/balkanlioglu-2026-osteotomy-depth-diameter-d1-cadcam-temperature]])
- **Sequential drill progression** — never skip steps; larger drills in dense bone generate disproportionate heat
- **Reduced RPM** (≤800 rpm in D1 bone) — logistic regression (Paessens 2025) shows speed OR 4.94 per +400 rpm for thermal critical events
- **CO lesion clearance**: even after the lesion source tooth is extracted, the sclerotic bone persists; treat the site as D1 for the entire osteotomy sequence
- Cross-reference: [[implants/osteotomy-thermal/osteotomy-thermal]] for full thermal management evidence

## 5. Pre-operative Diagnosis

Taghsimi 2022 and Kim 2026 agree: CBCT is the diagnostic minimum for hyperdense jaw lesions; panoramic and periapical radiographs undercharacterize extent and trabecular pattern.

**Minimum pre-operative workup**:
1. CBCT — measure lesion dimensions, assess buccal plate, map proximity to adjacent vital structures
2. Histopathology — mandatory where CBCT findings are ambiguous or when clinical features suggest neoplasm (OO, osteoblastoma)
3. Pulp vitality of adjacent teeth — rule out CO secondary to adjacent tooth pathology that may persist post-extraction

**CO differential diagnosis** (Hassan 2023 case differential list):
- Condensing osteitis — reactive periapical sclerosis; associated with non-vital tooth; resolves/stabilizes post-extraction
- Idiopathic osteosclerosis — no inflammatory source; dense bone island; asymptomatic
- Cementoblastoma — attached to root; requires root + lesion removal together

## 6. COD Failure Mechanisms and 3-Stage Protocol

Cemento-osseous dysplasia (especially FCOD) is the primary source of failures in the literature. Understanding why helps avoid errors in other hyperdense lesions.

**Failure pathway**:
1. Dysplastic COD tissue lacks normal vascular supply
2. Drilling generates heat → perilesional thermal necrosis
3. Necrotic avascular tissue cannot support osseointegration
4. Infection, sequestrum formation, implant failure

**FCOD 3-stage protocol (Taghsimi 2022)**:
- Stage 1: Drilling + betadine irrigation + hermetic mucosal closure + antibiotics (no implant)
- Stage 2 (3 weeks later, proliferative phase): Re-entry + implant placement
- Stage 3 (3 months after implant): Healing abutment connection

**Intralesional vs. peri-lesional**: Multiple authors favor placing the implant in peri-lesional normal bone rather than within the COD lesion itself; intralesional placement correlates with higher failure (Kim 2026).

## 8. Gaps and Future Directions

- No prospective studies or RCTs on any hyperdense jaw lesion + implant combination
- Protocol B (immediate basal implant in CO) has single-case evidence; needs replication
- Long-term follow-up data (≥5 years) absent for all case reports in this review
- Standardized outcome reporting (ISQ, marginal bone level, prosthetic success rate) absent across all cases
- FCOD 3-stage protocol needs RCT-level validation
- CO lesion size threshold for Protocol A vs B not established
