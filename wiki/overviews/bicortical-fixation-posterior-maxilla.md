## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **정의·기전**: 쌍겹질 고정 (Bicortical Fixation, BF)은 임플란트가 치조정 피질골 + 상악동저 피질골 **양쪽**을 교합해 이중 지지점을 확보하는 술식. 단겹질 고정 (Unicortical Fixation, UF)은 치조정 피질골만 교합. 핵심 기전: 상악동저 피질골이 치조정 피질골의 응력을 분담 → 치조정 응력 감소, 즉시 하중 시 변위 감소 (Yan 2015 유한요소분석 (FEA)).
> - **ISQ 역설 (핵심 해석 키)**: 쌍겹질 고정은 *축방향* 공명주파수를 선형적으로 올리지만, Osstell이 측정하는 *협설방향* 공명주파수(= 임플란트 안정성 지수 (Implant Stability Quotient, ISQ) 방향)는 상악동저 피질골 접촉에 **유의미하게 반응하지 않는다** (Yan 2015). → ISQ 단독으로 쌍겹질 이점을 평가하면 과소평가 가능; 삽입토크 (Insertion Torque, IT)가 더 민감한 쌍겹질 지표 (Lombardi 2024 임상 확인).
> - **In vitro 핵심**: 폴리우레탄 모델에서 잔존골고 (Residual Bone Height, RBH) 3–12 mm 전 구간에서 쌍겹질 ISQ > 단겹질 (p<0.001); B8군 ISQ 81 (최고); **삽입토크 역설**: M3군 삽입토크 71.8 Ncm에도 ISQ 51 — "빡빡한 삽입 = 안정성" 등식 성립 불가 (Han 2016). 피질골 품질(골무기질밀도 (BMD) >1.0 g/cm³, 침투 ≥2 mm)이 총 골두께보다 안정성 결정 (Rues 2020, p=0.827 vs p≈0.07).
> - **준비방식 의존성**: 표준 드릴 프로토콜에서만 쌍겹질 ISQ 이점 (p=0.039); **오스테오톰 (Osteotome)으로 달성한 쌍겹질은 ISQ 이득 없음** — 오스테오톰이 협설방향 골 압박을 변형시켜 RFA 반응성을 감소시키기 때문 (Ahn 2012). 단, 제거토크 이점은 준비방식 무관하게 유지 → 이차 안정성(골유착 품질)은 보전.
> - **FEA 계층**: 길이가 지배 변수 (6 mm > 5 > 4 > 3 mm, 모든 조건에서), 그 다음 술식 계층 = 치조정접근 상악동 거상 (Osteotome Sinus Floor Elevation, OSFE) > 쌍겹질 > 단겹질. RBH 5 mm에서 3 mm 임플란트: 쌍겹질이 단겹질 대비 취소골 변형률 **3.3–3.4배 감소** (Zheng 2025). CT 유래 모델에서 임플란트 von Mises: 단기 쌍겹질 219.63 MPa vs 45° 경사 499.50 MPa — 경사 임플란트 생역학적 최열위 (Seker 2014).
> - **이식재의 진실**: 상악동 이식재 응력 흡수 = 0.049 MPa (치조정 피질골 49.76 MPa의 1/1,000). 이식재 지지는 **하중 전달이 아니라 임플란트 길이 확보** 기전 → 짧은 쌍겹질 임플란트가 이 메커니즘을 우회해 동등·우월한 응력 분산 달성 (Seker 2014; Moriwaki 2016).
> - **RCT 2년 결과**: 쌍겹질 vs 간접 상악동거상 (Indirect Sinus Elevation, ISE) vs 단겹질 — 2년 페리오테스트 (Periotest, PTV) 차이 없음 (p=0.76); ISE가 수평 상악동 내 골형성 1.99 vs 1.47 mm (p=0.03)으로 우세하지만 안정성 결과 동등. 쌍겹질은 ISE보다 단순·비용절감·이식 불필요 (Hsu 2016 + Wolff 2022, RBH 7–11 mm).
> - **임상 생존율 계층**: 쌍겹질 100% (Ng 2018, 1–5년) vs 단겹질 6 mm **51.85%** (극적 차이); 7년 중앙값 코호트 94.2% (n=124, Malo 2014); 5년 변연골소실 1.56 mm (허용 범위); **브럭시즘 (Bruxism)**이 기계적 합병증의 61% 지배 → 교합 관리가 성공의 결정 변수.
> - **변연골소실 (PBR) 주의사항**: 쌍겹질 고정은 삽입토크 양의 예측인자이나 **변연골재형성 (Physiological Bone Remodeling, PBR)에는 독립적 영향 없음** (Lombardi 2024 다변량). 초기 PBR 지배 인자 = 점막두께 ≤2 mm; 후기 PBR 지배 = 어버트먼트 높이 ≤2 mm + 출현각 >30°. "쌍겹질 고정 = 골 보존 보장" 등식은 성립하지 않는다.
> - **임상 의사결정 요약**: ① RBH 7–11 mm → 쌍겹질 우선 (ISE보다 단순, 단겹질보다 긴 임플란트); ② RBH 3–6 mm → 6 mm 초단기 + OSFE 생역학 최적; ③ 직경 ≥5 mm 쌍겹질 시 응력 우위 극대화; ④ 술식은 **표준 드릴** 프로토콜; ⑤ 브럭시즘 환자 엄격 교합 관리; ⑥ 점막두께 ≥3 mm 확보가 골 보존에 쌍겹질 도달보다 우선.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 13 papers (3 in vitro, 4 FEA, 2 RCT, 2 prospective, 2 retrospective) on bicortical fixation — engaging both alveolar crest and sinus floor cortex — as a graft-avoidance strategy for the atrophic posterior maxilla.

Bicortical fixation reliably improves axial stability and reduces cancellous bone strain 3.3–3.4× vs unicortical (Zheng 2025 FEA), achieves IT but not ISQ superiority in clinical cohorts (Lombardi 2024), reaches 100% survival vs 51.85% unicortical at 6 mm (Ng 2018), and maintains 94.2% survival at median 7 years (Malo 2014); the ISQ benefit is preparation-dependent (standard drill only; Ahn 2012) and the FEA hierarchy ranks OSFE > bicortical > unicortical by stress distribution.

For RBH 7–11 mm, bicortical is the preferred technique over indirect sinus elevation (equivalent stability, simpler, no graft); for RBH 3–6 mm, 6 mm ultrashort + OSFE is biomechanically optimal; diameter ≥5 mm, standard drill preparation, and bruxism management are the key implementation prerequisites.

## 세줄요약

13편 종합 (체외 3편, 유한요소분석 (FEA) 4편, 무작위대조시험 (RCT) 2편, 전향 2편, 후향 2편): 쌍겹질 고정 (Bicortical Fixation) — 위축 후방 상악에서 골이식 없이 상악동저 피질골을 이차 지지점으로 활용.

쌍겹질 고정은 취소골 변형률을 3.3–3.4배 감소 (Zheng 2025 FEA); 임상에서 삽입토크 (IT)는 예측하나 임플란트 안정성 지수 (ISQ)는 예측 불가 (Lombardi 2024); 6 mm 임플란트 생존율 100% vs 단겹질 51.85% (Ng 2018); 7년 생존율 94.2% (Malo 2014); FEA 계층 = OSFE > 쌍겹질 > 단겹질.

잔존골고 (RBH) 7–11 mm에서 쌍겹질이 간접 상악동거상보다 단순·경제적 (동등 안정성); RBH 3–6 mm에서는 6 mm 초단기 + OSFE 최적; 직경 ≥5 mm, 표준 드릴, 브럭시즘 관리 필수.

## Definition & Mechanism

**Bicortical fixation (쌍겹질 고정)** is the intentional engagement of an implant apex with the sinus floor cortex in addition to the alveolar crest cortex, creating two cortical anchorage points. This contrasts with **unicortical fixation (단겹질 고정)**, where only the crestal cortex is engaged. A related variant uses the nasal floor cortex as the second anchor (Malo 2014).

The mechanistic basis is established by Yan 2015's FEA: bicortical implantation shifts stress concentration from the crestal cortex to the sinus floor cortex (von Mises: 58.69 MPa crestal vs 73.44 MPa sinus floor under immediate loading), reduces implant displacement (most dramatically under immediate loading), and increases axial resonance frequency linearly with apex penetration depth. The critical mechanistic insight is that buccolingual resonance frequency — the direction Osstell's Smartpeg predominantly measures, yielding the ISQ reading — is **not** significantly affected by sinus floor cortex engagement. This explains the clinical paradox where bicortical fixation predicts IT but not ISQ in multivariate analysis (Lombardi 2024).

Bicortical fixation is achievable when implant length exceeds RBH. When implant length equals RBH, the apex merely contacts the sinus floor; when length is less than RBH, only unicortical fixation results (Zheng 2025, Han 2016).

## In Vitro Evidence

### Stability Metrics and Cortical Condition

Han 2016 provides the most systematic in vitro map of bicortical vs monocortical ISQ across RBH levels (3, 5, 8, 12 mm) using polyurethane foam blocks (n=80). Bicortical ISQ was significantly higher than monocortical at all RBH levels (p<0.001), with peak stability at B8 (ISQ 81.03 ± 2.69). The B12 group failed to achieve bicortical fixation because a 10 mm implant could not reach across 12 mm of bone — confirming the geometric requirement that implant length must exceed RBH.

The **IT paradox** is the most clinically important in vitro finding: the monocortical M3 group had paradoxically high IT (71.78 Ncm) due to the tapered implant body engaging the apical cortex, yet showed the lowest ISQ (51.20 ± 6.67). IT–ISQ correlation was only r=0.58 (Han 2016; confirmed r=0.529 in Ahn 2012), insufficient to use IT as a proxy for ISQ-based loading decisions. ISQ–PTV correlation was much higher (r=−0.97), confirming these two metrics are interchangeable.

### Cortical Quality Over Bone Height

Rues 2020 (pig scapula, n=17, torsional micromotion) demonstrates that **total bone thickness is uncorrelated with primary stability** (p=0.827). The critical determinants are BMD (p=0.095 trend) and cortical engagement length (p=0.071 trend). Implants with cortical engagement ≥2 mm and BMD >1.0 g/cm³ achieved minimum micromotion (~4 μm). Five specimens loosened at <15 Ncm — all with inadequate cortical engagement or low BMD. This finding reframes RBH as a necessary but insufficient condition: cortical bone quality at the sinus floor determines bicortical primary stability, not the total vertical distance.

### Preparation Method Dependency

Ahn 2012 (2×3 factorial, polyurethane, n=60) reveals a significant cortical condition × preparation method interaction (p=0.045): bicortical ISQ advantage over monocortical exists **only with standard drill preparation** (p=0.039). With underpreparation or osteotome technique, no bicortical ISQ benefit is detected. However, removal torque (a proxy for secondary stability quality) showed bicortical advantage regardless of preparation method (p<0.001). The clinical interpretation: if osteotome-based OSFE is used to achieve bicortical apical engagement, the ISQ benefit of bicortical fixation is lost — but osseointegration quality (removal torque) may still benefit.

## FEA Evidence

### Mechanistic Basis: Axial vs Buccolingual Resonance

Yan 2015 (10 FEA models, 4×10 mm Nobel Biocare, 129 N at 30°) is the foundational mechanistic study. Key findings:
- Sinus floor cortex engagement shifts stress FROM crestal cortex TO sinus floor cortex
- Axial resonance frequency increases **linearly** with apex penetration depth (dose-response)
- Buccolingual resonance frequency (ISQ direction) shows **no significant change** with sinus floor contact
- Implant displacement is significantly smaller with bicortical engagement, most dramatically under immediate loading (frictional contact μ=0.3)
- Sinus floor cortex (0.5–2.0 mm thickness range) absorbs stress without meaningfully changing crestal cortex loading beyond the threshold engagement

This FEA reconciles the apparent conflict between in vitro ISQ advantages (Han 2016, Bhave 2023) and clinical ISQ non-significance (Hsu 2016, Lombardi 2024): ISQ captures only buccolingual stability, which is insensitive to sinus floor anchorage. The true bicortical advantage is axial stabilization, best reflected in IT and removal torque.

### Ultrashort Implants: Length Dominates, OSFE Leads

Zheng 2025 (14 FEA models, 4.1 mm diameter, 3–6 mm lengths × unicortical/bicortical/OSFE, RBH 3–5 mm) establishes the biomechanical hierarchy for severely atrophic bone. Implant length is the dominant variable — 6 mm consistently outperforms shorter lengths regardless of approach. Within a given length:

| Approach | Cancellous strain (3 mm implant, RBH=5 mm) | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Unicortical | ~23,000 (reference) | Highest |
| Bicortical | ~7,000 | 3.3–3.4× reduction |
| OSFE + 6 mm | Lowest (~35.77 MPa principal) | Best overall |

OSFE introduces 0.5 mm of new bone around the apex after sinus floor elevation, further distributing load. All models satisfied the 150 μm displacement threshold for osseointegration safety. For RBH 3–5 mm, 6 mm + OSFE is the biomechanically optimal approach; bicortical is superior to unicortical but does not match OSFE with appropriate implant length.

### Diameter Dependency and Comparison with Sinus Augmentation

Moriwaki 2016 (FEA, bone quantity A and C) adds the diameter dimension: a 4 mm diameter short implant with bicortical anchorage in bone quantity A shows the **greatest** stress (worst outcome). In atrophic bone (bone quantity C), 5 mm × 6 mm + bicortical achieves substantially lower stress than 4 mm × 13 mm + sinus augmentation. The diameter upgrade (4→5 mm) is a prerequisite for the biomechanical advantage of short bicortical implants — ridge width assessment must confirm 5 mm implant feasibility before selecting this strategy.

### Bicortical vs Grafted vs Tilted FPD Comparison

Seker 2014 (CT-derived 3D FEA, atrophic posterior maxilla, 3 FPD scenarios) provides the highest anatomical fidelity. The graft absorption finding is clinically important: graft material absorbs only 0.049 MPa vs 49.76 MPa at the crestal cortex — a factor of ~1,000. Sinus graft functions by enabling a longer implant platform, not by absorbing occlusal forces. Once a bicortical short implant achieves dual cortical purchase, the graft mechanism is biomechanically redundant.

| FPD scenario | Implant von Mises | Sinus cortex stress |
|---|---|---|
| 6×5 mm + bicortical | 219.63 MPa (lowest) | 14.144 MPa |
| 11×4 mm + sinus graft | Intermediate | N/A |
| 11×5 mm + 45° tilt | 499.50 MPa (highest) | N/A |

The 45°-tilted long implant (All-on-4-style) carries 2.3× the implant stress of the bicortical short option, making it biomechanically the least favorable when alternatives exist.

## Clinical Evidence

### RCT: Three-Way Stability Comparison (Hsu 2016 + Wolff 2022)

The Hsu 2016 RCT (38 patients, 45 implants, RBH 7–11 mm) and its 2-year follow-up (Wolff 2022, n=32/37) constitute the highest-quality clinical evidence for bicortical fixation.

**Primary ISQ** (Hsu 2016): ISE 75.9 > bicortical 71.4 > unicortical 69.6, but differences did not reach significance (p=0.13). The dominant predictor of primary ISQ was CBCT bone density (r=0.37, p significant), not technique choice. **Secondary ISQ** (3–6 months): all three techniques converged to ~80 (p>0.999). Bicortical allowed longer implants than unicortical (11.4 vs 8.1 mm) without requiring graft.

**Two-year Periotest** (Wolff 2022): bicortical −1.69 vs unicortical −1.76 vs ISE −2.22 — no significant difference (p=0.76). ISE generated significantly more intrasinus horizontal bone (1.99 vs 1.47 mm, p=0.03), but this extra bone provided no stability advantage. No implant losses or peri-implantitis events over 2 years.

**Clinical conclusion from this RCT pair**: Bicortical fixation is equivalent to ISE in stability outcomes but simpler (no osteotome, no graft), more economical, and allows longer implants than unicortical alone. It is the recommended approach for RBH 7–11 mm posterior maxilla.

### Prospective: Direct Bicortical vs Unicortical Comparison

Bhave 2023 (split-mouth prospective, 15 patients, 42 implants, OSFE-based bicortical, mean RBH ~11 mm) found significantly higher ISQ at all timepoints: 3-month bicortical ISQ 71.1 ± 3.37 vs unicortical 65.7 ± 3.13 (p<0.001). Crestal bone loss was equivalent at 3 months (~0.08–0.09 mm, p>0.05), with no sinus complications. This ISQ advantage contradicts the Hsu 2016 and Lombardi 2024 findings of non-significant ISQ differences, possibly because the split-mouth within-patient design increases statistical power, the OSFE protocol in Bhave 2023 used Summer's osteotome (which Ahn 2012 shows eliminates the ISQ advantage), or the longer bicortical implants (12.11 vs 10.78 mm mean) confound the ISQ comparison.

Lombardi 2024 (multicenter, 71 short implants, mean RBH 6.2 mm) provides the most nuanced clinical dissection: in multivariate regression, bicortical engagement was a significant positive predictor of **IT** at placement (p<0.05) but showed **no significant effect on ISQ or PBR** at 5 or 12 months. The dominant PBR predictor at 5 months was mucosal thickness ≤2 mm (p<0.001), and at 12 months, short abutment height ≤2 mm and emergence angle >30° in bone-level implants. This confirms that the clinical value of bicortical fixation lies in mechanical purchase at placement (IT), not in long-term marginal bone protection.

### Long-term Retrospective: Survival and Complications

Ng 2018 (retrospective, 53 patients, 1–5 years) provides the most dramatic survival data: bicortical 100% vs unicortical overall 74.68%, with the 6-mm unicortical subgroup at only 51.85%. Bicortical implants also showed significant intrasinus bone gain on both mesial and distal aspects — absent in the unicortical group — suggesting a tenting effect from sinus floor periosteal disruption stimulating bone formation without graft material.

Malo 2014 (retrospective, 88 patients, 124 implants, median 7 years, immediate function protocol) provides the longest follow-up. Cumulative survival 94.2% (4 losses in complete-edentulous patients only; partial-edentulous 100%); prosthetic survival 100%; 5-year MBL 1.56 mm (SD 0.87). Mechanical complications occurred in 56% of patients (49/88), with bruxism dominating (30/49 = 61%). Biological complications occurred in 19% (17/88). The study validates bicortical anchorage with immediate function as clinically viable at a 7-year median but establishes bruxism as the primary modifiable risk factor for mechanical complications.

## Key Controversies

### 1. Does Bicortical Fixation Improve ISQ?

The evidence is split: Bhave 2023 (prospective split-mouth) reports significant ISQ advantage (71.1 vs 65.7, p<0.001); Hsu 2016 (RCT) and Lombardi 2024 (multivariate prospective) report no significant ISQ effect. Yan 2015 FEA resolves this mechanistically: Osstell measures buccolingual resonance frequency, which is insensitive to sinus floor cortex contact. The true bicortical benefit is **axial** stability — measurable in IT (confirmed clinically by Lombardi 2024) and removal torque (Ahn 2012), but not reliably captured by ISQ. Studies reporting ISQ advantages (Bhave 2023) likely measure a confounded signal: the bicortical group used longer implants (12.11 vs 10.78 mm), which independently increase ISQ. The resolution: **do not use ISQ as the primary metric to assess bicortical fixation benefit; use IT and axial RFA where available.**

### 2. Does the Preparation Method Nullify ISQ Benefit?

Ahn 2012 shows that osteotome-based bicortical engagement eliminates the ISQ advantage (no significant difference from monocortical). Bhave 2023 used an osteotome (Summer's) but still found ISQ advantage — possibly confounded by implant length difference. The mechanistic explanation from Ahn 2012 is that osteotome lateral compaction modifies the bone-implant interface in a way that reduces RFA responsiveness. Standard drill preparation is required to convert dual cortical anchorage into measurable ISQ benefit.

### 3. Does Bicortical Fixation Protect Marginal Bone?

Ng 2018 (retrospective) and Bhave 2023 (3-month prospective) suggest bicortical-engaged implants have less crestal bone loss vs unicortical 6-mm implants. However, Lombardi 2024 (multivariate) found no independent PBR effect for bicortical status. The apparent benefit in Ng 2018 may reflect confounding: the 6-mm unicortical group had dramatically poor outcomes (51.85% survival), making marginal bone comparison between survivors unreliable. Lombardi 2024's multivariate design controlling for mucosal thickness and abutment geometry provides the more reliable estimate: bicortical fixation itself does not independently reduce marginal bone remodeling. Mucosal management and prosthetic factors dominate PBR.

### 4. When Does OSFE Outperform Bicortical?

Zheng 2025 FEA shows OSFE with 6 mm implant outperforms bicortical fixation alone in stress distribution for RBH 3–5 mm. However, OSFE introduces 0.5 mm of new bone in the model — which may overestimate OSFE's stability if bone maturation is incomplete at loading. Ahn 2012 in vitro data suggest osteotome technique reduces ISQ vs standard preparation. The clinical trade-off: OSFE enables longer implants in very short RBH while providing membrane protection, but may sacrifice some of the ISQ advantage of the bicortical anchorage mechanism. For RBH ≥7 mm, bicortical with standard drilling is preferred (Hsu 2016). For RBH 3–6 mm, the FEA evidence favors OSFE + maximum feasible length.

## Clinical Implications

### Patient Selection

Bicortical fixation is most appropriate for atrophic posterior maxilla with RBH 7–11 mm where sinus augmentation would otherwise be planned. Key selection criteria:
- RBH sufficient for chosen implant to reach sinus floor (implant length > RBH)
- Adequate ridge width for ≥5 mm diameter implant (critical for biomechanical advantage; Moriwaki 2016)
- CBCT evaluation of sinus floor cortex quality (thickness, density) — thin or poorly mineralized sinus floor may inadequately anchor the apex (Rues 2020)
- Absence of active sinus pathology
- Bruxism: can be managed with bicortical + immediate function but requires occlusal splint and prosthetic stress-breaking (Malo 2014: 56% mechanical complications in bruxers)

### Surgical Protocol

Standard drill preparation (not osteotome) maximizes the ISQ component of bicortical fixation (Ahn 2012). The apical cortex engagement can be confirmed radiographically. Implant length selection should ensure 1–2 mm of sinus floor cortex penetration; apex barely touching the floor (length ≈ RBH) provides incomplete bicortical fixation.

For RBH 3–6 mm, 6 mm + OSFE (standard osteotome greenstick technique) is biomechanically preferred over short bicortical alone (Zheng 2025). The choice should account that osteotome reduces ISQ response to bicortical engagement (Ahn 2012).

Immediate function is feasible with bicortical anchorage (Malo 2014) — the FEA evidence shows bicortical fixation most dramatically reduces implant displacement under immediate loading conditions (Yan 2015).

### Prosthetic Considerations

Marginal bone remodeling is not independently protected by bicortical status (Lombardi 2024). Mucosal thickness ≥3 mm, abutment height ≥2 mm, and emergence angle <30° remain the dominant PBR determinants. Tissue-level implant design reduces PBR vs bone-level in short implant applications (Lombardi 2024, p=0.004).

ISQ measurement understates bicortical stability (Yan 2015 FEA). Loading decisions should weight IT, removal torque, and CBCT bone density alongside ISQ.

## Research Gaps

1. **Long-term RCT with ISQ as secondary, IT as primary stability outcome**: No published RCT uses IT or axial RFA as primary bicortical stability metric. All existing RCTs rely on ISQ (Hsu 2016, Bhave 2023), which the FEA evidence (Yan 2015) predicts is insensitive to bicortical engagement.

2. **Diameter effect in clinical cohorts**: Moriwaki 2016 FEA identifies 5 mm diameter as the threshold for bicortical advantage vs sinus augmentation. No clinical cohort has prospectively compared 4 mm vs 5 mm bicortical implants in atrophic bone.

3. **Sinus floor cortex quality assessment**: Rues 2020 identifies BMD >1.0 g/cm³ and cortical thickness as predictors, but no clinical protocol exists for CBCT-based preoperative sinus floor cortex quality scoring to predict bicortical stability outcomes.

4. **OSFE vs bicortical RCT beyond FEA**: Zheng 2025 establishes FEA hierarchy (OSFE > bicortical in RBH 3–5 mm) but no RCT has compared these approaches at identical RBH ranges with standardized implant lengths and loading protocols.

5. **Bruxism-stratified survival data**: Malo 2014 identifies bruxism as the dominant mechanical complication driver (61%), but bruxism was not an exclusion criterion. A properly powered RCT stratifying by bruxism status would quantify the true risk-benefit profile of bicortical + immediate function in parafunction patients.

6. **Longer-term marginal bone data beyond 2 years**: Hsu/Wolff provides only 2 years (pilot design, n=37). The long-term natural history of bicortical-engaged short implants in terms of crestal bone trajectory requires 5+ year RCT data.
