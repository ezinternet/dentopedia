## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **논지**: "저작효율 (masticatory efficiency/performance) 이 좋아졌다"는 주장은 **어떤 검사로 쟀는지를 말하지 않으면 의미가 없다.** 이 오버뷰는 2026-11-13 인제스트한 5편(계측 SR·PROM SR·유병률 SR+MA·임플란트 크라운 비교·지르코니아 vs 금속도재)을 축으로, 계측 이질성이 임상 인용을 어떻게 망가뜨리는지 정리한다.
> - **1차 결론 — 표준 검사가 없다.** 저작성능 측정법 SR (Khaing 2026, 40편, COSMIN/Terwee 평가) 은 검사를 네 계열(혼합능·색변화 껌, 분쇄·체가름, 구미젤리, 포도당 유리) + 설문으로 나누고, **임상적으로 검증된 표준 검사는 존재하지 않는다**고 결론한다. 껌 혼합능 계열이 신뢰도·타당도가 가장 일관되고, 분쇄 계열은 프로토콜이 이질적이다.
> - **2차 결론 — 설문지는 별개의, 더 약한 도구군이다.** 저작기능 PROM SR (Fan 2021, 19종·23편·16,886명) 에서 **모든 측정특성을 고수준 근거로 갖춘 도구는 0개**, **측정오차 (measurement error) 는 단 한 번도 검증되지 않았다.** A등급은 CFQ 크로아티아어/알바니아어판 정도.
> - **3차 결론 — 그래서 유병률이 14배 벌어진다.** 60세 이상 지역사회 165,220명 SR+MA (Franco 2025, 22편) 의 자가보고 저작장애 통합 유병률 **36% (95% CI 28–43%)** 인데 **I²=100%**, 개별 연구 4.3–61.7%. 도구가 다르면 같은 "저작장애"도 다른 것을 센다.
> - **4차 결론 — 중재 연구 수치도 합산 불가.** 임플란트 단일 크라운 vs 3-unit FPD 는 **아몬드 분쇄 + 4단 체 (Kapur–Soman MP%)** 로 쟀고 (Patil 2026, n=60, 3/6/12개월 28.4/32.1/35.7 vs 22.6/25.4/28.3, p=0.001), 지르코니아 vs 금속도재는 **색혼합 비율(%) + 저작 VAS** 로 쟀다 (Zhang 2026, 79 vs 79, 3개월 78.6% vs 70.2%). **다른 검사·다른 단위·다른 모집단**이라 두 논문의 우열을 직접 비교할 수 없다.
> - **5차 결론 — 지르코니아 문헌은 기능을 재지 않는다.** 지르코니아에서 실제로 측정되는 것은 **교합접촉의 정확도**다: 합착만으로 교합접촉면적이 2배 이상 커지고 (Wang 2024, 30관, 2339→6281 µm²·2071→5545 µm², p<0.001), AI 설계는 모델 단계 정확도는 좋지만 구내 변연간격은 332–376 µm 로 남고 교합조정은 여전히 술자가 한다 (Liu 2025). **"접촉 정확도 → 씹는 능력"을 이어주는 연구는 없다.**
> - **임상 takeaway ①**: 논문·강의에서 "저작효율 N% 향상"을 인용할 때 **검사명·단위·추적기간**을 함께 말하라. 셋이 없으면 그 숫자는 다른 연구와 비교 불가다.
> - **임상 takeaway ②**: 환자에게 설명할 때 저작기능은 **주관(VAS/설문) 과 객관(분쇄·혼합) 이 다른 축**임을 전제하라. 두 축은 같은 방향으로 움직인다는 보장이 없다 (Fan 2021 이 보여준 PROM 의 측정오차 공백이 그 이유다).
> - **임상 takeaway ③**: 재료 선택을 저작효율로 정당화하지 마라. 근거가 있는 축은 마모·생존·교합접촉 정확도이고, 저작효율을 올리는 레버는 **교합면 설계·접촉 정확도·교합양식·훈련** 쪽이다.
> - **미해결**: ① 동일 코호트에서 객관검사·PROM·교합접촉 정확도를 함께 측정한 연구가 없다 ② 검사 간 최소임상중요차 (MIC/SDC) 가 확립되지 않았다 ③ 지르코니아 크라운에서 저작효율을 1차 결과로 삼은 RCT 는 0건이다.

## Three-line Summary

The field has **no standardized, clinically validated test of masticatory performance**: a COSMIN/Terwee systematic review of 40 studies finds gum-based mixing-ability tests the most consistent but concludes no single method qualifies as the reference standard, while the PROM review finds no instrument with high-level evidence across all measurement properties and no instrument in which measurement error has ever been tested. This measurement heterogeneity is not academic — self-reported masticatory disorder prevalence ranges 4.3–61.7% across studies with I²=100%, and two recent interventional comparisons of *different* prosthodontic materials report "masticatory efficiency" in incompatible units (almond-comminution MP% vs colour-mixing % + VAS), so their results cannot be ranked against each other.

## 세줄요약

저작성능에는 **표준 검사가 없다** — 40편 계측 SR 은 껌 혼합능 계열이 가장 일관된다고 보면서도 표준 검사 부재를 결론내고, PROM 19종 SR 은 모든 측정특성을 충족하는 도구가 없으며 측정오차는 한 번도 검증된 적이 없다고 밝힌다. 이 이질성은 학술적 문제가 아니라 **유병률 4.3–61.7% (I²=100%)** 라는 결과로 드러나고, 서로 다른 보철 재료를 비교한 두 중재 연구가 **단위가 다른 "저작효율"** (아몬드 분쇄 MP% vs 색혼합 % + VAS) 을 보고하기 때문에 두 연구를 직접 비교할 수 없다.

## Summary

This page exists because a clinical question kept failing. The question was: *does surface finishing (polishing vs glazing) of a zirconia crown improve chewing efficiency?* A surveillance sweep of PubMed found the answer was not "no" but "unaskable" — of 530 candidate records pairing mastication terms with occlusal/surface terms, four mentioned glazing and all four measured **wear or roughness**, none measured function; and a dedicated zirconia sweep found only 12 papers in the entire database that pair zirconia with a masticatory-performance term.

Chasing that emptiness produced a more useful finding than the original answer would have been: **the outcome itself is unstable.** Five papers ingested on 2026-11-13 let the instability be described precisely — one systematic review of the instruments, one of the patient-reported instruments, one prevalence meta-analysis that shows the downstream cost, and two intervention studies that demonstrate it in practice.

## The instrument landscape: four families, no reference standard

[[occlusion/khaing-2026-masticatory-performance-assessment-methods-sr]] appraised 40 studies with COSMIN risk-of-bias and Terwee quality criteria (PROSPERO CRD420251165166). Two findings matter clinically:

1. **The test families are not interchangeable.** Mixing ability (two-colour chewing gum, colour-change ratio), comminution (masticated food or synthetic bolus passed through sieves, reported as particle size or as % passing a threshold), gummy-jelly-type single-item tests, and glucose-release assays measure different links in the same chain — particle reduction, bolus mixing, and soluble release respectively. A material that improves one need not improve another.
2. **Gum-based mixing-ability tests carry the most consistent reliability/validity evidence, and comminution protocols are the most heterogeneous.** Yet the review's conclusion is a negative one: no method emerges as the clinically validated standard, because the validation studies do not converge on a single criterion.

The wrinkle worth remembering: **responsiveness is declared in scope but never synthesised per family**, and the two-coloured-wax section contradicts itself on measurement error. So even the best available appraisal cannot tell you the smallest change that matters.

## The patient-reported side is a separate, weaker instrument class

[[occlusion/fan-2021-patient-reported-outcome-measures-masticatory-function]] reviewed 19 PROMs across 23 studies and 16,886 adults with COSMIN. The result is stark:

- **No instrument has high-level evidence for all measurement properties.**
- **Measurement error was never tested for any of them** — which means a clinician cannot know whether a 2-point change on a chewing questionnaire exceeds noise.
- Only the Croatian and Albanian CFQ versions reached recommendation category A; most instruments sit in B or C because structural validity or responsiveness is unestablished.

This matters because PROMS and objective tests answer different questions. A patient who reports better chewing after a new crown may be reporting comfort, appearance, or absence of food packing — none of which is comminution.

## What unstable measurement costs: 14-fold spread in prevalence

[[occlusion/franco-2025-prevalence-self-assessed-masticatory-disorders]] pooled 22 studies (21 pooled) covering 165,220 community-dwelling adults aged ≥60: **pooled prevalence 36% (95% CI 28–43%) with I² = 100%** and individual estimates from **4.3% to 61.7%**. The authors could not attribute the heterogeneity to age; it tracks the instrument — no single validated questionnaire was used across studies, and items were grouped post hoc into chewing difficulty / disability / problem / discomfort.

The clinical corollary: "one in three older adults has a masticatory disorder" is a defensible headline, and so is "one in twenty" depending on which instrument and which country. Both are in the same meta-analysis.

## What unstable measurement costs: intervention numbers that cannot be ranked

Two 2026 papers ingested the same day make the point concretely.

| Study | Comparison | Metric | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[occlusion/patil-2026-masticatory-efficiency-single-implant-crown]] | single-piece implant crown vs 3-unit metal-ceramic FPD (n=60, prospective, consecutive allocation) | almond comminution, 4-sieve dry sieving, Kapur–Soman MP% | 3/6/12 mo: **28.4 / 32.1 / 35.7** vs **22.6 / 25.4 / 28.3** MP%, all p=0.001 |
| [[prosthetic-materials/zhang-2026-zirconia-restorations-inflammatory-masticatory-function]] | zirconia vs metal-ceramic full crowns (196 → 79 vs 79, propensity-matched, retrospective) | colour-mixing sieving (% passing) + chewing VAS 0–10 | 3 mo: **78.6%** vs **70.2%** mixing; VAS 7.8 vs 6.9 |

Both papers say "masticatory efficiency improved". Neither number can be compared with the other: different bolus, different sieve set, different reporting convention (MP% vs % passing), different design strength (prospective non-randomised vs retrospective propensity-matched), different teeth replaced. A clinician reading both could reasonably conclude "implant crowns are ~7 MP% better than FPDs" and "zirconia is ~8 percentage points better than PFM" — and then wrongly add, rank, or generalise those effects.

The same caution applies to the older literature: [[removable-partial-denture/liang-2015-effects-removable-dental-prostheses-masticatory]] synthesised 8 RPD studies and **deliberately did not meta-analyse** because comminution (X50) and mixing-ability studies could not be pooled. That decision is the correct template.

## Where the zirconia question actually stops

Neither zirconia paper in this wiki measures function as a material property. What the zirconia literature measures is **whether the occlusion arrived where it was designed to be**:

- [[occlusion/wang-2024-changes-occlusal-contacts-cementation-zirconia]]: in 30 zirconia crowns, cementation alone more than doubled occlusal contact area (2339 ± 1206 → 6281 ± 3310 µm² and 2071 ± 909 → 5545 ± 3491 µm², both p<0.001) and increased contact intensity, so the occlusal check must be repeated *after* cementation.
- [[occlusion/liu-2025-accuracy-occlusal-contact-zirconia-ai-design]]: AI-generated designs were more accurate at the model level (STL RMS 13 vs 45 µm) with model margin gaps of 57–92 µm, but intra-oral margin gaps were 332–376 µm vs 276–341 µm and chairside occlusal adjustment was still required by the dentist.

So the evidentiary chain is: *design/workflow → contact accuracy → (adjustment) → ?* The last arrow — contact accuracy to comminution or mixing ability — has not been studied. Any claim that a zirconia crown "chews better" because of its surface or its fit is an extrapolation across a missing link.

## Clinical implications

1. **Quote the instrument with the number.** "MP% by 4-sieve almond comminution" and "colour-mixing %" are not the same unit; a bare percentage should be treated as uninterpretable.
2. **Treat objective and subjective outcomes as two axes, not one.** With PROM measurement error unestablished (Fan 2021), a patient-reported improvement cannot be converted into a functional gain.
3. **Do not justify material choice by chewing efficiency.** For zirconia the defensible axes remain antagonist wear, survival/fracture, contact accuracy, and aesthetics. The levers for function are occlusal design, contact accuracy, occlusal scheme, and training — not the crown material.
4. **When reading a new "masticatory efficiency" paper, check three things first**: which test family, whether the change exceeds the instrument's known error (often unknown — say so), and the follow-up interval. Patil 2026 improves monotonically to 12 months; a 1-month result from another study is not comparable.
5. **Prefer within-study comparisons.** Because cross-study pooling fails, the only defensible ranking of materials on function would come from a single trial using one instrument — which, for zirconia crowns, does not exist.

## Open gaps

- **No head-to-head RCT** with masticatory performance as the primary outcome comparing zirconia with any other crown material.
- **No study linking occlusal contact accuracy to measured comminution or mixing ability** — the missing arrow in the zirconia chain.
- **No established MIC/SDC** for any masticatory performance test, and measurement error untested for every masticatory PROM.
- **No cohort measuring objective test + PROM + digital contact accuracy in the same patients**, which is the only design that could tell us how much of "better chewing" is contact, how much is material, and how much is expectation.
