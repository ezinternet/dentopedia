> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **부이관(Accessory Maxillary Ostium, AMO) ≠ 병리**: CBCT 단면연구(n=244)에서 AMO 존재는 상악동염과 무관(p=0.104); 진짜 문제는 원발 상악동공(Primary Maxillary Ostium, PMO) 폐쇄(p<0.001). 역설적 하비갑·분기형 하비갑은 오히려 정상군에 더 흔함 → 부이관 목록 작성보다 골비강복합체(Osteomeatal Complex, OMC) 개통 여부 평가가 핵심.
> - **AMO 성별 차이**: AMO 빈도는 성별에 의존(p=0.013) — 정상군에서는 여성에 많고, 상악동염군에서는 남성에 많음. 임상적 의의는 불명확하나 남성 상악동염 환자에서 AMO가 보이면 PMO 폐쇄와 동반 평가 필요.
> - **격벽(Septa)은 천공 위험을 높이나 자연공 침범은 악화시키지 않음**: 격벽군 천공 4/15(26.7%) vs 대조군 0/15; 그러나 1주 누두(Infundibulum) 폐쇄는 오히려 대조군에 많음(5/15 vs 3/15). 9개월에 폐쇄 모두 완전 소실 → 격벽이 있어도 장기 자연공 합병증은 걱정하지 않아도 됨.
> - **천공 크기가 수리 술식을 결정**: <5mm → 흡수성 콜라겐 막으로 94–99%; 5–10mm → 콜라겐+층판골 91.9%; >10mm → 협부지방대(Buccal Fat Pad) 포함 복합 재건 74.1%; >15mm → 서방형 막±혈소판농축혈장(Platelet-Rich Plasma, PRP). 예방(피에조서저리, CT 계획)이 1차 전략.
> - **Fugazzotto-Vlassis 분류(Class I–V)**: 등급이 높을수록 수리 난이도 상승·성공률 하락. 대형 천공(>10mm)은 수술 중단 후 4개월 재진입 선택지 고려.
> - **중증 치성 상악동염(OMS)의 해부학적 역설**: 원인부위는 자연공 하방 → 자연공 기반 내시경 부비동 수술(Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, ESS)은 해부학적으로 부적합하고 재폐쇄 위험. 하비도 대형 비강-상악동창(Nasoantral Window) + 점막 보존(Caldwell–Luc) + 치근단절제술(Apicoectomy)이 완전 회복 달성.
> - **보존적 상악동 관리(Conservative Maxillary Sinus Management, CMSM)의 핵심**: ① 슈나이더막 점막 보존(섬모 기능 유지 + 술후 상악동낭종(Postoperative Maxillary Cyst, POMC) 방지), ② 하비도 대형 비강-상악동창(중력 배농 + 장기 개방), ③ 동시 치근단 제거(원인 제거 + 자연치 보존).
> - **기존 대합성 분리**: [[overviews/sinus-lift-lateral-2026-synthesis]]는 격벽을 OR 4.03 천공 예측인자로, 술후 자연공 폐쇄를 일시적 생리로 다루지만 격벽군 vs 대조군 자연공 비교·AMO 분리·CMSM 술식은 미수록 → 본 페이지가 해당 세부 항목을 보완.

## Three-line Summary

Four papers add specific anatomical and procedural detail beyond the existing 37-paper lateral-SFE synthesis: (1) AMO vs PMO distinction on CBCT (n=244), (2) septa-vs-control ostium involvement time-course (n=30, 9-month CBCT), (3) size-stratified perforation repair ladder (narrative review, 7 studies, >436 implants), and (4) severe OMS refractory to antibiotics and endodontics managed with CMSM (n=1 case report).

AMO is not a sinusitis correlate (p=0.104) — PMO obstruction is (p<0.001); septa raise perforation risk (26.7% vs 0%) but paradoxically do not worsen ostium involvement from post-op edema (controls 5/15 vs septa 3/15 at 1 week, both resolved at 9 months); perforation repair success drops sharply from 94–99% (<5 mm, collagen membrane) to 74.1% (>10 mm, lamellar bone ± buccal fat pad); and severe OMS refractory to ESS is anatomically explained by the causative site lying below the natural ostium, with a large nasoantral window providing the correct drainage geometry.

Pre-SFE CBCT should prioritize PMO/OMC patency over AMO cataloguing; septa warrant modified window design but not additional concern about ostium sequelae; perforation repair should be size-matched using the Fugazzotto-Vlassis tier; and severe OMS not resolving with antibiotics + endodontics may need CMSM rather than ESS.

## 세줄요약

4편이 기존 37편 합성에 추가하는 해부·술기 세부사항: AMO vs PMO 구분(CBCT, n=244), 격벽군·대조군 자연공 경시 CBCT(n=30, 9개월), 크기별 천공 수리 성공률(서술 리뷰 7편, 임플란트 436개 이상), 항생제·근관치료 무효 중증 OMS의 CMSM 증례(n=1).

AMO는 상악동염 비연관(p=0.104)·PMO 폐쇄가 연관(p<0.001); 격벽은 천공 위험 상승(26.7% vs 0%)이나 자연공 침범 악화 없음(1주 대조군 5/15 vs 격벽군 3/15, 9개월 모두 소실); 천공 수리 성공률은 <5mm 94–99%→>10mm 74.1%로 급락; 중증 OMS는 원인부위가 자연공 하방이므로 ESS 기반 배농은 해부학적 불일치, 대형 하비도 창이 올바른 배농 경로.

부비동 리프트 전 CBCT는 PMO·OMC 개통 우선 평가; 격벽은 창 설계 수정 필요이나 자연공 합병증 추가 우려 불필요; 천공 수리는 Fugazzotto-Vlassis 크기 계층에 맞게; 항생제+근관치료로 해결 안 되는 중증 OMS는 ESS 대신 CMSM 고려.

## Summary

The [[overviews/sinus-lift-lateral-2026-synthesis]] (37 papers) established the broad landscape of lateral-window sinus floor elevation (SFE): septa as the dominant anatomical perforation risk (OR 4.03), post-operative ostium obstruction as a transient physiological event, and perforation repair as a predictor of outcome by size class. The [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]] covers OMS diagnosis, source control, and FESS sequencing.

These four papers occupy the gaps between those syntheses: they resolve the AMO-vs-PMO ambiguity on pre-operative CBCT, quantify exactly how septa modulate (and do not worsen) ostium involvement over 9 months, provide the granular repair-outcome data stratified by millimeter thresholds, and document why severe OMS refractory to conventional management has an anatomically principled alternative surgical pathway.

Together, they answer three focused clinical questions:
1. What does a CBCT finding of an accessory ostium actually mean for sinusitis risk?
2. If septa are present, should the clinician worry about post-operative sinus drainage beyond the perforation itself?
3. What repair approach is realistically expected to succeed when a Schneiderian perforation of a given size occurs, and what is the correct surgical option when OMS does not respond to antibiotics?

## Axis 1 — AMO vs PMO: Which Ostium Finding Actually Matters?

**Source**: Kashi 2023 — cross-sectional retrospective CBCT, n=244 scans (134 F / 110 M, mean age 34.16 y), sinusitis group (mucosal thickening >2 mm) vs normal (≤2 mm).

### Core finding

| Finding | Association with sinusitis | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| AMO presence | **Not significant** | 0.104 |
| PMO obstruction | **Significant** | <0.001 |
| Paradoxical concha | More frequent in **normal** group | <0.001 |
| Bifid concha | More frequent in **normal** group | 0.017 |
| Haller cell / septal deviation / concha bullosa | No significant difference | >0.05 |
| AMO ↔ any anatomical variation | No significant correlation | >0.05 |
| Gender effect on AMO presence | Significant | 0.013 |

### Clinical interpretation

The AMO is an accessory opening in the fontanelle region of the lateral nasal wall and is a common anatomical variant. Its intuitive association with sinusitis (an extra opening might facilitate mucosal disease) is not borne out: AMO presence carries no significant sinusitis correlation on CBCT. What correlates with sinus pathology is **PMO obstruction** — blockage of the natural sinus drainage pathway through the ostiomeatal complex (OMC).

Counterintuitively, two turbinate variants that are often perceived as at-risk anatomy (paradoxical concha, bifid concha) are actually **more common in healthy sinuses**. This cautions against over-reading turbinate morphology as automatically pathogenic.

The gender dependency of AMO (p=0.013) has unclear clinical significance, but the pattern (AMO more frequent in females in the normal group; more frequent in males in the sinusitis group) may suggest that when AMO is encountered in a male patient with sinusitis, it co-occurs with pathological PMO obstruction more often.

**Pre-SFE CBCT interpretation rule**: catalogue AMO as an anatomical note but direct risk assessment toward PMO patency and OMC anatomy. A sinus with a visible AMO but a patent PMO is not at elevated sinusitis risk from the AMO itself.

This finding reinforces the OMC-centric approach documented in [[sinus-lift/lateral/gulbey-2026-osteomeatal-complex-variations-maxillary]] and provides the mechanistic specificity that the large synthesis refers to broadly as "OMC anatomy."

## Axis 2 — Septa: Perforation Risk vs Ostium Involvement Over Time

**Source**: Kato 2021 — retrospective CBCT, n=30 sinuses: 15 with interfering septa vs 15 septa-free controls; all lateral-window SFE, residual crest ≤4 mm; CBCT at T0, T1w (1 week), T9m (9 months).

### Perforation data

| Parameter | Septa group (n=15) | Control group (n=15) |
|---|---|---|
| Perforations | **4 (26.7%)** | 0 |
| Mean perforation size | ≈1–5 mm | — |
| Repair method | Collagen membrane | — |

Septa unambiguously raise perforation risk (26.7% vs 0%), consistent with the OR 4.03 reported by Yang 2024 in the large synthesis. However, the causal mechanism is purely mechanical — the septum creates an irregular sinus floor that resists smooth membrane elevation — and does not imply any ongoing post-operative mucosal or ostium pathology.

### Ostium/infundibulum time-course

| Parameter | T0 | T1w (1 week) | T9m (9 months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infundibula obstructed — septa | 0/15 | **3/15 (20%)** | **0/15** |
| Infundibula obstructed — controls | 0/15 | **5/15 (33.3%)** | **0/15** |
| Mean mucosal height increase — septa | — | ~5.7 mm | normalized |
| Mean mucosal height increase — controls | — | ~7.1 mm | ~1.2 mm above T0 |
| Edema-to-ostium distance | — | septa 11.8 mm / controls 7.5 mm (p=0.310) | — |

Three observations stand out:

1. **Controls swelled more and obstructed more infundibula than the septa group at 1 week.** This counter-intuitive result likely reflects that septa physically limit edema spread toward the ostium; in a non-septated sinus the clot/edema can expand freely toward the palatal wall and ostium region.
2. **All infundibular obstructions resolved completely by 9 months in both groups.** No case developed clinical sinusitis.
3. **The difference in ostium involvement between groups was non-significant (p=0.310 for edema-to-ostium distance)**, confirming that septa, while a perforation hazard, do not predict worse ostium outcomes.

### Clinical decision point

When septa are detected on pre-operative CBCT, the surgical modification required is to the **window and osteotomy design** (two-window approach, adjusted entry angles, piezoelectric instrumentation) — not to post-operative monitoring for sinusitis beyond the standard protocol. The post-operative 1-week mucosal thickening and transient infundibular obstruction that can follow SFE are **physiological events independent of septa** and should not trigger alarm without clinical symptoms.

This finding adds time-resolved granularity to the septa section of [[overviews/sinus-lift-lateral-2026-synthesis]], which correctly flags septa as OR 4.03 for perforation but does not break down the differential ostium involvement by septa status over time.

## Axis 3 — Perforation Repair by Size: The Clinical Outcome Ladder

**Source**: Lim 2011 — Korean narrative review, PubMed 1990–2010, 7 clinical studies selected, >436 implants, success stratified by perforation size and repair technique.

### Repair decision by size

| Perforation size | Recommended repair | Implant success |
|---|---|---|
| Very small (<~2 mm) | No repair; membrane self-folds over | Normal expected |
| Small (<5 mm) | Resorbable collagen membrane (Bio-Gide / Bio-Mend) ± 6-0 Vicryl suture | **94–99%** |
| Medium (5–10 mm) | Collagen membrane + lamellar bone from osteotomy site | **91.9%** |
| Large (>10 mm) | Lamellar bone ± buccal fat pad; block bone graft | **74.1%** |
| Very large (>15 mm) | Slow-resorbing collagen membrane (1–2 sheets, pin-fixed) ± PRP; microsurgical 8-0 suture + fibrin glue; collagen pouch | ~100% (case reports only, very small n) |

### Fugazzotto-Vlassis classification (Class I–V)

| Class | Description | Manageability |
|---|---|---|
| I | Near osteotomy edge, mesiodistal direction | Most manageable |
| II | Moderate, crossing osteotomy line | Intermediate |
| III | Large, central | Difficult |
| IV | Complex / multiple tears | Very difficult |
| V | Within prior tear / re-entry | Least manageable |

Higher class = more difficult repair and lower expected success. This classification predates size-only cutoffs and provides spatial/situational context that millimeter thresholds alone cannot capture.

### Key risk factors for perforation (Becker et al. data, from review)

| Risk factor | Approximate contribution |
|---|---|
| Thin membrane | 28% |
| Septa | 22% |
| Adhesions | 17% |
| Prior surgery | 17% |
| Scar tissue | 11% |
| Sinus cyst | 5% |

### Decision rules

- **<5 mm**: proceed with collagen membrane coverage and complete the graft procedure; 94–99% success.
- **5–10 mm**: add lamellar bone from the osteotomy as a structural backing to the collagen; 91.9%.
- **>10 mm**: consider buccal fat pad graft (available from the same surgical field via a vestibular extension) and/or block graft. Success drops to 74.1% — this outcome should be discussed with the patient.
- **>15 mm / uncontrollable**: abandon the graft procedure, place temporary membrane coverage, and plan re-entry at approximately 4 months to allow membrane cicatrization.
- **Suturing caution**: thin Schneiderian membrane may enlarge with needle passage; 8-0 microsurgical suture with fibrin glue is preferred over conventional 6-0 for large perforations.

### Prevention emphasis

Lim 2011 synthesizes the consistent message across included studies: the most reliable outcome is no perforation. **Prevention strategy**: piezosurgery (reduces tearing force), pre-operative CT (identifies membrane thickness and septa), rounded osteotomy window corners (eliminates stress concentration at corners), and allergy/rhinitis screening (pre-existing mucosal fragility).

The quantitative outcome ladder here is more granular than the binary manageable/unmanageable framing in the large synthesis and provides specific percent-success anchors for informed consent and intraoperative decision-making.

## Axis 4 — Severe Refractory OMS: Pathophysiology-Guided Surgical Management

**Source**: Ohyama 2022 — single case report (n=1); 54-year-old man; OMS from underfilled teeth #25/#26 spreading to the frontal sinus; refractory to endodontic re-treatment and long-term antibiotics.

### Why conventional approaches fail in severe OMS

| Approach | Weakness |
|---|---|
| Maxillary sinus radical surgery (MSRS) | Strips maxillary sinus mucosa → destroys mucociliary clearance → postoperative maxillary cyst (POMC), sinus deformation, wall sclerosis decades later |
| Endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) | Drains via natural ostium → **anatomically mismatched**: OMS causative site lies below the natural ostium, pus gravitates to the inferior meatus by gravity; natural ostium route is geometrically uphill and prone to reclosure |
| Antibiotics + endodontic re-treatment | Address the infection but cannot establish adequate drainage when the sinus is already severely opacified and the drainage route is obstructed |

### CMSM procedure components

| Component | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Caldwell–Luc access (canine fossa) | Direct access to the sinus without disturbing the natural ostium circuit |
| **Maxillary sinus mucosa (MSM) preservation** | Preserves ciliated pseudostratified epithelium → mucociliary recovery; avoids POMC |
| **Large nasoantral window, inferior nasal meatus** | Gravity-appropriate drainage (inferior meatus = lowest point of the sinus in upright posture); long-term patency by size |
| **Apicoectomy + retrograde root-end filling** | Eliminates the odontogenic source; impossible under ESS; allows tooth preservation |
| Clarithromycin 1 month post-op | Residual antibacterial support while mucosa recovers |

### Outcome

At 2-year follow-up: complete resolution of OMS (including frontal sinus extension); maxillary sinus anatomy and function fully restored; nasoantral window patent; causative teeth preserved. No recurrence.

### Anatomical principle (the inferior-meatus drainage logic)

In a standing or seated patient, the natural maxillary ostium opens into the middle meatus at the upper portion of the medial sinus wall — geometrically unfavorable for gravity drainage of inferior-pooling pus. ESS enlarges this path, but pus generated below the ostium level still requires active mucociliary transport to reach it. When mucociliary function is compromised by longstanding infection, ESS drainage fails. A window positioned in the **inferior nasal meatus**, at or below the sinus floor level, allows passive gravity drainage and ventilation without relying on ciliary function — and its large size (enlarged with bone forceps after saw-and-hammer entry) resists reclosure that commonly complicates natural-ostium ESS enlargement.

### Clinical decision criteria for CMSM vs ESS

| Factor | Favors ESS | Favors CMSM |
|---|---|---|
| Odontogenic source below ostium level | — | Yes |
| Pus/opacification in inferior sinus | — | Yes |
| Mucociliary function likely compromised | — | Yes (longstanding disease) |
| No tooth root involvement | Yes | — |
| Mild/moderate OMS, antibiotic-responsive | Yes | — |
| Frontal sinus extension / severe spread | — | Consider CMSM |
| Refractory to antibiotics + endodontics | — | Yes |

**Note**: evidence level is case report (n=1). CMSM represents a principled alternative backed by anatomical reasoning; prospective studies are not yet available. The [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]] provides the broader OMS management framework within which CMSM is one node.
