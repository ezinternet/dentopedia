## Three-line Summary

High-quality SR+MA evidence (Hamadé 2024; 21,066 implants, 24 studies) shows hypertension per se does not significantly raise implant failure odds (OR 1.100, p=0.671); the real clinical signal lies in antihypertensive medication status — users consistently outperform normotensive controls across three independent cohorts (Wu 2016 HR 0.12; Masri 2024 2.29% vs 4.33% EIF; Chidiac 2026 HR 0.23).

Unmedicated hypertension is the single worst state (Masri 2024: 28.6% patient-level EIF vs 14.5% normotensive), and the protective mechanism is multi-layered: RAAS inhibitors reduce osteoclastogenic RANKL signaling (Saravi 2021 ISQ +3.59, Khalilurrahman 2026), beta-blockers suppress sympathetic-driven bone resorption (Huang 2024, Uchibori 2020), and thiazides enhance osteoblast differentiation.

A critical counterpoint is that survival does not equal success: antihypertensive users who retain all implants still carry an OR 7.38 for peri-implantitis (Seki 2020), and a perioperative BP surge occurs in 26.77% of implant-plus-bone-augmentation cases (Liu 2024), mandating intraoperative monitoring.

## 세줄요약

고품질 SR+MA(Hamadé 2024; 21,066개, 24편) 기준 고혈압 자체는 임플란트 실패 위험을 유의하게 높이지 않으나(OR 1.100, p=0.671), 진짜 임상 신호는 항고혈압약 복용 여부 — 복용군은 3개 독립 코호트에서 일관되게 정상혈압 대조군보다 낮은 실패율(Wu 2016 HR 0.12; Masri 2024 2.29% vs 4.33%; Chidiac 2026 HR 0.23).

미복용 고혈압이 최악의 상태(Masri 2024 환자 수준 28.6% vs 14.5%)이며, 보호 메커니즘은 다층적 — RAAS 억제제는 RANKL 매개 파골세포 증가 (RANKL-driven Osteoclastogenesis)를 줄이고(Saravi 2021 ISQ +3.59), 베타차단제 (BB)는 교감신경 경유 골흡수를 억제하며(Huang 2024, Uchibori 2020), 티아지드는 조골세포 분화를 촉진.

그러나 '생존 ≠ 성공' 함정 존재 — 복용군에서도 임플란트 주위염 OR 7.38(Seki 2020), 임플란트+골증대 동시 시술 시 수술 전후 혈압 급등 26.77%(Liu 2024) — 수술 중 모니터링 및 장기 치주 감시가 필수.

## Thesis

**Clinical question**: Should a hypertensive patient on antihypertensive medication receive dental implants, and how does their medication profile affect outcomes?

**Current best-evidence answer**: Yes, well-controlled hypertensive patients on medications are good implant candidates — their failure rates are paradoxically *lower* than normotensive controls. The key risk stratifier is not the diagnosis of hypertension but whether the patient is actively taking and complying with their antihypertensive regimen. Unmedicated hypertension remains the highest-risk state. The single critical gap in the evidence is that no study has established whether the bone-protective effect is preserved long-term, given that peri-implantitis risk appears elevated even in surviving implants.

## Mechanism Synthesis

### Why Hypertension Harms Bone

Angiotensin II acts on osteoblast surface receptors to upregulate RANKL → osteoclast activation → trabecular bone loss. Simultaneously, chronic hypertension impairs calcium metabolism: renal calcium wasting → secondary hyperparathyroidism → further bone resorption and reduced mineralized bone density. Microvascular rarefaction decreases peri-implant blood supply during the osseointegration window.

### Why Antihypertensives Reverse and Exceed That Harm

Three independent pathways:

1. **RAAS inhibitors (ACEi/ARB)**: Block angiotensin II → RANKL reduction → osteoclast suppression. The foundational animal mechanism (Shimizu 2008, FASEB J): Ang II activates osteoblast AT1 receptors → RANKL upregulation → osteoclastogenesis; olmesartan completely blocks this pathway; AT1a knockout mice show no OVX-induced bone loss. Human confirmation at three levels: ISQ (Saravi 2021: +3.59, p=0.025), mandibular bone quality (Aşantoğrl 2024: FD ↑ in ARB/ACEi subgroups, p<0.001), and fracture risk (Solomon 2011: ARB HR 0.76 in n=376,061). Khalilurrahman 2026 adds a contradictory periodontal signal — RAAS inhibitors may increase periodontal susceptibility via kinin-pathway inflammation despite bone benefit.

2. **Beta-blockers**: Block adrenergic input on osteoclast activity via β-AR. Both Huang 2024 (BV/TV +93% with propranolol+PTH, Bmal1/RUNX2 pathway) and Uchibori 2020 (all 3 β-blocker types ↑ alveolar bone, ↓ RANKL and sclerostin in SHR rats) confirm the mechanism in dental-context animal models. Clinical translation remains incomplete: Saravi 2021 found no significant ISQ benefit for beta-blockers alone.

3. **Thiazide diuretics**: Enhance intestinal calcium absorption, reduce urinary calcium losses, stimulate osteoblast differentiation — reversing the calcium-PTH dysregulation cascade of hypertension. Solomon 2011 (n=376,061) provides large-scale fracture evidence: HR 0.85 vs CCB (p=0.03). Aşantoğrl 2024 shows thiazide improves cortical but not trabecular FD — consistent with a distinct calcium-mediated mechanism operating mainly on cortical bone. Small representation in implant cohorts (5% in Masri 2024) limits direct osseointegration quantification.

### Net Effect: A Dual-Action Protective Phenomenon

The drug effect is not merely correcting disease-induced harm — it appears to add an independent osteoanabolic benefit beyond what normotensive patients receive. This is the most likely explanation for why HTN+med patients outperform normotensive controls in Wu 2016 (HR 0.12), Masri 2024 (2.29% vs 4.33%), and Chidiac 2026 (HR 0.23).

## The Survival ≠ Success Distinction (Seki 2020)

Seki 2020 is the most important counterpoint in this corpus. In a 7-year TiUnite cohort, antihypertensive users and non-users had **identical implant survival (0 losses in both groups)**, yet the antihypertensive group had dramatically higher **peri-implantitis prevalence**: 26.5% vs 4.7% (OR 7.38, p<0.001). Marginal bone loss was also greater.

The mechanism is speculative but plausible: antihypertensive medications (particularly those causing xerostomia — thiazides, beta-blockers) may alter salivary flow and composition → dysbiotic peri-implant microbiome → subclinical tissue inflammation that does not reach the failure threshold but accumulates as bone loss and tissue inflammation.

**Clinical implication**: a "successful" implant outcome in an antihypertensive-using patient should not lead to relaxed recall intervals. The tissue-health story diverges from the osseointegration story.

> Cross-reference: Ramirez-Martinez-Acitores 2020 (antihypertensive → xerostomia SR) and Kohli 2023 (medication-induced xerostomia → orofacial risk) provide the salivary mechanism context.

## Clinical Decision Points

### Point 1: Can I place implants in this hypertensive patient?

| Patient state | Risk profile | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled HTN + compliant on medication | Lower EIF than normotensive (HR 0.12–0.23) | Proceed; standard protocol |
| Controlled HTN + non-compliant / occasional missed doses | Unknown; likely intermediate | Confirm compliance before scheduling |
| Unmedicated HTN (newly diagnosed, refuses treatment) | Highest risk: 28.6% patient-level EIF | Postpone; refer for medical management first |
| Malignant/uncontrolled HTN | Very high systemic risk | Contraindicate elective implants |

**The single most predictive question**: "Are you taking your blood pressure medications every day?" — compliance status is a stronger predictor of outcome than the diagnosis of hypertension alone.

### Point 2: Does the drug class matter?

Current evidence suggests **yes**, but class-level evidence is limited:

- **RAAS inhibitors** (ACEi/ARB): strongest clinical bone signal (Saravi 2021 ISQ +3.59); ~54% of Wu 2016 cohort, likely driving the landmark result. Watch for periodontal risk (Khalilurrahman 2026: inconsistent data; external evidence suggests 1.6–4.8× periodontitis risk with ACEi/ARB).
- **Beta-blockers**: mechanistically supported (Huang 2024, Uchibori 2020) but Saravi 2021 found no independent ISQ benefit (only RAAS was significant). Masri 2024 shows BB was the *most common* drug class (61%) in the protected group, but no class-level analysis was performed.
- **Thiazides**: physiologically best-positioned to reverse calcium dysregulation, but very small clinical representation (5% in Masri 2024). No direct implant-level evidence.
- **No head-to-head RCT exists** — drug-class recommendations are mechanistic, not clinical-trial-grounded.

### Point 3: What to monitor long-term?

Seki 2020 data mandate heightened peri-implant surveillance for antihypertensive users, even with intact osseointegration:

- Assess salivary flow at each recall (antihypertensives → xerostomia risk)
- Probe peri-implant sulcus every 6 months (not annually)
- Monitor radiographic marginal bone loss yearly (Carlos 2024: MBL prevalence 78.95% in HTN patients — confounded but a warning)
- Screen for xerostomia and discuss saliva-substitute use where indicated

### Point 4: Perioperative blood pressure management

Liu 2024 establishes that 26.77% of patients undergoing implants *with simultaneous bone augmentation* experience perioperative hypertension. Risk is highest in:
- Male patients (RR 1.74)
- Age ≥60 years (RR 2.35)
- Surgical time >60 minutes (RR 1.65; most prominent in GBR)

Mandatory for complex implant + grafting cases: intraoperative BP monitoring, pre-procedure medication compliance check, and contingency plan (immediate BP management) for high-risk patients.

## Gaps & Future Research

1. **No RCT with medication-class stratification**: all clinical studies are retrospective, none randomize by drug class. The RAAS inhibitor ISQ signal (Saravi 2021) is now supported by mechanistic (Shimizu 2008), radiographic (Aşantoğrl 2024), and epidemiological (Solomon 2011) convergent evidence — but prospective RCT validation of implant-specific outcomes remains absent.

2. **Medication compliance unmeasured**: all cohorts rely on self-reported or prescription-record compliance. Objective measures (drug level assay, pharmacy refill) would clarify the unmedicated vs non-compliant HTN confusion.

3. **Blood pressure control status rarely documented**: Hamadé 2024 meta-analysis identified this as the primary quality limitation across all 24 included studies. Whether outcomes track with BP value (e.g., SBP ≥140 vs SBP 120–139) is unknown.

4. **The Seki 2020 peri-implantitis paradox needs replication**: OR 7.38 in a 7-year single-cohort study is compelling but based on n=83 patients. Does this hold across longer follow-up, other implant surfaces, and larger samples?

5. **Perioperative BP surge and osseointegration**: Liu 2024 quantifies incidence but does not connect intraoperative BP events to later failure outcomes. Does the perioperative surge translate to higher EIF or MBL rates?

6. **Drug withdrawal and implant risk**: no study evaluates whether withholding antihypertensives perioperatively (sometimes done for ACEi/ARB due to hypotension risk — Waters 2022) impacts osseointegration. This is a real clinical tension.
