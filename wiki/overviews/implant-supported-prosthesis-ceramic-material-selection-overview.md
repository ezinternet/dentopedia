## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 임플란트 지지 보철물의 세라믹 **상부구조(superstructure)** 재료 선택은 (1) 생존율은 재료 간 대체로 동등, (2) 진짜 차이는 파절/칩핑 양상에 있고, (3) 그 양상은 재료과학(피로거동·응력분포)으로 설명된다는 3단 논리로 정리된다. 이 페이지는 임플란트 **고정체(fixture)** 재료([[overviews/zirconia-implant-clinical-outcomes]])나 일반 재료 결정([[overviews/prosthetic-materials-decision-ladder]])이 아니라, **임플란트 위에 올라가는 세라믹 상부구조 자체의 재료 선택**을 다룬다.
> - 출발점(Pjetursson 2023 SR+MA, 32편, 후방부 임플란트 다단위 FDP): PFM 98.3%·베니어 지르코니아 97.5%·단일체/미세베니어 지르코니아 98.9%·리튬디실리케이트(LDS) 97.0% — 3년 생존율 전 재료군 통계적 동등. 단, **베니어 지르코니아 FDP가 칩핑·파절률 유의하게 높음(p<0.01)** — 코어 강도와 무관하게 베니어 층 자체가 약점.
> - Le 2023 RCT(다기관, n=71, 84 crowns, 3년)로 단관 수준에서 재확인: 고투명 지르코니아(ZC)·ZC+부분베니어(ZC-V)·LDS 세 재료 생존율 98.8% 동등, **파절 0건** — 후방부 단관에서 5Y급 고투명 지르코니아도 3년까지 안전.
> - Talmazov 2020 FEA(일체형 지르코니아 vs 티타늄 임플란트, 전치부 3개 시나리오)로 "왜 재료가 응력을 다르게 분산하는가"의 기전을 제공: 지르코니아(탄성계수 ~200 GPa)가 티타늄(~110 GPa)보다 순측-치경부 피질골 von Mises 응력을 유의하게 낮춤(치유 소켓·골폭감소 모델, p<0.05) — 단, 이식 소켓 모델에서는 피질골 차이 없음(이식재에는 지르코니아가 여전히 유리).
> - Spitznagel 2025(in-vitro 피로, n=72, multilayer 4Y/5Y 지르코니아 어금니 수복물)로 "두께 vs 형태" 교차작용을 정량화: 0.5mm 초박형에서는 부분피개(PCR)가 크라운을 압도(2047 vs 695N, p<0.001), 1.0mm는 동등, 1.5mm는 크라운 우위(3503 vs 2777N, p=0.005) — **크라운은 반드시 ≥1.0mm 필요**, PCR은 전 두께에서 안전.
> - Comba 2022(in-vitro 피로, n=84 근관치료 대구치)로 재료×준비형태 교차비교: 오버레이(1.6mm)는 접착크라운(2.0mm)의 유효 대안, 교합면비니어(1.2mm)는 열위(p=0.042); LDS가 PICN보다 정적 파절강도 우수(p=0.0002)하나 PICN은 주기하중에서 재난성 파절 0건 — 강도와 피로내구성은 다른 축.
> - 임상 결정 프레임 — 전치부 단관: 고투명 지르코니아(5Y) 또는 LDS 둘 다 안전(Le 2023 파절 0). 심미 우선이면 LDS/4Y-PSZ, 저작력 우려 시 5Y-ZC.
> - 임상 결정 프레임 — 후방부 다단위 FDP: 단일체(모놀리식) 지르코니아 1차 선택 — 생존 최고(98.9%)이자 칩핑 최저. 베니어 디자인은 심미가 절대적으로 필요한 경우만, 칩핑 위험 counseling 필수.
> - 임상 결정 프레임 — 초박형/최소침습 후방 수복(0.5–1.0mm): 크라운 대신 부분피개(PCR/veneer/overlay) 우선 — 두께가 제한적일 때 기계적으로 더 안전. 크라운 형태를 쓸 거면 최소 1.0mm 확보.
> - 임상 결정 프레임 — 캔틸레버/디자인: Pjetursson 2023에서 pontic FDP vs splinted crown 생존·합병증 차이 없음 — 디자인 자유도 있음, 재료 선택이 우선 변수.
> - 근거 한계: 모든 소스 3년 이하 관찰(Le, Pjetursson) 또는 in-vitro/FEA(Talmazov, Spitznagel, Comba) — 5–10년 임상 파절 분화 데이터 부재, in-vitro 피로시험은 실제 구강 열/화학적 노화를 완전히 재현 못함.

## One-line Summary

Synthesizing an SR+MA outcomes anchor (Pjetursson 2023), a head-to-head RCT (Le 2023), and three mechanistic studies (Talmazov 2020 FEA, Spitznagel 2025 fatigue, Comba 2022 fatigue), implant-supported ceramic superstructure survival is largely material-agnostic (~97–99% at 3 years) while complication *type* is strongly material- and design-dependent — monolithic zirconia minimizes chipping, ultrathin sections favor partial-coverage over full-crown geometry, and higher elastic modulus reduces peri-implant cortical bone stress in non-grafted anterior scenarios.

## 한줄요약

임플란트 상부구조 세라믹 재료 선택 종합: SR+MA(Pjetursson 2023)·RCT(Le 2023)·기전연구 3편(Talmazov FEA·Spitznagel 피로·Comba 피로)을 통합 — 생존율은 재료 간 대체로 동등(~97-99%, 3년)하나 합병증 "유형"은 재료·형태·두께에 크게 좌우됨: 단일체 지르코니아가 칩핑 최소, 초박형은 크라운보다 부분피개가 유리, 높은 탄성계수가 비이식 전치부에서 치경부 피질골 응력을 낮춤.

## Summary

This overview addresses a distinct angle from the wiki's existing zirconia/prosthetic overviews: not the implant *fixture* material ([[overviews/zirconia-implant-clinical-outcomes]]) and not general crown/bridge/attachment decisions ([[overviews/prosthetic-materials-decision-ladder]]), but the **ceramic superstructure material and geometry choice specifically on implant-supported prostheses** — all-ceramic crown types, zirconia FDP design (monolithic vs veneered), and the fatigue/biomechanical reasoning behind observed clinical failure patterns.

The synthesis follows a three-tier logic:

1. **Outcomes anchor** (Pjetursson 2023 SR+MA) establishes which materials/designs have the best survival and complication data for implant-supported multi-unit FDPs.
2. **Head-to-head confirmation** (Le 2023 RCT) validates the same material-equivalence pattern at the single-crown level, in a controlled multicenter trial.
3. **Mechanistic explanation** (Talmazov 2020 FEA, Spitznagel 2025 fatigue, Comba 2022 fatigue) supplies the material-science reasoning for *why* these clinical patterns occur — stress distribution in one-piece zirconia implants, thickness×design fatigue interactions in multilayer zirconia, and post-fatigue fracture resistance differences between lithium disilicate and polymer-infiltrated ceramic.

## Tier 1 — Outcomes Anchor: Pjetursson 2023 SR+MA (Implant-Supported Multi-Unit FDP)

Pjetursson et al. (2023, *Clin Oral Implants Res*, 32 studies/42 arms) is the highest-evidence-weight source in this synthesis for implant-supported FDP material selection. Using the Pjetursson group's robust Poisson regression model, pooled 3-year survival was:

| Material | 3-year Survival | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| PFM iFDP | 98.3% | 95.6–99.3% |
| Veneered zirconia iFDP | 97.5% | 95.5–98.7% |
| Monolithic/micro-veneered Zr iFDP | **98.9%** | 96.8–99.6% |
| Lithium disilicate iFDP | 97.0% | 84.8–99.9% |

No pairwise comparison reached statistical significance — **survival alone does not differentiate these materials at 3 years**. The clinically actionable finding is elsewhere: **veneered zirconia FDPs showed significantly more ceramic chipping and fracture than monolithic/micro-veneered designs (p<0.01)**, with veneered zirconia carrying the highest fracture rate of any group despite having comparable survival. Design (FDP-with-pontic vs implant-splinted-crown) showed no significant difference in survival (98.3% vs 99.1%) or complications — so pontic vs cantilever/splinted design choice is not itself a material-selection driver.

**Takeaway**: at the multi-unit implant FDP level, monolithic zirconia is the material of first choice — it matches or exceeds PFM/LDS survival while avoiding the chipping penalty that veneered zirconia specifically incurs.

## Tier 2 — Head-to-Head Confirmation: Le 2023 RCT (Single Posterior Crowns)

Le et al. (2023) provide the tooth-supported (not implant-supported, but directly comparable ceramic-selection evidence) 3-arm multicenter RCT — high-translucency monolithic zirconia (ZC, likely 5Y-PSZ), ZC with partial buccal veneer (ZC-V), and lithium disilicate (LDS), n=71 patients / 84 crowns at 3 years.

**Result**: 98.8% combined survival, **zero fractures in any group** — including the partial-veneer ZC-V arm. This reinforces the Pjetursson pattern at the single-crown scale: even a partially veneered high-translucency zirconia crown can survive fracture-free through 3 years, suggesting the *implant-FDP*-level veneered-chipping signal is more about multi-unit flexural loading and connector stress than a universal indictment of any veneer. Marginal integrity, surface, and anatomical form showed no between-group differences; patients rated esthetics more favorably than clinicians across all three materials.

**Synthesis point**: Pjetursson (implant FDP, multi-unit) shows veneered zirconia has an elevated *chipping* risk specifically in multi-unit prostheses; Le (tooth-supported, single crown) shows a partial-veneer design can still be fracture-free at the single-unit level. Read together, the risk of veneer failure scales with span/complexity, not simply with the presence of a veneer layer.

## Tier 3 — Mechanistic Explanation

### 3a. Why stiffness matters: Talmazov 2020 FEA (one-piece zirconia vs titanium)

Talmazov's FEA modeled one-piece zirconia (Y-TZP, ~200 GPa elastic modulus) vs titanium (Ti-6Al-4V, ~110 GPa) implants in three CBCT-derived anterior maxillary scenarios — healed socket (HS), reduced bone width (RB), and grafted extraction socket (EG) — under 200N axial + 178N oblique loading.

| Model | Region | Zir vs Ti | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| HS | Labial-cervical cortical | Zir < Ti | p < 0.05 |
| RB | Labial-cervical cortical | Zir < Ti | p < 0.05 |
| EG | Labial-cervical cortical | No difference | NS |
| EG | Graft material | Zir < Ti | p < 0.05 |

Mechanistically: titanium's lower stiffness deforms more under load, transducing more force into the surrounding cortical bone; zirconia's higher stiffness absorbs load internally, reducing peri-implant bone overloading — in scenarios where bone is intact or grafted. This is not superstructure ceramic per se (it studies a one-piece zirconia *implant*), but it establishes the biomechanical principle that underlies why **higher-modulus ceramic materials (zirconia >> lithium disilicate in stiffness) redistribute occlusal load differently through the prosthesis-abutment-bone complex** — the same stiffness logic that determines whether a monolithic zirconia FDP or a bilayer veneered design concentrates stress at the weakest interface (the veneer-core bond).

### 3b. Why thickness and geometry interact: Spitznagel 2025 fatigue (multilayer zirconia, crown vs partial-coverage)

Spitznagel's in-vitro fatigue study (n=72, composition-gradient 4Y/5Y-PSZ multilayer zirconia molar restorations) is the clearest mechanistic demonstration in this synthesis of a **crossed design×thickness interaction** — directly relevant to selecting crown vs partial-coverage (PCR) geometry for thin-section implant-supported restorations (e.g., limited interocclusal space cases common around implants):

| Group | Thickness (occl/bucc) | Mean failure load (N) |
|---|---|---|
| C-1.5 (crown) | 1.5/0.8 mm | 3503 |
| PCR-1.5 | 1.5/0.8 mm | 2777 |
| PCR-0.5 | 0.5/0.4 mm | 2047 |
| PCR-1.0 | 1.0/0.6 mm | 2018 |
| C-1.0 (crown) | 1.0/0.6 mm | 1957 |
| C-0.5 (crown) | 0.5/0.4 mm | **695** (near parafunctional ceiling) |

At 0.5mm, PCR dramatically outperforms the crown (2047 vs 695N, p<0.001) — the crown's full-coverage geometry at ultrathin section concentrates stress and fails catastrophically (Type IV longitudinal fracture), while the PCR's non-retentive partial design fails more benignly (Type III combined ceramic+substrate). At 1.5mm the relationship reverses (crown 3503 > PCR 2777N, p=0.005) — once ceramic bulk is sufficient, full-coverage geometry becomes an advantage. All specimens survived 1.2M fatigue cycles (~5 years simulated) regardless of design.

**Clinical translation for implant prostheses**: where interocclusal or restorative space is restricted (a frequent implant scenario, especially screw-retained designs with access-channel constraints reducing effective ceramic bulk), a partial-coverage/monolithic-veneer geometry is mechanically safer than forcing a full-crown contour at <1.0mm. Full-coverage crown geometry should not be used below 1.0mm.

### 3c. Why material and preparation both matter, independently: Comba 2022 fatigue (lithium disilicate vs PICN)

Comba's in-vitro study (n=84 endodontically-treated molars, 2×3 factorial: material × preparation design) isolates material choice from geometry choice as two independent, additive variables (interaction p=0.8218, NS):

| Preparation | PICN (N) | LDS (N) |
|---|---|---|
| Occlusal veneer (1.2mm) | 1806.6 ± 270.1 | 2029.5 ± 295.3 |
| Overlay (1.6mm) | 1726.9 ± 301.1 | 2059.5 ± 308.0 |
| Adhesive crown (2.0mm) | 1555.4 ± 393.9 | 1859.2 ± 232.0 |

LDS significantly outperformed PICN on static post-fatigue fracture load (p=0.0002) — consistent with LDS's higher flexural strength as a lithium-disilicate glass-ceramic vs. PICN's lower-modulus polymer-ceramic hybrid. But PICN showed **zero catastrophic failures during cyclic loading**, while the LDS adhesive-crown group had one core fracture — meaning static strength and fatigue tolerance are not the same axis, and material selection should weigh both. Preparation design also mattered independently: overlay (1.6mm) was statistically equivalent to full adhesive crown (2.0mm), while the more minimal occlusal veneer (1.2mm) was significantly weaker (p=0.042) — a design threshold effect echoing Spitznagel's thickness finding, but here specifically flagging that endodontically-treated (structurally compromised) teeth need at least overlay-level coverage, not minimal veneers.

**Relevance to implant prostheses**: while Comba studies tooth-supported ETT restorations rather than implant-supported ones, the material/design decoupling directly informs implant-adjacent restorative choices (e.g., an implant-supported overlay/onlay-type superstructure, or restoring an adjacent ETT abutment tooth in a mixed implant-tooth-supported prosthesis) — LDS for peak static load resistance, PICN where cyclic/parafunctional resilience is prioritized over peak strength, and a minimum of overlay-level coverage rather than minimal veneers when structural loss is significant.

## Integrated Material-Selection Decision Framework

| Clinical scenario | First-choice material/design | Rationale (this synthesis) |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior single implant crown, esthetic priority | High-translucency zirconia (5Y-ZC) or LDS | Le 2023: both fracture-free at 3y; patient-rated esthetics favorable for both |
| Anterior implant, intact/reduced bone (non-grafted) | Higher-modulus material (zirconia-based restoration/abutment complex) | Talmazov FEA: higher elastic modulus reduces labial-cervical cortical stress (p<0.05) in HS/RB models |
| Anterior implant, grafted extraction socket | Material choice less critical for cortical bone stress (NS); zirconia still favors graft protection | Talmazov FEA: no cortical-bone difference in EG model, but Zir < Ti stress on graft (p<0.05) |
| Posterior implant-supported multi-unit FDP | Monolithic (or micro-veneered) zirconia | Pjetursson 2023: highest survival (98.9%) + lowest chipping vs veneered (p<0.01) |
| Posterior implant FDP where full veneering is esthetically required | Veneered zirconia acceptable but counsel chipping risk | Pjetursson 2023: survival equivalent (97.5%) but significantly higher fracture/chipping rate |
| FDP design: pontic vs splinted/cantilever | Either — design does not drive material selection | Pjetursson 2023: no significant survival/complication difference by design |
| Restricted interocclusal space (<1.0mm ceramic bulk) | Partial-coverage/non-retentive design over full crown | Spitznagel 2025: PCR ≫ crown at 0.5mm (2047 vs 695N, p<0.001); crown needs ≥1.0mm |
| Adequate space (≥1.5mm ceramic bulk) | Full-coverage crown geometry | Spitznagel 2025: crown > PCR at 1.5mm (3503 vs 2777N, p=0.005) |
| Structurally compromised abutment tooth (adjacent to implant prosthesis) needing minimally invasive restoration | Overlay (≥1.6mm), not minimal occlusal veneer | Comba 2022: overlay ≈ adhesive crown; veneer significantly weaker (p=0.042) in ETT |
| Peak static load priority (heavy occlusion, parafunction) | Lithium disilicate over PICN | Comba 2022: LDS > PICN static fracture load (p=0.0002) |
| Cyclic/fatigue resilience priority, catastrophic-failure avoidance | PICN as an alternative consideration | Comba 2022: PICN had zero catastrophic cyclic failures vs 1 LDS core fracture |

## Gaps & Future Research

- All five sources cap out at 3 years (clinical: Le, Pjetursson) or are in-vitro/FEA proxies (Talmazov, Spitznagel, Comba) — none report 5–10 year implant-supported ceramic superstructure fracture differentiation, which is precisely where material differences are expected to diverge (per Pjetursson's own stated limitation).
- No study in this set directly fatigue-tests implant-supported (vs tooth-supported) monolithic zirconia crowns/FDPs under the same thickness-gradient protocol as Spitznagel — the thickness×design interaction is established for tooth-borne molar restorations but not confirmed for the implant-abutment-crown assembly specifically, where screw-access channels further reduce effective ceramic bulk in some designs.
- Talmazov's FEA assumes 100% osseointegration and isotropic bone — no micromotion, no long-term remodeling; cannot be extrapolated to superstructure material choice directly, only to the general stiffness-transduction principle.
- No head-to-head in this set directly compares lithium disilicate vs zirconia implant-supported multi-unit FDPs under standardized fatigue protocols with implant-specific loading (off-axis, screw-access-channel geometry).
- PICN's favorable cyclic-fatigue profile (Comba 2022) has not been tested in an implant-supported multi-unit FDP context — current PICN evidence for implant superstructures is essentially absent from this synthesis.
