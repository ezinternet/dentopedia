## 한국어 핵심요약

> - 핵심: 임플란트·상악동거상술 계획 전 CBCT에서 우연히 발견되는 상악동 병변의 전체 스펙트럼 — 유병률(2편) + 상악동석(Antrolith) 심층 분석(증례 2편)을 통합해 "그대로 진행 vs ENT 협진" 분류 프레임워크 제시.
> - 유병률: 임플란트 후보 환자에서 상악동 병변 전체 유병률은 63.5%(Salari 2021, n=140) ~ 68%(Küçükkurt 2025, n=1,000) — **절반 이상**에서 어떤 형태로든 병변 발견.

> - 두 번째로 흔한 병변은 점액저류낭종 (Mucous Retention Cyst, MRC): Salari 17.1%. Küçükkurt는 "2번째로 흔함"만 보고(수치 미상세).

> - 상악동석(Antrolith)은 별개 카테고리 — 유병률 0.15~3.2%로 드묾(Tan 2020), 여성호발(55-60%). 두 증례가 스펙트럼 양극단을 보여줌: Tan 2020은 무증상 소형(3.1×3.6mm) 우연 발견, Shenoy 2013은 증상성 대형(2×1cm)이 재발성 부비동염·구강상악동루(Oroantral Fistula, OAF) 유발.
> - 상악동석 관리: 무증상·소형 → 경과관찰(예방적 제거 불필요); 증상성·대형·합병증 동반 → 수술 제거(Caldwell-Luc ± 내시경부비동수술, Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, ESS). 상악동석 자체가 임플란트의 절대 금기는 아님.
> - **근거등급 주의**: 유병률 2편은 후향적·단면 연구(중간 등급), 상악동석 2편은 **증례보고(case report, n=1)** — 최하위 근거등급. 관리 알고리즘은 문헌고찰 기반 합의이지 RCT 근거 아님.
> - 트리아지(triage) 원칙: (1) 점막비후 <2-3mm 또는 국소·경도 → 그대로 진행, (2) MRC·용종 등 dome-shaped 병변 → 크기·증상 기반 stratification(별도 overview 참조), (3) Antrolith·불명확 방사선불투과성 병변 → 감별진단 우선, 무증상 소형은 진행 가능하나 조직학적 확인 고려, (4) 전체 혼탁화·부비동염 증상·구조 이상(구강상악동루 등) → ENT 협진 필수.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 4 papers (2 retrospective CBCT prevalence studies, n=140 and n=1,000, and 2 antrolith case reports) mapping incidental maxillary sinus pathology in implant/sinus-lift candidates: overall pathology prevalence is 63.5–68% (threshold-dependent), mucosal thickening dominates (31.4–47%), followed by mucous retention cysts (17.1%), while antroliths are rare (0.15–3.2%, female-predominant) but span from asymptomatic incidental (Tan 2020, 3.1×3.6 mm, observe) to symptomatic large-with-complications (Shenoy 2013, 2×1 cm, recurrent sinusitis and oroantral fistula requiring Caldwell-Luc/ESS).

Mucosal thickening is significantly associated with odontogenic causes (periodontal disease/periapical lesions) on logistic regression (Küçükkurt 2025), implying the dentist can identify and treat the underlying cause; the two prevalence studies use different thresholds (>5 mm vs >3 mm) and are not directly comparable, so any quoted prevalence figure must be qualified by the cutoff used.

The triage principle is: mild/focal mucosal thickening (<2–3 mm) → proceed as planned; dome-shaped retention cysts/polyps → size/symptom stratification (see separate pseudocyst overview); antrolith or other radiopaque lesion → differential diagnosis first, symptomatic/large → surgery, asymptomatic/small → observe (not an absolute implant contraindication); complete opacification/sinusitis symptoms/structural anomaly → ENT referral mandatory.

## Summary

Before implant placement or sinus-lift surgery, clinicians routinely encounter incidental maxillary sinus findings on CBCT. This overview synthesizes two cross-sectional prevalence studies quantifying how common these findings are and what they consist of, plus two case reports characterizing a specific, less-common but clinically important entity — the antrolith (maxillary sinus stone) — across its symptomatic and asymptomatic ends. The goal is a working triage: which incidental findings are compatible with proceeding as planned, and which require further diagnostic workup or ENT referral before surgery.

This page deliberately does **not** re-derive the antral pseudocyst (AP) / mucous retention cyst (MRC) treatment decision tree already built in [[overviews/sinus-lift-pseudocyst-mrc-overview]] — that page owns the subtype/size-based management of dome-shaped cystic lesions. Here, the emphasis is (a) baseline prevalence of the whole pathology spectrum in implant candidates, and (b) the antrolith, a radiopaque entity not covered by the pseudocyst overview.

## Prevalence of Sinus Pathology in Implant Candidates

Two cross-sectional CBCT studies quantify how often *any* maxillary sinus pathology is present in patients being worked up for dental implants.

| Study | Design | Sample | Overall pathology prevalence | MT threshold used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/salari-2021-maxillary-sinus-pathologic-findings-cbct-implant]] | Cross-sectional, 2 radiology clinics, northern Iran | 140 patients / 280 sinuses | **63.5%** | >5 mm |
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/kucukkurt-2025-maxillary-sinus-pathologies-cbct-prevalence]] | Retrospective, single center | 1,000 CBCT scans (500M/500F) | **68%** | >3 mm |

**Reading the two numbers together requires care.** Salari used a higher mucosal-thickening threshold (>5mm) than Küçükkurt (>3mm), yet still landed a comparable — even slightly lower — overall prevalence. This is consistent, not contradictory: a stricter threshold under-detects thickening, so Salari's 63.5% likely undercounts mild pathology relative to a >2-3mm consensus standard. The take-away is that **the true population base rate is highly threshold-dependent**, and any single prevalence figure quoted to a patient or in a chart note should be qualified by which mm cutoff was used.

### Pathology type breakdown

| Pathology type | Salari 2021 (n=280 sinuses, >5mm MT) | Küçükkurt 2025 (n=1,000 scans, >3mm MT) |
|---|---|---|
| Mucosal thickening (MT) | **31.4%** (88/280) | **~47%** |
| Mucous retention cyst (MRC) | **17.1%** (48/280) | 2nd most common (exact % not detailed in extracted text) |
| Partial/complete opacification | 9.3% (26/280) | included under "non-specific opacifications", minor |
| Polypoidal mucosal thickening | 5.7% (16/280) | not separately broken out |
| Healthy / no pathology | 36.5% (102/280) | ~32% |

Both studies agree on the **rank order**: mucosal thickening > retention cyst > everything else. This ordering is the single most robust, cross-replicated finding across the two datasets, and is the anchor fact for patient counseling — "some degree of sinus lining thickening is the norm, not the exception, in implant-candidate populations."

### Odontogenic association — the mechanistic link

Both papers converge on the same causal hypothesis: mucosal thickening reflects **odontogenic irritation** rather than a primary ENT disease process in most implant candidates.

- Küçükkurt 2025 formally tested this with logistic regression: periapical lesions and periodontal disease were **significantly and independently associated** with mucosal thickening.
- Salari 2021 makes the same claim narratively ("mucosal thickening likely reflects odontogenic irritation — consistent with adjacent periapical/periodontal pathology") without a formal regression model.

**Synthesis [claude해석]:** this reframes mild-to-moderate mucosal thickening found on a pre-implant CBCT as often a *treatable, dentist-manageable* finding (extract/endodontically treat the offending tooth, resolve periodontal disease) rather than an automatic red flag requiring ENT referral. It also implies that **mucosal thickening prevalence is not a fixed population constant** — it should trend down in a practice that aggressively treats periapical and periodontal disease before implant workup, and a persistently thickened membrane after odontogenic source control merits closer scrutiny.

### Demographics — what did NOT predict pathology

Salari 2021 explicitly tested and found **no significant association** between sinus pathology and:
- Age (<46y vs ≥46y)
- Sex (male 65.5% vs female 63.5%)
- Dentition status (partial edentulism trended highest at 90.9%, but p=0.061 — did not reach significance)

Küçükkurt 2025 similarly reports no significant gender difference. **Synthesis [claude해석]:** clinicians should not use age or sex to pre-screen who "needs" a more careful sinus read on CBCT — the finding is common enough, and demographically non-selective enough, that every pre-implant/pre-sinus-lift CBCT in the posterior maxilla warrants the same systematic sinus evaluation regardless of patient profile.

## Antrolith: Deep-Dive on a Rare But Distinctive Entity

Neither prevalence study above singles out antroliths as a distinct category (they fall under "opacification" or aren't captured at all), so the two case reports fill that gap. An antrolith (상악동석) is a calcified mass forming when mineral salts (calcium phosphate, calcium carbonate) deposit around a central nidus inside the sinus — rare, with a reported prevalence of **0.15–3.2%** and a **female predisposition (55–60%)** ([[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/tan-2020-maxillary-antrolith-case-report-management]]). Fewer than 30 cases existed in world literature up to 2005, and the maxillary sinus is the single most common site for this entity ([[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/shenoy-2013-maxillary-antrolith-recurrent-sinusitis-case]]).

### Two ends of the clinical spectrum

| | [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/tan-2020-maxillary-antrolith-case-report-management]] | [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/shenoy-2013-maxillary-antrolith-recurrent-sinusitis-case]] |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | 67-year-old female, pre-Denosumab dental clearance | 47-year-old female, prior left Caldwell-Luc (1984) |
| Size | 3.1 × 3.6 mm (small) | 2 × 1 cm (large) |
| Presentation | Asymptomatic, incidental panoramic/CBCT finding | Recurrent sinusitis, facial heaviness ×2 months, oroantral fistula, foul-smelling purulent discharge |
| Nidus | Not specified / presumed idiopathic | Residual bony chips from prior Caldwell-Luc surgery (endogenous nidus) |
| Sinus membrane | No mucosal thickening or opacification | Bilateral maxillary opacification on CT |
| Microbiology | Not applicable (no infection) | *Pseudomonas* spp. (ampicillin-resistant) |
| Management | Caldwell-Luc excision (patient elected removal given breast-cancer history) | Combined ESS + repeat Caldwell-Luc (too large for ESS alone) |
| Outcome | Histopathology: metaplastic bone + calcification; excellent prognosis | Resolved after surgery; author recommends thorough sinus irrigation post-ESS to prevent recurrence |

**Pathogenesis (from Shenoy 2013):** nidus formation (endogenous — tooth/bone fragments, blood, mucus, fungi; or exogenous — gutta-percha, dental burs, silver points, cotton) → long-standing infection + poor sinus drainage → concentration of purulent fluid → precipitation of calcium phosphate/carbonate → progressive encrustation over years to decades. Shenoy's case is a striking illustration: bone chips retained from a Caldwell-Luc performed in **1984** became the nidus for a stone that grew to 2×1cm and caused symptoms roughly 30 years later — a direct argument for **thorough intraoperative sinus irrigation during any lateral-window or ESS procedure** to avoid leaving future nidus material behind.

### Differential diagnosis of a radiopaque sinus-floor lesion (pre-implant relevance)

Both antrolith papers stress that a discrete radiopacity at the sinus floor on a pre-implant CBCT is not self-evidently an antrolith. The differential includes:

- Root fragments, impacted teeth
- Calcified MRC or polyp
- Osteomyelitis
- Ossifying fibroma
- Fungal ball (aspergilloma)
- Benign or **malignant tumour**

Shenoy 2013's case was initially read on CT as possible osteoma vs. malignancy before endoscopic exploration revealed a mobile, gritty, brownish mass — underscoring that **CT/CBCT appearance alone is not diagnostic**; histopathology after decalcification (with PAS staining to exclude fungal concretions/rhinoscleroma) is what confirms an antrolith. Tan 2020 makes the same point for the small/asymptomatic end — the patient's breast-cancer history was itself the reason histological confirmation was pursued despite the lesion being asymptomatic.

### Antrolith management decision

| Scenario | Management |
|---|---|
| Small, asymptomatic (incidental CBCT finding) | Surveillance — prophylactic removal **not** necessary |
| Symptomatic (facial pain, chronic sinusitis, fistula, discharge) | Surgical removal |
| Large / poorly accessible by endoscope | Caldwell-Luc (wider exposure) ± ESS |
| Any surgical case | + concurrent treatment of sinus infection |
| History of malignancy or diagnostic uncertainty | Consider histological confirmation even if asymptomatic |

**Key message for implant planning (both papers converge on this): an antrolith alone does not preclude implant placement or sinus-lift surgery.** The decision to observe vs. remove is driven by symptom status and size/accessibility, not by the mere presence of the calcification. Prognosis after removal is excellent in both reports, with no recurrences documented in the wider literature review.

## Integrated Triage Framework: Proceed vs. Workup/Refer

Combining the prevalence data (what's common and usually benign) with the antrolith case spectrum (what's rare but needs differentiation) yields a practical pre-surgical triage:

```
CBCT read of the maxillary sinus (pre-implant / pre-sinus-lift)
│
├─ Mucosal thickening, mild-moderate (roughly <5mm, localized)
│   → Most common finding (31–47% of implant candidates)
│   → Check for periapical/periodontal source (Küçükkurt 2025: significant association)
│   → Treat odontogenic cause if present; proceed with implant/sinus-lift planning
│   → Not, by itself, a contraindication
│
├─ Dome-shaped lesion (retention cyst / pseudocyst / polyp)
│   → 2nd most common finding (~17% MRC in Salari 2021)
│   → Route to subtype/size-based decision tree —
│      see [[overviews/sinus-lift-pseudocyst-mrc-overview]] for full stratification
│      (Type 1 PsC / Type 2 MRC <20mm / Type 3 MRC >20mm)
│
├─ Discrete, well-circumscribed radiopacity at sinus floor
│   → Differential: antrolith vs. root fragment vs. calcified MRC vs.
│      osteomyelitis vs. ossifying fibroma vs. fungal ball vs. tumour
│   → Asymptomatic + small + no other red flags:
│       → Antrolith likely; surveillance reasonable; does NOT block implant/sinus-lift
│       → Consider histological confirmation if cancer history or diagnostic doubt
│   → Symptomatic (sinusitis, fistula, discharge) or large / inaccessible:
│       → Surgical removal first (Caldwell-Luc ± ESS) + treat infection
│       → Defer implant/sinus-lift until resolved
│
└─ Full/bilateral opacification, invasive-appearing margins, active sinusitis
    signs, or oroantral fistula
    → ENT referral mandatory before any elective sinus/implant surgery
    → Consider MRI ± biopsy if margins are invasive or atypical
    → See [[overviews/odontogenic-maxillary-sinusitis-overview]] for source-control
      → FESS sequencing → OAF closure workflow
```

**Synthesis [claude해석]:** the two prevalence studies establish that finding *some* sinus pathology on a pre-implant CBCT is the statistical norm (roughly 2 in 3 patients), not the exception — so the clinical question is almost never "is there a finding," it is "does this specific finding change the surgical plan." Mucosal thickening under a few millimeters, especially with an identifiable odontogenic cause, is compatible with proceeding. Dome-shaped cystic lesions get routed to the dedicated pseudocyst/MRC decision tree. Antroliths, despite being rare, are the entity most likely to be misread as something more ominous (osteoma, tumour) on imaging alone — their asymptomatic small form is safe to leave alone, but any diagnostic uncertainty or symptom overlap (sinusitis, fistula) should trigger workup before it triggers surgery.

## Evidence Quality — Read the Numbers With Appropriate Caution

| Source | Confidence tier | What that means here |
|---|---|---|
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/kucukkurt-2025-maxillary-sinus-pathologies-cbct-prevalence]] | `retrospective`, n=1,000 | Largest dataset; single-center selection bias (all implant candidates, not general population); cross-sectional so no causal inference despite the regression framing |
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/salari-2021-maxillary-sinus-pathologic-findings-cbct-implant]] | `cross-sectional`, n=140 | Smaller, single-region (northern Iran) cohort; single examiner (no inter-rater calibration data); seasonal collection window may have skewed retention-cyst prevalence |
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/tan-2020-maxillary-antrolith-case-report-management]] | `case-report`, n=1 | **Lowest evidence tier.** Management "algorithm" is a literature-review synthesis of ~30 historical cases, not a controlled comparison |
| [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/shenoy-2013-maxillary-antrolith-recurrent-sinusitis-case]] | `case-report`, n=1 | **Lowest evidence tier.** Single patient; pathogenesis sequence is inferred/narrative, not experimentally established |

**Explicit caveat:** the antrolith "management decision" table in this overview reflects expert narrative consensus distilled from two case reports plus their literature reviews (spanning roughly 30 historical cases total) — it is **not** derived from a systematic review, cohort study, or RCT. Treat the observe-vs-remove framework as a reasonable clinical heuristic, not a validated protocol. Similarly, the two prevalence studies use different mucosal-thickening thresholds (>5mm vs >3mm) and different populations (Iran vs. unspecified single-center), so their percentages should not be pooled or averaged — they are two independent data points bracketing a plausible range, not a meta-analytic estimate.

## Gaps

- No systematic review or meta-analysis yet in the wiki pools prevalence estimates across populations/thresholds for sinus pathology in implant candidates specifically.
- Antrolith evidence base wiki-wide is limited to 2 case reports (n=1 each) — no cohort or case-series data on prevalence, natural history, or recurrence risk after removal in an implant-planning context specifically.
- Neither prevalence study reports antrolith frequency as a distinct category — it is unclear from these two papers alone how often antroliths specifically appear among "opacification"/"other" categories in a general implant-candidate population.
- No data in these 4 papers on outcomes of proceeding with sinus-lift/implant surgery *despite* an untreated antrolith (contrast with the pseudocyst overview's 15-year untreated AP case, [[sinus-lift/pseudocyst/grun-2023-antral-pseudocyst-15year-sinus-implant-case]]) — this is a natural follow-up ingest target.
- No Korean-cohort prevalence data for the general pathology spectrum (contrast with the Korean case anchors available in the pseudocyst overview).

## Bottom Line (한 줄 결론)

[근거강함] Roughly **2 in 3 implant candidates** show some maxillary sinus pathology on CBCT (63.5–68% across two studies), and mucosal thickening — usually of odontogenic origin — accounts for the large majority of it (31–47% depending on threshold). This is the statistical norm, not an alarm signal, and most mild-to-moderate thickening is compatible with proceeding once any dental source is addressed.

[합의수준] Dome-shaped cystic lesions (MRC/pseudocyst, ~17% prevalence) route to the dedicated subtype/size-based decision tree in [[overviews/sinus-lift-pseudocyst-mrc-overview]]. Discrete radiopaque lesions raise a broader differential (antrolith vs. root fragment vs. tumour); small asymptomatic antroliths (prevalence 0.15–3.2%) can be observed and do not block implant/sinus-lift surgery, while symptomatic or large antroliths need surgical removal (Caldwell-Luc ± ESS) before elective sinus surgery proceeds.

[claude해석] Evidence quality is markedly two-tiered here — the prevalence numbers rest on reasonably sized cross-sectional/retrospective studies, but the entire antrolith management framework rests on two case reports (n=1 each) plus their narrative literature reviews. Use the antrolith algorithm as a clinical heuristic, communicate it to patients as such, and treat any diagnostic ambiguity (invasive margins, malignancy history, atypical CT appearance) as a trigger for histological confirmation or ENT referral rather than assumption.
