## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 범위: 투명교정(Clear Aligner Therapy, CAT)의 골격성 부정교합(Class II·III)과 상악 확장을 모체 [[overviews/clear-aligner-indications-limitations]]에서 분리한 하위 종합. 절 번호(2·3·5)는 모체의 axis 번호를 그대로 유지한다. [확인]
> - Class II 구치 원심이동: 투명교정으로 최대 약 2 mm 가능하나 전치부 고정 소실(anterior anchorage loss)은 매우 가능성이 높다. 예측성은 36–78%(Bhate 2025, 11편)·31–88%(Badia 2026, 16편, 461명). [확인]
> - Class II 보완: 골격 고정원(TAD)과 IPR을 병용하는 하이브리드가 예측성·안정성을 높이며, 큰 overjet에서 특히 권장된다(Inchingolo 2023, 19편). [확인]
> - 하악전방이동(MA) 투명교정: 세 SR+MA(Yu 2023, Huang 2026, Sohn 2025)가 기능성 장치와 골격 Class II 교정이 비슷하다고 본다. 하악 전치 조절은 더 낫지만, 하악 ramus 성장 차이(Yu, 1.10 mm)를 보면 골격 성장 효과보다 치아·자세 효과가 크다. [확인]
> - Class III 근거 수준: 성인 단일 증례(Zhang 2025, 28세)를 포함해 증례 수준이 주류다. 전 방식 Class III 위장술 근거의 86%가 4–5등급이라 이는 투명교정만의 공백이 아니라 분야 전체의 기저선이다(Rutili 2026, 128편). [확인]
> - Class III 직접 비교: 후향 연구(Panda 2025, n=50)에서 투명교정은 기간이 짧고(18 vs 22개월) 순응도가 높았지만(92% vs 75%), 고정장치가 골격 ANB 개선이 컸다(1.5 vs 0.8°). [확인]
> - 수술 후 투명교정: SR(Alghafli 2026, 10편, RCT 2편, n=258)은 골격·치아 안정성이 고정장치와 비슷하고, 삶의 질(OHIP-14, p=0.004)은 투명교정이 우위라고 보고한다. [확인]
> - 혼합치열 교차교합(pseudo-Class III): RCT(Gok 2025, n=30)에서 두 방식 모두 교정에 성공했고, Z-spring이 더 빨랐다(48 vs 96일). 투명교정은 치열궁 깊이를 늘린다. [확인]
> - 상악 확장: 성장기 투명교정 확장은 전통 확장장치보다 작고(intermolar −1.77 mm, Fonseca-Planells 2026) 주로 치아성(dentoalveolar)이라, 진짜 골격성 횡적 결손에는 한계가 명확하다. [확인]
> - 확장 예측성: 성인 치아 부위별 예측성은 소구치 약 80%, 견치·대구치 약 72%(Xianggang 2026, 233명). 영구치열 SR+MA(Serafin 2026, 23편, 1,391명)는 상악 75.5%, 하악 80.6%. [확인]
> - 혼합치열 확장: Invisalign First 예측성은 상악 65%·하악 71%(Serafin 2026, 9편, 이질적 예비 합산)이며, 계획량과 예측성 사이에 용량-반응이 없었다. 2년 무보정 추적(Gazzani 2026, n=20)에서 측방 확장은 대체로 유지되었다. [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 골격 교정은 "증례 수준 근거 + MA 장치와의 부분적 동등성"이라는 위치이고, 확장은 치아성 확장이므로 골격성 결손에는 쓰지 않는다. 예측성 수치는 상담용 기준치로만 쓰고 보장치로 말하지 않는다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the skeletal-correction and expansion sections of the parent clear-aligner synthesis: Class II (molar distalization and mandibular advancement), Class III (camouflage and growth modification), and maxillary expansion.
Evidence is strongest for Class II mandibular-advancement aligners (three SR+MAs), case-level for Class III (with the field-wide baseline at 86% level 4–5), and quantified for expansion through region-stratified predictability of roughly 65–81%.
Treat expansion as mainly dentoalveolar, Class III as case-level in informed consent, and predictability figures as planning benchmarks, not guarantees.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 투명교정 종합의 골격 교정·확장 절을 담는다: Class II(구치 원심이동과 하악전방이동), Class III(위장술과 성장 조절), 상악 확장.
근거는 Class II 하악전방이동 장치에서 가장 강하고(SR+MA 3편), Class III는 증례 수준이며(분야 전체 기저선 86%가 4–5등급), 확장은 부위별 예측성 약 65–81%로 정량화된다.
확장은 주로 치아성이고, Class III는 동의서에서 증례 수준으로 설명하며, 예측성 수치는 보장치가 아니라 계획용 기준치로 읽는다.

### 2. Class II correction — two aligner strategies

**Molar distalization**: achievable with aligners (up to ~2 mm, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bhate-2025-cat-maxillary-molar-distalization-class-ii-sr]], 11 Invisalign studies, achieved/predicted 36–78%) but **anterior anchorage loss is highly probable**; predictability and stability improve markedly with skeletal anchorage (TADs) and IPR — a hybrid approach is best, especially with large overjet ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/inchingolo-2023-clear-aligner-distalization-predictability-sr]], 19 studies). The broadest SR to date ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/badia-2026-maxillary-molar-distalization-clear-aligner-sr]], 16 studies, 461 patients, search to April 2026) confirms the 31–88% predictability range, formalizes the inverse relationship between prescribed movement magnitude and predictability, and rates overall GRADE certainty as **very low** for both outcomes — the first SR in this subfield to apply GRADE; meta-analysis was not possible due to substantial heterogeneity in outcome definitions, measurement methods, and timing of assessment. Longer aligner-change intervals (14 days) were associated with higher predictability in several of its included studies, though evidence is insufficient for a definitive recommendation. A biomechanical constraint often overlooked at the treatment-planning stage: a fully-erupted **third molar** reduces second-molar distalization efficiency by 10–22% and raises aligner/attachment stress (FEA evidence, axis 4) — worth screening for before staging a distalization case. A dedicated SR of temporary anchorage devices in CAT ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/marinelli-2025-temporary-anchorage-devices-clear-aligner-sr]], PRISMA, 14 studies, mostly case reports; ROBINS-I 1 low/9 some-concerns/4 high) confirms that adding TADs/miniscrews improves control of distalization, intrusion, extrusion and vertical movements, but the evidence is **low-certainty** with no controlled comparisons — the hybrid concept is sound, the quantitative backing is not yet. A direct clinical consequence of this anchorage-loss risk is now quantified: a retrospective study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/shi-2026-pre-treatment-associated-factors]], n=75) found **17.3% incidence of open gingival embrasure (OGE, "black triangle")** of the maxillary central incisors after sequential molar distalization, with age, anteroposterior overlap, ANB angle, and U1-NA angle as independent pre-treatment predictors (AUC 0.839) — a candidate chairside risk-screening tool, pending external validation.

A retrospective iTero digital-model study confirms the tipping-dominated mechanism at the individual-tooth level: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/liu-2025-maxillary-molar-distalization-invisalign-adult]] (n=12 adults, mean 31.8y, 19 U6 + 19 U7, T0/T1 active + T2 short-term follow-up) measured Invisalign distal arch expansion with Class II elastics. Upper first-molar (U6) achieved **1.71±0.89 mm distalization** accompanied by −5.20±2.62° distal tipping; upper second-molar (U7) achieved 2.04±1.16 mm with −5.35±4.19° tipping. Distopalatal rotation of U6 was +2.83±2.45°. Despite Class II elastics, **anterior anchorage loss occurred**: U1 sagittal displacement −0.88±0.99 mm and torque +3.58±4.69°, confirming that elastics did not fully prevent anchorage escape. At T2, U7 showed a minor partial positional rebound of −0.21 mm (NS) representing progressive anchorage compromise during the U6 movement phase. This is the smallest and most individual-level of the distalization studies in this wiki, but it directly quantifies the tipping-first mechanism that Bhate 2025's SR and Inchingolo 2023's SR describe at the aggregate level — tipping/rotation magnitude (~5°) at clinically "acceptable" distalization distances is consistent across studies, and anchorage loss persists even with Class II elastic use, reinforcing that TADs are needed for bodily distalization rather than tipping control.

**Mandibular advancement (MA)**: three SR+MAs now converge — [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/yu-2023-mandibular-advancement-aligner-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] (9 controlled trials, n=283), the larger [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/huang-2026-clear-aligner-mandibular-advancement-vs-functional-class-ii-sr-ma]] (9 studies, n=465), and [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/sohn-2025-clear-functional-vs-traditional-fa-class-ii-sr-ma]] (5 non-RCT studies, n=166; Korean J Orthod; van Beek included as comparator) — all find MA aligners give skeletal Class II correction **comparable to traditional functional appliances** (Twin Block/Herbst/van Beek; SNA/SNB/ANB n.s.) while **better controlling lower-incisor proclination** (Huang IMPA MD −0.90°; Yu 1.94° less) — i.e. superior sagittal dental control but a more dental/postural than skeletal-growth effect (Yu: ramus growth Co-Go 1.10 mm less). Sohn-2025 additionally confirms equivalence on Wits appraisal, mandibular plane angle, overjet, and mandibular length, with a slight (NS) trend favoring CA on vertical control.

### 3. Class III correction — camouflage & growth-modification (evidence now maturing beyond case reports)

Unlike Class II, where SR+MA-level evidence exists (axis 2), the wiki's Class III aligner evidence started from two single case reports bracketing the age spectrum and has since gained a narrative-review umbrella plus its first controlled comparative studies. In an **adult**, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/zhang-2025-clear-aligner-based-multidisciplinary]] (n=1, 28-year-old, skeletal Class III just outside surgical thresholds — Wits −4.92 mm, Holdaway 11° vs. cutoffs of −5.8 mm/10.3°) achieved non-surgical camouflage via aligner-driven bite opening (mandibular incisor intrusion/retroclination + maxillary proclination), Class III elastics, and molar-attachment bite ramps, combined with extraction/implant replacement of a compromised incisor; the result was **stable at 7-year follow-up** — the longest reported CAT-only Class III camouflage interval identified in this wiki. In a **growing patient**, [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/ye-2025-combined-use-of-miniscrews]] (n=1, 11-year-old, unilateral posterior scissor bite + skeletal Class III) combined facemask maxillary protraction with clear aligners (attachments used as the anchorage interface for facemask elastics) and posterior miniscrew anchorage/intrusion, correcting the scissor bite and occlusal cant over 17 months — illustrating CAT's auxiliary role (anchorage interface, occlusal-plane correction) alongside orthopedic appliances, rather than as the primary corrective mechanism, complementing the dental/postural (not skeletal) profile seen for Class II MA aligners above.

A SANRA-compliant narrative review spanning all adult Class III camouflage modalities ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/rutili-2026-class-iii-camouflage-modalities-narrative-review]], 128 studies: 110 case reports/series + 18 observational) now contextualizes those two case reports within the field as a whole: **86% of all Class III camouflage evidence — across every modality, not just aligners — is level 4–5** (case report/small series), so the wiki's case-report-heavy aligner evidence is not an aligner-specific evidence gap but the field's baseline. Within that landscape, CAT-specific lower-arch distalization achieves only 2–3 mm with ~74% crown-level but just ~47% root-level efficiency (predominantly tipping, not bodily movement — the same crown-vs-root discrepancy pattern seen in axis 1's extraction data), and overall Class III camouflage relapse runs ~30% with extraction-based protocols showing superior long-term stability over non-extraction. The review's verdict: aligner-based Class III camouflage suits mild-to-moderate, low/normo-angle, high-compliance adults, but its torque/vertical/root-level limitations mean it is not interchangeable with fixed-appliance or TAD-assisted approaches for more severe or bodily-movement-heavy cases — directly echoing axis 1's torque/rotation findings (Ubuzima, Gonçalves) in a Class III-specific context.

The field's first head-to-head comparative data have also arrived. A retrospective study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/panda-2025-clear-aligner-braces-class-iii-comparison]], n=50, 25 CAT vs 25 fixed braces, mild-to-moderate Angle Class III) found CAT achieved shorter treatment duration (18±3 vs 22±4 months) and higher compliance (92% vs 75%) with comparable overjet correction, but **fixed braces achieved significantly greater skeletal (ANB) improvement** (1.5±0.5° vs 0.8±0.3°) and needed less refinement — a direct quantitative echo of Rutili's root/skeletal-control limitation. A prospective controlled study in the surgical end of the spectrum ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/cunha-2025-class-iii-surgery-first-aligner-fixed-ohrqol]], n=34, CA 20 vs FA 14, Surgery-First Approach bimaxillary orthognathic surgery) found **no significant between-group difference in OHRQoL trajectory** (OQLQ, OHIP-14, SF-36) through 6 months post-surgery — suggesting that once skeletal correction is handled surgically, appliance choice (aligner vs fixed) for the surgery-first orthodontic phase does not differentially affect patient-reported quality of life, though small unequal groups and non-randomized allocation limit strength.

The growing-patient side gains its first RCT-level counterpart to Ye 2025's case report, though for a narrower **isolated dental (pseudo-Class III)** presentation rather than true skeletal Class III: an RCT in mixed dentition ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/gok-2025-clear-aligner-z-spring-anterior-crossbite-mixed-dentition]], n=30, 7–12y, Angle Class I molar relationship + isolated anterior crossbite) compared clear aligners against a Z-spring appliance. Both corrected the crossbite in all patients with comparable OHRQoL, but the Z-spring was markedly faster (48.4±27 vs 96.3±22.7 days) while CAT alone produced significant arch-depth increases — consistent with a tipping-dominant (Z-spring) vs. tipping+alignment+bodily-movement (CAT) mechanism difference; this is abstract-only (methodological detail unconfirmed) and notably a dental pseudo-Class III rather than the skeletal cases the rest of this axis addresses. Taken together, the four new studies do not overturn the case-report-era conclusion that CAT is feasible for mild-to-moderate Class III camouflage while ceding skeletal correction to fixed appliances/surgery — they substantiate it with the field's first comparative numbers, while confirming (via Rutili's 86% figure) that Class III camouflage evidence overall, aligner or otherwise, remains structurally thin.

Post-orthognathic surgery — the furthest end of the skeletal-intervention spectrum — now has its own dedicated SR for the aligner phase: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/alghafli-2026-postsurgical-aligners-sr]] (10 studies including 2 RCTs, n=258). Post-surgical CAT showed **skeletal and dental stability comparable to fixed appliances** at short-term follow-up; the two RCTs found CA significantly better on OHIP-14 (p=0.004) and OQLQ-22 (p=0.002) as well as periodontal health markers; a Surgery-First protocol with CA achieved 18.05 vs 22.83 months total treatment time (p<0.05). This complements Cunha 2025 (which found no OHRQoL trajectory difference for Class III surgery-first CA vs FA at the 6-month mark) by extending the post-surgical evidence base to orthognathic patients generally, adding stability data, and providing time-to-completion figures. The combined reading: **post-surgical CA is a viable alternative to fixed appliances** on stability, OHRQoL, and treatment-duration grounds, with the caveat that the non-RCT majority of alghafli's evidence base and short follow-up window mean this is an encouraging but not yet SR+MA-level certainty finding.

### 5. Maxillary expansion

In growing patients, CAT can expand the maxilla but **significantly less than conventional expanders** ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/fonseca-planells-2026-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-growing]], 15 studies/7 pooled: intermolar width −1.77 mm, palatal volume −460.6 mm³, arch perimeter −1.75 mm favoring conventional), and the expansion is **mainly dentoalveolar** (tipping of anterior/deciduous teeth) rather than skeletal — a clear limitation for true transverse skeletal deficiency. A prospective multicenter cohort with a predefined non-inferiority design ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/kim-2026-efficacy-and-stability-of]], n=48 growing patients, CA vs. slow maxillary expander/SME) **refines** this SR+MA picture: CA was **not inferior** to SME on intermolar expansion and showed *greater* canine cusp-tip expansion, but the CA group alone showed significant buccal molar tipping relapse during retention (ΔMI −7.1±9.8°) — i.e., for milder, growing-patient cases CA can "catch up" on total expansion magnitude while still moving via more tipping/less bodily translation than SME, a mechanistic nuance rather than a contradiction of the SR+MA's dentoalveolar-vs-skeletal thesis. In adults the limitation is one of *predictability*: a retrospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/de-la-rosa-gay-2025-expansion-predictability-clear-aligner]], 98 patients, multilevel GLMM) found a mean Invisalign expansion error of **0.92 mm with 72.2% of sites under-expanded**, worsening in the maxilla, on posterior teeth, in crossbite cases and as the planned expansion magnitude increases — so planned expansion should be over-corrected and concentrated anteriorly.

The first SR+MA to quantify Invisalign adult expansion predictability by individual tooth region provides complementary, and slightly more optimistic, benchmarks: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/xianggang-2026-predictability-maxillary-transverse-changes-invisalign]] (PROSPERO CRD42024584778; 6 retrospective studies, 233 adults; search Jan 2014–Aug 2024; GRADE assessed per tooth) found region-stratified predictability of: **canine 72.01%** (95% CI 59.67–84.35%, I²=74.7%, GRADE low); **first premolar 80.73%** (73.78–87.68%, I²=43.5%, GRADE high — the most reliably predicted tooth); **second premolar 78.74%** (70.41–87.08%, I²=57.4%, GRADE moderate); **first molar 71.57%** (62.41–80.73%, I²=50.2%, GRADE moderate). The per-tooth achieved-to-planned ratio was ~0.72–0.81 mm per 1 mm planned across the arch — consistent with De La Rosa-Gay's 0.92 mm mean error finding and with Bruni 2024's 0.6 mm/mm at the permanent molar in mixed dentition. Together with Bruni 2024's anterior-to-posterior accuracy gradient, this SR+MA confirms that **premolars are the most reliable expansion targets** (first premolar at GRADE high), while canines and first molars need the most overcorrection — a region-stratified perspective that de-la-rosa-gay's overall-mean-error framing did not provide.

A younger, more homogeneous slice of the growing-patient population — **mixed dentition** specifically, rather than "growing patients" broadly — is quantified by a prospective cohort ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/bruni-2024-clear-aligner-maxillary-expansion-mixed-dentition]], n=40, mean age 8.7y, Invisalign First, transverse discrepancy ≤5 mm). It refines Kim 2026's non-inferiority finding with region-stratified accuracy: expansion accuracy declined anterior→posterior (deciduous canine 87.7% cusp-level → permanent first molar 67.9%), and regression showed only ~0.6 mm of clinical expression per 1 mm planned (overall 80.2% of target) — with cusp-level accuracy consistently exceeding gingival-level accuracy, fingerprinting **buccal crown tipping** as the dominant mechanism at the molar, not bodily translation. Practical corollary: plan roughly 7 mm of ClinCheck expansion to net 5 mm clinically at the permanent molar in mixed-dentition patients, and reserve rapid maxillary expansion (RME) for cases requiring a true sutural/orthopedic effect.

The first SR+MA specific to **Invisalign First mixed-dentition expansion** provides pooled predictability benchmarks: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/serafin-2026-invisalign-first-mixed-dentition-expansion-sr-ma]] (9 studies; PROSPERO) found maxillary expansion predictability of **65%** and mandibular of **71%**, with notably **no dose-response between the amount of planned expansion and achieved predictability** — larger planned amounts were not less accurately achieved in the Invisalign First age range, unlike the adult pattern (de-la-rosa-gay 2025) where predictability worsens with magnitude. This is a preliminary pooling from 9 heterogeneous studies, and should be read against the Mathew 2026 umbrella review's finding that growing-patient evidence overall is critically-low to low quality. Clinically: the ~65–71% mixed-dentition benchmark is more optimistic than adult first-molar predictability (Bruni: 67.9%), consistent with deciduous dentition's shorter roots and more tippable anatomy; the absence of a dose-response suggests that mixed-dentition overcorrection planning can follow a fixed multiplier rather than a magnitude-adjusted one.

The **post-treatment stability** of Invisalign First expansion in mixed dentition is addressed by the first prospective follow-up with a 2-year horizon and no retainer: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/gazzani-2026-mixed-dentition-2year-stability]] (n=20, Invisalign First, Herbst-absence design — tracked without retainer for 2 years post-treatment). Lateral expansion, derotation, and alignment achieved during active treatment were largely stable across the follow-up window; the only statistically significant regression was mandibular first-molar torque (mean −1.1°), while Little's Irregularity Index held at 0.4 mm from post-treatment through 2 years. This is the first prospective retention-phase dataset for Invisalign First, and it is cautiously optimistic — noting that the mandibular molar torque regression mirrors exactly the movement category Bruni 2024 identified as least accurately expressed at the permanent first molar (67.9%), suggesting a buccal-crown-tipping mechanism that is both the hardest to achieve and the first to partially rebound. Practical corollary for both expansion and retention planning: any residual over-tipping in the active phase will likely recover; any under-tipping will likely relapse.

The largest SR+MA on this topic to date — [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/serafin-2026-invisalign-expansion-permanent-dentition-sr-ma]] (PROSPERO CRD420251123184; 23 studies, 1391 patients; search through November 2025) — extends xianggang-2026 to both arches, using logit-transformed proportions in random-effects models with multivariable regression. Maxillary predictability averaged 75.5% (premolars 81–83%, canines/1st molars ~76–78%, 2nd molars 78%); mandibular predictability averaged 80.6% (canines/premolars 86–88%, 2nd molars 73% — least reliable site in either arch). In tooth groups with both cusp-tip and gingival-margin data, gingival predictability was consistently ~13.5 percentage points lower (~83% of cusp-level), providing the first pooled evidence that expansion is dominated by buccal crown tipping rather than bodily displacement. Multivariable regression (R²=0.524) identified three independent predictors of higher predictability: younger age (−1.1%/year, p=0.001), SmartTrack/LD30 material (+32.7% vs EX30, p<0.001), and mandibular arch (+13.1% vs maxilla, p=0.001) — while aligner-change interval and tooth type were non-significant. A notable meta-regression finding is that more recent publications trend toward lower predictability (β=−0.150, p=0.010), likely reflecting improved methodological rigor rather than deteriorating clinical outcomes. Wide prediction intervals (roughly 4–99% across tooth groups), high residual heterogeneity (I²>84%), significant publication bias (Egger/Begg p<0.001), and GRADE very low certainty across both arches temper the pooled estimates — absolute numbers should be used as benchmarks, not as guarantees. Together, xianggang-2026 and serafin-2026 converge on the same clinical rules: premolars are the most reliable expansion targets; molars and canines need the most overcorrection; overcorrection should be selectively applied per arch, age, and material; and large expansions (>3–4 mm) warrant CBCT to assess the alveolar envelope before planning.

Stepping back from expansion specifically to the full clinical outcome picture for growing children, the first JBI-methodology umbrella review covering all outcome domains arrives: [[orthodontics/clear-aligner/mathew-2026-clear-aligners-growing-children-umbrella-review]] (Mathew 2026; 1,609 records → 6 SRs; PROSPERO CRD420251054252; J Indian Soc Pedod Prev Dent 2026;44:359–373). AMSTAR-2 rated 5/6 SRs critically low and 1/6 low, with a high corrected covered area signalling substantial primary-study overlap across the included reviews. The headline finding is what the expansion data above already implies: **dentoalveolar correction (alignment, overjet reduction, arch development) is the consistent, reproducible outcome** across all six reviews. Skeletal evidence is discordant — 2 SRs reported possible mandibular changes, 3 found minimal or no skeletal effects, and 1 found insufficient evidence — while meta-analytic pooling of ANB, SNA, and SNB showed low heterogeneity (favouring pooling quality, not effect magnitude, given the critically-low underlying studies). Periodontal outcomes and patient-reported outcomes were each addressed by only 2 SRs, precluding any conclusion. The umbrella review's message for clinical practice: offer CA in growing patients as a **predictable dentoalveolar tool**, set appropriate expectations about the skeletal ceiling (which the expansion SR+MA data above quantify more precisely), and do not extrapolate from this evidence base to claim meaningful skeletal modification or long-term periodontal benefit — the methodological quality is too low and the domains too underreported.

A measurement-validity question that is often conflated with achievability is addressed by a method-agreement study ([[orthodontics/clear-aligner/de-la-rosa-gay-2026-arch-width-measurements-invisalign-tables]], n=35 adult Invisalign patients, 840 paired measurements, mixed-effects Bland-Altman analysis): the **ClinCheck arch-width measurement tables are a reliable measurement tool** — the occlusal-view bias was 0.03 mm and limits of agreement ±0.8 mm, confirming that what the software *measures* about the planned arch width is accurate. This is a measurement-validity finding, not a treatment-efficacy finding: it does not contradict de-la-rosa-gay-2025's 0.92 mm mean under-expansion or xianggang-2026's 72–81% per-tooth predictability — those studies show that *planned expansion is not fully achieved*, while this study shows that *the planning table accurately reflects what is being planned*. The two findings coexist and clarify separate questions: clinicians can trust the ClinCheck width readout as a planning tool, but should still apply overcorrection per de-la-rosa-gay-2025 and xianggang-2026 benchmarks because the gap is in expansion achievement, not in measurement.
