> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 이 페이지는 치과불안(dental anxiety)·치과공포증(dental phobia)의 **비약물적(non-pharmacological)** 관리법 중 서로 다른 기전을 가진 두 축 — 최면(hypnosis)과 가상현실(virtual reality, VR) — 을 비교 종합한다.
> - **최면 (Cernega 2022, SR+MA)**: 성인 19편(1979–2021) 종합, 근거는 "유망하지만 비일관적". 메타분석은 STAI 사용 5편만 가능했고 효과크기 범위가 −4.30~6.20으로 이질성이 매우 커 **신뢰할 만한 pooled effect 없음**. 오히려 인지행동치료(CBT)가 가장 일관된 효과를 보임.
> - **VR — 소아 (Barros-Padilha 2023, SR)**: 22편 RCT(총 2,558명, 5–12세) 종합, 몰입형 3D VR이 불안·통증을 유의하게 감소시켰고 **전통적 Tell-Show-Do보다 VR이 열등했던 연구는 단 하나도 없음**. 단, 일부 연구에서 태블릿/화면 주의분산이 VR과 동등하거나 더 나았음(특히 하치조신경 블록 시).
> - **VR — 성인 임플란트 (Ghobadi 2024, RCT)**: n=73 교차설계(환자 자신이 대조군), 고해상도 Oculus Quest 2 자연영상 VR이 경험 통증(NRS −1.71)·상태불안(STAI-S −3.45)·치과불안(MDAS −4.55)을 유의하게 낮췄고(전부 p<0.001) EMG·심박·GSR 등 생리지표로도 검증됨. 90.4%가 재사용 희망.
> - **기전 차이**: 최면=인지적 재구성/이완(top-down, 치료자 또는 표준화 오디오 유도 필요) vs VR=주의분산(attentional distraction)·몰입 노출(immersive exposure)(장비만 있으면 즉시 적용 가능, 치료자 훈련 불요).
> - **근거 등급**: 최면(SR+MA, 그러나 pooled effect 도출 실패) < VR-소아(SR, 22 RCT, 일관된 방향성) ≈ VR-성인(단일 RCT지만 교차설계+생리지표 이중검증으로 강건).
> - **연령대**: 최면 근거는 성인 중심(포함 19편 중 2편만 소아), VR 근거는 소아(Barros-Padilha)와 성인(Ghobadi) 양쪽에 걸쳐 있어 상대적으로 전 연령 적용 근거가 더 두텁다.
> - **실무 트레이드오프**: VR은 헤드셋 구입·위생·소아 연령 제한(제조사 권장 12–13세 이상, 그러나 연구 대부분 그 이하에서 오프라벨 사용)·시술자 시야/접근성 방해(Ghobadi에서 술자 불편도 유의하게 증가)라는 비용이 있고, 최면은 숙련된 시술자/표준화 오디오 필요 + 효과의 개인차(피암시성)가 크다.
> - **임상 의사결정 프레임**: (1) 급성 시술 중 주의분산이 필요하고 헤드셋 사용 가능 연령/협조도라면 → VR 우선, (2) 만성적·범불안 성향의 치과공포증으로 반복 내원이 필요하다면 → CBT ± 최면(오디오 자가최면부터 시작), (3) 아주 짧은 처치나 술자 접근성이 중요한 부위(예: 후방 임플란트, 신경블록)라면 → 태블릿/화면 주의분산 같은 저비용 대안도 VR과 대등할 수 있음.
> - **핵심 한계**: 세 논문 모두 척도 이질성(DAS/VAS/STAI/MCDAS 등)으로 인해 직접적 head-to-head 메타비교는 불가능하며, VR의 소아 오프라벨 사용과 cybersickness 미평가는 안전성 신호로 남아있다.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 3 papers comparing two non-pharmacological dental anxiety management mechanisms — hypnosis (Cernega 2022, SR+MA, 19 adult trials, 1979–2021) and immersive virtual-reality (VR) distraction (Barros-Padilha 2023, SR, 22 pediatric RCTs, n=2,558 ages 5–12; Ghobadi 2024, RCT, n=73 adult implant patients, crossover): hypnosis shows a promising but statistically inconclusive effect (pooled effect size range −4.30 to 6.20, very high heterogeneity, only 5 STAI-eligible trials), with CBT outperforming it on consistency.

Immersive VR produces a more consistent and physiologically corroborated anxiety/pain-reduction signal — no pediatric RCT found conventional non-screen techniques superior to VR (Barros-Padilha 2023); in adults undergoing implant surgery, VR reduced experienced pain (NRS −1.71), state anxiety (STAI-S −3.45), and dental anxiety (MDAS −4.55), all p<0.001, corroborated by EMG/EKG/GSR biofeedback, with 90.4% wanting to reuse it (Ghobadi 2024).

The practical tradeoffs favor VR for acute procedural distraction (headset available, patient age-appropriate) but require caution in surgical contexts where headset bulk measurably increases operator distress; hypnosis/CBT suits chronic phobia with repeated visits; when operator access is paramount (posterior implants, nerve blocks), screen-based distraction without a headset may be equally effective and operationally simpler.

## 세줄요약

비약물적 치과불안 관리 3편 종합(최면 vs 가상현실): 최면(Hypnosis, Cernega 2022, SR+MA, 성인 19편)은 유망하나 pooled 효과크기 범위 −4.30~6.20으로 통계적 결론 미도출(이질성 매우 높음, STAI 사용 5편만 메타분석 가능) — CBT가 더 일관된 효과.

몰입형 VR 주의분산(Virtual Reality, VR)은 더 일관적이고 생리지표로 검증된 신호: 소아 22 RCT(Barros-Padilha 2023, n=2,558) 어느 것도 전통 비화면 기법이 VR보다 우월하지 않았음; 성인 임플란트 교차설계 RCT(Ghobadi 2024, n=73)에서 VR이 경험 통증(NRS −1.71)·상태불안(STAI-S −3.45)·치과불안(MDAS −4.55) 모두 p<0.001 감소, EMG/EKG/GSR 생리지표 검증, 재사용 희망 90.4%.

임상 의사결정: 급성 시술 중 주의분산 → VR 우선; 만성 치과공포증 반복 내원 → CBT ± 최면; 술자 접근성 중요 부위(후방 임플란트·신경블록) → 헤드셋 없는 태블릿/화면 주의분산도 VR과 대등 가능 — VR 헤드셋은 수술 상황에서 술자 불편을 유의하게 증가시킨다(Ghobadi 2024).

## Summary

Dental anxiety and phobia are managed along a spectrum from purely psychological/cognitive techniques (hypnosis, CBT, relaxation) to technology-mediated attentional interventions (VR distraction/exposure), before pharmacological sedation is even considered. This overview synthesizes the wiki's evidence on the two non-pharmacological poles: **hypnosis** — a top-down cognitive-reframing/relaxation technique requiring either a trained hypnotist or a standardized audio induction — and **VR-based distraction** — a bottom-up attentional-capture/immersive-exposure technique requiring only a headset and content. The three papers span the evidence-quality spectrum (SR+MA → SR → single RCT) and two age groups (adult-only hypnosis evidence; VR evidence in both pediatric and adult populations), allowing a genuine head-to-head comparison of mechanism, population, effect size, and evidence quality — followed by a practical decision framework.

## Key Contributions of the Synthesis

- **Mechanism contrast made explicit**: hypnosis operates via cognitive reframing and induced relaxation, modulating threat appraisal and possibly limbic activation (fMRI: reduced amygdala/ACC/insula/hippocampal activity in phobic subjects under hypnosis, per Cernega 2022); VR operates via competitive attentional capture and immersive exposure to a pleasant virtual environment, modulating in-the-moment pain/anxiety perception rather than underlying threat appraisal or trait anxiety (Ghobadi 2024 found VR affected *state* anxiety/*experienced* pain but not *trait* anxiety or *expected* pain).
- **Evidence-quality asymmetry**: hypnosis has the more rigorous meta-analytic attempt (Cernega 2022, PRISMA/PROSPERO SR+MA) yet fails to produce a reliable pooled effect; VR's pediatric evidence (Barros-Padilha 2023) is "only" a systematic review without meta-analysis, yet the narrative direction is far more consistent (no included pediatric study found conventional non-screen techniques superior to VR).
- **Population complementarity**: hypnosis evidence is almost entirely adult (17/19 studies); VR evidence covers both pediatric (Barros-Padilha, ages ~5–12) and adult (Ghobadi, mean age 44.3) populations, and in the adult case for a specifically surgical, high-anxiety procedure (implant placement) rather than routine restorative care.
- **Physiological corroboration differs**: Cernega 2022 cites only one included study with physiological confirmation (reduced diastolic BP/HR under hypnotherapy); Ghobadi 2024 built physiological validation into the primary design (10-channel EMG/EKG/GSR biofeedback), giving VR's adult effect a stronger objective-measure backbone.
- **Practical deployability trade-offs surfaced**: audiotape self-hypnosis needs no trained hypnotist but is less effective than live individualized hypnosis; VR needs no behavioral-therapy training but does need hardware, hygiene protocols, and — in Ghobadi's surgical context — measurably increases operator (surgeon) distress due to headset bulk impeding access.

## Mechanism Comparison

| Dimension | Hypnosis (Cernega 2022) | VR distraction (Barros-Padilha 2023 / Ghobadi 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Core mechanism | Cognitive reframing + induced relaxation; hypnotic suggestion reduces limbic threat response | Attentional distraction / immersive exposure; competes for attention with the noxious stimulus |
| What it modulates | Anticipatory/trait anxiety, subjective threat appraisal | In-the-moment state anxiety and experienced pain during the procedure itself |
| Delivery requirement | Trained hypnotist (most effective) OR standardized audiotape self-hypnosis (most practical, less effective) | Headset + content; no specialist training required |
| Neuro/physio evidence | fMRI: reduced amygdala, ACC, insula, hippocampus activation (phobic subjects); one study showed reduced BP/HR | EMG, EKG (HR/HRV), GSR consistently corroborate self-report reductions (Ghobadi) |
| Timing of effect | Can be used pre-procedurally (anticipatory anxiety) and intra-procedurally | Primarily intra-procedural / perioperative (during the noxious stimulus) |

## Effect Sizes and Outcome Numbers

**Hypnosis (Cernega 2022)**
- Meta-analysis (5 STAI studies only, due to scale heterogeneity): effect size range **−4.30 to 6.20**, high heterogeneity → **no reliable pooled effect established**.
- Narrative: audiotape self-hypnosis showed significant anxiety reduction in 7/10 studies using it (1 marginal).
- **CBT** (a comparator technique within the same review) showed the most consistent efficacy signal of all reviewed interventions, including one-session formats.
- One study: reduced diastolic BP and heart rate intraoperatively with hypnotherapy vs increases in controls.

**VR — pediatric (Barros-Padilha 2023)**
- VR's benefit on anxiety (16 studies measuring it) and pain (12 studies) was statistically significant vs control in most included trials; **no study found conventional non-screen techniques superior to VR**.
- Physiological/behavioral corroboration: reduced salivary cortisol (p<0.001), shorter treatment time, less anxiety in MIH-affected children with 3D VR games (p<0.05).
- Notable exceptions where VR did *not* win: Felemban et al. (VR ≈ screen distraction on HR/pain during buccal infiltration); Al-Halabi (tablets > VR during inferior alveolar nerve block); Alshatrat et al. (no significant pain benefit in non-painful procedures).

**VR — adult implant surgery (Ghobadi 2024)**, all VR vs no-VR, GEE-modeled:
- Experienced pain (NRS): **−1.71**, p<0.001 (expected/pre-op pain: not significant, p=0.526)
- State anxiety (STAI-S): **−3.45**, p<0.001 (trait anxiety STAI-T: not significant, p=0.382)
- Dental anxiety (MDAS): **−4.55**, p<0.001
- Patient satisfaction: **+1.14**, p<0.001
- Physiological: heart rate **−10.3**, EMG RMS amplitude **−14.2**, GSR RMS **−5.4** (all p<0.001)
- Surgeon distress (Treatment Distress Rating): **+3.45 (worse)**, p<0.001 — the key operator-side cost
- **90.4%** of patients wanted to reuse VR at future visits

## Evidence Quality Assessment

- **Hypnosis** has the nominally "higher" evidence-synthesis method (SR+MA with PROSPERO registration), but the meta-analysis itself fails — 15 different anxiety-measurement methods forced pooling down to just 5 STAI studies, and even that pool shows non-reliable, wide-ranging effect sizes. The review's own bottom line favors **CBT** over hypnosis for consistency.
- **VR in pediatric dentistry** (SR without meta-analysis) has *no* pooled statistic either (same scale-heterogeneity problem: CPMAS/MCDAS/FIS/VPT for anxiety, WBFS/VAS/FLACC/MBPS for pain), but the qualitative direction across 22 independent RCTs is unusually consistent — a stronger narrative signal than hypnosis's SR+MA despite the "lower" study-design label.
- **VR in adult implant surgery** is a single RCT, but its crossover (within-subject) design and dual self-report + multi-channel physiological validation make it a methodologically strong data point, comparable in rigor to a well-controlled trial embedded within a systematic review.
- **Bottom line on evidence weight**: VR (across both pediatric SR and adult RCT) currently has a more consistent — though not meta-analytically pooled — effect than hypnosis, whose SR+MA explicitly could not establish a reliable pooled effect and flagged CBT as the stronger non-pharmacological comparator.

## Practical Decision Framework

1. **Intra-procedural distraction need, patient age/cooperation allows headset use** → reach for **VR**. Evidence is consistent in both pediatric (Barros-Padilha) and adult surgical (Ghobadi) contexts, with physiological corroboration in the adult data.
2. **Discrete, short painful stimulus where headset logistics are impractical or operator access is critical** (e.g., posterior surgical sites, nerve blocks, very brief procedures) → consider **tablet/screen distraction** as a lower-cost alternative; Barros-Padilha documents cases (Al-Halabi, nerve block) where tablets matched or beat VR, and Ghobadi documents measurably increased surgeon distress from headset bulk.
3. **Chronic, trait-level dental phobia requiring management across multiple visits, or anticipatory (pre-appointment) anxiety** → hypnosis's evidence base is weaker on pooled effect, so consider **CBT** first per Cernega 2022's own conclusion; audiotape self-hypnosis is a reasonable adjunct that needs no specialist training, but live individualized hypnosis (more effective per the review) requires referral to a trained practitioner.
4. **Pediatric patients below headset manufacturer minimum age** (~12–13 y, per Oculus/Sony/Samsung guidance) → note that nearly all VR pediatric trials in Barros-Padilha used devices off-label in younger children, and cybersickness was assessed in only 1 of 22 studies — a safety signal, not a contraindication, but one to disclose and monitor.
5. **Neither modality is a substitute for pharmacological sedation in severe phobia or highly invasive procedures** — none of the three papers position hypnosis or VR as replacing sedation; they are adjuncts for anxiety/pain modulation during non-sedated care.

## Limitations of This Synthesis

- No direct head-to-head trial comparing hypnosis to VR exists in the wiki (or, to these papers' knowledge, in the literature) — the comparison here is cross-study, not within-study.
- Outcome-scale heterogeneity (DAS/VAS/STAI/mDAS for hypnosis; CPMAS/MCDAS/FIS/VPT/WBFS/VAS/FLACC/MBPS for VR) means the numeric effect sizes above are not on a common metric and should not be averaged or ranked numerically against each other — only their statistical significance and consistency patterns are comparable.
- Hypnosis evidence is adult-skewed and VR evidence is split across two different clinical contexts (routine pediatric treatment vs adult surgical implant placement), so the "VR wins" narrative partly reflects different procedural intensities, not a pure like-for-like mechanism comparison.
- Long-term/repeated-exposure effects, cost-effectiveness, and combined hypnosis+VR protocols are not addressed by any of the three source papers.
