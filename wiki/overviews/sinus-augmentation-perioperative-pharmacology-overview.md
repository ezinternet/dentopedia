> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 상악동거상술 (Sinus Floor Elevation, SFE) 주위수술기 약물요법을 6축으로 종합. **위키는 SFE 전용 약물 RCT가 거의 없어**, 대부분 임플란트·구강외과 근거를 SFE 맥락에 적용한 것이다 — 유일한 SFE 특화 pooled 근거는 Díaz 2025 우산리뷰(항생제 축).
> - **진통**: 술후 통증은 염증성이므로 비스테로이드소염제 (NSAID)가 backbone. 1차 = 이부프로펜 (Ibuprofen) 400 mg + 아세트아미노펜 (Acetaminophen / Paracetamol, APAP) 병용, 치료필요수 (Number Needed to Treat, NNT) ≈ 1.5 (Miroshnychenko 2023 Network MA). 오피오이드는 비오피오이드 대비 우월성 없음 (Feldman 2024 RCT n = 1,815). 2024 미국치과협회 (ADA) 임상진료지침 (CPG)과 일치.
> - 병용 방식은 **교대 (alternate) > 동시 (concurrent)** — Gaballah 2025 RCT에서 rescue 필요율 15 % vs 53 % (p = 0.002). 두 약을 각각 q6h로 엇갈려 4시간마다 진통을 덮는 구조.
> - **항생제 예방 — 위험 기반**: 단순 SFE의 루틴 예방은 근거 불충분 (Díaz 2025 우산리뷰, 7 SR/MA, GRADE LOW). 정당화되는 고위험: 슈나이더막 (Schneiderian Membrane, SM) 천공(특히 > 10 mm), 광범위 측방창, 흡연, 기왕 부비동염, 전신질환.
> - **연장 투여는 무익**: 술후 24시간 초과 지속은 감염률을 낮추지 않는다 (Torof 2023, Dubedout 2025 RCT 7편, Mohammadpour 2025). 인포그래픽이 인용한 Shuster 2021은 위키 미보유이나 결론 방향은 보유 논문들과 일치.
> - 예방 시 약제: 아목시실린 (Amoxicillin) 2 g 술전 1시간; SFE 특화 문헌에선 아목시실린/클라불란산 (Amoxicillin/Clavulanate) 병용이 가장 일관되게 1차 (Díaz 2025).
> - **페니실린 알러지 대안 — 위키는 독시사이클린 (Doxycycline)을 권고하지 않는다**: 보유 SFE·구강외과 논문 전부 클린다마이신 (Clindamycin) 300–600 mg 또는 3세대 세팔로스포린 (Cephalosporin) (Díaz 2025, Kim 2019, Torof 2023, Mohammadpour 2025).
> - 단, 위키의 항생제 스튜어드십 개요는 clindamycin을 클로스트리디오이데스 디피실 (C. difficile) 위험(치명적 이상반응 2.9 / 백만, Thornhill 2019)으로 회피 대상으로 보고 **세팔렉신 (Cephalexin) · 아지스로마이신 (Azithromycin)** 으로의 이동을 권고 — 구강외과 적응증에서는 clindamycin이 아직 유효하다는 단서.
> - **코르티코스테로이드**: 위키 근거는 전부 사랑니 · 악교정 수술이며 **SFE 전용 시험은 0편**. 그 범위에서 덱사메타손 (Dexamethasone) 4–8 mg PO/IM 술전이 부종 · 개구제한 (Trismus) 1차; 8 mg 초과 최적 용량은 미정의(명시적 연구공백). Scaini 2025가 SFE에서 "항생제 + 스테로이드 + 비충혈제거제 표준요법"을 이환율 감소 요인으로 언급(용량 명시 없음).
> - **비강 비충혈제거제 (옥시메타졸린, Oxymetazoline)**: 위키 3,515개 페이지에 언급 0건, "72시간 제한" 수치도 없음. 오히려 Suzuki-Yamazaki 2020은 고위험 SFE에서 혈관수축제 / 비충혈제거제 **회피**를 권고(이미 낮은 상악동 산소분압을 추가로 낮춤). Craig 2022는 부비동염에서 식염수 세척 · 비충혈제거제 보조요법이 "미연구 (unstudied)"라고 명시.
> - **확립된 감염**: 봉쇄 (contained) vs 확대 (escalation) 판단 → 경증 · 봉쇄 = 조기 항생제 (amox/clav · clindamycin · fluoroquinolone) + 항염 + 비충혈제거제, 미개선 시 이식재 부분 / 전체 제거; 진행성 · 비봉쇄 = 전산화단층촬영 (CT) / 콘빔 CT (CBCT)로 부비동 침범 평가 → 전위 · 오염 이식재 제거 → 이비인후과 (Ear-Nose-Throat, ENT) 협진, 난치성은 기능적 내시경 부비동수술 (Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery, FESS). 내시경 부비동수술 (ESS) + 항생제 해소율 94.7 % (Allevi, Díaz 2025 경유).
> - **인포그래픽 대조 결론**: 진통 · 위험기반 예방 · 감염관리 경로는 위키와 부합. 어긋나는 2곳 = ① 페니실린 알러지 독시사이클린(위키 미지지) ② 옥시메타졸린 루틴 프레이밍(위키는 고위험군 회피). 스테로이드 8 mg · 옥시메타졸린 72 h 등 구체 수치는 위키에 근거 없음.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of the drug axis of maxillary sinus floor elevation (SFE) — analgesia, antibiotic prophylaxis, penicillin-allergy alternatives, corticosteroids, nasal decongestant/saline, and management of established graft infection — drawn from ~20 wiki pages and anchored by the only SFE-specific pooled source, the Díaz 2025 antibiotic umbrella review (7 SR/MAs, >18,900 patients); nearly all other axes borrow implant- and oral-surgery-wide evidence because SFE-specific trials do not exist in the wiki.

NSAID + acetaminophen is first-line analgesia (NNT ≈ 1.5; opioids non-superior — Feldman 2024 RCT n = 1,815; alternate dosing beats concurrent — Gaballah 2025 rescue 15% vs 53%); antibiotic prophylaxis is risk-based not routine (GRADE LOW) and courses beyond 24 h add nothing (Torof 2023, Dubedout 2025); the wiki's penicillin-allergy alternative is clindamycin or a cephalosporin — not doxycycline; corticosteroid evidence is entirely third-molar (dexamethasone 4–8 mg, optimal dose undefined above 8 mg) with no SFE-specific trial.

The wiki holds no source recommending routine nasal oxymetazoline — Suzuki-Yamazaki 2020 advises against decongestants in high-risk SFE — and established graft infection follows a contain-vs-escalate pathway: early antibiotics + anti-inflammatory + decongestant for contained/mild, CT/CBCT + graft removal + ENT/FESS for progressing/uncontained (ESS + antibiotics 94.7% resolution).

## 세줄요약

상악동거상술 (Sinus Floor Elevation, SFE)의 약물 축 — 진통 · 항생제 예방 · 페니실린 알러지 대안 · 코르티코스테로이드 · 비강 비충혈제거제/식염수 · 확립된 이식재 감염 관리 — 을 약 20개 위키 페이지에서 종합했으며, 유일한 SFE 특화 pooled 근거인 Díaz 2025 항생제 우산리뷰(7 SR/MA, 환자 18,900명+)를 축으로 한다; 나머지 축은 SFE 전용 시험이 위키에 없어 임플란트 · 구강외과 전반 근거를 차용한다.

비스테로이드소염제 (NSAID) + 아세트아미노펜이 1차 진통(NNT ≈ 1.5; 오피오이드 비우월 — Feldman 2024 RCT n = 1,815; 교대 투약 > 동시 — Gaballah 2025 rescue 15% vs 53%); 항생제 예방은 루틴이 아니라 위험 기반(GRADE LOW)이고 24시간 초과 투여는 무익(Torof 2023, Dubedout 2025); 위키의 페니실린 알러지 대안은 클린다마이신 또는 세팔로스포린이지 독시사이클린이 아니다; 코르티코스테로이드 근거는 전부 사랑니(덱사메타손 4–8 mg, 8 mg 초과 용량 미정의)이며 SFE 전용 시험은 없다.

위키에는 비강 옥시메타졸린 루틴 사용을 권고하는 근거가 없으며 — Suzuki-Yamazaki 2020은 고위험 SFE에서 비충혈제거제를 회피하라고 한다 — 확립된 이식재 감염은 봉쇄 대 확대 경로를 따른다: 봉쇄 · 경증은 조기 항생제 + 항염 + 비충혈제거제, 진행성 · 비봉쇄는 CT/CBCT + 이식재 제거 + ENT/FESS(내시경 부비동수술 + 항생제 해소율 94.7%).

## Summary

This page synthesizes what the wiki holds on the **perioperative drug protocol** for maxillary sinus floor elevation (SFE), organized on the six axes of the modern risk-stratified paradigm: (1) analgesia, (2) antibiotic prophylaxis, (3) penicillin-allergy alternatives, (4) corticosteroids, (5) nasal decongestion / saline, and (6) management of an established graft or sinus infection.

**One structural caveat governs the whole page.** The wiki contains essentially no drug RCT performed *in an SFE population*. The single exception on the pooled side is [[drug/antibiotics/diaz-2025-antibiotics-sinus-lift-infection-umbrella]] (antibiotic axis). Every other recommendation here is an *application* of implant-wide or oral-surgery-wide evidence to the SFE setting — defensible, because SFE is a clean surgical wound with a graft compartment adjacent to a paranasal sinus, but it means dose numbers and durations are inherited, not SFE-validated. Where a claim has no wiki support at all, it is stated as a gap rather than filled in.

## 1. Analgesia

**Backbone: NSAID + acetaminophen, opioid-sparing.** The wiki's [[overviews/drug-analgesics-postop-pain-overview]] is unambiguous and is echoed by the 2024 ADA clinical practice guideline ([[drug/analgesics/carrasco-labra-2024-pharmacologic-management-acute-dental-pain-guideline]]): nonopioid therapy is first-line for every surgical scenario.

- **First choice:** ibuprofen 400 mg + acetaminophen (APAP) — NNT ≈ 1.5 in the 82-RCT network meta-analysis (Miroshnychenko 2023, n = 9,095). The Cochrane distillation [[drug/analgesics/bailey-2014-ibuprofen-paracetamol-wisdom-bdj-summary]] puts the fixed combination at RR 1.77 / NNT 3 vs placebo for third-molar pain.
- **APAP dose:** the wiki cites **1000 mg** per dose (the ADA CPG uses 500 mg). The infographic's "paracetamol 500 mg" matches the ADA figure; both are within accepted range.
- **Dosing pattern:** the held RCT evidence favours **alternating** ibuprofen and APAP over taking them together — [[drug/analgesics/gaballah-2025-ibuprofen-paracetamol-concurrent-alternate-rct]] found rescue-analgesic need of 15% (alternate) vs 53% (concurrent), p = 0.002. Practically: each drug on its own q6h interval, staggered so analgesic cover lands every ~4 h. The infographic's "combined every 6 hours" reads as concurrent; the wiki would stagger it.
- **Opioids:** not superior. [[drug/analgesics/feldman-2024-nonopioid-opioid-third-molar-rct]] (n = 1,815, bony third-molar extraction) — ibuprofen 400 mg + APAP 500 mg beat hydrocodone 5 mg + APAP 300 mg at every timepoint. Tramadol/codeine/oxycodone rank at placebo level in network meta-analysis.

**Verdict vs infographic:** ✅ concordant in substance. Minor divergences: APAP dose figure, and concurrent-vs-alternate framing.

## 2. Antibiotic Prophylaxis

**The "7-day reflex" is not supported; prophylaxis is risk-based.** [[drug/antibiotics/diaz-2025-antibiotics-sinus-lift-infection-umbrella]] pooled 7 SR/MAs (>18,900 patients, >28,000 implants) with AMSTAR-2 + GROOVE + GRADE triple appraisal:

- Post-SFE infection 0.3–11.6% (lateral window 1.7–2.9% > transcrestal 0.3–1.0%) — **surgical approach is a stronger determinant than the antibiotic regimen**.
- Implant survival >90% regardless of antibiotic protocol; Salgado-Peralvo 2023 (AMSTAR-2 high) found no significant difference in implant failure with vs without antibiotics.
- **GRADE certainty for prophylaxis efficacy: LOW.** Routine coverage in uncomplicated SFE is unsupported.
- Risk factors that *do* justify prophylaxis: **SM perforation (especially >10 mm)**, extensive lateral-window surgery, smoking, pre-existing sinusitis, systemic comorbidity, graft displacement into the sinus. This list matches the infographic almost verbatim.

**Duration.** Prolonged courses add nothing:

- [[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]]: single preoperative amoxicillin 2 g reduces early failure; **postoperative continuation adds no benefit** and raises AMR exposure.
- [[drug/antibiotics/dubedout-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-early-complications]]: 7 double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs, no significant difference in failure or complications; two trials included simultaneous grafting.
- [[drug/antibiotics/mohammadpour-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-oral-surgery-sr]]: perioperative prophylaxis (≤24 h) is supported for higher-risk oral surgery, but **courses beyond 24 h do not reduce SSI** and associate with more late complications.
- [[drug/antibiotics/esposito-2013-antibiotics-dental-implant-placement-cochrane]]: the classic basis for "2 g amoxicillin one hour before" — a *single preoperative* dose.

The infographic's cited **Shuster et al. 2021** (24 h vs 7 d after lateral sinus augmentation, no difference) is **not in the wiki** — but its conclusion is the consensus direction of every held paper above.

**Agent choice when prophylaxis is used.** Díaz 2025 lists the most common regimens as amoxicillin 2 g PO 1 h preop, then amoxicillin/clavulanate 7–10 days postop; it frames amox/clav as the most consistent first-line for the SFE-specific literature while its own conclusion questions the postoperative tail. [[sinus-lift/lateral/scaini-2025-lateral-sinus-floor-elevation-ridge-augmentation]] names a "standardized postoperative antibiotic + steroid + decongestant regimen" as morbidity-reducing for lateral SFE — the closest thing in the wiki to an SFE-specific perioperative triad, though it gives no doses.

**Verdict vs infographic:** ✅ concordant, including the risk-factor list and the challenge to routine prolonged prophylaxis.

## 3. Penicillin-Allergy Alternatives

**The wiki does not recommend doxycycline for this purpose.** No held SFE, implant, or oral-surgery source proposes doxycycline as the penicillin-allergy substitute. Every one names clindamycin (± a cephalosporin):

- [[drug/antibiotics/diaz-2025-antibiotics-sinus-lift-infection-umbrella]]: "Penicillin allergy → Clindamycin 300–600 mg q6–8h".
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/kim-2019-maxillary-implants-otolaryngologist-perspective]] (Korean ENT): for acute bacterial rhinosinusitis, "3rd-generation cephalosporin or clindamycin for penicillin allergy".
- [[drug/antibiotics/torof-2023-antibiotic-dental-implant-procedures-sr-ma]]: "Penicillin allergy alternative: clindamycin 600 mg single dose".
- [[drug/antibiotics/mohammadpour-2025-antibiotic-prophylaxis-oral-surgery-sr]]: "clindamycin is reserved for penicillin allergy only".

**Where the wiki *does* agree with the infographic's instinct:** [[overviews/drug-antibiotic-stewardship-overview]], on [[drug/antibiotics/thornhill-2019-adverse-reactions-oral-antibiotics-dentists]] (NHS 7-year data), flags clindamycin for the highest fatal-ADR rate (2.9/million, mostly *C. difficile* colitis) and moves the recommended penicillin-allergy alternative toward **cephalexin 2 g or azithromycin 500 mg** — while noting clindamycin "remains valid" specifically in oral-surgery indications. Macrolides in the same dataset carry a QT-prolongation / torsades fatality signal (a *safety* concern, not the resistance concern the infographic cites); Díaz 2025 lists fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin, then moxifloxacin) as second-line for resistance with no safety caveat in our sources.

**Verdict vs infographic:** ⚠️ the specific recommendation (doxycycline) has **no wiki support**; the broader "clindamycin is problematic" premise is partly supported, but the wiki's answer is cephalexin/azithromycin.

## 4. Corticosteroids

**All held corticosteroid evidence is third-molar or orthognathic — there is no SFE-specific trial.** Within that borrowed evidence base ([[overviews/drug-corticosteroids-perioperative-dentistry-overview]]):

- Effect is **asymmetric by outcome:** strong, consistent reduction of edema (SMD 1.1–1.4) and trismus (2.7–4.1 mm mouth-opening) across three SR+MAs; pain effect inconsistent.
- **Dexamethasone 4–8 mg PO/IM, ~1 h preoperative** is the first-choice regimen for edema + trismus; dexamethasone > methylprednisolone for trismus (Singh 2023 SR+MA, SMD −0.69 mm, p < 0.0001).
- [[drug/analgesics/canellas-2022-corticosteroids-third-molar-nma]] (61 RCTs, 3,561 subjects): **dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal** is the top-ranked agent for edema, and the only regimen also reaching significance for pain.
- The overview states explicitly that the **optimal dexamethasone dose above 8 mg is not well defined** — a named research gap that matches the infographic's "optimal standardized dose or drug has not been definitively proven".
- Safety: single perioperative doses carry no increased infection risk (Dan 2010, RR 1.004).
- Note: for pure *analgesic* intent, the 2024 ADA CPG ([[drug/analgesics/miroshnychenko-2023-corticosteroids-third-molar-acute-pain-sr-ma]]) recommends *against* adding a corticosteroid — but anti-edema use is out of that guideline's scope, so there is no conflict with using dexamethasone to limit post-SFE swelling.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/scaini-2025-lateral-sinus-floor-elevation-ridge-augmentation]] includes a steroid in its "standardized postoperative" SFE regimen but specifies no drug or dose.

**Verdict vs infographic:** 🟡 directionally consistent (dexamethasone ~8 mg, optimal dose unproven), but the wiki carries **zero SFE-specific corticosteroid data** — the 8 mg figure is extrapolated from third-molar surgery.

## 5. Nasal Decongestion and Saline

**The wiki holds no source recommending routine nasal oxymetazoline**, and the term does not appear on any of the 3,515 wiki pages; there is no "72-hour maximum" figure anywhere in the repo.

- [[sinus-lift/lateral/suzuki-yamazaki-2020-sinusitis-prevention-sinus-lift-high-risk]] — in a high-risk SFE prevention protocol — states that **vasoconstrictors / nasal decongestants should be avoided**: they risk further reducing an already-low sinus oxygen tension. This is *more conservative* than the infographic's "reserve for actively congested patients."
- The same paper's positive protocol is a **topical nasal steroid** (fluticasone propionate) ± leukotriene-receptor antagonist for 2 months preoperatively in patients with a reversible ENT contraindication who decline FESS.
- [[oral-medicine/odontogenic-sinusitis/craig-2022-odontogenic-sinusitis-state-art]]: adjunctive **saline irrigation and decongestants are "unstudied"** in odontogenic sinusitis.
- [[drug/antibiotics/diaz-2025-antibiotics-sinus-lift-infection-umbrella]] mentions decongestants **only as a treatment adjunct for established infection** (with nasal corticosteroids + CHX), never as prophylaxis.

Saline spray's supportive role for mucociliary clearance is clinically uncontroversial but has **no direct evidence in the wiki**; the infographic's point that it is for the nasal mucosa and not an irrigation tool for the graft is consistent with how our sources treat it.

**Verdict vs infographic:** ⚠️ the "oxymetazoline, reserved, max 72 h" framing has no wiki basis, and one held paper argues *against* decongestants in the higher-risk SFE population.

## 6. Management of Established Graft / Sinus Infection

The infographic's contain-vs-escalate pathway aligns well with the held case-level evidence.

**Contained + mild → pharmacological, with close monitoring.**
- Díaz 2025 (via Allevi 2022): early antibiotic therapy — typically **amoxicillin/clavulanate, clindamycin, or a fluoroquinolone** — plus anti-inflammatory and decongestant therapy resolves most graft infections *if started before* OAF formation or implant/graft displacement.
- Escalate to **partial or total graft removal** if there is no improvement.

**Not contained / progressing → imaging, source removal, ENT.**
- **CT / CBCT** to map sinus (and ethmoid) involvement — [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]] documents ethmoidal co-involvement once the ostium obstructs, making cross-sectional imaging essential.
- **Remove displaced / contaminated graft material.** [[sinus-lift/lateral/felisati-2012-late-recovery-foreign-body-sinusitis]]: displaced xenograft acts as a foreign-body nidus, antibiotic-refractory; CT is needed to confirm complete clearance, and a staged less-invasive-first escalation (targeted endoscopic clearance before repeat open surgery) is favoured.
- **Coordinate with ENT for FESS** when there is persistent sinus disease or impaired drainage. ESS + systemic antibiotics achieved clinical resolution in **94.7%** of post-implant sinusitis cases (Díaz 2025). [[sinus-lift/lateral/nam-2014-fess-intraoral-implant-related-sinusitis]] shows FESS alone is insufficient when an intraoral procedure (OAF closure, removal of a sinus-intruding implant >4 mm) is simultaneously needed — combined ENT–OMFS surgery under GA.
- [[sinus-lift/lateral/park-2024-surgical-drainage-simultaneous-sinus-floor]] offers an intraoral-only alternative (intentional membrane incision for drainage + irrigation) for mild chronic maxillary sinusitis when the patient refuses FESS.
- Referral thresholds: [[sinus-lift/lateral/craig-2024-ent-oral-surgeon-collaboration-sinus-elevation]] (Felisati grade ≥ 3 → ENT); [[sinus-lift/lateral/kozuma-2017-chronic-sinusitis-sinus-augmentation-infection]] identifies pre-existing chronic sinusitis as the #1 predictor of both post-SFE infection and implant loss — screen and clear it *before* augmentation (macrolide ≥ 12 weeks or FESS).

**Verdict vs infographic:** ✅ concordant with the held evidence.

## Gaps / Open Questions

1. **No SFE-specific analgesia or corticosteroid trial in the wiki.** Every dose and schedule on those axes is inherited from third-molar / implant surgery.
2. **Shuster et al. 2021** (antibiotic duration RCT after lateral sinus augmentation) is not held — ingest the PDF to convert the "7-day reflex" argument from inherited to direct evidence.
3. **Doxycycline for penicillin allergy in sinus/implant surgery** — the infographic's ENT-aligned recommendation has no representation in the wiki; a modern ENT rhinosinusitis guideline would be needed to hold it.
4. **Oxymetazoline** — no wiki source; the "72-hour cap / rhinitis medicamentosa" rhinology teaching is uncaptured, and the one relevant SFE paper argues against decongestant use in high-risk cases.
5. **Nasal saline** as post-SFE supportive care — "unstudied" per Craig 2022; no trial to cite.
6. A prospective SFE perioperative-medication protocol study (analgesia + prophylaxis + steroid + nasal care as a bundle) would replace most of this page's borrowed evidence.
