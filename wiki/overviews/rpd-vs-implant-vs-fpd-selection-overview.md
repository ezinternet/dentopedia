>
> - **생존 vs 사용:** 5년 RPD 생존율 95.1% (주조 클라스프 Drummond 2024) vs 5년 내 ~40% 미사용 (Campbell 2017) — 두 수치는 모순이 아니라 다른 종점을 측정한다; 기술적 생존율을 환자에게 성공률로 말하면 오도.
> - **SDA 합법화:** Walter 2021 15년 RCT — 대구치를 채우든 안 채우든 보철 성공률 차이 없음 (14.2 vs 14.3년에 50% 미만 성공; 어느 분석도 유의차 없음); 견치+소구치 양측 보존 시 SDA는 타협이 아니라 근거 기반 계획.
> - **환자 수요 기울기:** 제2대구치만 결손 = **3%**만 보철 원함; 제1·2대구치 결손 = **58%**; 소구치까지 결손 = **93%** (Nassani 2013, Fueki 데이터); 대구치 간극의 대부분은 술자가 느끼는 문제이지 환자가 느끼는 문제가 아니다.
> - **Witter 3단계:** 경도 단축 (대구치 결손) = 연장 근거 없음; 중등도 = 예외적으로; 고도 = 중등도 수준까지만 연장 (완전치열 회복이 아님).
> - **OHRQoL 지평:** OHIP-14 3~6개월은 개선 (very low certainty); 12개월 NS (MD −12.7, p = 0.06, n = 62) — 근거 부족으로 인한 결과이지 효과가 사라진 것이 아님 (Choong 2022).
> - **임플란트 vs 통상 보철:** 환자 관점 OHRQoL은 임플란트가 통상 치아지지 보철보다 유의하게 낫지 않음 (Duong 2022); 비용효과 순서는 **치아 보존 → 실패 후 임플란트 ≥ 고정성/가철성 국소의치** (Beikler 2015 EAO 합의).
> - **지대치 비용:** RPD를 선택하면 5년 지대치 발치 위험 OR 1.99, 실활 지대치 HR 2.96 (Drummond 2024) — "의치가 치아를 상하게 하는가"의 답은 '치주조직은 아니지만 지대치는 그렇다'.
> - **순응 예측인자:** 전치부 대체 = 가장 강한 착용 예측인자; 후치부만 대체하는 RPD는 가장 착용 안 되는 설계 (Nassani 2013).

> - **원위 유리단 (Distal Extension)의 함정:** 점막-치주인대 압축률 차이 → 회전 → 지대치 스트레스·치조골 흡수; 하악에서 더 심하다; 이것이 Drummond OR 1.99의 기계적 근거 (Nassani 2013).

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 12 papers on the partially dentate selection decision — when to place an RPD versus accept a shortened dental arch (SDA), versus a fixed partial denture (FPD) or implant — spanning the prosthesis-survival, patient-reported, case-selection, periodontal, and economic axes.

Over fifteen years, replacing missing posterior teeth and not replacing them (SDA) produced indistinguishable prosthetic success (Walter 2021 RCT: 14.2 vs 14.3 years to <50% event-free success, no analysis significant), while ~40% of RPD patients discontinue use within five years (Campbell 2017) even though the prosthesis itself survives in ≈95% of cases at five years (Drummond 2024 SR+MA); the demand gradient (3% / 58% / 93% of SDA patients seeking restoration by extent of shortening) and Witter's three-rung ladder narrow the indication to the subset that actually wants treatment (Nassani 2013).

Once the case warrants treatment, the cost axis (Beikler 2015 EAO consensus) places tooth preservation first, implant after failed retreatment ahead of conventional partial dentures, and from the patient's own perspective implant-supported prostheses do not produce measurably better oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) than conventional alternatives (Duong 2022).

치료가 필요한 케이스에서 비용효과 순서는 치아 보존 → 실패 후 임플란트 ≥ 통상 보철이며 (Beikler 2015 유럽골유착학회 European Association for Osseointegration, EAO 합의), 환자 관점 구강건강관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL) 은 임플란트가 통상 보철 대비 유의하게 낫지 않다 (Duong 2022).

## The Five Arguments

### 1. The prosthesis survives; the abutment does not — necessarily

[[removable-partial-denture/drummond-2024-rpd-long-term-periodontal-health-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 46 studies, 4,359 prostheses) establishes the quantitative baseline: 5-year prosthesis survival is 95.1% for cast-clasp designs and 91.7% for telescopic-crown-retained designs — a 3.4% difference that is not statistically significant (P = .71). The periodontium adjacent to a well-maintained RPD is largely stable: mobility (P = .98) and probing depth (P = .50) do not change.

What does change is the abutment tooth itself. Abutment extraction risk doubles by five years (OR 1.99, 95% CI 1.32–3.01) and nearly triples for nonvital abutments (HR 2.961, 95% CI 2.023–4.335). The clinical implication: the correct question at treatment planning is not "will this denture damage the mouth" but "which teeth am I about to make abutments, and can they carry that role for a decade?"

[[removable-partial-denture/gotfredsen-2021-removable-partial-prosthesis-periodontitis-sr]] confirms from an EFP systematic review of the periodontitis population that RPDs do not, per se, cause periodontal destruction — plaque accumulation is consistent but conversion to attachment loss is not established. [[removable-partial-denture/kalsi-2021-periodontal-health-shortened-dental-arch]] reports a 10-year clinical attachment level (Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) difference favouring the SDA arm by 0.79 mm (study jaw) from the same German trial as Walter 2021, but the commentary authors classify this as unlikely to be clinically significant.

The mechanical account is in [[removable-partial-denture/nassani-2013-fesrpd-shortened-dental-arch-reappraisal]]: a distal-extension denture rests on tissues with different compressibility — periodontal ligament (Periodontal Ligament, PDL) at the abutment versus mucosa at the saddle. Under load the denture rotates about the axis joining the most distal rests, transmitting a lever arm to the abutment and to the residual ridge. This rotation is greater in the mandible (no palate to spread load), which explains the asymmetric ridge resorption and connects directly to the abutment extraction signal in Drummond.

### 2. The 15-year RCT closes the "does it matter?" question — and the answer is no

[[removable-partial-denture/walter-2021-shortened-dental-arch-15year-rct]] is the only long-term randomised comparison of replacing versus not replacing missing posterior teeth. Across 14 German dental schools, patients missing molars in one jaw who retained a canine and at least one premolar bilaterally were randomised to a precision-attachment partial removable dental prosthesis (PRDP, N = 81) or to a shortened dental arch (SDA, N = 71).

Over 15 years, event-free success (defined as freedom from moderate-or-worse failure) fell below 50% at 14.2 years in the PRDP group and 14.3 years in the SDA group. No analysis reached statistical significance. About half of all patients remained complication-free for more than 10 years in both arms.

Three caveats condition the extrapolation:
- The removable arm used **precision attachments**, not cast clasps — a better-than-typical RPD. The null does not transfer unchanged to a conventional clasp design.
- The eligibility criterion required bilateral canine and premolar — a relatively favourable residual dentition. The most severe cases (Kennedy I with bilateral molar and premolar loss) are not represented.
- University clinic maintenance intensity exceeds typical private practice; maintenance effects are folded into both arms.

[[removable-partial-denture/kalsi-2021-periodontal-health-shortened-dental-arch]] brings the periodontal axis of the same trial: the small CAL difference (0.79 mm) favours SDA, the commentators classify it as not clinically significant.

### 3. Prosthesis survival and prosthesis use are different endpoints

[[removable-partial-denture/campbell-2017-rpd-clinical-need-for-innovation]] is the correction for the 95.1% figure. Almost 40% of RPD patients no longer use their denture within five years, driven by sociodemographic factors, pain, and esthetics. The modifiable levers — design, materials, repairability, patient education, follow-up — are chairside and relational rather than laboratory.

[[removable-partial-denture/choong-2022-ohrqol-after-rpd-rehabilitation-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 13 eligible, 8 pooled) quantifies what the patients who do continue to use their RPD report: OHIP-14 improves at 3 months (MD −12.0; very low certainty) and 6 months (MD −10.5; very low certainty), but the 12-month OHIP-14 estimate is not significant (MD −12.7, 95% CI −26.1 to 0.6, P = 0.06). OHIP-49 at 12 months does reach significance (MD −34.8; low certainty). The 12-month null is almost certainly **underpowered** (n collapses from 222 to 62) rather than a demonstrated absence of effect, but the evidence base supporting durable benefit does not currently exist.

Taken together: at five years about 60% of patients still use the prosthesis, most of those report better OHRQoL for the first six months, and no study confirms a significant 12-month OHIP-14 benefit. The consent conversation should present RPD benefit as real but short-horizon and not yet proven to persist.

### 4. Case selection — who actually needs treatment

[[removable-partial-denture/nassani-2013-fesrpd-shortened-dental-arch-reappraisal]] is the case-selection key. It assembles three pieces:

**The demand gradient (Fueki data):** The proportion of SDA patients who actually seek restoration rises with the extent of shortening:

| Extent | Patients seeking restoration |
|---|---|
| Second molar(s) only missing | **3%** |
| First and second molars missing | **58%** |
| Premolars also missing | **93%** |

Most molar gaps are not perceived by patients as a problem requiring treatment. Patient-initiated demand, not radiographic observation, is the trigger.

**Witter's case-selection ladder:**

| Category | Missing teeth | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Slightly shortened | Molars only | No prosthetic extension supported by evidence |
| Moderately shortened | Molars + first premolar | Exceptional cases only, particularly esthetic |
| Extremely shortened | Molars + premolars (incl. asymmetric) | Extend — but only to moderately-shortened level; a cantilever resin-bonded bridge may suffice |

The third rung is the one most commonly skipped in practice: even an extremely shortened arch should not be restored to full dentition — only back to a moderately shortened arch.

**Compliance predictor:** The single strongest predictor of whether an RPD is actually worn is whether it replaces **anterior teeth** (Jepson, Armellini, cited in Nassani). A posterior-only RPD is the design least likely to be used — making the demand gradient and Witter's ladder the pre-commitment filter before designing the prosthesis.

[[removable-partial-denture/nakai-2022-ohrqol-nonmetal-clasp-sda-crossover-rct]] (3-period crossover RCT, n = 24) provides within-patient ranking: non-metal clasp denture (NMCD) and SDA were both superior to conventional cast-clasp RPD; conventional RPD ranked lowest. This is the within-patient confirmation that if patients are willing to try SDA, many will not want to revert.

### 5. Cost hierarchy and the implant comparison

[[removable-partial-denture/beikler-2015-eao-economic-evaluation-implant-prostheses]] (EAO consensus, narrative review) provides the economic ordering:

| Clinical situation | Cost-effective choice |
|---|---|
| Restorable tooth | Preserve it — root canal treatment, retreatment, periodontal therapy |
| Class I furcation molar | Non-surgical periodontal therapy (more effective **and** cheaper than implant) |
| After failed endodontic retreatment | Single implant crown > fixed or removable partial denture |
| Single missing tooth | Implant crown vs bridge — conflicting evaluations, no consensus |

The RPD appears in this hierarchy only as a comparator and consistently loses to the implant after tooth loss following failed retreatment — but that is a narrow situation (one tooth in an otherwise supported arch).

[[removable-partial-denture/duong-2022-ohrqol-implant-supported-prostheses]] answers the patient's own question: from the patient perspective, OHRQoL is **not significantly greater for implants** than for conventional tooth-supported prostheses. Clinicians apply stricter esthetic standards than patients do, and preoperative expectations colour outcome perception while being poor predictors of satisfaction.

**Survival discrepancy — hold both numbers:** Duong cites Kurosaki's 6-year comparison showing RPD cumulative survival of 33.3% — a figure that cannot be reconciled with Drummond's 5-year 95.1% as a like-for-like statistic. Neither source is ingested here in sufficient detail to identify the endpoint definitions; the gap is almost certainly definitional (repair/reline = failure vs prosthesis still in service = survival). Neither number should be quoted as *the* RPD survival rate.

## Kennedy-Class Decision Branches

The Kennedy classification maps to both SDA eligibility and prosthesis design considerations.

| Kennedy class | Arch topology | SDA option | Prosthesis design note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** — bilateral free-end | Both sides molar/premolar missing | If canine + ≥1 PM bilateral: **SDA is RCT-supported** (Walter 2021) | Distal extension; rotation risk; abutment OR 1.99; consider precision attachment |
| **II** — unilateral free-end | One side molar/premolar missing, other side tooth-supported | Asymmetric SDA is within Witter "extremely shortened" category — extend only to moderately shortened | Unilateral extension; weaker evidence than bilateral |
| **III** — bounded posterior saddle | Missing posterior teeth with distal abutment | No free-end; tooth-supported — more stable biologically | Consider FPD or implant crown as direct alternatives |
| **IV** — anterior saddle | Anterior teeth crossing midline | High demand for replacement (anterior compliance predictor); RPD is likely to be worn | Anterior teeth present: best compliance scenario |

## Open Questions and Evidence Gaps

1. **Korean RPD discontinuation rate** — Campbell's ~40% figure comes from a narrative review and is not a pooled estimate. Coverage, cost-sharing, and denture culture differ substantially; a Korean-specific rate is unavailable in this corpus.
2. **12-month OHRQoL** — the Choong 2022 null at 12 months is almost certainly underpowered (n = 62). A properly powered 12-month RCT with RPD versus SDA versus FPD arms is the most direct gap.
3. **RPD survival endpoint heterogeneity** — the 33.3% vs 95.1% discrepancy between Kurosaki (cited by Duong) and Drummond is unresolved; both papers are abstract-only or full-text but the survival definitions were not extractable. A direct primary-source comparison would collapse this gap.
4. **Kennedy I vs II RPD outcomes** — Drummond pools by clasp design, not Kennedy class; whether bilateral versus unilateral free-end drives the abutment risk difference is not resolved.
5. **Digital/CAD-CAM RPD performance** — all survival data precede current digital removable workflows; cost and comfort figures for milled frameworks are not in this corpus.

## Clinical Synthesis: Decision Pathway

```
Is the natural tooth restorable?
  └── YES → Preserve (root canal, periodontal tx first; Beikler 2015)
        └── Failed retreatment? → IMPLANT > RPD/FPD (Beikler 2015)

  └── NO / already extracted → Assess arch topology

        Kennedy I/II (free-end saddle)?
        ├── Canine + ≥1 PM bilateral preserved?
        │     └── Patient ≥ 35 yrs, no extreme occlusal demand?
        │           → SDA is a RCT-supported plan (Walter 2021)
        │           → Screen demand: does patient actually seek restoration?
        │                 Molar only missing: ~3% seek Rx → counsel, defer
        │                 Both molars: ~58% → individualise
        │                 Premolars: ~93% → treat
        │
        └── Patient wants treatment:
              → Witter ladder: slightly shortened = no extension
                              moderately shortened = exceptional only
                              extremely shortened = extend to mod-shortened level only
              → If RPD chosen:
                    Abutment vitality check (nonvital HR 2.96 → highest risk)
                    Non-metal clasp / implant retention preferred (Nakai 2022)
                    Consent: ~40% stop using within 5 yr (Campbell 2017)
                              OHRQoL improves 3–6 mo (very low certainty; Choong 2022)

        Kennedy III/IV (bounded saddle / anterior)?
        └── Tooth-supported → FPD or implant crown direct alternatives
              Anterior teeth involved → high compliance if RPD chosen
              Cost axis: implant > RPD after failed retreatment (Beikler 2015)
              Patient OHRQoL: implant not measurably better (Duong 2022)
```
