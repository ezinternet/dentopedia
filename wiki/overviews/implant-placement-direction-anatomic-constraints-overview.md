> - **후방 상악 + TSFE** (jia 2025): 잔존 치조골 높이(RBH) 4–9 mm 지연식립에서 r-CAIS + 1-mm 안전 마진 → 각도 편차 **1.25°**, 슈나이데리안 막 천공 **2.5%** (오스테오톰 단독 7.2%); 방향 정밀도가 막 안전성의 핵심 변수.
> - **비축방향의 생물역학적 대가** (kim 2026): 비축방향 임플란트의 5년 변연골 소실(MBL) **0.22 mm vs 축방향 0.10 mm** (P<.05); 비축방향 × 임플란트 지지 대합치 상호작용 시 Δ0.373 mm 추가 — 해부학이 기울기를 강제하더라도 교합 설계로 보정 필요.

> - **공통 원칙**: 해부학이 비축방향을 강제할 때 → ① 최소 필요 각도로 기울임 ② CBCT로 계량화 ③ 각도형 지대주는 최소 각도 + 멀티유닛(나사유지) ④ 교합 최적화(협설 하중 방향 제어).

## Three-line Summary

Region-by-region synthesis of 6 primary papers (fettouh 2026, najm 2024, behfarnia 2025, alqaood 2026, jia 2025, kim 2026) plus the tilted-axial SR/MA overview: the governing anatomic constraints for implant placement direction differ by arch and tooth position, each with a quantified threshold — Safe Angle for anterior maxilla, ILAA 18.3° for upper premolars, IAC ≥2 mm margin for posterior mandible (shortest at 2nd molar 4.65 mm), CPDU D-I type as the highest LPP risk, and 1-mm sinus floor safety margin for posterior maxilla.

Anatomy almost always forces off-axis placement at posterior maxillary sites (TSFE needed) and upper premolars (ILAA 18.3°), while true axial placement is achievable in the anterior maxilla (with technique control) and lower anterior sites; this forced deviation carries a real biomechanical cost — 0.22 vs 0.10 mm MBL at 5 years — that must be addressed through occlusal design.

CBCT is the irreducible prerequisite because no 2D method can quantify IAC proximity, CPDU ridge type, lingual concavity depth, or residual sinus floor height — and all threshold values in this overview were derived from CBCT studies.

## 세줄요약

6편 종합 (fettouh 2026, najm 2024, behfarnia 2025, alqaood 2026, jia 2025, kim 2026) + 경사·축방향 오버뷰: 임플란트 식립 방향을 제한하는 해부 구조는 부위별로 다르며 각각 정량 임계값이 있다 — 전치부 Safe Angle, 상악 소구치 ILAA 18.3°, 후방 하악 IAC ≥2 mm 마진(2대구치 4.65 mm 최단), CPDU D-I형 LPP 최고위험, 후방 상악 1-mm 상악동 저부 안전 마진.

해부학은 상악 구치부(TSFE 필수)와 상악 소구치(ILAA 18.3°)에서 거의 항상 비축방향 식립을 강제하며, 비축방향의 생물역학적 대가는 5년 변연골 소실 0.22 vs 0.10 mm(P<.05)로 실재한다 — 교합 설계로 보정 필요.

CBCT는 모든 정량 임계값이 CBCT 연구에서 도출되었으므로 환원 불가한 전제조건이며, 2D 방사선은 IAC 근접도·능선 형태·설측 함몰 깊이·잔존 상악동 높이 중 어느 것도 계량화하지 못한다.

## Overview Thesis

Implant placement direction is taught as a simple rule — "axial to the occlusal load" — but anatomy overrides that rule at most posterior sites. This overview maps the actual anatomic constraints by region, quantifies the safe angle windows and safety distances derived from CBCT studies, and links forced off-axis placement to its biomechanical consequence. The central message: **anatomy determines the axis; CBCT quantifies the margin; biomechanics sets the price of deviation.**

## Region 1 — Anterior Maxilla (전치부 상악)

**Governing constraint**: the palatal socket slope diverts the drill labially.

**Primary paper**: [[immediate-implant/esthetic-soft-tissue/fettouh-2026-safe-angle-guided-iip-anterior-maxilla-deviation]]

In anterior maxilla immediate implant placement (IIP), the palatal wall of the alveolus creates a slope that pushes drilling instruments facially — the path of least resistance. A labially displaced implant shoulder carries a ~3× higher midfacial recession risk and typically requires an angled abutment. The Safe Angle Concept defines a range for the implant long axis based on the incisal root angle; the plateau-drill 2-guide protocol first mills a flat surface at the socket apex (eliminating the palatal slope), then runs the conventional osteotomy drill through a second guide.

**Key numbers:**
- Angular deviation (planned vs placed): **4.56 ± 3.14°**
- Platform deviation: 0.91 ± 0.61 mm; Apex deviation: 1.47 ± 0.88 mm
- 26/26 implants landed within safe angle — zero angled abutments needed
- Labial bone at 9 months: 2.13 mm mean; PES 11.89 ± 1.75

**Direction rule — anterior maxilla:**
> Always plan toward the palatal shoulder position. Use CBCT to verify the root trajectory and available palatal apical bone. When the safe angle cannot be achieved freehand, a guided or plateau-drill approach is indicated. True axial placement (perpendicular to occlusal) IS achievable here — the constraint is positional (stay palatal), not angular.

## Region 2 — Maxillary Premolars (상악 소구치)

**Governing constraint**: palatal root concavity forces buccal tilt.

**Primary paper**: [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/najm-2024-immediate-implant-premolar-perforation-cbct]]

The maxillary premolar root sits buccally in its socket with a prominent palatal concavity (LCA mean 156°). Prosthetically ideal axis (tooth long axis = line A) perforates the labial plate, nasal floor, or sinus floor in **84.1%** of virtual placements. Even the optimized bone-driven axis (shortest non-perforating path with ≥4 mm apical anchorage) perforates in **40.5%** of cases. The angular difference between these two axes — the implant-long axis angulation (ILAA) — quantifies the mandatory buccal tilt.

**Key numbers:**
- ILAA (buccal tilt to escape perforation): **18.3° ± 8.0°** globally
- By tooth: #15 20.6°; #14 17.5°; #24 16.7°; #25 20.3°
- Bone-driven minimum length needed: 13.0 ± 2.1 mm
- Risk factors: female sex (OR 2.36), older age (OR 1.034/yr), shorter tooth-apex distance, smaller LCA

**Direction rule — maxillary premolars:**
> Preoperative CBCT is mandatory — 2D cannot reveal this perforation risk. Expect to tilt buccally ~18° from the tooth long axis; plan for an angled or angled-screw-channel abutment from the outset. First premolar slightly safer than second premolar. Male and younger patients have relatively lower risk.

## Region 3 — Posterior Mandible: IAC Safety Distance (후방 하악: IAC 안전거리)

**Governing constraint**: inferior alveolar canal proximity limits implant length and mandates axial placement.

**Primary paper**: [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/behfarnia-2025-anatomic-immediate-implant-mandibular-posterior]]

SR+MA of 12 CBCT studies (1,834 patients) pooled the root-apex-to-IAC distance at each posterior mandibular position. The **2nd molar** is paradoxically the *shortest* distance despite being the most posterior — driven by mesial IAC ascent at that site. The **1st molar** has the most comfortable margin. Ridge cross-section matters: U-type undercut is dominant at the 2nd molar (73%), compounding IAC risk with LPP risk simultaneously.

**Key numbers:**

| Site | Apex-to-IAC distance | U-type ridge |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd premolar | **5.14 mm** | — |
| 1st molar | **6.32 mm** | — |
| 2nd molar | **4.65 mm** (shortest) | 73% |

- Safe IAC margin: **≥2 mm** → maximum safe implant length = site distance − 2 mm
- At 2nd molar with 4.65 mm: maximum ≈ 2.65 mm of bone usable above safety limit → short implants mandatory

**Direction rule — posterior mandible (IAC):**
> Measure apex-to-IAC on CBCT preoperatively. Apply ≥2 mm safety margin from IAC. The 2nd molar is the highest-risk site. Implant axis must be truly axial in the mesiodistal direction (any mesial or distal tilt shortens the effective IAC clearance on the tilted side).

## Region 4 — Posterior Mandible: Ridge Morphology & LPP Risk (후방 하악: 능선 형태)

**Governing constraint**: lingual concavity forces lingual plate perforation if implant axis not adjusted.

**Primary paper**: [[immediate-implant/anatomic-assessment/alqaood-2026-cpdu-classification-posterior-mandible]]

The CPDU classification provides the preoperative language for lingual plate perforation (LPP) risk:

| CPDU code | Shape | Undercut | LPP risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | Convergent (wide base, narrow crest) | None | Low |
| P | Parallel | None | Low |
| C-U-N / P-U-N | Any + undercut | Non-influential (below IAC or off-path) | Moderate |
| C-U-I / P-U-I | Any + undercut | Influential (above IAC, in implant path) | **High** |
| D-N | Divergent (narrow base, wide crest) | Inherent, non-influential | Moderate |
| D-I | Divergent | Inherent, influential | **Highest** |

**Key numbers:**
- U-type ridge prevalence: **54.4%** (left), **52.1%** (right)
- Lingual concavity depth with undercut: **1.72 mm** vs without 0.61 mm (p=0.001)
- Concavity angle ~55–57° in undercut ridges
- Nearly all U-type ridges show a lingual undercut

**Direction rule — posterior mandible (ridge morphology):**
> Default assumption before CBCT: undercut present (54% prevalence). CBCT-determine CPDU type before planning axis. D-I and U-I/C-U-I/P-U-I → consider: (a) axial tilt away from the lingual concavity (buccal shift), (b) shorter implant, (c) bone mapping / intraoperative tactile check. IAC and LPP risks operate simultaneously at 2nd molar sites — the highest-complexity posterior site.

## Region 5 — Posterior Maxilla: Sinus Floor Proximity (후방 상악: 상악동 저부)

**Governing constraint**: sinus pneumatization limits residual bone height → TSFE required; any angular deviation risks Schneiderian membrane perforation.

**Primary paper**: [[sinus-lift/transcrestal/jia-2025-accuracy-robotic-cais-tsfe-posterior-maxilla]]

When RBH is 4–9 mm in the posterior maxilla (most common delayed placement scenario), transcrestal sinus floor elevation (TSFE) is combined with implant placement. The drill path must stay within a narrow axial window: too far buccal or palatal risks perforating the sinus membrane through the angled drill path rather than the vertical one. A 1-mm safety margin protocol reserves the final 1 mm for manual osteotome elevation.

**Key numbers (robotic r-CAIS + Summers osteotome TSFE):**
- Angular deviation: **1.25° ± 0.61°** (planned vs placed)
- Global coronal deviation: 0.50 mm; Global apical: 0.54 mm
- Schneiderian membrane perforation: **2.5%** (1/40) with robotic guidance
- Benchmark (osteotome alone, Stacchi MA): 7.2% perforation
- Mix: 80% delayed (32/40), 20% early; 75% molars (#16/17/26/27)
- Only timing (early vs delayed) significantly affected accuracy in LMM; registration method (CBCT vs IOS) did NOT

**Direction rule — posterior maxilla:**
> Measure RBH on CBCT. If RBH 4–9 mm → plan TSFE. Program implant axis perpendicular to the occlusal plane (axial preferred; posterior maxilla bone quality III/IV tolerates off-axis less than dense bone). Set a 1-mm safety margin below sinus floor in the drilling protocol. Robotic or static-guided approaches significantly reduce membrane perforation risk vs freehand.

## Region 6 — Biomechanical Consequence of Off-Axis Placement (비축방향의 생물역학적 대가)

**Governing paper**: [[implants/peri-implantitis/kim-2026-implant-angulation-peri-implant-bone]]

When anatomy forces off-axis placement, the cost is not zero. Kim 2026 used CAD-derived 3D angulation (capturing both mesiodistal AND buccolingual tilt, fixing the prior literature's 2D-only blind spot) in a 5-year retrospective of 506 implants under controlled plaque scores.

**Key numbers:**
- MBL — nonaxial: **0.22 ± 0.48 mm** vs axial: **0.10 ± 0.39 mm** (P<.05)
- Maxilla > mandible (P<.001) — thinner cortex, lower density
- Nonaxial × implant-supported FDP antagonist interaction: **Δ0.373 mm** additional MBL
- The old "2D angulation studies found no effect" — explained as measurement artifact (missed buccolingual tilt)

**Implication for the overview:**
Off-axis is sometimes anatomically mandatory. But the biomechanical debt is real: 0.12 mm extra crestal bone loss over 5 years per nonaxial implant. Where anatomy forces off-axis (premolars, sinus proximity), the occlusal strategy should compensate:
- Minimize occlusal contact on the nonaxial implant (especially cantilever or FDP against another implant)
- Prefer angled-screw-channel abutments over high-angle cemented abutments (stress management)
- Monitor MBL more closely at nonaxial maxillary sites

## Decision Framework — Placement Direction by Site

| Site | Primary constraint | Safe axis target | Key threshold | Consequence of violation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior maxilla | Palatal slope → labial drift | Palatal shoulder position | Safe angle range (CBCT) | Midfacial recession, angled abutment |
| Maxillary premolar | Palatal concavity, LCA | Buccal tilt ~18° from tooth axis | ILAA 18.3°; LCA, tooth-apex distance | Cortical perforation 84% (prosthetic axis) |
| Posterior mandible (IAC) | IAC proximity | True axial; length = IAC − 2 mm | IAC margin ≥2 mm; 2M shortest (4.65 mm) | IAN paresthesia; early failure |
| Posterior mandible (LPP) | Lingual concavity (CPDU) | Axis clear of D-I / U-I concavity | Undercut depth, IAC position | Lingual cortical perforation, vascular injury |
| Posterior maxilla | Sinus floor (RBH 4–9 mm) | Axial within 1-mm margin; TSFE | 1-mm sinus safety margin; RBH | Schneiderian membrane perforation (7.2% vs 2.5%) |
| All sites | Nonaxial loading → MBL | Axial whenever anatomy permits | 0.22 vs 0.10 mm MBL at 5 yr | Crestal bone loss; amplified by FDP antagonist |

## Cross-Cutting Principles

**1. CBCT is non-negotiable for posterior sites.**
Every quantitative threshold in this overview — IAC distance, CPDU type, LCA, RBH — was derived from CBCT studies. None is estimable from periapical/panoramic radiographs. Behfarnia 2025 found 70–73% of second molar sites have <6 mm IAC clearance (IAC-closer-than-expected); Najm 2024 found 84.1% cortical perforation under clinical prosthetic axis. Both are invisible on 2D imaging.

**2. Bone-driven > prosthetically-driven axis — but they must eventually meet.**
Najm 2024 shows the bone-driven axis still perforates in 40.5% of upper premolars. The solution is not to abandon prosthetic planning but to quantify the ILAA gap pre-operatively (CBCT) and communicate to the restorative clinician that an angled abutment is planned.

**3. Anatomy forces axis → technology narrows the execution error.**
The Safe Angle Concept (fettouh 2026) provides planning guidance; the plateau-drill guide achieves 4.56° deviation in practice. r-CAIS achieves 1.25° at the posterior maxilla. Static guides achieve ~2.3° (Xing 2024 benchmark). The larger the anatomic margin is consumed, the more important it is to execute precisely.

**4. Nonaxial placement is not a contraindication — it is a risk factor requiring management.**
Kim 2026 gives the magnitude: 0.22 vs 0.10 mm MBL, plus a 0.373 mm amplification when facing another implant-supported FDP. Neither value is catastrophic, but they are additive over time. Management: minimize cantilever occlusal contacts on off-axis implants; prefer low-angle abutments where possible.

**5. The 2nd molar site is the highest-complexity posterior site.**
IAC shortest (4.65 mm), U-type ridge most common (73%), lingual concavity deepest — three independent constraints converge. CBCT analysis is mandatory; consider whether a shorter implant with TSFE (if posterior maxilla) or a prosthetically-tilted planning (distal tilt to gain length, avoiding IAC corner) is preferable to a long implant on a compromised axis.

## Key Gaps

- **Posterior mandible healed-site anatomy**: Behfarnia 2025 and Alqaood 2026 both used immediate placement CBCT data. True delayed-placement anatomy (remodeled ridge) may differ — no PMC paper available.
- **Posterior maxilla anatomy (healed ridge)**: No PMC paper quantifies buccolingual bone width or palatal-buccal axis constraints for delayed posterior maxillary implants outside the TSFE context. Couso-Queiruga 2023 (PMID 37493201) is a relevant candidate without PMC access.
- **Upper premolar delayed placement direction**: Najm 2024 is an IIP study; ILAA 18.3° applies to fresh sockets — healed-ridge anatomy may be less extreme.
- **Mandibular anterior and canine**: No papers in this wiki specifically quantify safe placement direction in this region.
