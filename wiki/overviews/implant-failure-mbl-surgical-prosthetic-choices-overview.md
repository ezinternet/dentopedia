> - 경사 임플란트의 MBL 불이익은 시간 의존적이다. 단기에는 없다가 3년에 +0.08 mm, 장기에 +0.18 mm로 커진다(Malak 2024). 실패 위험은 경사와 축 방향이 같다(RR 1.02). 해부 구조를 피하려는 경사 식립은 금기가 아니고, 더 긴 치조골 모니터링이 필요하다. [확인]
> - 같은 연구진의 두 SR+MA가 반전을 재현했다. Del Fabbro 2014(19편, 670명)는 1년(P=.09)과 3년 이상(P=.30)에서 비유의였고, Del Fabbro 2022(24편, 2,637명, 3–18년)는 축 임플란트 주위 MBL이 유의하게 낮았다(P<.0001). 표본이 약 4배 늘어난 점은 교란이지만 Malak의 시간 층별 분석이 시간 효과를 지지한다. [확인]
> - 플랩리스 수술은 MBL(MD −0.07 mm, P=0.16)과 감염(RR 0.96)에서 차이가 없다. 실패 RR 1.75(P=0.03)는 저위험 편향 5편으로 제한하면 사라져 편향 신호다. 표준 길이를 위해 능선 증대를 하면 더 짧은 임플란트를 쓰는 것보다 MBL이 많았다(Ting). [확인]
> - 단축 임플란트(≤6 mm): Lin 2026 첫 ≥10년 메타분석은 장 임플란트 대비 생존이 낮지만(환자 수준 RR 0.920, 임플란트 수준 0.943) 10년 생존이 89% 이상이고 MBL(0.277 mm)과 합병증은 같았다. 나사 유지의 생존이 83.6–80.7%로 시멘트 유지 97.0–96.8%보다 낮았다. [확인]
> - 정의와 시점이 결과를 가른다. 60개 SR을 모은 umbrella(Arbildo-Vega 2025)는 단축 임플란트와 표준 임플란트의 생존, 실패, 보철 합병증이 같고 단축 쪽이 MBL이 적다고 보고한다. 두 결과는 범위가 달라서 충돌하지 않는다: 결핍은 가장 짧은 길이와 가장 긴 추적에서만 나타난다. 6.5년 코호트(Kim 2026)에서는 삽입 깊이만 MBL과 상관이 있었다(r=0.372). [확인]
> - 4 mm 임플란트(Barausse 2024, 212명/496개, 평균 8.02년): 누적 생존 95.36%, MBL은 1년 0.47 mm에서 10년 0.59 mm로 3년 이후 유의한 증가가 없었다. 위생 방문 빈도가 높을수록 실패가 유의하게 적었다(p<0.001). 경사 임플란트 MBL은 시간에 따라 커지고 단축 임플란트 MBL은 3년 이후 평탄하다. [확인]
> - 즉시 부하: 단일 임플란트 크라운 RCT 11편의 SR+MA(Benic 2014)에서 생존(1년 OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.32–1.76)과 MBL(SMD −0.05 mm)이 같았으나, 삽입 토크 ≥20–45 Ncm 또는 ISQ ≥60–65이고 동시 골증대가 없는 범위 안에서만 입증되었다. Gjelvold 2021(n=50, ≥30 Ncm, 5년)에서도 MBL이 같았다(−0.50 vs −0.54 mm, p=0.782). 위험은 시점이 아니라 안정성 관문과 환자다. [확인]
> - 관골 임플란트: 중증 위축 상악에서 생존 90–100%이고 ZAGA 술식이 원래 술식보다 부비동염(3.7% vs 27.2%)과 즉시 부하 가능성(89.6% vs 22.3%)에서 우위다. 두경부암 환자(Scocca 2026, 17편, 294명, 679개)의 생존은 97%였고 방사선의 실패 OR은 3.42(95% CI 0.97–12.06, p=0.0546)로 검정력 부족의 near-miss이지 null이 아니다. 높은 생존율만으로 합병증 공개 의무가 사라지지 않는다(Dambrosio 2026). [확인]
> - 종합 한 줄: 경사 식립은 배치 결정이 아니라 모니터링 간격을 바꾸고, 단축 임플란트에서는 길이보다 보철 유지 방식과 삽입 깊이가 중요하며, 즉시 부하는 안정성 관문을 통과한 경우에만 같다. [미검증]

## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the surgical and prosthetic choice passages of the parent late-failure and marginal-bone-loss synthesis: tilted implants, flapless surgery, short implants, loading timing, and zygomatic implants.
The tilted-implant bone-loss penalty is a late cost, flapless failure signals are bias artefacts, short-implant results depend on the length definition and follow-up horizon, and immediate loading is equivalent only inside a stability envelope.
Zygomatic implants reach high survival even in irradiated cancer patients, with an irradiation signal that is an underpowered near-miss rather than a null.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 후기 실패·변연골 소실 종합의 외과·보철 선택 단락을 담는다: 경사 식립, 플랩리스 수술, 단축 임플란트, 부하 시점, 관골 임플란트.
경사 임플란트의 변연골 불이익은 후기에 나타나는 비용이고, 플랩리스의 실패 신호는 편향 산물이며, 단축 임플란트 결과는 길이 정의와 추적 기간에 따라 달라지고, 즉시 부하는 안정성 범위 안에서만 동등하다.
관골 임플란트는 방사선 치료를 받은 암 환자에서도 높은 생존을 보이며, 방사선 신호는 null이 아니라 검정력이 부족한 near-miss다.

## Synthesis

Surgical/anatomic choices show a more nuanced picture than older literature suggested. [[implants/mbl/malak-2024-implant-failure-marginal-bone-loss]] resolves an apparent tension with [[implants/mbl/ting-2017-surgical-patient-factors-affecting-marginal]] (which found no significant tilted-vs-axial MBL effect across 4 shorter-follow-up SRs): the tilted-implant MBL penalty is **time-dependent**, absent short-term but significant and widening by 3 years (+0.08mm) and long-term (+0.18mm) — Ting's earlier null result reflected shorter-follow-up source studies, not a true absence of effect. Failure risk itself remains equivalent between tilted and axial (RR=1.02), so tilting to avoid anatomic structures is not contraindicated, but warrants longer-term crestal-bone monitoring.

**The time-dependence claim does not rest on Malak's reconciliation alone — the same research group reproduced the flip in its own data.** [[implants/mbl/del-fabbro-2014-marginal-bone-axial-tilted-sr-ma]] (19 studies, 670 patients, 1,494 axial + 1,338 tilted, ≥1 year) found crestal bone loss **not significantly different** at 1 year (axial 0.43–1.13 vs tilted 0.34–1.14 mm, P=.09) or at ≥3 years (P=.30) — the foundational SR+MA that established tilted implants as predictable. Eight years later [[implants/full-arch/del-fabbro-2022-full-arch-tilted-axial-implants-sr-ma]] (24 studies, 2,637 patients, 5,594 tilted + 5,611 axial, **3–18 year** follow-up) reports MBL **significantly lower around axial implants (P<.0001)**. Same investigators, same question, opposite verdict — and in the direction Malak's time-stratified pooling predicts. This is stronger corroboration than the Ting-vs-Malak reconciliation the paragraph above offers, because it removes between-group methodology as the explanation. **One honest confound:** the 2022 update brought both longer follow-up *and* roughly four times the sample, so "the effect needed time to appear" and "the 2014 analysis was underpowered" are not separable from this pair alone. Malak breaks the tie — its stratification shows the effect *growing* within a single analysis (NS → +0.08 mm at 3 y → +0.18 mm long-term), which power alone would not produce. The practical reading is unchanged and now better supported: **tilt freely for anatomy, but the crestal-bone cost is a late cost, so the monitoring interval — not the placement decision — is what tilting should change.** Several other surgical variables Ting examined (flapless vs flapped, 1- vs 2-stage, insertion torque, soft-tissue grafting) showed **no proven MBL effect** — useful for de-prioritizing intuitively-appealing-but-unproven technique debates. The flapless case is worth opening up, because "no MBL effect" is only half of it and the other half is a small lesson in this page's own thesis. [[implants/chrcanovic-2014-flapless-vs-conventional-implant-surgery-ma]] (23 studies: 10 RCT, 7 CCT, 6 retrospective) confirms Ting on bone — **MBL MD −0.07 mm, P=0.16, NS** — and on infection (RR 0.96, P=0.96). But on *failure* it found flapless carrying a **75% higher relative risk (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.07–2.86, P=0.03)** — which **disappeared when the analysis was restricted to the 5 low-risk-of-bias studies**, revealing the signal as an artefact of the 16 high-RoB studies (funnel plots showed no publication bias). So flapless is not a *bone-loss* question at all, and its *failure* signal is a bias signal. This is Giok's "nothing is convincing" operating at the level of a single technique: the same dataset yields a significant harm or a null depending only on how strictly you filter methodology. One directly actionable Ting finding: **ridge augmentation to fit a standard-length implant produced more MBL than simply placing a shorter implant instead** — a load-bearing argument for the short-implant papers below.

Short implants (≤6mm) get their most rigorous long-term evaluation yet in this batch. [[implants/short-narrow/lin-2026-ten-year-outcomes-short-dental]] (first ≥10-year meta-analysis for the strict ≤6mm definition) finds statistically lower survival vs long implants (RR 0.920 patient-level/0.943 implant-level) but a still-acceptable >89% 10-year survival after LTFU correction, with equivalent MBL (0.277mm) and complications — and identifies **screw-retention as a major, actionable survival deficit** (83.6–80.7% vs 97.0–96.8% for cement-retention). A complementary single-center 6.5-year cohort ([[implants/short-narrow/kim-2026-long-term-outcomes-short-standard-implants]]) found MBL statistically indistinguishable between 6mm short and 8.5mm standard implants (0.05 vs 0.12mm), with **insertion depth — not implant length or crown-implant ratio — the only variable significantly correlated with MBL** (r=0.372). Read together: the "short implant" literature's real lesson may be less about length per se and more about **prosthetic retention design and precise subcrestal positioning**.

**But Lin's survival deficit is contested by a larger synthesis, and the disagreement is informative rather than embarrassing.** [[implants/short-narrow/arbildo-vega-2025-short-vs-standard-implants-edentulous-umbrella-review]] (umbrella of **60 systematic reviews**, 790 records screened, PROSPERO CRD42020218497) finds **survival, failure, and prosthetic complications all non-significant** between short and standard implants — and reports short implants showing **less MBL and fewer biological complications, at high-confidence grading**. That is the opposite sign to Lin on survival, from far more source reviews. The two do not actually collide once scope is read: Arbildo-Vega pools the **broad short-implant band across all follow-up horizons**, while Lin is deliberately the strict **≤6 mm definition at ≥10 years**. The deficit appears only at the shortest length *and* the longest horizon — which is the same **definition-and-time-dependent** structure this page already documented for tilted implants, arriving independently in a different literature. Practical consequence: quoting "short implants survive as well as standard" is defensible for the general short band, and *not* defensible for ≤6 mm at ten years; and in both readings **short implants win on MBL**, which is the endpoint this page is about.

The primary layer beneath both fills in what length alone cannot explain. [[implants/short-narrow/barausse-2024-4mm-short-implants-posterior-atrophic-8year]] takes the extreme case — **4 mm** implants, 212 patients / 496 implants, mean **8.02 years** in severely atrophic posterior jaws — and reports **95.36% cumulative survival** (95% CI 93.12–97.04) with **MBL of 0.47 mm at 1 year progressing only to 0.59 mm at 10 years, with no significant increase after year 3**. That plateau is a genuine contrast worth holding next to the tilted-implant finding above: **tilted MBL grows with time; short-implant MBL flattens after year 3.** "Late MBL" is not one behaviour — it depends on what is driving it. Barausse also surfaces the single most actionable variable in this entire page: **higher hygiene-visit frequency significantly reduced failure risk (p<0.001)** — a modifiable, prosthesis-independent protective factor that none of the ten umbrella reviews above put a number on. (Its maxillary-risk finding, p=0.02, feeds the arch axis established earlier.) [[implants/short-narrow/emfietzoglou-2024-short-implants-6mm-survival-posterior-edentulism-sr]] frames the same ≤6 mm question as an RCT-only SR positioning short implants as a **regeneration-sparing** alternative in resorbed posterior sites — the same conclusion Ting reaches from the augmentation side — though its pooled figures sit in full text this wiki does not hold. And the historical baseline [[implants/short-narrow/lee-2009-marginal-bone-single-short-implant]] (n=40, machined-surface Brånemark, 1 year) reported short-vs-standard MBL non-significant **and no effect of crown-to-implant ratio** (neither C:I ≤1 nor >1 differed) — an early null on the mechanical intuition that short implants must fail from leverage, later corroborated by Kim's finding that insertion depth, not C:I, tracks MBL. Machined surfaces and one year of follow-up make Lee a reference point, not evidence for current practice.

Loading-protocol timing ([[implants/loading-protocol/rismanchian-2025-immediate-nonimmediate-loading-umbrella-review]]) and anatomic-avoidance strategy (zygomatic implants, [[implants/dambrosio-2026-clinical-risk-medico-legal-implications]]) round out the picture. Immediate vs nonimmediate loading of single implants shows no significant survival or MBL difference given adequate primary stability — though this narrative-umbrella conclusion sits in some tension with giok-2026's formally graded finding that immediate loading reaches "suggestive" risk-factor status; the difference is likely one of scope (single-implant loading *timing* alone vs. immediate placement-and-loading combined scenarios in giok-2026's pooled observational data) rather than a true contradiction.

**"Given adequate primary stability" is the load-bearing clause, and the umbrella layer never quantifies it. Benic does.** [[implants/loading-protocol/benic-2014-loading-protocols-single-implant-crowns-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 11 RCTs) found immediate and conventional loading of single-implant crowns equivalent on survival (1-year OR 0.75, 95% CI 0.32–1.76) and MBL (SMD −0.05 mm, 95% CI −0.41 to +0.31) at 1, 2, 3 and 5 years, with no papilla-level difference — but states the **envelope inside which that equivalence was demonstrated: insertion torque ≥20–45 Ncm or ISQ ≥60–65, and no simultaneous bone augmentation.** Outside that envelope the evidence does not support immediate loading. That converts decision point 7 below from a disposition ("reserve for confirmed stability") into a **checkable threshold** — and it also dissolves much of the Rismanchian-vs-Giok tension, since Giok's pooled observational data is not restricted to this envelope while the RCTs are. [[immediate-implant/loading-protocol/gjelvold-2021-immediate-delayed-loading-single-tooth-5year-rct]] then tests the envelope prospectively at its stated boundary (n=50 healed maxillary single-tooth sites, IL eligibility **≥30 Ncm**, 5 years): survival 100% vs 95.8%, **MBL −0.50 vs −0.54 mm (p=0.782)**, PES/WES equivalent, and the 1-year papilla advantage for delayed loading **resolved by 5 years**. Equivalence therefore holds out to five years *at the threshold Benic specifies* — and, as noted in the host-factor section, the only variable that did move MBL in that trial was **smoking**. The consistent message across the loading literature is that timing is not the risk; the stability gate and the patient are. For patients requiring implants in severely atrophic maxillae, zygomatic implants achieve high survival (90–100%) at the cost of common (if often under-reported) complications — chiefly sinusitis — with the ZAGA/anatomy-guided technique outperforming the original surgical technique on both sinusitis risk (3.7% vs 27.2%) and immediate-loading feasibility (89.6% vs 22.3%); Dambrosio's explicit medico-legal framing (informed consent, surgeon experience, "no viable alternative" case selection) is a reminder that high survival numbers alone do not discharge the duty to disclose realistic complication rates.

**Zygomatic implants may partly escape the irradiation penalty documented above — a signal worth holding at exactly its measured strength, no more.** [[implants/full-arch/scocca-2026-zygomatic-implants-head-neck-cancer-sr-ma]] (SR+MA, 17 studies, 294 head-and-neck oncology patients, 679 zygomatic implants) pools **97% survival (95% CI 94–99, I²=42.4%)** in precisely the population Pacheco identifies as worst-off, and finds **radiotherapy raising failure odds only non-significantly (OR 3.42, 95% CI 0.97–12.06, p=0.0546)**. Set against Pacheco's conventional-implant irradiated survival of 81.52%, the contrast invites a mechanistically plausible reading — zygomatic bone is denser, better vascularized, and often outside the primary radiation field — but the honest statement is narrower: **p=0.0546 with a CI running from 0.97 to 12.06 is an underpowered near-miss, not a null.** The point estimate (3.42) is entirely compatible with real harm; what the data rule out is precision, not risk. Two further findings are usable: failure was **2% when ZIs were placed at the primary resection vs 9% at secondary surgery** (p=0.4073, NS — directionally favouring primary placement but not established), and patient-reported outcomes (UW-QOL, EORTC QLQ-H&N35) showed satisfactory function **even in irradiated patients** — which is the outcome the medico-legal framing above is ultimately about. Scocca's own caution matches Dambrosio's: high survival does not eliminate a substantial prosthodontic and maintenance burden.
