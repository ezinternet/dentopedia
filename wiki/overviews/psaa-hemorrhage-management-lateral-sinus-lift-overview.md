## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - **범위**: 측방창 상악동거상술 (Lateral Sinus Floor Elevation, LSFE) 중 후상치조동맥 (Posterior Superior Alveolar Artery, PSAA) 출혈을 예방·관리하기 위한 술전 해부 평가부터 술중 처치까지의 완전한 임상 근거 종합. [확인]
> - **PSAA 분류**: 3유형 — 골내형 (intraosseous, Gulec 2025 기준 ~63%), 점막하형 (submucosal, Urechescu 2026 기준 48.6% 최다), 동외형 (extraosseous). 유형별 출혈 위험·처치 전략이 다르다. [확인]
> - **코호트 간 유형 분포 차이**: 골내형이 Gulec(터키) 63% vs Urechescu(루마니아) 17.6%로 큰 차이 → 모집단·장비·분류 기준 차이 반영; 어느 단일 수치로 개별 환자를 판단할 수 없다. 술전 개인 CBCT 평가가 필수인 이유. [확인]
> - **직경과 거리**: 직경 >1 mm 동맥은 동저·치조정 거리가 유의하게 감소 → 직경이 클수록 더 위험하다. 치조정 기준 평균 ~16 mm(Gulec), 동저 기준 평균 8.30 mm(Urechescu). [확인]
> - **출혈 중증도 — Radmand 2023 SR+MA (37편, 11,402상악동; 2026-11-27 추가)**: 평균 직경 1.06 mm; 55%는 <1 mm, **97%는 <2 mm**, 2%만 >2 mm — "상악동거상술 중 PSAA 중증 출혈은 실질적 임상 문제가 아님(생명위협적 출혈 아님)." 반응성 혈관수축이 출혈량을 제한. 직경 >2 mm인 2%만 과다출혈(비생명위협적) 위험 → 사전 계획 필요. [확인]
> - **측방창 높이와 위험 분류**: 창 높이 >11 mm에서 골내형 PSAA 비율 유의하게 증가(χ²=19.11, p=0.004; Urechescu 2026). 창 높이 4분류 체계로 술전 계획 단계에서 위험을 예측할 수 있다. [확인]
> - **출혈 없이 통과하는 전략 (Basma 2021)**: CT에서 대직경 PSAA 분지가 확인되면 — 절제·전기소작 대신 — 피에조전기 기구 (piezoelectric device) 로 슈나이더막 (Schneiderian membrane) 과 함께 혈관을 en bloc 거상. 1증례, 출혈 없음. [확인]
> - **임상 의사결정 흐름**: CBCT에서 PSAA 위치·유형·직경 확인 → 창 높이 계획(>11 mm 필요 시 추가 주의) → 대직경 혈관 확인 시 피에조 "막과 함께 거상" 전환 → 골내형은 절개선 계획에서 회피 최우선. [미검증 — 이 흐름 자체는 추론이고 직접 연구 없음]
> - **한계**: 모든 해부 데이터는 후향적 CBCT; Basma 2021은 단일 증례(n=1); 혈관 직경은 CBCT에서 과소 측정 경향; 대형 무작위 출혈 관리 연구 없음.

## Three-line Summary

This overview synthesizes the available evidence on posterior superior alveolar artery (PSAA) anatomy characterization (Gulec 2025, Urechescu 2026, Radmand 2023 SR+MA) and intraoperative hemorrhage management (Basma 2021) for lateral-window sinus floor elevation.
PSAA is present in 68–79% of sinuses; the largest pooled analysis (Radmand 2023: 37 studies, 11,402 sinuses) confirms mean diameter 1.06 mm with 97% < 2 mm — "life-threatening hemorrhage from PSAA injury is not a genuine clinical concern"; larger arteries sit closer to key landmarks, and lateral windows > 11 mm are more likely to encounter an intraosseous PSAA.
Preoperative CBCT-based individualized assessment drives safe window design; the 2% of PSAAs > 2 mm require pre-planned management (piezoelectric en-bloc elevation per Basma 2021 is the only documented intraoperative hemorrhage-avoidance strategy).

## 세줄요약

이 종합 페이지는 측방창 상악동거상술에서 후상치조동맥 (Posterior Superior Alveolar Artery, PSAA) 출혈 관리에 관한 현재 보유 근거를 통합한다: 해부 특성화(Gulec 2025, Urechescu 2026, Radmand 2023 SR+MA 37편)와 술중 처치(Basma 2021).

## §1. PSAA Anatomy — What Two Cohorts Tell Us

### 1.1 Prevalence and Bilaterality

PSAA is detectable on CBCT in **68–70%** of sinuses (Gulec 2025: 70.2% bilateral patients; Urechescu 2026: 68.1% of sinuses). Absence on CBCT does **not** exclude hemorrhage risk — superficially coursing arteries may be radiologically invisible.

### 1.2 Course Type Distribution (Two Cohorts)

The two cohorts report strikingly different intraosseous proportions — a critical discordance:

| Course type | Gulec 2025 (Turkey, n=510) | Urechescu 2026 (Romania, n=1152) |
|---|---|---|
| Intraosseous (골내형) | ~63% of identified | 25.9% of identified / **17.6%** of all sinuses |
| Submucosal (점막하형) | — | 71.4% of identified / **48.6%** of all sinuses |
| Intrasinus / Extraosseous | ~13% | 2.7% of identified |
| Superficial / Not visible | ~3% | 31.9% not visible |

**Why the discordance matters**: Population variation, different CBCT voxel size (Gulec 0.35 mm vs Urechescu 0.15 mm), classification system differences (Danesh-Sani vs study-specific), and cohort selection all contribute. Neither figure can be assumed to describe an individual patient. The practical takeaway remains: **preoperative CBCT assessment of the specific patient's PSAA course is non-negotiable**.

### 1.3 Diameter and Hemorrhage Risk

Gulec 2025 measured PSAA diameter distribution: **<1 mm in ~55%, 1–2 mm in ~22%, >2 mm in <2%**.

**Radmand 2023 SR+MA (37 studies, 11,402 sinuses — largest pooled data)** confirms: mean diameter 1.06 ± 0.05 mm; **55% < 1 mm · 41% 1–2 mm · only 2% > 2 mm**. Crucially, the study concludes: *"life-threatening hemorrhage resulting from injury to the superior alveolar arteries is not a genuine clinical concern"* — reactive vasoconstriction in small-calibre arteries limits blood loss. Only the 2% > 2 mm pose risk of excessive (non–life-threatening) bleeding and require pre-planned management.

A key finding: **larger-diameter arteries (>1 mm) sit significantly closer to both the sinus floor and the alveolar crest** — i.e., the arteries that bleed most are positioned least favorably for safe window placement. This is the anatomical basis for diameter-based risk stratification.

| Risk category | Diameter | Pooled prevalence | Expected bleeding (Radmand 2023) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manageable | < 1 mm | 55% | Trivial; vasoconstriction stops it |
| Cautious | 1–2 mm | 41% | Standard local measures sufficient |
| Pre-plan | > 2 mm | **2%** | Excessive, non–life-threatening; plan beforehand |

### 1.4 Distance to Surgical Landmarks

| Landmark | Gulec 2025 (mean ± SD) | Urechescu 2026 (mean ± SD) |
|---|---|---|
| PSAA → sinus floor | R: 7.08±2.89 mm / L: 8.53±3.41 mm | 8.30±4.31 mm overall |
| PSAA → alveolar crest | R: 15.93±3.45 mm / L: 17.24±3.71 mm | (by window class — see §2) |

Clinical implication: the alveolar-crest mean of ~16 mm is the principal osteotomy height cap in Gulec's framework. Urechescu's window-class distances refine this further (see §2).

## §2. Lateral Window Height and PSAA Risk — Urechescu 2026's 4-Class Framework

Urechescu 2026 introduced a novel **4-class lateral window height classification** correlating the simulated osteotomy height above the sinus floor with the probability of encountering each PSAA course type:

| Window class | Height above sinus floor | Mean PSAA-floor distance | Intraosseous PSAA frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class 1 | <3 mm | 2.0 ± 0.7 mm | 30.4% |
| Class 2 (small) | 3–8 mm | 5.9 ± 2.9 mm | 20.9% |
| Class 3 (medium) | 8–11 mm | 9.4 ± 0.9 mm | 25.9% |
| Class 4 (large) | >11 mm | 14.2 ± 2.9 mm | **35.0% ↑** |

**Statistical evidence**: χ²(6)=19.11, p=0.004; Cramer's V=0.111. Large windows (>11 mm) have a significantly overrepresented intraosseous PSAA frequency (standardized residual +2.3). Sex did not modify the relationship (p=0.332).

**Surgical implication**: extended superior osteotomies (required when residual bone height is very low, or when grafting demands high elevation) carry a significantly greater risk of intraosseous PSAA encounter. When large windows are unavoidable, preoperative CBCT must map the PSAA course precisely — and the management strategy should be pre-decided.

## §3. Intraoperative Management — The Evidence We Hold

### 3.1 Conventional hemorrhage management (background knowledge — no held paper)

Standard approaches (electrocautery, pressure, vessel ligation) are described in surgical texts but no held paper directly studies their efficacy for PSAA hemorrhage. This gap is real — see §5.

### 3.2 Piezoelectric en-bloc elevation (Basma 2021)

The only held paper with direct intraoperative PSAA management data is Basma 2021 (case report, n=1):

**Case**: 58-year-old healthy female, planned for implant-supported fixed prosthesis. Preoperative CT revealed a large PSA branch within a bony canal on the lateral sinus wall.

**Management**: Rather than risking laceration, the surgeon deployed a **piezoelectric device** to elevate the PSA branch *intact, en bloc with the Schneiderian membrane*, throughout the entire membrane elevation. No suturing, cauterization, or separate vessel handling was required.

**Outcome**: No intraoperative hemorrhage. No membrane perforation. Uneventful postoperative healing.

**Mechanism**: Piezoelectric ultrasonic cutting frequency selectively disrupts calcified tissue (bone) while leaving soft tissue and vessels intact. This selective tissue discrimination is what enables the vessel to be elevated rather than lacerated.

**Limitations of this evidence**: Single case (n=1); artery diameter not reported numerically; no hemorrhage actually occurred, so hemostatic efficacy cannot be directly assessed; no control arm.

## §4. Integrated Clinical Decision Framework

Based on the three held papers, the following decision logic is supportable:

### Pre-operative (CBCT reading)

1. **Map PSAA course**: Is it visible? What type (intraosseous / submucosal / extraosseous)? What diameter? What distance from sinus floor and alveolar crest?
2. **Plan window height class**: What osteotomy height does the clinical scenario require (determined by residual bone height and desired graft volume)? Window >11 mm → classify as high-risk for intraosseous PSAA.
3. **Identify high-alert cases**: Large-diameter PSAA (<1 mm is low-risk; >1 mm → closer to landmarks → higher risk). Intraosseous type → highest risk; submucosal type → usually elevates with membrane, lower risk.

### Intraoperative (strategy selection)

| Scenario | Recommended approach |
|---|---|
| PSAA not visible on CBCT | Standard technique; remain vigilant below alveolar crest + 16 mm |
| Submucosal PSAA, small diameter | Standard membrane elevation; expect artery to elevate with membrane |
| Intraosseous PSAA, small diameter | Plan osteotomy to stay inferior to canal; reposition window if possible |
| **Large-diameter PSAA confirmed on CT** | **Piezoelectric device; plan en-bloc elevation with membrane (Basma 2021)** |
| Unexpected hemorrhage (vessel lacerated) | Pressure packing, electrocautery — no held paper; call on surgical training |

> **Caveat**: The "intraoperative strategy" column for large-diameter PSAA is based on a single case report. This is the best available held evidence; there is no RCT. For unexpected hemorrhage management, no held paper exists.
