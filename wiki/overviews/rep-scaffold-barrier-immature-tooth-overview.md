## Three-line Summary

Two papers (Alqedairi 2026 narrative review; Li 2026 SR+MA, 15 studies, 4 RCTs, PROSPERO CRD420261337704) add material-selection refinements to the established REP framework: PRP/PRF scaffolds outperform blood clot for dentinal wall thickening while periapical healing equivalence is maintained, and White MTA achieves near-perfect radiographic success (pooled 0.99) but carries a 58.33% tooth discolouration rate versus 7.69% for alternative bioceramics (P=.01).

The scaffold finding is nuanced — platelet concentrates (PC) favour root lengthening, blood clot favours dentinal wall/canal-area thickening (Li 2026); combined with Alqedairi's synthesis of two decades of literature, blood clot remains the most widely used scaffold for its simplicity and non-inferior efficacy, while PRP+MTA subgroup analysis shows the strongest superiority signal (OR 7.49, 95% CI 1.16–48.22).

Clinical rule: for posterior/non-esthetic zones WMTA is an effective coronal barrier; for anterior/esthetic teeth, use Biodentine or Totalfill — the discolouration disadvantage of WMTA is now meta-analytically quantified and actionable; overall GRADE certainty is very low, so clinical judgment remains primary.

## Summary

The existing REP overview ([[overviews/regenerative-endodontics-rep-overview]]) established that regenerative endodontic procedures (재생근관치료, REP) and MTA apical plug (무기삼산화물집합체 근단 폐쇄, MAP) achieve equivalent survival and success rates while REP is significantly superior for structural root development. Two new papers (2026) add two material-selection refinements that complete the decision framework:

1. **Scaffold axis**: PRP (혈소판풍부혈장) and PRF (혈소판풍부 피브린) scaffolds outperform blood clot (혈병) for dentinal wall thickening, while periapical healing equivalence is maintained. The two scaffold types also differ in the dimension of root development they preferentially drive.

2. **Barrier axis**: White MTA (WMTA) delivers near-perfect radiographic success as a coronal barrier or apical plug (pooled 0.99), but carries a clinically meaningful and statistically significant discolouration risk (58.33%) compared to alternative bioceramics such as Biodentine and Totalfill (7.69%, P=.01).

These two axes, layered onto the existing REP-vs-MAP survival equivalence framework, produce a complete material-selection matrix for clinical decision-making in necrotic immature permanent teeth.

## Axis 1 — Scaffold: PRP/PRF vs Blood Clot

### Mechanism and rationale

Scaffolds in REP serve as the physical matrix for stem cell homing, attachment, and differentiation. The three-component REP biology (viable stem cell source + scaffold + biological signal) means scaffold quality directly determines the tissue-engineering environment. Blood clot (혈병) — the original and most widely used scaffold — provides fibrin, platelets, and growth factors from periapical bleeding. Platelet concentrates (혈소판 농축물, PC) — PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma), PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin), i-PRF (injectable PRF), PP (Platelet Paste) — provide concentrated PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF, and IGF-1, amplifying the growth-factor milieu.

### What the evidence shows

**Alqedairi 2026 (narrative review)** synthesizes two decades of literature (2004–2025) and concludes:
- PRP/PRF scaffolds show superior dentinal wall thickening vs blood clot alone
- Reference RCT: Alagl et al. 2017, n=40, the most-cited head-to-head
- Periapical healing (85–94%) and root development (74–81%) are consistently favorable with either scaffold
- Blood clot remains the most widely used scaffold due to simplicity, cost, and availability — its efficacy is not inferior for overall success

**Li 2026 SR+MA** (15 studies, 4 RCTs, PROSPERO CRD420261337704) adds quantitative nuance:
- PC vs blood clot, radiographic success (4 studies, 145 observations): OR 3.56 (95% CI 0.87–14.52), not significant overall
- PRP+MTA subgroup: OR 7.49 (95% CI 1.16–48.22), the only subgroup reaching significance
- Critical divergence in root maturation pattern:
  - PC → favours **root length increase** (P=.004 vs blood clot in one study)
  - Blood clot → favours **dentinal wall/canal-area thickening** (P<.05 vs most PC groups in another study)
- GRADE certainty for the PC vs BC comparison: very low (indirectness, imprecision)

### Clinical interpretation

| Scaffold | Best for | Evidence level | Practical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood clot (혈병) | Overall success; dentinal wall thickening | Low (indirect) | Simplest; most widely used; non-inferior for periapical healing |
| PRP/PRF | Dentinal wall thickening superiority; root length increase when combined with MTA | Very low | Requires centrifuge or external preparation; adds chair time and cost |
| LPRF (백혈구풍부 피브린) | NOT recommended | Very low (case series, 2/14 retreatments) | See existing REP overview — inferior outcomes vs standard scaffold |

**Bottom line, Axis 1**: Blood clot is the defensible default. PRP/PRF is a reasonable upgrade for cases where maximizing dentinal wall thickening or root length is the specific clinical priority (e.g., thin-walled teeth at high fracture risk), particularly when combined with MTA (PRP+MTA subgroup shows the clearest benefit signal). The choice does not significantly alter periapical healing rates.

## Axis 2 — Barrier: WMTA vs Alternative Bioceramics (Discolouration)

### The WMTA discolouration problem

White MTA (WMTA) has been the dominant coronal barrier in REP protocols since the early 2000s, valued for its biocompatibility, setting expansion (marginal seal), and antimicrobial properties. However, clinicians have observed coronal discolouration (tooth crown staining) after WMTA placement, attributed to bismuth oxide and calcium silicate oxidation products diffusing into adjacent dentinal tubules.

### Li 2026 SR+MA quantification

The first meta-analytic quantification of this risk:

| Outcome | WMTA | Alternative bioceramic | Statistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiographic success | **0.99** (95% CI 0.96–1.00) | — | Pooled proportion, I²=0%, n=6 studies |
| Clinical success | — | 100.00% | OR 0.18 (95% CI 0.02–1.68), NS |
| Tooth discolouration | **58.33%** | **7.69%** (Biodentine) | P=.01, Aly et al. |
| Tooth discolouration | 14.30% | 0.00% | Not significant, Bartaw et al. |

- 2-study base for discolouration analysis (n=67 total observations)
- GRADE certainty for WMTA radiographic success: **very low** (risk of bias, imprecision)
- GRADE certainty for WMTA vs bioceramic clinical success: **moderate** (downgraded only for imprecision)

### Alternative bioceramics

- **Biodentine** (트라이칼슘 실리케이트, tricalcium silicate cement): No bismuth oxide; 12-minute setting; compressive strength comparable to dentin; dentinal tubule penetration confirmed; 7.69% discolouration in the Aly et al. study.
- **Totalfill BC RRM** (bioceramic root repair material): Hydraulic calcium silicate; no bismuth oxide; excellent biocompatibility; 0% discolouration in Bartaw et al.

### Clinical rule

**Posterior/non-esthetic teeth**: WMTA remains effective (pooled success 0.99) — the discolouration, while likely, is clinically acceptable in non-visible zones.

**Anterior/esthetic teeth (전치부·심미 영역)**: The 58.33% discolouration rate with WMTA is a clinically unacceptable aesthetic risk. Use Biodentine or Totalfill BC RRM as the coronal barrier.

This is the same zone-based principle already applied to intracanal antibiotic paste selection (TAP staining → DAP preferred in anterior teeth) — now extended to the coronal barrier material.

## Decision Matrix — REP Material Selection

Combining both axes into a clinical decision table for necrotic immature permanent teeth:

| Clinical scenario | Scaffold choice | Coronal barrier | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posterior tooth, thick walls, standard case | Blood clot | WMTA | Simplest protocol; discolouration acceptable; success 0.99 |
| Posterior tooth, thin walls (fracture risk priority) | PRP or PRF | WMTA | PRP+MTA subgroup shows strongest dentinal wall thickening signal; discolouration acceptable |
| Anterior/esthetic tooth, any wall thickness | Blood clot | Biodentine or Totalfill | WMTA discolouration 58.33% — esthetically unacceptable; Biodentine 7.69% |
| Anterior tooth, thin walls (root length priority) | PRP or PRF | Biodentine or Totalfill | Maximize root development AND avoid discolouration |
| Any tooth — if centrifuge unavailable | Blood clot | Zone-dependent (see above) | Blood clot periapical healing = PC; dentinal wall thickening slightly inferior |

**Notes on GRADE**: All recommendations above carry very-low-to-low GRADE certainty. The discolouration finding (P=.01) is based on 2 studies (n=67). Use clinical judgment and patient preference alongside these estimates.

## Integration with Existing REP Overview

The existing overview ([[overviews/regenerative-endodontics-rep-overview]]) established:

1. REP ≈ MAP for survival (97.8% vs 97.1%) and success (91.3% vs 94.6%), LOW evidence (Torabinejad 2017 SR+MA)
2. REP > MAP for root elongation and dentinal wall thickening (Panda 2022, Stefanidou 2024, two independent SR+MAs)
3. Disinfection: CH ≈ TAP (both ~97% 1-year success) > DAP (84.2%), Alovisi 2026 SR+MA — equal efficacy → prefer CH (no crown discolouration risk)
4. LPRF scaffold: NOT recommended (2/14 retreatments + persistent periapical periodontitis at 3 years)

This overview adds:

5. Scaffold refinement within REP: PRP/PRF > blood clot for dentinal wall thickening; overall radiographic success equivalent; PC vs BC scaffold choice diverges by root maturation dimension
6. Barrier selection: WMTA effective (0.99 success) but 58.33% discolouration → reserve WMTA for posterior zones; use Biodentine/Totalfill for anterior/esthetic teeth

**Complete decision flow (updated)**:

```
Necrotic immature permanent tooth
  ↓
Goal: structural root completion?
  Yes → REP (not MAP)
  No (rapid apical closure only) → MAP
  ↓ (REP path)
Tooth zone?
  Anterior/esthetic → Biodentine or Totalfill as coronal barrier
  Posterior → WMTA acceptable
  ↓
Wall thickness / fracture risk priority?
  High fracture risk (thin walls) → PRP or PRF scaffold
  Standard case → blood clot (simpler, non-inferior periapical healing)
  ↓
Intracanal medicament?
  Either CH or TAP (~97% 1-year success, GRADE high); avoid DAP in anterior (84.2% + staining)
```
