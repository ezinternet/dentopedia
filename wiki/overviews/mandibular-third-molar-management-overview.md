## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 결론: 하악 사랑니(Mandibular Third Molar, M3M) 관리는 본질적으로 **예방적 발치 (prophylactic extraction) vs 치료적 발치 (therapeutic extraction) vs 보존·관찰 (retention/monitoring)** 의사결정 문제다 — 9편 종합: 병리·적응증·급성치료 코어 4편(Ye 2021, Sun 2026, Schmidt 2021, Al-Aroomi 2025) + 발치 주위수술기 보조요법 3편(Canellas 2022, Canellas 2020, Firoozi 2022) + 인접 대구치를 대신 뽑는 교정 분기 2편(de la Rosa-Gay 2006, Livas 2011).
> - 매복 (impaction)은 측정 가능한 병리 부담을 만든다(Ye 2021, 후향 432개): 치관주위염 (pericoronitis) 82.4%, 제2대구치 (M2M) 원심 우식 18.8%, M2M 원심 치주병변 14.8%. 이 수치가 적응증 판단의 토대.
> - 매복 형태가 병리를 예측한다(Ye 2021): 연조직·수직 매복 → 치관주위염 (p<0.0001); 근심경사·얕은(position A) → M2M 원심 우식 (p<0.0001); 근심·수평·깊은 매복 → M2M 원심 치주병변 (p<0.0001). 즉 "어떤 매복이냐"가 "무슨 병이 생기냐"를 가른다.
> - 연령 효과(Ye 2021): 청년층은 치관주위염 우세, ≥30세부터 우식·치주병변 누적(p<0.0001) → 깊은 매복은 지연 가능하나 무기한 보존은 금물.
> - 전문가 합의 적응증 3단계(Sun 2026, 중국 다기관 합의문): ① 확정적 병리 적응증(재발성 치관주위염·간극감염, 우식, M2M 치주손상, 낭종/종양, 중등도↑ 치근흡수), ② 치료적 발치(교정·악교정·TMD·방사선/항암/면역요법 전·임신 전), ③ 예방적 발치(현재 무증상이나 위험 매복) — 3단계는 **공유의사결정 (shared decision-making)** 강조, >35세·완전골매복·고위험은 관찰 관리.
> - 급성 적응증 1순위 = 치관주위염(Schmidt 2021): 일차치료는 **국소 세척·변연제거 (local irrigation/debridement)** 이며 항생제는 감염 확산·전신 증상(발열·개구장애·연하곤란)에만 한정. 그런데 치과의사 약 75%가 항생제 처방 → 치과 항생제 과처방의 핵심 원인. 단계 치료: 국소요법+NSAIDs → 재발 첫 에피소드 판절제술 (operculectomy) → 2번째 이상 발치(NICE).
> - **주위수술기 보조요법은 목표 결과별로 고른다 (2026-09-13 통합)** — 부종·초기 통증: **덱사메타손 (Dexamethasone) 8mg 점막하 주사**가 61 RCT 네트워크 메타분석 (Network Meta-Analysis, NMA)에서 위약 대비 부종 −3.58, 술후 1·2일 통증 −30.95/−15.25 VAS, 전 약제 심각한 부작용 없음 (Canellas 2022; 초록 기반 페이지).
> - **치조골염 (Alveolar Osteitis, AO) 예방**: 발치와 내 중재 전체 RR 0.39, 그중 **혈소판 풍부 피브린 (Platelet-Rich Fibrin, PRF) OR 0.28**·**0.2% 클로르헥시딘 (Chlorhexidine, CHX) 겔 OR 0.52**가 유의 효과와 연구량을 함께 갖춘 선택지 (Canellas 2020, 37 RCT NMA, 중등도 근거). 비약물 통증 보조요법 순위: 초기 통증 = 오존 (SMD −0.84), 후기 통증 = PRF (SMD −1.03), 저출력레이저 (Low-Level Laser Therapy, LLLT)는 초기 통증 최약 (SMD −0.32) (Firoozi 2022 umbrella NMA, 포함 리뷰 비뚤림 불명확).
> - 농축성장인자 (Concentrated Growth Factor, CGF) (Al-Aroomi 2025, 11 RCT SR): 통증 75%·진통제 소비 100%·방사선 골충전 80%·M2M 원심 치주지표 100% 개선이나 AO는 1/3편, 부기·개구장애도 불확실, 메타분석 불가 → **AO 예방이 목적이면 CGF보다 PRF 근거가 강하다**; CGF는 골충전·M2M 치주 보호 목적의 보조.
> - **교정 분기 — M3 대신 인접 대구치를 뽑는 경우**: 교정 목적 제2대구치 발치 후 M3 자연 맹출 성공 **상악 96.2% vs 하악 66.2%**, 하악 실패는 과도한 근심경사·인접접촉 미형성, 높은 놀라 (Nolla) 발육단계·고령이 위험 (de la Rosa-Gay 2006); 상악 제1대구치 발치 교정은 **상악** M3 직립을 성장 대비 약 4배 (19.9° vs 5.6°) 촉진 (Livas 2011) → 교정 중인 젊은 환자의 무증상 M3는 자동 예방발치 대상이 아니라 교정 발치 계획에 종속된 결정이며, 하악 대체는 상악보다 예측성이 낮다.
> - 임상 takeaway: 매복 형태로 위험 층화(Ye) → 교정 계획이 M3를 대체치로 쓰는지 확인(de la Rosa-Gay·Livas) → 3단계 적응증으로 발치/보존 결정(Sun) → 치관주위염은 국소우선·항생제 절제(Schmidt) → 발치 시 부종·통증엔 덱사메타손(Canellas 2022), AO 예방엔 발치와 PRF 또는 CHX 겔(Canellas 2020), CGF는 골·치주 보조(Al-Aroomi). **하치조신경 (Inferior Alveolar Nerve, IAN) 손상 위험·술식 선택**은 별도 프로토콜 참조([[overviews/third-molar-ian-risk-protocol]]).
> - 주의: 근거 위계가 섞여 있다 — Ye는 단일기관 후향(증상환자 의뢰 편향), Sun은 GRADE 미적용 전문가 합의, Al-Aroomi는 메타분석 불가 SR, Canellas 2022는 초록만 확보, Firoozi는 포함 리뷰 비뚤림 불명확, 교정 2편은 후향 방사선 연구. 보조요법 병용(예: 덱사메타손+PRF)을 직접 비교한 논문은 보유하지 않음 — 개별 임상 판단이 우선.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 9 papers framing mandibular third molar (M3M) management as a prophylactic-vs-therapeutic-vs-retention decision across the full pipeline: pathology burden, extraction indication, acute pericoronitis treatment, perioperative morbidity control, and the orthodontic scenario in which an adjacent molar is extracted instead.

Impaction carries a measurable pathology burden whose type is predicted by impaction morphology (Ye 2021, n=432 M3Ms: pericoronitis 82.4%, second-molar distal caries 18.8%, periodontal pathology 14.8%), which Sun 2026 converts into a 3-tier indication framework, and pericoronitis is treated locally first despite ~75% of dentists prescribing antibiotics (Schmidt 2021); once extraction is chosen, dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal best reduces edema and early pain (Canellas 2022 NMA), intrasocket PRF (OR 0.28) or 0.2% chlorhexidine gel (OR 0.52) prevents alveolar osteitis (Canellas 2020 NMA), ozone and PRF lead the nonpharmacological pain adjuncts (Firoozi 2022), and CGF improves pain, bone fill and distal-second-molar periodontal parameters (Al-Aroomi 2025); when orthodontics removes the second molar instead, third molars erupt successfully in 96.2% of maxillary vs 66.2% of mandibular cases (de la Rosa-Gay 2006), and maxillary first-molar extraction uprights the maxillary third molar about four times more than growth alone (Livas 2011).

Clinical takeaway: stratify risk by impaction morphology (Ye) → check whether an orthodontic plan will use the third molar as a replacement → apply the 3-tier indication framework (Sun) → manage pericoronitis locally with reserved antibiotics (Schmidt) → if extracting, match the adjunct to the target outcome (dexamethasone for edema/pain, intrasocket PRF or CHX gel for alveolar osteitis, CGF for bone fill and second-molar periodontal repair), noting that no held paper tests adjunct combinations.

## 세줄요약

하악 사랑니 관리 9편 종합: 매복은 측정 가능한 병리 부담(치관주위염 82.4%, M2M 원심 우식 18.8%, 치주병변 14.8%; Ye 2021, n=432)을 만들고 매복 형태가 병리 종류를 예측하며, 전문가 합의(Sun 2026)가 3단계 적응증(확정적 병리·치료적·예방적)을 제시하고, 치관주위염은 국소 세척·NSAIDs 우선·항생제는 확산·전신 증상 시에만(Schmidt 2021, 실제 약 75% 처방).

발치 결정 후 보조요법은 목표 결과별: 부종·초기 통증은 덱사메타손 8mg 점막하(Canellas 2022 NMA), 치조골염 예방은 발치와 PRF(OR 0.28)·CHX 0.2% 겔(OR 0.52)(Canellas 2020 NMA), 비약물 통증은 초기 오존·후기 PRF 우위(Firoozi 2022), CGF는 통증·골충전·M2M 치주지표 개선이나 AO 효과 불확실(Al-Aroomi 2025); 교정적 제2대구치 발치 후 M3 맹출 성공은 상악 96.2% vs 하악 66.2%(de la Rosa-Gay 2006), 상악 제1대구치 발치는 상악 M3 직립 약 4배(Livas 2011).

임상 핵심: 매복 형태로 위험 층화 → 교정 계획이 M3를 대체치로 쓰는지 확인 → 3단계 적응증으로 발치/보존 결정 → 치관주위염 국소 우선·항생제 절제 → 발치 시 목표별 보조요법(덱사메타손·PRF/CHX 겔·CGF) 선택; 단 병용요법 비교 근거는 없고 Canellas 2022는 초록 기반, Firoozi 포함 리뷰는 비뚤림 불명확임을 명심.

## Summary

Mandibular third molar (M3M) management is, at its core, a **prophylactic-versus-therapeutic extraction decision** layered on a third option — informed retention with surveillance. This synthesis assembles nine papers that supply five inputs a clinician needs for that decision: (1) *what pathology burden impaction actually carries and which impaction patterns predict which disease* (Ye 2021), (2) *a framework for when to remove versus retain/monitor* (Sun 2026), (3) *how to manage pericoronitis — the most common acute indication — and where antibiotics do and do not belong* (Schmidt 2021), (4) *how to control perioperative morbidity once removal is chosen, matched to the outcome being targeted* (Canellas 2022, Canellas 2020, Firoozi 2022, Al-Aroomi 2025), and (5) *when an orthodontic plan extracts an adjacent molar and the third molar becomes the replacement tooth* (de la Rosa-Gay 2006, Livas 2011). The orthogonal question of inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injury risk and surgical technique selection (coronectomy, imaging-staged risk assessment) is covered in a companion overview rather than duplicated here.

**The pathology burden — and its morphology dependence (Ye 2021).** Ye et al. (2021), a single-center retrospective review of 432 M3Ms in 262 Shanghai patients, is the first study to assess all three major M3M-associated pathologies in one cohort using a unified classification (impaction depth, Pell & Gregory position, Winter angulation). Pericoronitis was overwhelmingly the dominant pathology (82.4%), followed by second-molar (M2M) distal caries (18.8%) and M2M distal periodontal pathology (14.8%); 64 teeth carried two or three pathologies simultaneously. The clinically pivotal finding is that **different impaction features predict different pathologies** — so the impaction pattern is itself a risk-stratification tool. Soft-tissue and vertically angulated impactions drove pericoronitis (p=0.0097 and p<0.0001). Shallow (position A) mesio-angular impactions drove M2M distal caries (p<0.0001). Deep (position B/C) mesio-angular and horizontal impactions drove M2M distal periodontal pathology including external root resorption (p<0.0001). Pathology also accumulates with age: pericoronitis dominated younger patients, but caries and periodontal lesions rose significantly in patients ≥30 (p<0.0001). The cost of indefinite retention is concrete — 10 M2Ms required extraction (5 for degree III mobility, 5 for uncurable root caries), clustering in soft-tissue, position A/B, mesio-angular/horizontal impactions at a mean patient age of 44.

**The indication framework (Sun 2026).** Sun et al. (2026), a Chinese multi-center expert consensus published in *Int J Oral Sci*, organizes extraction indications into **three tiers**: (1) *definite pathologic indicators* — recurrent pericoronitis/space infections, caries (in the M3M or the adjacent tooth), periodontal damage to the M2M, cyst/tumor, moderate-to-severe root resorption; (2) *therapeutic extraction* — for orthodontic, orthognathic, TMD, pre-radiotherapy/chemotherapy/immunotherapy, or pre-pregnancy (with deep periodontal involvement) indications; and (3) *prophylactic removal* of potentially risky but currently asymptomatic teeth (partially erupted mesial/horizontal, soft-tissue-impacted unerupted, or complete bony impaction with distal alveolar bone deficiency reaching the crown). The third tier is explicitly governed by **shared decision-making**, and the consensus favors observational management for patients over 35 with complete bony impaction and high surgical risk. The consensus epidemiology corroborates Ye: pericoronitis ~82.4% mandibular, impacted M3Ms cause 50–70% of fascial space infections, ~19.5% develop distal M2M caries, ~5% develop odontogenic cysts/tumors. Notably, the consensus explicitly leaves the optimal extraction *timing* for asymptomatic teeth unresolved — a deliberate acknowledgment that the prophylactic tier is a judgment call, not a rule.

**The leading acute indication and its antibiotic trap (Schmidt 2021).** Pericoronitis is the most common acute reason a M3M presents for care (95% of pericoronitis is the lower third molar; peak age 20–29). Schmidt et al. (2021) — a dual narrative-review-plus-systematic-review (11 studies) — establishes the evidence-based management hierarchy and exposes the practice gap. **First-line treatment is local: irrigation and debridement of the pericoronal stagnation space, plus NSAIDs for pain — not systemic antibiotics.** Antibiotics (metronidazole + amoxicillin; erythromycin if penicillin-allergic) are reserved only for *spread of infection or systemic involvement* (fever, trismus, dysphagia, lymphadenopathy, Ludwig's angina risk → hospital transfer). For prevention: operculectomy (± gingivoplasty) is preferred for a first recurrent episode, and extraction is indicated from the second episode onward (NICE) and should not be delayed once decided. Yet Schmidt's systematic review found ~75% of dentists prescribe antibiotics for pericoronitis (>50% of patients receive them in cohort studies), making pericoronitis a top-4 driver of inappropriate dental antibiotic use — driven by diagnostic uncertainty, time pressure, and patient expectation rather than clinical need.

**Perioperative morbidity control — choose the adjunct by target outcome (Canellas 2022, Canellas 2020, Firoozi 2022, Al-Aroomi 2025).** Once removal is chosen, four syntheses address different components of post-extraction morbidity, and they do not compete so much as target different outcomes.

- *Edema and early pain — corticosteroids.* Canellas et al. (2022), a frequentist network meta-analysis of 61 RCTs (3,561 subjects) ranking five corticosteroids by agent, dose and route, found **dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal injection** significantly reduced edema versus placebo (−3.58 [−6.98; −0.17]) and pain on postoperative days 1 and 2 (−30.95 and −15.25 VAS units); pterygomandibular dexamethasone 8 mg also reduced edema (−3.56), and dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal and oral ranked highest by P-score. No serious adverse effects were detected across any agent. The wiki page is built from the abstract only, so route-level detail is incomplete.
- *Alveolar osteitis prevention — intrasocket agents.* Canellas et al. (2020), an SR+NMA of 37 RCTs (6,175 surgeries in 4,716 patients), found intrasocket interventions reduced alveolar osteitis overall (RR 0.39, p<0.0001). **Platelet-rich fibrin (OR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10–0.71) and chlorhexidine 0.2% gel (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.32–0.95)** were the options combining significant effect with substantial study volume (moderate evidence); several other agents reached significance only with very wide intervals.
- *Nonpharmacological pain adjuncts.* Firoozi et al. (2022), an umbrella review with network ranking of seven complementary interventions (19 SRs; 10 meta-analyses in the quantitative synthesis), ranked **ozone first for early pain** (48–72 h; SMD −0.84) and **PRF/derivatives first for late pain** (5–7 d; SMD −1.03), with LLLT weakest for early pain (SMD −0.32) and surgical drainage ineffective for late pain. Most included reviews had unclear risk of bias and no intervention has a standardized application protocol; A-PRF favored early and L-PRF late pain.
- *Bone fill and second-molar periodontal repair — CGF.* Al-Aroomi et al. (2025), a PROSPERO-registered SR of 11 RCTs, evaluated **Concentrated Growth Factor (CGF)** — a denser-fibrin platelet concentrate (Medifuge protocol). CGF consistently improved pain (6/8 studies, 75%), analgesic consumption (2/2, 100%), radiographic bone fill (4/5, 80%), distal-M2M periodontal parameters (4/4, 100%), and soft-tissue healing (2/2, 100%), whereas swelling (4/7, 57%), trismus (2/4, 50%) and alveolar osteitis (1/3, 33%) were inconsistent; heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis. The periodontal benefit is mechanistically attractive because distal-M2M periodontal damage is exactly one of the pathologies impaction causes (Ye) and one of Sun's definite indications, and Sun's consensus independently recommends CGF (alone or with bone substitute) for sockets with preoperative distal alveolar bone deficiency.

Read together, **platelet concentrates carry the broadest multi-outcome support** in this page — PRF for alveolar-osteitis prevention (Canellas 2020) and late pain (Firoozi 2022), CGF for bone fill and distal-M2M periodontal repair (Al-Aroomi 2025) — but the alveolar-osteitis evidence belongs to PRF, not CGF. Corticosteroids target a different component (inflammatory edema and early pain), so the choice is not either/or; no held paper, however, tests these adjuncts in combination.

**The orthodontic branch — when a different molar is extracted (de la Rosa-Gay 2006, Livas 2011).** Sun's therapeutic tier treats orthodontic need as a reason to *remove* the M3. Two retrospective studies document the reverse scenario, in which the orthodontic plan extracts an adjacent molar and the third molar becomes the replacement tooth. De la Rosa-Gay et al. (2006; 48 patients, 128 extracted second molars, ages 11–23, median follow-up 3–4 years) defined success as proximal contact with the first molar and an inter-tooth angle ≤35°: **maxillary third molars succeeded in 96.2% but mandibular third molars in only 66.2%**, with mandibular failures caused by excessive mesial tilting or absent proximal contact, and higher Nolla developmental stage and older age at extraction predicting failure in both jaws. Livas et al. (2011; 37 Class II Division 1 patients treated with maxillary first-molar extraction vs 54 untreated controls) showed the extraction protocol uprighted the **maxillary** third molar about four times more than growth alone (19.9° vs 5.6° relative to the functional occlusal plane). For M3M management the implication is that an asymptomatic third molar in a young orthodontic patient is not automatically a prophylactic-extraction candidate — its fate is tied to the orthodontic extraction plan — while the mandibular arch, the subject of this page, is precisely where spontaneous replacement is least predictable (Livas supplies maxillary data only).

### The thesis, assembled

The nine papers form a coherent decision pipeline. **Ye 2021** quantifies the pathology burden and shows impaction morphology is itself a risk-stratifier. **De la Rosa-Gay 2006** and **Livas 2011** add a branch point before the indication decision: if orthodontics will extract an adjacent molar, the M3 may be the replacement — reliably in the maxilla, far less so in the mandible. **Sun 2026** converts the burden into a tiered indication framework that distinguishes definite, therapeutic, and prophylactic removal, with shared decision-making and surveillance for low-risk asymptomatic teeth. **Schmidt 2021** governs the most common acute presentation — local therapy first, antibiotics only for spread, extraction from the second episode — and warns against the field's antibiotic over-reflex. At the back end, **Canellas 2022**, **Canellas 2020**, **Firoozi 2022** and **Al-Aroomi 2025** turn "reduce morbidity" into outcome-specific choices: dexamethasone for edema and early pain, intrasocket PRF or CHX gel for alveolar osteitis, ozone or PRF as nonpharmacological pain adjuncts, and CGF where bone fill and distal-M2M periodontal repair matter.

### Indication framework (synthesized)

| Tier | Trigger | Action | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Definite pathologic** | Recurrent pericoronitis / space infection; M3M or adjacent-tooth caries; M2M distal periodontal damage / root resorption; cyst or tumor | **Extract** (do not delay) | Sun 2026; Ye 2021 |
| **Therapeutic** | Orthodontic / orthognathic / TMD need; pre-radiotherapy, chemo-, or immunotherapy; pre-pregnancy with deep periodontal involvement | **Extract** as part of the broader treatment plan | Sun 2026 |
| **Orthodontic replacement (adjacent molar extracted)** | Orthodontic plan extracts the second (or maxillary first) molar in a young patient | **Retain the M3 as the replacement molar** — predictable in the maxilla (96.2%), less so in the mandible (66.2%); earlier Nolla stage and younger age favorable | de la Rosa-Gay 2006; Livas 2011 |
| **Prophylactic (asymptomatic, risky)** | Partially erupted mesial/horizontal; soft-tissue-impacted unerupted; complete bony impaction with distal bone deficiency to the crown | **Shared decision-making**; remove if risk profile high, otherwise monitor — esp. >35 y / high surgical risk → observe | Sun 2026 |
| **Retain + surveillance** | Asymptomatic, low-risk, deep/bony impaction; high surgical risk | **Monitor** (periodic exam/imaging) — but note caries/periodontal pathology accumulates after age 30, so retention is not indefinite | Sun 2026; Ye 2021 |

| **Mesio-angular / horizontal**, deep (position B/C) | **M2M distal periodontal pathology** (incl. external root resorption) | p<0.0001 |
| Age ≥30 (any deep impaction retained) | Rising caries + periodontal lesions | p<0.0001 |

### Pericoronitis management ladder (Schmidt 2021)

| Scenario | Treatment | Antibiotics? |
|---|---|---|
| Mild/moderate pericoronitis | Local irrigation + debridement; NSAIDs | **No** |
| Recurrent (1st episode, not severe) | Operculectomy ± gingivoplasty | No |
| 2nd or subsequent episode | Extraction (don't delay once decided) | No |
| Infection spread / systemic signs (fever, trismus, dysphagia) | Local + **metronidazole + amoxicillin** (erythromycin if PCN-allergic); hospital transfer if airway threatened | **Yes — reserved** |

**Practice-gap caveat:** ~75% of dentists prescribe antibiotics for pericoronitis (Schmidt 2021) — the evidence supports local therapy first; reserve antibiotics for genuine spread/systemic involvement.

### Perioperative adjunct ladder — by target outcome

| Target outcome | Best-supported adjunct | Effect | Source (design) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edema, early pain | **Dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal** (oral also top-ranked) | Edema −3.58 vs placebo; pain day 1 −30.95, day 2 −15.25 VAS; no serious adverse effects | Canellas 2022 (NMA, 61 RCTs; abstract-only page) |
| Alveolar osteitis prevention | **Intrasocket PRF** or **CHX 0.2% gel** | OR 0.28 / OR 0.52 (all intrasocket interventions RR 0.39) | Canellas 2020 (SR+NMA, 37 RCTs; moderate evidence) |
| Early pain (48–72 h), nonpharmacological | Ozone | SMD −0.84 (LLLT weakest, −0.32) | Firoozi 2022 (umbrella review + NMA) |
| Late pain (5–7 d), nonpharmacological | PRF / derivatives | SMD −1.03 | Firoozi 2022 |
| Bone fill, distal-M2M periodontal repair | **CGF** | Bone fill 4/5 and periodontal parameters 4/4 studies favorable; pain 6/8; alveolar osteitis only 1/3 | Al-Aroomi 2025 (SR, 11 RCTs, no meta-analysis) |

The periodontal benefit of CGF directly protects the M2M structure that impaction endangers (Ye 2021) and that Sun 2026 likewise targets with CGF for sockets with preoperative distal bone deficiency. For alveolar-osteitis prevention specifically, prefer PRF or CHX gel over CGF. Treat all adjuncts as directionally favorable layers on sound surgical technique rather than established standards, and note that combination regimens are untested in the held papers.

### Evidence-weight caveat

The nine papers sit at mixed evidence levels: Ye 2021 is a single-center **retrospective** review with referral (symptomatic-patient) selection bias and no community-prevalence data; Sun 2026 is an **expert consensus** without GRADE grading or resolution of asymptomatic-timing controversy; Schmidt 2021 is a narrative review + descriptive **SR** (no meta-analysis); Al-Aroomi 2025 is a **SR** that could not pool data; Canellas 2022 is an **NMA** held only as an abstract; Canellas 2020 is an **SR+NMA** with moderate evidence; Firoozi 2022 is an **umbrella review** whose included reviews mostly carry unclear risk of bias; de la Rosa-Gay 2006 and Livas 2011 are **retrospective radiographic** studies, and Livas concerns the maxillary arch only. Individual clinical judgment — including IAN-risk assessment ([[overviews/third-molar-ian-risk-protocol]]) — governs the final decision.

> **Excluded by design:** `shandilya-2025-saline-irrigation-oral-cancer-surgery` was **deliberately left out** of this synthesis — it concerns saline irrigation in oral cancer surgery and is unrelated to third-molar management.

## Related Papers

### Core pipeline — pathology, indication, acute treatment

- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/ye-2021-mandibular-third-molar-impaction-pathologies]] — Retrospective (432 M3Ms): quantifies the impaction pathology burden (pericoronitis 82.4%, M2M distal caries 18.8%, periodontal pathology 14.8%) and shows impaction morphology predicts which pathology occurs — the empirical basis for risk-stratified indication.
- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/sun-2026-third-molar-health-management-expert-consensus]] — Expert consensus: the 3-tier extraction-indication framework (definite pathologic / therapeutic / prophylactic) with shared decision-making and surveillance for low-risk asymptomatic teeth; also recommends CGF for sockets with distal bone deficiency.
- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/schmidt-2021-pericoronitis-management-antibiotic-prescribing-recommendations]] — Review + SR: pericoronitis management ladder (local irrigation/debridement first → operculectomy → extraction from 2nd episode), with antibiotics reserved for spread/systemic signs despite ~75% of dentists over-prescribing.

### Perioperative adjuncts

- [[drug/analgesics/canellas-2022-corticosteroids-third-molar-nma]] — NMA (61 RCTs, 3,561 subjects): dexamethasone 8 mg submucosal is the top-ranked corticosteroid for edema and early pain; no serious adverse effects across five agents (abstract-only page). (nma, 2022)
- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/canellas-2020-intrasocket-ao-third-molar-sr-nma]] — SR+NMA (37 RCTs, 6,175 surgeries): intrasocket interventions reduce alveolar osteitis (RR 0.39); PRF (OR 0.28) and CHX 0.2% gel (OR 0.52) are the well-supported options. (sr+nma, 2020)
- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/firoozi-2022-nonpharmacological-interventions-third-molar-pain-sr]] — Umbrella review + network MA (19 SRs): ozone best early pain (SMD −0.84), PRF best late pain (SMD −1.03), LLLT weakest early (SMD −0.32) — unclear risk of bias in included reviews.
- [[oral-surgery/third-molar/al-aroomi-2025-cgf-third-molar-postop-sr]] — SR of 11 RCTs: CGF in the socket improves pain, analgesic use, bone fill, and distal-M2M periodontal parameters after extraction; swelling/trismus/alveolar-osteitis effects uncertain.

### Orthodontic branch — adjacent molar extracted, third molar as replacement

- [[orthodontics/de-la-rosa-gay-2006-spontaneous-third-molar-eruption-second-molar-extraction]] — Retrospective (48 patients, 128 extracted second molars, ages 11–23): spontaneous third-molar eruption success 96.2% maxillary vs 66.2% mandibular; mandibular failure from excessive mesial tilting or absent proximal contact; higher Nolla stage and older age predict failure.
- [[orthodontics/livas-2011-extraction-maxillary-first-molars-molar-inclinations]] — Retrospective cephalometric (37 Class II Div 1 extraction patients vs 54 untreated controls): maxillary first-molar extraction uprights the maxillary second molar (26.7°→6.9° to the palatal plane) and third molar about four times more than growth (19.9° vs 5.6°).
