## 한국어 핵심요약

> [!summary] 한국어 핵심요약
> - 핵심 명제: 자가면역질환(Autoimmune Disease)은 치과치료에 **3개 축**으로 영향 — ① 임플란트/외과 성적, ② 치주-전신 양방향 연관, ③ 구강점막 병변·구강건조. 질환이 **조절되면 대부분 치료 가능**하나 위생·추적·약물관리가 관건. 위키 11편 + 기존 페이지로 합성.
> - **임플란트 생존 — 대체로 일반인과 동등**: 9개 자가면역질환 55편 종합(Esimekara 2022)에서 생존율 일반인 수준; 단 **2차 Sjögren·미란성 구강편평태선(erosive OLP)** 에서 peri-mucositis·변연골소실(MBL)↑.
> - **전신질환 일반 원칙(ITI)**: 조절된 당뇨·골다공증·류마티스관절염(RA)·HIV는 골유착(osseointegration) 저하 근거 없음; SSRI/PPI는 예비적 부정 신호; **항흡수제→약물관련 악골괴사(MRONJ)** 주의 (Aghaloo 2019).
> - **Sjögren 증후군**: 임플란트 생존 ~94%·삶의질 개선(Almeida 2017); 그러나 **구강건조(xerostomia)가 재발우식·수복물 실패 2.6–2.9배**↑(Poudel 2026) → 예방(불소·타액관리)이 성패 좌우.
> - **전신경화증(Systemic Sclerosis, scleroderma)**: 임플란트 생존 89–100%, 질환 자체 영향 없음; **소구증(microstomia)** 으로 접근·위생 곤란이 핵심 난제(Mosaddad 2023).
> - **류마티스관절염(RA) ↔ 치주염**: 치주염 환자에서 RA 위험 **69%↑**(OR 1.69, >100만명; Qiao 2020) — 공유 염증·시트룰린화(citrullination, *P. gingivalis*) 기전; 치주치료가 RA 활성도에도 영향 가능.
> - **전신홍반루푸스(SLE)**: 구강궤양이 가장 흔한 구강증상(경구개 호발), 타액분비저하·Sjögren 동반 흔함(García-Ríos 2022).
> - **자가면역 수포성질환**: 천포창(Pemphigus Vulgaris, PV)·점막유천포창(Mucous Membrane Pemphigoid, MMP)은 **구강이 주병소**(PV→협점막·MMP→박리성 치은염), 진단은 **직접면역형광(DIF)** (IgG/C3); PV 전신 스테로이드·MMP 국소 치료(Morais 2024).
> - **전신홍반루푸스(SLE) 치주치료 RCT**: 활동성 SLE 90명 RCT(Maybodi 2022)에서 SRP가 CRP는 유의하게 낮췄으나(P=0.032) 질병활동도지수(SLEDAI)는 대조군과 차이 없음(P=0.894) — RA에서 확립된 "치주치료→전신염증↓" 패턴이 SLE에서는 부분적으로만 재현됨.
> - **SLE 타액 렙틴 대조군 연구**: SLE 38명 vs 건강대조군 29명(da Silva 2022)에서 SLE 자체가 타액 렙틴을 독립적으로 낮췄으나(표준화계수 −0.289) 치주상태는 유의한 직접효과 없음 — 치주-전신 바이오마커 연결이 모든 자가면역질환·모든 지표에 균일하게 적용되지 않음을 보여주는 한정 사례.
> - **베체트병(Behçet's Disease, BD)**: 구강 미생물총(특히 *S. sanguinis/mutans*)·치주 상태가 BD 병인·중증도의 원인적 요인(Mumcu & Fortune 2021) — 치주 불량·치태 축적이 (특히 남성에서) 중증 BD와 연관, 1개월 구조화된 치주치료가 (초기 기계적 외상 악화에도 불구하고) 6개월 시점 재발성 구강궤양 빈도를 감소시킴; BD 활동기에는 치과치료 연기 권고.
> - **약물 고려**: 장기 코르티코스테로이드(부신억제→스트레스 용량), 면역억제제(감염위험), 항흡수제(MRONJ), NSAID(심혈관·소화기) — 협진·병력 청취 필수.
> - **확신도**: 임플란트 생존 동등성 = 중등도(다수 SR, 단 증례군·후향 위주); RA-치주 = 중등도(SR+MA); 구강점막·구강건조 결과 = 낮음~중등도(서술적 SR, RoB 높음). 전반적으로 **"조절된 질환 + 엄격한 위생/추적/예방"** 이 공통 성공 조건.

## Three-line Summary

Synthesis of 11 wiki papers on dental care in autoimmune disease, organized across three axes — implant/surgical outcomes, the periodontal–systemic link, and oral mucosal/xerostomia management — showing that controlled autoimmune disease is rarely an absolute contraindication to dental treatment.

Implant survival approximates the general population in most autoimmune conditions (Esimekara 2022, 55 studies), with elevated peri-mucositis/MBL only in secondary Sjögren and erosive OLP; periodontitis raises rheumatoid arthritis risk by ~69% (OR 1.69; Qiao 2020, >1 million subjects); and xerostomia raises restoration failure 2.6–2.9× (Poudel 2026).

Clinical takeaway: confirm disease control before treating; front-load caries prevention wherever xerostomia exists; plan around microstomia in scleroderma; biopsy/DIF any desquamative gingivitis; manage medication risks (steroid cover, MRONJ, infection); evidence is mostly low-to-moderate certainty.

## 세줄요약

위키 11편(임플란트·치주-전신 연관·구강점막/구강건조 3축) 종합 — 자가면역질환은 조절되면 치과치료의 절대금기가 드물고, 임플란트 생존도 일반인과 유사(Esimekara 2022, 55편)하나 이차성 쇼그렌·미란성 구강편평태선에서 주위점막염·변연골소실↑.

치주염은 류마티스관절염(Rheumatoid Arthritis, RA) 위험을 약 69% 높이고(OR 1.69; Qiao 2020, >100만 명), 구강건조증(Xerostomia)은 수복물 실패를 2.6–2.9배 증가시키며(Poudel 2026), 루푸스(SLE)는 구강궤양, 천포창/점막유천포창(Pemphigus/MMP)은 직접면역형광(Direct Immunofluorescence, DIF)으로 진단.

임상 결론: 질환 조절 확인 후 치료, 구강건조 시 불소·타액관리 우선, 소구증(microstomia)은 접근 계획, 탈락성 치은염은 생검/DIF 필수, 약물 위험(스테로이드·약물관련 악골괴사증(MRONJ)·감염) 관리; 전반 근거 낮음~중등도.

## Synthesis

According to the 11 autoimmune-dental papers held in this wiki (plus existing Sjögren-classification, OLP, and corticosteroid pages), the clinical bottom line is: **autoimmune disease is rarely an absolute contraindication to dental treatment — with disease control, strict hygiene, prevention, and follow-up, implant and restorative outcomes approximate those of healthy patients — but each disease imposes a characteristic burden** (xerostomia, microstomia, mucosal fragility, bone/medication risk). Three axes organize the evidence.

### 1. Implant & surgical outcomes — generally comparable, disease-modified

The comprehensive critical-review anchor is [[implants/esimekara-2022-dental-implants-autoimmune-diseases-sr]] (55 studies, 9 autoimmune diseases): implant **survival rates are comparable to the general population**, but patients with **secondary Sjögren's syndrome or erosive oral lichen planus** show more peri-mucositis and marginal bone loss. The systemic-risk framework comes from [[implants/aghaloo-2019-systemic-diseases-medications-implant-osseointegration-sr]] (ITI consensus): controlled diabetes, osteoporosis, RA, and HIV show **no evidence of reduced osseointegration**, while antiresorptive-related osteonecrosis (MRONJ) and preliminary SSRI/PPI signals warrant caution. Disease-specific evidence: **Sjögren's** implants survive ~94% with improved quality of life ([[implants/almeida-2017-dental-implants-sjogren-syndrome-sr]]); **systemic sclerosis** implants survive 89–100% with no discernible disease effect, the central challenge being **microstomia** limiting access and hygiene ([[implants/mosaddad-2023-dental-implants-systemic-sclerosis-sr]]).

### 2. The periodontal–systemic (autoimmune) link

Periodontitis is bidirectionally tied to rheumatoid arthritis: [[periodontics/qiao-2020-rheumatoid-arthritis-risk-periodontitis-sr-ma]] (13 studies, >1 million subjects) found periodontitis patients carry a **69% higher RA risk (OR 1.69)**, strongest for incident RA and disease duration >5 years — consistent with shared inflammatory pathways and *Porphyromonas gingivalis*–driven protein citrullination generating anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA). Clinically this makes periodontal screening/treatment part of the medical co-management of RA patients.

### 3. Oral mucosal manifestations & xerostomia

Autoimmune disease frequently presents in the mouth, sometimes first: **SLE** — oral ulcers are the most common oral feature (hard-palate predilection), with frequent hyposalivation/secondary Sjögren ([[oral-medicine/immune-mediated/garcia-rios-2022-oral-manifestations-systemic-lupus-erythematosus-sr]]); **autoimmune blistering disease** — pemphigus vulgaris (PV) and mucous membrane pemphigoid (MMP) are mouth-dominant (PV → buccal mucosa erosions, MMP → desquamative gingivitis), diagnosed by **direct immunofluorescence** (intercellular IgG in PV, basement-membrane IgG/C3 in MMP), treated with systemic corticosteroids (PV) vs more localized therapy (MMP) ([[oral-medicine/immune-mediated/morais-2024-pemphigus-vulgaris-mucous-membrane-pemphigoid-oral-sr]]). Cutting across diseases, **xerostomia** (Sjögren, post-radiation, drug-induced) is a major outcome modifier: it raises restoration failure ~2.6–2.9× through recurrent caries and modestly lowers implant survival in radiation-induced cases ([[oral-medicine/salivary-chemosensory/poudel-2026-xerostomia-dental-treatment-outcomes-sr]]) — making aggressive caries prevention (fluoride, saliva management) decisive.

### 4. Periodontal–systemic axis, continued: SLE and Behçet's disease

Does the RA "periodontal treatment lowers systemic autoimmune inflammation" pattern generalize to other autoimmune diseases, and does it generalize uniformly? Two recent SLE papers give a nuanced answer, and a third paper extends the periodontal-systemic axis into a different disease entirely. In a double-blinded RCT of 90 patients with active SLE and periodontitis, Maybodi et al. (2022) ([[periodontics/maybodi-2022-periodontal-treatment-sle-disease-activity-rct]]) randomized SRP+OHI (n=45) against OHI-only (n=45): SRP produced clinically meaningful periodontal improvement (PD −2.10 mm, CAL −2.02 mm) and a significant between-group **reduction in CRP** (P=0.032), but **SLEDAI** (the SLE disease-activity index) did not differ between groups at 3 months (P=0.894) — the RA-derived pattern extends to SLE only partially: local/acute-phase inflammation improves, but overall lupus disease activity is not demonstrably changed by periodontal treatment alone. da Silva et al. (2022) ([[periodontics/dasilva-2022-periodontal-status-salivary-leptin-sle]]) add a limiting, contrasting finding from a case-control study of 38 SLE patients vs 29 healthy controls: structural equation modeling showed **SLE status itself** (not periodontal status) independently and inversely predicted salivary leptin (standardized estimate −0.289, P=0.023), while the periodontal-status latent variable had **no significant direct effect** on salivary leptin — a caution against assuming the RA-style "periodontal treatment moves systemic autoimmune biomarkers" pattern generalizes to every autoimmune disease/biomarker pairing.

Mumcu & Fortune (2021) ([[oral-medicine/aphthous-stomatitis/mumcu-2021-oral-health-aetiological-behcet]]) extend the periodontal-systemic axis to Behçet's disease (BD), a chronic mucocutaneous vasculitis with recurrent oro-genital ulceration as its hallmark. Reviewing oral microbiome (notably *Streptococcus sanguinis*/*mutans* overgrowth), innate/adaptive immune dysregulation, and periodontal status, they argue oral dysbiosis and periodontal infection are **aetiological drivers of BD severity**, not incidental findings: poorer periodontal health and plaque accumulation predict a more severe disease course (especially in males), and a structured 1-month dental/periodontal intervention **reduced recurrent oral ulcer frequency at 6 months** — despite an early trauma-triggered flare in the first days of treatment. The practical implication is BD-specific: defer elective dental treatment during active flares (mechanical trauma itself can trigger exacerbation), but treat periodontal disease proactively and reinforce oral hygiene during quiescent periods as an adjunct to systemic BD management.

### 5. Medication & cross-cutting management

Autoimmune patients are usually on immunomodulators that reshape dental risk: long-term **corticosteroids** (adrenal suppression — consider stress-dose; candidiasis), **immunosuppressants/biologics** (infection risk, possible antibiotic prophylaxis), **antiresorptives** (MRONJ risk before extractions/implants), and **NSAIDs** (GI/cardiovascular). These are detailed in the existing [[overviews/drug-systemic-disease-dental-management-overview]] and corticosteroid/MRONJ pages.

### Clinical bottom line

Treat the **controlled** autoimmune patient much like a healthy one, but: (1) confirm disease control + coordinate with the rheumatologist/physician; (2) front-load **caries prevention** wherever xerostomia exists; (3) plan around **microstomia** in scleroderma; (4) maintain **meticulous peri-implant hygiene + close recall** (erosive OLP / secondary Sjögren raise peri-mucositis); (5) **biopsy + DIF** any desquamative gingivitis / chronic erosion; (6) manage **medication risks** (steroid cover, MRONJ, infection). Evidence is mostly low-to-moderate certainty (case series / descriptive SRs), so individualized risk assessment remains essential.
