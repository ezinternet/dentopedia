## Three-line Summary

This sub-overview holds the first four key contributions of the parent high-insertion-torque synthesis: the sign flip of the torque reading across bone types, Faot's non-monotonic bands, the crestal and mandibular localization of harm, and why Lemos's null is absence of evidence.
The same torque number marks good anchorage in atrophic mandible and a dose of crestal compression in healed dense bone, so pooled analyses average a sign that flips and the harm concentrates in the mandibular crest.
No human study assigned torque, and the preclinical studies bound rather than overturn each other, so torque is best read as a correlate that must be interpreted within site and bone-density strata.

## 세줄요약

이 하위 종합은 모체 고삽입토크 종합의 앞 네 가지 핵심 기여를 담는다: 뼈 종류에 따른 토크 해석의 부호 반전, Faot의 비단조 구간, 치조정·하악에 국한된 손상, Lemos 무효 결과가 증거 부재인 이유.
같은 토크 값이 위축 하악에서는 좋은 고정의 표지이고 치유된 치밀골에서는 치조정 압박 용량이므로, 풀링 분석은 부호가 뒤집히는 값을 평균내며 손상은 하악 치조정에 몰린다.
토크를 배정한 인체 연구는 없고 전임상 연구들은 서로를 뒤집지 않고 범위를 좁히므로, 토크는 부위와 뼈 밀도 층 안에서 해석해야 하는 상관 지표로 읽는다.

### 1. The same torque number carries opposite prognostic sign in different bone

This is the load-bearing claim of the page, and it is what makes Faot and Aldahlawi stop contradicting each other.

- In **Faot's** atrophic edentulous mandible with ø2.9 mm narrow implants, reaching ≥45 Ncm means the surgeon *found* unexpectedly good anchorage in a bone-poor site. Torque is a **marker of bone availability**. It selects for good sites — hence G4's 95% survival, its highest and most stable ISQ, and its status as the only group with positive marginal bone change (+0.29 mm, NS).
- In **Aldahlawi's** and **Marconcini's** healed dense sites, exceeding 50–55 Ncm means the surgeon *forced* compression into bone that was already adequate. Torque is a **dose of crestal compression**. It selects for over-compressed sites — hence 0.95 mm vs 0.18 mm MBL, and all 6 failures in the high group.

Same instrument, same units, opposite meaning. An SR+MA that pools both populations under one PICO ("IT ≥50 vs <50") is averaging a sign that flips — which is exactly what Lemos did, and exactly why the result is null.

### 2. Faot's G3 paradox refutes dose-response directly

| Group | IT (Ncm) | n | 12-mo survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | <10 | 12 | 75% |
| G2 | 10–30 | 16 | 81.3% |
| G3 | 30–45 | 14 | **64.3%** |
| G4 | ≥45 | 20 | **95%** |

If torque magnitude were causing outcomes, survival would be monotonic in torque — rising (mechanical stability hypothesis) or falling (osseocompression hypothesis). It is neither. The worst band sits between two better bands on both sides. No dose-response curve produces this. Something other than torque magnitude — site quality, remaining bone, patient factors — is driving both the torque reading and the outcome. G3 is the single cleanest piece of evidence in this cluster that torque is a *correlate*, not a *cause*.

Note the honest limit: G3 has n=14 with 5 failures. This is a small, fragile number and should not be over-read. But its direction is not explicable by either causal hypothesis, whereas both harm and benefit findings are fully explicable by a confounded-marker reading.

### 3. Harm is crestal and mandibular — not a whole-interface phenomenon

This is the compartment argument, and it is the reason Trisi does not actually rebut Marconcini.

- **Aldahlawi**: mandible 2.12±1.99 mm vs maxilla 0.25±0.65 mm at the same torque range — roughly 8× — with the mandible-vs-maxilla contrast significant (P<0.05).
- **Marconcini**: mandible most affected; **BBT ≥1 mm attenuated recession even in the high-IT group**, meaning the outcome is governed by how much cortical plate is available to lose, not by the torque reading alone.
- **Manfredini 2025** (scoping review, 11 studies) reports the same gradient: at 3 years the high-IT penalty is ~2.5× larger in the mandible than the maxilla for MBL (1.53 vs 1.03 mm; maxilla 1.16 vs 0.96 mm) and ~2× for facial recession (−1.40 vs −0.26 mm; maxilla −0.54 vs +0.03 mm), and failures cluster in D1 and D4 bone with D2–D3 the "safer zone." These numbers are cited to what appears to be a single 3-year trial matching Marconcini's design, so this is a restatement of the harm evidence, not independent corroboration.
- **Khayat 2011** (prospective, 66 tapered implants) is the human counterpart to Trisi at the same torque: implants seated >70 Ncm (mean 110.6, up to 176 Ncm) all osseointegrated, and MBL at 1 year was 1.24 vs 1.09 mm (NS) against a 9-implant 30–50 Ncm control. Its horizon, not its verdict, is the informative part: the high-torque group started with *less* loss at loading (0.72 vs 1.03 mm) and lost ~0.52 mm over the following year vs ~0.06 mm in controls (derived from group means, untested, underpowered). A 1-year null whose trajectory points toward Marconcini's 3-year direction is consistent with this section's horizon argument, not a rebuttal of it.
- **Trisi** measured *total bone apposition* and *removal torque* — both dominated by the full implant body — plus necrosis markers across the interface. He did not report a crest-specific outcome, and at 6 weeks there is no 3-year crestal remodeling to see.

Trisi refuted the **mechanism** Marconcini proposed (osseocompression → ischemia → necrosis → resorption): at 110 Ncm in dense sheep cortical bone there was no osteocyte lacunar emptying and no avascular necrotic zone. Marconcini's **outcome** (3-year crestal MBL and recession) is untouched by that. A finding of "no necrosis" does not license "no crestal bone loss," because necrosis was never the only path to crestal loss.

#### 3-bis. Coyac 2019 reopens the osteocyte-death mechanism — bounded, not restored (2026-07-17)

Trisi's refutation was, until now, unopposed within this cluster: the one study that assigned its mechanical exposure by design looked for osteocyte death at 110 Ncm and did not find it. [[implants/coyac-2019-preclinical-model-links-osseo-densification-misfit|Coyac et al. 2019]] (rat maxillary extraction sockets, minimal-misfit control vs high-misfit test) is the **second study in this cluster to assign the exposure by design — and the first to find harm**, reporting exactly the marker Trisi reported absent: **an extensive zone of dying osteocytes**, together with micro-fractures, a pro-resorptive environment (TRAP activity, cathepsin K), absent new bone formation (no alkaline phosphatase activity, negative collagen I), and early failure presenting as a crater-like lesion of fibrous inflamed granulation tissue. The authors formalize it as **excessive osseo-densification → osseo-destruction**.

Three qualifications keep this from being read as a reversal, and they matter more than the finding:

1. **Coyac assigned *misfit*, not torque.** Misfit (undersized osteotomy relative to implant) is an upstream cause of both densification and torque, not torque itself. So the page's central design claim — *no human study assigned torque, and no held paper separates "high torque" from "dense cortical bone"* — **stands unchanged**. What changes is narrower: "the only assigned-exposure study found no harm" is no longer true.
2. **Both results can be true simultaneously.** Trisi: sheep **mandibular cortical** bone, torque 110 vs 10 Ncm, 1–6 weeks. Coyac: rat **maxillary extraction socket**, misfit magnitude unstated, early. Different bed, different exposure, different magnitude. The consequence is that **Trisi's refutation of the necrosis mechanism narrows from universal to bed- and magnitude-bounded** — it no longer licenses "osseocompression cannot kill osteocytes," only "it did not at 110 Ncm in sheep mandibular cortex."
3. **Coyac cannot supply a threshold.** The page for it is built from a structured abstract with **no n, no torque values, no misfit magnitudes, no BIC percentages, and no p-values**. It establishes that a ceiling exists; it does not locate it. Any number attributed to it would be invented.

[[implants/isq/nascimento-2024-measurement-bone-deformation-insertion-torque|Nascimento 2024]] supplies the mechanical precursor at time zero, in a bench model (D2-equivalent polyurethane, strain-gauge extensometry during insertion, abstract-only): trapezoidal threads and conical bodies each *raised* insertion torque yet compressed the block without damage, whereas **machined-surface implants exceeded the block's mechanical resistance and cracked it**. Harm tracked design and surface, not the torque number — a design-level version of this page's thesis, and a candidate reason why the same Ncm reading can be benign with one implant and destructive with another. Like Coyac, it reports no Ncm values, strain magnitudes, or n, so it adds a direction and no threshold; and synthetic bone has no biology, so "crack" is a mechanical endpoint, not necrosis or MBL.

Net effect on this synthesis: the harm side gains a *candidate biological pathway* it previously lacked, while the *clinical* argument is unmoved — the human evidence still cannot separate torque from site. See [[overviews/osseodensification-clinical-applications]] §1 for the densification-side reading of the same paper, where its transfer limits (rat ≠ human jaw, socket ≠ healed ridge, misfit ≠ bur-mediated OD) are set out in full.

**[미검증 — hypothesis, not established by any held paper]** One reconciliation consistent with everything we hold: Trisi's own 7-day stability dip *is* the resorption phase — bone briefly resorbs, then reforms in greater quantity. Along the implant body there is surrounding bone to reform into, so the net at 6 weeks is a gain. At the crest there is nothing coronal to reform into, so the same resorptive phase may register as permanent loss. **No held paper tests this.** Coyac now supplies a *plausible biological staging* for the resorptive half of that bridge (micro-fracture → osteocyte death → TRAP/cathepsin-K resorption → absent ALP/collagen I → no replacement bone), which is why the bridge is no longer purely speculative — but Coyac studied a rat extraction socket and **did not report a crest-specific compartment**, so the coronal-space half of the argument remains untested. It is offered as a reasoning bridge, not a finding, and must not be cited as one.

### 4. Lemos's null is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence

| Outcome | Effect | 95% CI | P |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival (RR) | 0.51 | 0.06–4.06 | .52 |
| MBL (MD) | 0.15 mm | −0.14–0.44 | .30 |

Three separate reasons this null cannot be read as "high IT is safe":

1. **The CI is uninformative.** RR 0.51 with bounds 0.06 to 4.06 spans a 20-fold protective effect to a 4-fold harmful effect. That interval excludes essentially nothing.
2. **The MBL upper bound is clinically live.** MD 0.15 mm sounds negligible, but the CI reaches 0.44 mm — the same order as Aldahlawi's observed 0.95 mm high-IT loss. The meta-analysis cannot rule out the effect the other papers measured.
3. **It pools a sign-flipping population.** Six studies, mostly delayed loading, mixing site types. Per §1, the effect direction is not constant across those strata, so the pooled mean is not estimating a coherent quantity.

Lemos's own directional signal actually favors high IT (failure 1.1% vs 4.2%), and the authors attribute it largely to a single immediate-loading study (n=50/group). That is worth keeping — see §5.

**The 2025 synthesis runs the other way — and does not resolve the conflict.** Manfredini 2025 (PRISMA-ScR scoping review, 11 human studies, 2011–2024) concludes that IT >50 Ncm is associated with more MBL, more facial recession, and more failure — the opposite bottom line to Lemos. It should not be read as overturning Lemos: it has no meta-analysis, excluded prior SRs (so never engaged Lemos), draws its headline numbers from one trial, and does not discuss post-hoc torque classification. Under §1 the two are not really in contradiction — one synthesis leans on the dense-mandibular-crest stratum where harm lives, the other averages across strata whose sign flips. Separately, Khayat 2011 is very likely one of Lemos's six constituent studies (by its print year, "Khayat 2013"); its per-timepoint data (§3) show a loading→1-year trajectory that a single pooled MBL difference cannot display.
