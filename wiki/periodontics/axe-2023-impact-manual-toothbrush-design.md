## Three-line Summary

In vitro robot-brushing study (8 manual toothbrushes, KaVo artificial dentition, 3 movements: horizontal/rotating/vertical) testing 5 bristle-design parameters — head-size, filament-diameter, cutting-height, hardness, interdental-height difference — for simulated plaque removal via automated plaque planimetry (30 fields/tooth).

Plaque removal was significantly better with smaller head-size, smaller filament-diameter (0.12mm vs 0.15mm), larger cutting-height (12mm vs 9mm), softer filaments (0.15/0.18mm vs 0.23mm), and greater interdental-height difference (8.5/11mm vs 10/11mm); rotating movement was overall the most effective brushing motion across parameters.

Industry-sponsored (Haleon/Dr Best) in vitro study — findings on flexible, longer, softer bristle designs are mechanistically plausible but require in vivo clinical confirmation (plaque indices, blinded photographic assessment) before being generalized to real-world toothbrushing.

## Summary

Toothbrush marketing routinely claims superior plaque removal from new bristle geometries, but clinical trials rarely isolate *which single design parameter* drives an effect, since technique, brand, and bristle configuration are usually confounded together. Axe et al. (2023) sidestep this by using a validated 6-axis brushing robot (Kawasaki FS002N) to apply three standardized movements — horizontal, rotating, vertical — to eight Dr Best manual toothbrushes that each isolate one design variable: head-size, filament-diameter, cutting-height, hardness, or interdental tuft-height difference. Simulated plaque removal (a red inorganic plaque analog on KaVo artificial teeth) was quantified via automated optical plaque planimetry across 30 standardized tooth fields.

The consistent direction across parameters favored **flexible, softer, longer bristles**: smaller filament diameter (0.12mm vs 0.15mm), softer bristles (0.15/0.18mm vs 0.23mm filament), taller tufts (12mm vs 9mm), and a larger interdental-height difference (8.5/11mm vs 10/11mm) all significantly out-performed their stiffer/shorter/more uniform counterparts in most movements. Head-size effects were movement-dependent (compact head better horizontally, full head better vertically) rather than uniformly superior either way. **Rotating movement** — mechanically similar to the modified Bass technique and to oscillating-rotating powered brushes — produced the most consistent design-dependent gains and was overall the most efficient movement tested.

As an in vitro, industry-sponsored study (Haleon/Dr Best, with independent statistical analysis by the ORMED Institute), the findings establish design-parameter *mechanism* rather than clinical proof; the authors explicitly call for in vivo confirmation with clinical/planimetrical plaque indices.

## Key Contributions

- First (per the authors) English-literature study to test all five bristle-design parameters together with multiple brushing movements in one robot-based, plaque-planimetry-scored design — isolating brush hardware from technique/individual variability.
- Establishes a consistent "flexible bristle" direction of effect: thinner filament diameter, softer bristles, taller tufts, and greater interdental tuft-height difference each independently improved simulated plaque removal versus their stiffer/shorter counterparts.
- Shows brushing-movement type interacts with design — rotating movement was overall the most effective and the most consistent in rewarding design-optimized brushes, while head-size effects reversed direction between horizontal and vertical movements.
- Demonstrates a reproducible, examiner-independent robot + automated-planimetry protocol as a laboratory alternative to clinical toothbrush-comparison trials for early-stage prototype screening.

## Methodology

- **Robot**: 6-axis Kawasaki FS002N programmed to reproduce horizontal (5mm), vertical (10mm), and rotating (10mm diameter) brushing movements individually, 5 runs each, at fixed 3.5N force.
- **Model**: KaVo artificial plastic mandibular dentition (10 teeth: 4 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars) coated with a validated red inorganic simulated-plaque formula.
- **Brushes**: 8 Dr Best manual toothbrushes (end-rounded nylon filaments) each isolating one variable — S1/S2 (head-size), S3/S4 (filament diameter), S5/S2 (cutting-height), S2/S4/S6 (hardness), S7/S8 (interdental tuft-height difference).
- **Outcome**: % simulated plaque reduction pre-/post-brushing via automated optical plaque planimetry at 30 fields/tooth, reported across 7 surface variables (buccal, lingual, ABCDF risk fields, mesial, distal, total).
- **Statistics**: Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test per variable; independent two-sample t-test (Levene-corrected) or Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney-U-test for non-normal variables; significance at p<0.05 (two-tailed) per pairwise brush comparison.

## Results

- **Filament diameter**: extra-soft/thinner (0.12mm) brush (S3) significantly outperformed the soft/thicker (0.15mm) brush (S4) in horizontal (86.75% vs 75.66%, p<0.05) and rotating (79.98% vs 72.63%, p<0.01) movements.
- **Cutting-height**: taller tuft (12mm, S2) significantly outperformed shorter tuft (9mm, S5) in all three movements (horizontal 68.11% vs 47.22%, p<0.01; rotating 62.33% vs 57.96%, p<0.05; vertical 67.94% vs 51.57%, p<0.05).
- **Hardness**: soft brushes (S4, S6) generally outperformed the medium-hard brush (S2) in horizontal and rotating movements (e.g., S6 vs S2 rotating: 77.32% vs 62.33%, p<0.05); no consistent advantage in vertical movement.
- **Interdental tuft-height difference**: the larger-difference brush (S8, 8.5/11mm) significantly outperformed the smaller-difference brush (S7, 10/11mm) in rotating (81.29% vs 66.47%, p<0.01) and vertical (73.65% vs 66.95%, p<0.01) movements, with mixed results for horizontal movement.
- **Head-size**: no uniformly "best" brush — compact head (S1) better in horizontal movement (82.42% vs 77.89%, p<0.05), full head (S2) better in vertical movement (75.86% vs 70.91%, p<0.05), and statistically similar in rotating movement.
- No bristle deformation observed in any brush post-testing.
