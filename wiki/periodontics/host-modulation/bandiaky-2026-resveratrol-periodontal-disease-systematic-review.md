## Three-line Summary

PRISMA-2020 systematic review of resveratrol (stilbene polyphenol) as an adjunct in periodontal therapy — 22 studies (16 preclinical animal, 6 double-blind RCTs); heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis.

Animal ligature models: resveratrol reduced alveolar bone loss by 7.09-60.60% (12 of 13 ABL studies) via osteoclast/RANKL inhibition, lower IL-1beta/IL-6/TNF-alpha and NADPH oxidase; the 6 RCTs were variable but generally positive for PPD, BI, PI and CAL, strongest in aggressive periodontitis (Stage III-IV grade C).

Resveratrol may serve as an adjunct to non-surgical periodontal therapy, but the clinical evidence is limited by small samples, heterogeneous dosing/routes, short follow-up and zero human bone-loss data — no standardised protocol can yet be defined.

## Summary

Resveratrol (3,5,4'-trihydroxystilbene) is a plant stilbene polyphenol with anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-osteoclastic properties, proposed as a host-modulating adjunct in periodontal therapy. This PRISMA-2020 systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42022247114) searched five databases to October 2025 and pooled **22 studies — 16 preclinical animal experiments and 6 double-blind randomised clinical trials**. Heterogeneity in models, induction protocols, doses, routes, and outcomes precluded meta-analysis, so a narrative synthesis was performed with alveolar bone loss (ABL) as the primary outcome.

In animal ligature-induced periodontitis, resveratrol reduced ABL by **7.09-60.60%** (12 of 13 studies reporting ABL) through osteoclast-formation inhibition and RANKL downregulation, and lowered gingival IL-1beta/IL-6/IL-8/TNF-alpha, NF-kB signalling, and NADPH oxidase while raising SOD/SIRT1/Nrf2. Importantly, resveratrol did not alter subgingival Aa/Pg/Tf load, indicating a host-modulatory rather than antimicrobial mechanism. The six RCTs were more heterogeneous: four reported improvements in PPD, BI, PI and/or CAL and three showed reduced inflammatory cytokines, with the largest effect seen in **aggressive periodontitis (Stage III-IV grade C)**. No clinical trial measured radiographic or clinical bone loss, so human ABL efficacy remains unproven. The authors conclude resveratrol may be a promising adjunct to non-surgical periodontal therapy, especially under high inflammatory/oxidative burden (diabetes, smoking, aggressive disease), but small samples, methodological heterogeneity, low oral bioavailability, and short follow-up prevent any standardised protocol.

## Key Contributions

- Updates resveratrol-periodontitis evidence to October 2025, pooling 16 preclinical + 6 clinical studies under a PICOS framework with ABL as primary outcome.
- Quantifies preclinical bone protection: **ABL reduction 7.09-60.60%** (7.09-28.58% in eight comparable-dose studies).
- Establishes the mechanistic triad: anti-inflammatory (NF-kB, IL-1beta/IL-6/TNF-alpha), antioxidant (NADPH oxidase/NOX, SOD/SIRT1/Nrf2), anti-osteoclastic (RANKL, osteoclast formation).
- Identifies the decisive clinical gap — **no human study measured bone loss** — and that dose/duration determine efficacy (two null preclinical studies used low dose or 7-day treatment).

## Methodology

- **Design**: PRISMA-2020 systematic review; PROSPERO CRD42022247114; narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis).
- **Databases**: Medline, Cochrane Library, Lilacs, Wiley, ScienceDirect to Oct 2025 + hand-search; English only.
- **PICOS**: mice/rats or periodontitis patients; resveratrol alone/adjunct vs placebo; primary O = ABL; secondary = plaque, cytokines, TAC, BI, CAL, PPD, adverse effects. Excluded derivatives/red wine, systemic-only, no-control, reviews, in vitro.
- **Selection**: 1,290 records → 22 included (11 primary + 11 from a prior SR+MA), screened by 3 investigators.
- **Risk of bias**: SYRCLE (animal), Cochrane RoB (RCT).

## Results

**Preclinical (16 studies)** — 10 Wistar, 3 Sprague-Dawley, 3 mice; all ligature-induced, some with LPS or P. gingivalis; doses 5-25 mg/kg, 7-140 days, mostly gavage.
- ABL reduced in 12/13 studies reporting it (**7.09-60.60%**).
- Two null studies: Wagner 2019 (low 0.05 mg/ml dose), Chin 2016 (7-day treatment only) → dose/duration dependent.
- Mechanisms: down IL-1beta/IL-6/IL-8/TNF-alpha/IL-17, down NF-kB p65/p38MAPK/STAT3/TLR4, down NADPH oxidase, down RANKL/osteoclasts; up SOD/SIRT1/Nrf2/HO-1/OPG/IL-4.
- Cirano 2016: no change in Aa/Pg/Tf load → host-modulatory, not antimicrobial.

**Clinical (6 double-blind RCTs; 143 placebo, 215 resveratrol participants)**
- Mohammed 2025 (n=57, mouthwash 4wk + NSPT): improved PI, BI, PPD, not CAL; down salivary IL-6.
- Lucchesi 2025 (n=38 smokers, 500 mg/d x180d + NSPT): improved PPD, CAL, PGM; down IL-1beta/IL-6 in deep sites.
- Nikniaz 2023 (n=40, 480 mg/d x4wk + NSPT): improved PI only.
- Zhang 2022 (n=160 aggressive PD, 125/200/500 mg/d x8wk, no periodontal Tx): improved CAL, BI, OHI-S, PPD; down all inflammatory markers at all doses.
- Javid 2019 (n=50 T2DM, 480 mg/d x28d): down serum IL-6; no change TNF-alpha/TAC/CAL.
- Javid 2017 (n=50 T2DM, 480 mg/d x28d): down insulin/insulin resistance/PPD.
- Greatest effect in aggressive periodontitis (Stage III-IV grade C); **no RCT measured bone loss**.

**Risk of bias**: preclinical predominantly unclear-to-high (performance 60% high); RCTs lower risk (none high, but performance/attrition unclear in 67%).
