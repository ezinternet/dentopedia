## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (J Periodontol 2026) updating the decision tree for combined defects (CDs) — gingival recession (GR) coexisting with a non-carious cervical lesion (NCCL), which occurs in approximately 50% of GRs.

NCCL classified by Pini-Prato 2010 and GR by Cairo 2011 plus gingival phenotype: A-, A+ and B- NCCLs need root coverage surgery alone, while B+ or V-shaped NCCLs need a composite restorative protocol plus root coverage (about 25% of cases are B+).

NCCL severity, not mere presence, decides whether to restore; graft choice follows phenotype — abstract-only expert framework without pooled outcome data.

## Summary

Combined defects (CDs) — GR plus an NCCL on the same root surface — are common (~50% of GRs) and require protocols that differ from recession on an intact, sound root. Santamaria and colleagues (with Pini-Prato, originator of the NCCL classification used) searched Medline/PubMed, Embase, BIREME and Google Scholar and proposed an updated decision tree. The NCCL component is classified by Pini-Prato et al. (2010); the GR component by Cairo et al. (2011) and gingival phenotype. Shallow NCCLs (A-, A+, B-) are handled as ordinary recessions — root coverage surgery only, technique dictated by the GR characteristics. Deep lesions (B+) or V-shaped NCCLs need a composite restorative protocol combined with root coverage. Graft selection is guided by phenotype. This is an abstract-only ingest.

## Key Contributions

- Frames CDs as a distinct clinical entity needing combined restorative-periodontal planning.
- Two-axis classification (NCCL: Pini-Prato; GR: Cairo + phenotype) feeding one decision tree.
- Operational rule separating "surgery alone" from "restore + surgery" by NCCL severity.
- Updates graft recommendations with newer soft-tissue graft evidence.

## Methodology

Narrative review; four databases searched (Medline/PubMed, Embase, BIREME, Google Scholar). No systematic protocol, risk-of-bias assessment, or meta-analysis reported in the abstract.

## Results

**Decision tree (as stated in the abstract and plain-language summary):**

| NCCL (Pini-Prato 2010) | Share of CDs | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| A- | "most cases" (mild, shallow) | Root coverage surgery alone; technique by GR characteristics |
| A+ | "most cases" (mild, shallow) | Root coverage surgery alone |
| B- | "most cases" (mild, shallow) | Root coverage surgery alone |
| B+ | ~25% (deep) | Composite restorative protocol + root coverage |
| V-shaped | not quantified | Composite restorative protocol + root coverage |

**Graft selection by gingival phenotype:**

| Phenotype | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Thin | Autogenous (patient's own tissue) graft usually gives best results |
| Moderate | Autogenous or commercial substitute graft can improve long-term success |
| Thick | Graft may not be necessary |

Note: the briefing's "75% surgery alone" is the complement of the ~25% B+ figure; the abstract says only "most cases". The abstract does not name CTG specifically for thin phenotype.

**Clinical implication:** in the majority of combined defects, periodontal root coverage surgery alone suffices; the need for a cervical composite is triggered by NCCL depth/step (B+) or V-shaped morphology, not by the mere presence of an NCCL.
