## One-line Summary
Cochrane SR (25 RCTs, 1422 adults, pub2): behavioural interventions for oral hygiene in periodontitis/gingivitis — very low certainty evidence throughout; insufficient evidence to conclude on effectiveness.

## 한줄요약
코크란 SR (RCT 25편, 1422명, pub2): 치주질환 성인의 구강위생 행동 중재 (Behavioural Intervention) 효과 — 전체 근거의 확신도 매우 낮음, 효과 결론 도출 불가.

## Three-line Summary

O'Malley et al. (2026) published the second completed update of the Cochrane systematic review on behavioural interventions for improving oral hygiene in adults with periodontal diseases (periodontitis and gingivitis), synthesising 25 RCTs involving 1,422 adults, applying the Michie 2013 Behaviour Change Technique (BCT) taxonomy to categorise interventions (mobile apps, motivational interviewing, personalised advice, intra-oral cameras, diary-keeping). Follow-up ranged from 3 weeks to 12 months.

All evidence was rated very low certainty (GRADE) throughout all clinical outcomes (BOP, GI, plaque, PPD, CAL). The primary reasons for downgrading certainty were unblinded participants (performance bias), inconsistency across studies, and imprecision due to small sample sizes. Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) methodology was required due to clinical and methodological heterogeneity preventing pooled analysis. The review cannot conclude whether behavioural interventions are effective for improving clinical oral hygiene outcomes in adults with periodontal disease.

This is the first completed Cochrane SR applying BCT taxonomy systematically to oral hygiene behaviour change in periodontal disease, and with 25 RCTs it is the largest SR in this area. Despite the very low certainty baseline, the review identifies mobile app-based monitoring and personalised feedback as interventions that showed partial benefit in some individual studies, warranting further investigation in higher-quality trials.

## Summary
This Cochrane SR evaluated behavioural interventions aimed at improving oral hygiene in adults with periodontal disease (periodontitis and gingivitis). Twenty-five RCTs involving 1422 adults were included. Interventions used a range of behaviour change techniques (BCTs) classified by the Michie 2013 BCT taxonomy, including mobile apps, motivational interviewing, personalised advice, intra-oral cameras, and diary-keeping. All evidence was judged to be very low certainty due to risk of bias, inconsistency across studies, and imprecision (small sample sizes). The review cannot conclude whether behavioural interventions are effective for improving clinical oral hygiene outcomes in adults with periodontal disease.

## Key Contributions
- First completed Cochrane SR (pub2) applying BCT taxonomy to oral hygiene behaviour change in periodontal disease
- 25 RCTs identified — largest SR in this area
- Separates evidence by disease (periodontitis vs gingivitis) and treatment phase (active vs supportive)
- Establishes very low certainty as the baseline for all current evidence
- SWiM synthesis used (heterogeneity prevents meta-analysis)

## Methodology
- 25 RCTs, 1422 adults; follow-up 3 weeks to 12 months
- Studies: 19 periodontitis-only, 5 gingivitis-only, 1 both
- BCT taxonomy: 14 cluster labels applied
- Outcomes: BOP, GI, plaque, PPD, CAL, self-reported oral hygiene behaviour
- Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB 1
- Synthesis: SWiM (Synthesis Without Meta-analysis)
- Certainty: GRADE (all outcomes very low)

## Results
| Population | Outcome | Finding | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | BOP | Inconsistent | Very low |
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | GI | Inconsistent | Very low |
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | Plaque | Mixed (1 benefit, 2 no diff) | Very low |
| Periodontitis (SPT) | All clinical | Little/no difference | Very low |
| Gingivitis | Gingival bleeding | 3/5 studies found reduction | Very low |
| Any | CAL, adverse events | No data found | — |

GRADE downgrade reasons: unblinded participants (performance bias), inconsistency, imprecision.
