## Summary
This Cochrane SR evaluated behavioural interventions aimed at improving oral hygiene in adults with periodontal disease (periodontitis and gingivitis). Twenty-five RCTs involving 1422 adults were included. Interventions used a range of behaviour change techniques (BCTs) classified by the Michie 2013 BCT taxonomy, including mobile apps, motivational interviewing, personalised advice, intra-oral cameras, and diary-keeping. All evidence was judged to be very low certainty due to risk of bias, inconsistency across studies, and imprecision (small sample sizes). The review cannot conclude whether behavioural interventions are effective for improving clinical oral hygiene outcomes in adults with periodontal disease.

## Key Contributions
- First completed Cochrane SR (pub2) applying BCT taxonomy to oral hygiene behaviour change in periodontal disease
- 25 RCTs identified — largest SR in this area
- Separates evidence by disease (periodontitis vs gingivitis) and treatment phase (active vs supportive)
- Establishes very low certainty as the baseline for all current evidence
- SWiM synthesis used (heterogeneity prevents meta-analysis)

## Methodology
- 25 RCTs, 1422 adults; follow-up 3 weeks to 12 months
- Studies: 19 periodontitis-only, 5 gingivitis-only, 1 both
- BCT taxonomy: 14 cluster labels applied
- Outcomes: BOP, GI, plaque, PPD, CAL, self-reported oral hygiene behaviour
- Risk of bias: Cochrane RoB 1
- Synthesis: SWiM (Synthesis Without Meta-analysis)
- Certainty: GRADE (all outcomes very low)

## Results
| Population | Outcome | Finding | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | BOP | Inconsistent | Very low |
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | GI | Inconsistent | Very low |
| Periodontitis (active Tx) | Plaque | Mixed (1 benefit, 2 no diff) | Very low |
| Periodontitis (SPT) | All clinical | Little/no difference | Very low |
| Gingivitis | Gingival bleeding | 3/5 studies found reduction | Very low |
| Any | CAL, adverse events | No data found | — |

GRADE downgrade reasons: unblinded participants (performance bias), inconsistency, imprecision.
