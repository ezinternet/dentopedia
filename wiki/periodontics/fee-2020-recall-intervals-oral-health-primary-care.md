## Three-line Summary

Cochrane SR (5th update, 2 RCTs, n=1736) comparing 6-month, 24-month, and risk-based dental recall intervals in primary care adults and children/adolescents.
In adults with 4-year follow-up (high certainty), risk-based and 24-month recall showed little/no difference vs 6-month for caries surfaces (MD range −0.60 to 0.15), gingival bleeding, and OHRQoL (OHIP-14).
Extending dental recall beyond 6 months is not clinically detrimental for adults; evidence for children remains very low certainty and insufficient for recommendations.

## 세줄요약

코크란 체계적 고찰(5차 업데이트, 2 RCT, n=1736)로 1차 의료기관에서 치과 검진 간격 (Dental Recall Interval, DRI) 6개월·24개월·위험 기반 리콜 (Risk-based Recall)을 비교하였다.
성인 4년 추적(고확실성)에서 위험 기반 및 24개월 DRI는 6개월 대비 우식 경험 지수 (DMFS/dmfs), 치은 출혈, 구강건강 관련 삶의 질 (Oral Health-Related Quality of Life, OHRQoL) 모두 임상적으로 의미 있는 차이가 없었다 (MD −0.60 to 0.15).
성인에서 6개월 초과로 DRI를 연장해도 임상적 손실이 없어 정책적 유연성이 뒷받침되나, 소아·청소년 근거는 매우 낮은 확실성으로 불충분하다.

## Summary

This Cochrane systematic review (5th update of CD004346) pooled evidence from 2 RCTs enrolling 1736 participants to evaluate the optimal dental recall interval in primary care. The larger UK RCT (adults, 4-year follow-up) provided high-certainty evidence that risk-based recall and 24-month recall intervals produce outcomes equivalent to the standard 6-month recall for caries surfaces, gingival bleeding, and oral health-related quality of life. A Norwegian RCT in children and adolescents (12-month vs 24-month; 2-year follow-up) yielded only very low certainty evidence with confidence intervals crossing zero, making any conclusion for pediatric populations unreliable. The review concludes that the traditional universal 6-month recall for adults lacks evidence-based justification, and that extending intervals — whether to a fixed 24 months or guided by individual risk assessment — causes no measurable clinical harm.

## Key Contributions

1. **High-certainty equivalence of recall strategies in adults**: Risk-based = 24-month = 6-month recall for caries surfaces, gingival bleeding, and OHRQoL — the first Cochrane update to reach high certainty on this question.
2. **Risk-based recall not superior to fixed extended intervals**: Despite the intuitive appeal of individualized risk stratification, risk-based recall offered no measurable benefit over fixed 24-month recall (moderate certainty), challenging the assumption that personalization improves outcomes.
3. **Policy implication for the 6-month recall tradition**: Provides the strongest available evidence base for moving away from the universal 6-month recommendation in adult primary care — supports longer recall intervals as a clinically safe option.
4. **Persistent pediatric evidence gap**: Confirms that 2024 evidence for optimal recall in children and adolescents remains insufficient, underscoring the need for dedicated pediatric RCTs.

## Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Systematic review of RCTs (Cochrane, 5th update) |
| Prior versions | 2005, 2007, 2013 updates |
| Databases searched | Cochrane Oral Health Group Trials Register, CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase |
| Included RCTs | 2 trials, n=1736 |
| RCT 1 (Norway) | Public dental clinic; <20 years; 12-month vs 24-month recall; 2-year follow-up |
| RCT 2 (UK) | General dental practices; adults (≥1 visit prior 2 years); 6-month vs 24-month vs risk-based; 4-year follow-up |
| Primary outcomes | Caries increment (DMFS/dmfs), gingival bleeding, OHRQoL (OHIP-14) |
| Secondary outcomes | Moderate-to-extensive caries prevalence (ICDAS 3-6) |
| Evidence grading | GRADE; certainty reported per outcome per comparison |

## Results

### Adults — UK RCT (4-year follow-up)

| Comparison | Outcome | Effect size (95% CI) | n | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Caries surfaces (DMFS) | MD 0.15 (−0.77 to 1.08) | 1478 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Gingival bleeding | MD 0.78% (−1.17% to 2.73%) | 1472 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | OHRQoL (OHIP-14) | MD −0.35 (−1.02 to 0.32) | 1551 | High |
| Risk-based vs 6-month | Caries prevalence (ICDAS 3-6) | RR 1.04 (0.99–1.09) | — | Moderate |
| 24-month vs 6-month | Caries surfaces (DMFS) | MD −0.60 (−2.54 to 1.34) | 271 | Moderate–High |
| 24-month vs 6-month | Gingival bleeding | MD −0.91% (−5.02% to 3.20%) | 271 | Moderate–High |
| 24-month vs 6-month | OHRQoL (OHIP-14) | MD −0.24 (−1.55 to 1.07) | 305 | Moderate–High |
| Risk-based vs 24-month | Caries / gingival bleeding | Little/no difference | — | Moderate |

All confidence intervals cross zero → no clinically meaningful difference detected.

### Children/Adolescents — Norway RCT (2-year follow-up)

| Age group | Outcome | MD (95% CI) | Certainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5 years | dmfs increment | MD 0.90 (−0.16 to 1.96) | Very low |
| 16–20 years | DMFS | MD 0.86 (−0.03 to 1.75) | Very low |

Confidence intervals cross or touch zero; clinical significance unclear.
