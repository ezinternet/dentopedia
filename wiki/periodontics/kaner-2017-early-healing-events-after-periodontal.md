## Three-line Summary

Prospective descriptive split-mouth study (Int J Mol Sci 2017; 30 patients: 18 enamel matrix derivative/biphasic calcium phosphate (EMD/BCP) regenerative surgery, 12 surgical crown lengthening (SCL), each with an untreated healthy control site) characterizing 14-day early periodontal wound healing by laser Doppler flowmetry (LDF), gingival crevicular fluid/wound fluid (GCF/WF) cytokines, and flap closure.
Both surgical groups showed an immediate LDF perfusion drop with recovery by D14 and an early pro-inflammatory surge — IL-1β concentration 7.5-fold (EMD/BCP) vs 6-fold (SCL) at D1, IL-1β amount 39-fold/19-fold and IL-6 amount 39-fold/22-fold at D1 (p<0.001) — with IL-10 suppressed to 0.25–0.4-fold; SCL healed better (EHI p=0.032) with fewer dehiscences (3/12, 25%) than EMD/BCP (13/18, 72%; p=0.024).
Dehiscent sites carried 1.5-fold more GCF/WF, 4-fold more IL-1β and 2.5-fold more TNF-α than closed sites: primary flap closure, rather than the graft, appears decisive for uncomplicated early healing; the 14-day window and non-randomized procedure allocation limit causal inference.

## Summary

This prospective descriptive split-mouth study followed 30 patients for two weeks after periodontal surgery — 18 receiving regenerative surgery of intrabony defects with enamel matrix derivative plus biphasic calcium phosphate (EMD/BCP), and 12 undergoing surgical crown lengthening (SCL) — with each patient serving as their own healthy untreated control. Flap microcirculation (laser Doppler flowmetry), gingival crevicular fluid/wound fluid (GCF/WF) volume and four cytokines, and soft-tissue closure (Early Healing Index, EHI; dehiscence yes/no) were assessed at baseline, immediately post-operatively, and on days 1, 3, 7 and 14. Surgery produced a stereotyped early inflammatory reaction in both groups: an immediate drop in perfusion that recovered within two weeks, an early peak of IL-1β, TNF-α and IL-6 with reciprocal suppression of IL-10, and elevated GCF/WF — all largely reversible when the flap remained closed. SCL sites healed significantly better than EMD/BCP sites (EHI p=0.032; dehiscence 3/12 vs 13/18, p=0.024), and dehiscence itself was associated with 1.5-fold higher GCF/WF, 4-fold higher IL-1β and 2.5-fold higher TNF-α. Clinically, the authors conclude that preservation of primary flap closure — not the regenerative graft — is the critical precondition for uncomplicated early healing, while cautioning that EMD's anti-inflammatory effects seen in vitro were not detectable in clinical wound fluid.

## Key Contributions

- Provides rare in-vivo human data on the first 14 days of periodontal wound healing, combining perfusion, wound-fluid cytokine kinetics, and objective soft-tissue closure scores against intra-patient healthy controls.
- Defines the expected temporal cytokine signature of early periodontal healing: day-1 pro-inflammatory peak (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6), IL-10 suppression for about a week, and gradual resolution by day 14 if closure is maintained.
- Establishes flap dehiscence as the pivot between transient physiologic inflammation and prolonged inflammatory exudation (IL-1β 4-fold, TNF-α 2.5-fold vs closed sites).
- Flags a previously under-recognized risk of the EMD/BCP combination — 72% interproximal dehiscence — plausibly linked to supracrestal graft overfilling rather than to surgical trauma or impaired microcirculation.
- Documents the failure to translate in-vitro EMD immunomodulation into measurable clinical GCF/WF cytokine changes, with a mechanistic discussion of why mixed-cell wound fluid may mask such effects.

## Methodology

Prospective, non-randomized, split-mouth descriptive study (single surgeon; n=30: EMD/BCP 18, SCL 12), each patient contributing one surgical and one clinically healthy untreated control site. EMD/BCP used papilla-preservation flaps, 24% EDTA, Emdogain + Bone Ceramic BCP (including supracrestal overfill), and 6-0 modified vertical mattress sutures; SCL used mucoperiosteal flaps with ostectomy/osteoplasty and periosteally anchored vertical mattress sutures. Outcomes: LDF perfusion (D0a, D0b, D1, D3, D7, D14), GCF/WF volume and IL-1β/TNF-α/IL-6/IL-10 concentration and total amount per sample (D0a, D1, D3, D7, D14; Luminex multiplex immunoassay), and D14 EHI (1–5) plus dehiscence scored by two blinded calibrated examiners. Non-parametric statistics (Wilcoxon, Mann–Whitney U, Pearson χ²), patient as unit of analysis, SPSS 22, α=0.05. Evidence level `prospective` — a prospectively followed clinical cohort/case series without randomized allocation.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Microcirculation (LDF) | Significant perfusion drop at D0b vs D0a in both surgical groups; significant recovery by D1 but still below controls at D0b/D1; similar recovery to D14, no between-group difference |
| GCF/WF volume | At D1/D3 both surgical groups > controls (p<0.05); EMD/BCP > SCL at D1 and > controls at D3/D7 |
| IL-1β concentration (D1) | 7.5-fold (EMD/BCP) and 6-fold (SCL) vs controls (p<0.05); declined from D3 to D7 |
| IL-1β total amount (D1/D3) | 39-fold (EMD/BCP) and 19-fold (SCL) vs controls (p<0.001); at D14 EMD/BCP still 6-fold vs SCL and 12-fold vs controls (p<0.05) |
| TNF-α total amount | Rose at D1/D3 in both surgical groups vs controls; at D14 EMD/BCP 2-fold vs SCL and 2.5-fold vs healthy (p<0.05) |
| IL-6 | Concentration > controls at D1/D3/D7; total amount at D1 39-fold (EMD/BCP) and 22-fold (SCL) (p<0.001); both groups > controls through D14 |
| IL-10 concentration | Fell to 0.25–0.4-fold of healthy sites at D1/D3/D7 in both surgical groups (p<0.05); total amount unchanged over time |
| Soft-tissue healing (D14) | EHI 1–5: EMD/BCP 2/2/1/4/9 (11/11/6/22/50%) vs SCL 0/5/4/1/2 (0/42/33/8/17%), favouring SCL (p=0.032) |
