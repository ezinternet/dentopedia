## Three-line Summary

Cross-sectional study of 160 periodontally healthy patients (21–40 y and 41–60 y, equal gender) measuring gingival angle (GA), gingival zenith angle (GZA), and gingival thickness (GT) on maxillary central, lateral, and canine teeth.

GA and GZA were consistently lower in females; GT 1.15 ± 0.21 (F) vs 2.56 ± 0.26 mm (M) at 21–40 y and 1.13 ± 0.21 vs 2.54 ± 0.27 mm at 41–60 y; no direct correlation GA–GZA or GZA–GT.

Zenith angle differs by sex but not age and is not predicted by gingival thickness.

## Summary

Kolte and colleagues asked whether the angular shape of the gingival margin around the zenith tracks with tissue thickness, sex, or age. Men had wider gingival and zenith angles and much thicker gingiva (~2.5 vs ~1.1 mm) in both age bands; age made little difference. Zenith angle did not correlate with thickness. For restorative design this suggests zenith shape and tissue phenotype are separate inputs — a thick phenotype does not by itself dictate a particular zenith form. The measurement methods are not in the abstract.

## Key Contributions

- Sex-specific GA/GZA and GT reference data; no age effect; GZA independent of GT.

## Methodology

- 160 subjects, two age groups, maxillary CI/LI/CA; methods detail not in abstract.
