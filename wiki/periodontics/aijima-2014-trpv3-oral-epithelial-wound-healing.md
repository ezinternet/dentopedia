## One-line Summary
In vivo/in vitro mouse study identifying TRPV3, a thermosensitive Ca²⁺ channel activated above 33°C, as a driver of oral epithelial proliferation and wound closure that is more highly expressed in oral mucosa than skin.

## 한줄요약
마우스 생체·시험관 연구: 구강상피에서 피부보다 높게 발현되는 열감응성 Ca²⁺ 채널 TRPV3가 33°C 이상에서 활성화되어 구강상피세포 증식 및 발치와 창상 봉합을 촉진하며, TRPV3 결핍 마우스에서 두 지표 모두 저하.

## Three-line Summary
In vivo/in vitro mouse study (C57BL/6 WT vs. TRPV3KO, Kyushu University) identifying TRPV3 thermosensitive Ca²⁺ channel as a driver of oral epithelial proliferation and wound closure.
TRPV3 mRNA higher in oral epithelia than skin; temperatures >33°C activate TRPV3 → EGFR phosphorylation → proliferation; TRPV3KO mice showed reduced BrdU+ proliferation and delayed molar extraction wound closure vs. WT.
Mouse-to-human extrapolation required; molar extraction model may not generalize to gingival/mucosal wounds; Ca²⁺ threshold and EGFR linkage need further mechanistic study.

## Summary
Aijima et al. (2014) investigated why the oral mucosa heals faster than skin by focusing on TRPV3 (Transient Receptor Potential Vanilloid 3), a thermosensitive, Ca²⁺-permeable ion channel. Using qRT-PCR, the authors showed that TRPV3 mRNA is more abundantly expressed in murine oral epithelia than in skin. Temperatures above 33°C—consistent with the resting oral cavity environment—activated TRPV3 and promoted oral epithelial cell proliferation. TRPV3 knockout (TRPV3KO) mice showed a significantly reduced oral epithelial proliferation rate and delayed wound closure in a molar extraction model compared to wild-type controls. TRPV3 mRNA was upregulated in wounded oral tissue, further supporting its role in the repair response. The proposed signaling axis involves Ca²⁺ influx via TRPV3 → EGFR (ErbB) phosphorylation.

## Key Contributions
- First evidence that TRPV3 expression is higher in oral epithelia than skin and functionally contributes to the faster healing rate of oral wounds
- Demonstrated temperature-dependence of oral epithelial proliferation with a 33°C threshold for TRPV3 activation
- TRPV3KO model directly shows that its loss reduces both proliferation rate and wound closure speed in vivo
- TRPV3 mRNA upregulation at wound sites suggests an injury-responsive amplification mechanism
- Identified TRPV3→Ca²⁺→EGFR as a potential oral-specific therapeutic target for wound healing

## Methodology
- In vivo: C57BL/6 wild-type vs. TRPV3KO mice; molar extraction wound model for closure timing
- In vitro: cultured murine oral epithelial cells; temperature-controlled proliferation assays
- qRT-PCR for TRPV3 mRNA expression (oral vs. skin; unwounded vs. wounded tissue)
- BrdU incorporation for proliferation rate quantification
- ErbB (EGFR) phosphorylation by immunohistochemistry as signaling readout

## Results
- TRPV3 mRNA significantly higher in oral epithelia than skin (qRT-PCR)
- Temperatures >33°C → TRPV3 activation → increased oral epithelial cell proliferation
- TRPV3KO mice: **proliferation rate significantly reduced** vs. wild-type (BrdU)
- TRPV3KO mice: **oral wound closure delayed** vs. wild-type (molar extraction model)
- TRPV3 mRNA upregulated in wounded oral tissue
- EGFR phosphorylation reduced in TRPV3KO cells (downstream signaling impaired)
