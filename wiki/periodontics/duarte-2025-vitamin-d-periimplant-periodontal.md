## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (SANRA, PubMed-only) synthesizing vitamin D's roles in peri-implant healing and periodontal disease; vitamin D deficiency is consistently linked to impaired osseointegration, reduced bone-to-implant contact (BIC), and accelerated marginal bone loss (MBL) while a critical upper threshold exists — hypervitaminosis D (>70 ng/mL) → 21.1-fold increased peri-implant bone loss.

Mechanistically, active vitamin D (calcitriol) upregulates RUNX2, ALP, OPN, and OCN in periodontal ligament and peri-implant cells, reduces RANKL/TNF-α/IL-1 signaling, and enhances mineral nodule formation — both systemic supplementation and topical implant-surface coating strategies are documented.

Evidence base is predominantly preclinical (animal and in vitro); only limited clinical studies exist, no standardized dosing protocol can be defined, and populations with diabetes, osteoporosis, or other systemic comorbidities may respond differently.

## Background

Vitamin D deficiency affects more than 1 billion individuals globally, with associated costs exceeding USD 1.56 billion in the United States alone. As an active hormone (calcitriol), vitamin D regulates intestinal calcium absorption, bone remodeling, and immune response — all critical for dental implant osseointegration and periodontal tissue homeostasis. Its incorporation into implant surface coatings, hydrogels, or bone graft materials represents an emerging local delivery strategy to overcome systemic absorption variability.

## Key Findings — Peri-Implant

### Osseointegration
- Vitamin D3 surface coating: +10% BIC at 12 weeks vs controls (animal model, Salomó-Coll 2016)
- Topical vitamin D before implant insertion: increased stability, reduced MBL and BOP at 6 months (Ayyad 2025, clinical)
- Vitamin D deficiency → reduced BIC + impaired cortical bone; supplementation 2400 IU/kg×8 weeks → significant improvement (Dvorak 2012, animal)
- Combined vitamin D + teriparatide → increased bone volume + quality in ovariectomized rats (Gomes-Ferreira 2023)

### Dose-Response (U-curve — critical finding)
- Serum vitamin D **deficient/insufficient** → lower implant survival, greater MBL (Tabrizi 2022, Mahendra 2025)
- Serum vitamin D **>70 ng/mL (hypervitaminosis)** → 21.1-fold increased peri-implant bone loss risk vs intermediate (Cheng 2024)
- Optimal range: intermediate serum levels (likely 30–60 ng/mL range, though not explicitly defined)

## Key Findings — Periodontal

- Serum 25(OH)D <50 ng/mL associated with periodontitis (Laky 2017, case-control)
- Vitamin D deficiency → elevated GCF MMP-9, indicating periodontitis severity (Yildirim 2025)
- PDL cells treated with 1,25(OH)₂D₃: RUNX2↑, ALP↑, OCN↑, mineral nodule formation↑ (Pereira 2024, in vitro)
- Animal model: 25-hydroxyvitamin D3 injection ×8 weeks → RANKL↓, IL-1↓, TNF-α↓, alveolar bone loss limited (Han 2019)
- Clinical: 60,000 IU/week ×8 weeks in T2DM+periodontitis → significant improvement in PI, BI, PD, CAL (RamaPrabha 2023)

## Mechanisms

| Pathway | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| RUNX2/ALP/OCN/OPN upregulation | Enhanced osteoblast differentiation and mineralization |
| RANKL/OPG modulation | Reduced osteoclastogenesis |
| TNF-α/IL-1 suppression | Anti-inflammatory in both peri-implant and periodontal tissues |
| Calcium/phosphorus homeostasis | Systemic bone density support |

## Clinical Takeaway

Vitamin D deficiency should be screened and corrected before implant placement — particularly in patients with diabetes, osteoporosis, or history of implant failure. However, supplementation should target adequate levels (not excess); hypervitaminosis D (>70 ng/mL) is a risk factor, not a benefit. For periodontal patients with T2DM, adjunctive vitamin D supplementation (60,000 IU/week ×8 weeks) shows clinical benefit. Topical implant surface coating is promising but remains preclinical.
