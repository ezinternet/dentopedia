## Three-line Summary

Case-control study (n=38 SLE patients, all female, mean age 39.6y vs n=29 healthy controls, mean age 46.3y; São Luís, Brazil) measuring periodontal parameters (PPD, CAL, BOP) and stimulated-saliva leptin, using a structural equation model (SEM) to test SLE status and periodontal status as independent predictors of salivary leptin.

SLE patients had significantly lower salivary leptin than controls (P=0.002); SEM showed SLE status had a significant independent inverse effect on salivary leptin (standardized estimate −0.289, P=0.023), but the periodontal-status latent variable did not; salivary leptin also correlated with serum triglycerides (Rs=−0.534), creatinine (Rs=−0.331), creatinine clearance (Rs=−0.445), leukocytes (Rs=−0.331), and total cholesterol (Rs=0.434, positive) within the SLE group.

In SLE patients, salivary leptin tracks systemic/serum metabolic-renal markers rather than local periodontal clinical parameters — a small case-control study (n=67 total) that should not be over-generalized, but it cautions against assuming a uniform periodontal-systemic biomarker link across all autoimmune conditions.

## Summary

This case-control study compared 38 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) to 29 healthy controls in São Luís, Brazil, to determine whether periodontal status, clinical variables, or serum biomarkers explain salivary leptin levels. Periodontal probing depth (PPD), clinical attachment level (CAL), and bleeding on probing (BOP) were recorded by a single calibrated examiner, and stimulated saliva leptin was assayed via multiplex immunoassay. A structural equation model (SEM) tested age, SLE status, and a latent periodontal-status variable as simultaneous predictors of salivary leptin.

SLE patients had significantly lower salivary leptin than controls, and this reduction was independently attributable to SLE status itself (not periodontal condition, age, or BMI). Within the SLE group, salivary leptin correlated with several serum markers — negatively with triglycerides, creatinine, creatinine clearance, and leukocytes, and positively with total cholesterol — but showed no significant relationship to periodontal clinical parameters. The authors propose that SLE-related inflammatory or vascular mechanisms alter salivary leptin secretion/clearance independent of local periodontal disease, contrasting with prior literature showing salivary leptin elevated in periodontitis alone.

## Key Contributions

- First reported structural-equation-model analysis jointly evaluating periodontal status, age, and SLE status as predictors of salivary leptin.
- Confirms salivary leptin is reduced in SLE (opposite direction to periodontitis-only literature, where salivary leptin rises), independent of periodontal condition.
- Identifies serum correlates of salivary leptin specific to the SLE population: triglycerides, creatinine, creatinine clearance, leukocytes (inverse), total cholesterol (positive).
- Provides a direct empirical test — and a negative result — for whether periodontal inflammation drives salivary leptin change in an autoimmune disease population.

## Methodology

- Case-control design, single time point; 38 SLE patients (University Hospital, Federal University of Maranhão) vs 29 healthy controls (Dentistry School patients), ages 18–60.
- Exclusions: diabetes, smoking/ex-smoking <10y, orthodontic appliances, periodontal treatment within 6 months, active infectious disease, salivary gland pathology, pregnancy/breastfeeding, recent antibiotics.
- Periodontal exam: single calibrated examiner (intra-examiner reliability coefficient >0.8), Williams probe, PPD/CAL at 6 sites/tooth, BOP at 4 sites/tooth, full dentition.
- Saliva: stimulated (chewing), 7–10 am, ≥1h post-meal, protease-inhibitor cocktail, −80°C storage; leptin via MAGPIX System / Milliplex MAP (Merck Millipore), duplicate.
- Serum panel (SLE group): triglycerides, total cholesterol, glucose, creatinine, creatinine clearance, urea, GOT, GPT, leukocytes, hemoglobin, platelets.
- Statistics: t-test/Mann-Whitney/Spearman in Stata v18; SEM in Mplus v7.0 with latent periodontal-status variable (mean PPD, mean CAL, BOP); fit criteria chi-square P>0.05, TLI/CFI>0.95, RMSEA<0.05, SRMR<0.05.

## Results

- Salivary leptin significantly lower in SLE group vs controls (P=0.002).
- SEM direct effects on salivary leptin: SLE status significant and inverse (standardized estimate = −0.289, P=0.023); periodontal-status latent variable NOT a significant direct predictor.
- Indirect pathway: age had an inverse effect on SLE status and a direct effect on periodontal condition; SLE had an inverse effect on periodontal status — but this indirect chain did not produce a significant periodontal→leptin effect.
- Serum correlations within SLE group (Spearman): triglycerides Rs=−0.534 (P=0.003); creatinine Rs=−0.331 (P=0.048); creatinine clearance Rs=−0.445 (P=0.025); leukocytes Rs=−0.331 (P=0.041); total cholesterol Rs=0.434 (P=0.016, positive).
- BMI uncorrelated with salivary leptin (P=0.385); no association between SLE medication type and salivary leptin.
