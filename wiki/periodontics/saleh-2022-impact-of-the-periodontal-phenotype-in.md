## Three-line Summary

Single-arm prospective clinical trial (26 subjects, 52 adjacent posterior teeth) measuring vertical/horizontal bone changes on teeth included in a full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap (FTF) during posterior implant surgery, using superimposed CBCT at baseline (T0) and 12 months (T1) with periodontal phenotype from digital-scan/DICOM superimposition.
Vertical bone loss was significant (P = 0.013) and greatest at mesial (−0.31 ± 0.30 mm) and facial (P < 0.05) sites; horizontal changes 5 mm subcrestally did not differ by location (P = 0.086), while bone-width loss was greatest closest to the crest (P = 0.001).
Overall bone loss was clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm) with no soft-tissue-thickness correlation — baseline bone thickness (>2 mm appears protective) governs remodeling, and FTF shows no deleterious effect on the adjacent-tooth periodontium (abstract-only report).

## Summary

This single-arm prospective clinical trial followed 26 subjects (52 adjacent posterior teeth) whose teeth were included in a full-thickness mucoperiosteal flap raised during posterior implant surgery, asking whether the periodontal phenotype modifies flap-associated bone remodeling. Superimposed CBCT at baseline and 12 months showed statistically significant but small vertical bone loss (P = 0.013), greatest at mesial sites (−0.31 ± 0.30 mm) and facial sites (P < 0.05); horizontal change 5 mm subcrestally did not differ by location (P = 0.086), while width loss was greatest nearest the crest (P = 0.001). Soft tissue thickness showed no correlation with bone change, whereas baseline bone thickness appeared to influence the extent of horizontal remodeling, with a possible protective effect above 2 mm. All changes were clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm), supporting the conclusion that FTF does not deleteriously affect the adjacent-tooth periodontium at 12 months. Interpretation is limited by the single-arm design, small sample, and the abstract-only source available here.

## Key Contributions

- Prospective clinical quantification of bone remodeling on **adjacent natural teeth** included in an FTF during posterior implant surgery, using superimposed CBCT.
- Demonstrates that the 12-month marginal bone change is clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm) — FTF is not deleterious to the adjacent-tooth periodontium.
- Shows that bone remodeling is influenced by baseline **bone** thickness rather than **soft tissue** thickness (no soft tissue thickness ↔ bone change correlation).
- Suggests a tentative protective threshold of >2 mm baseline bone thickness against horizontal bone loss.
- Describes a site-specific vertical pattern: greatest loss mesially and facially, and crestal width loss greatest closest to the crest.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Single-arm prospective clinical trial; no unflapped control group.
- **n**: 26 subjects, 52 adjacent posterior teeth (mesial and distal to each planned implant site).
- **Intervention**: At posterior implant site preparation, an FTF was extended one tooth mesial and one tooth distal to the planned site and elevated both facially and lingually.
- **Imaging / outcomes**: Vertical and horizontal linear bone changes on the adjacent teeth from superimposed CBCT at T0 (pre-implant) and T1 (12 months). Periodontal phenotype from superimposition of baseline digital models and DICOM files.
- **Reporting scope**: Full statistical model and error-of-measurement details unavailable in the retrieved abstract-only PDF.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Vertical bone change, T0 → T1 | Statistically significant (P = 0.013) |
| Mesial vertical change | −0.31 ± 0.30 mm; significantly greater than other sites |
| Facial vertical change | Significantly greater (P < 0.05) |
| Horizontal change, 5 mm subcrestal | Similar among locations (P = 0.086) |
| Bone-width loss | Higher closest to the crest (P = 0.001) |
| Soft tissue thickness ↔ bone change | No correlation |
| Baseline bone thickness | Influences extent of horizontal bone remodeling; preventive effect suggested >2 mm |
| Overall magnitude | Clinically insignificant (≤0.4 mm), vertical and horizontal |

**Conclusion**: 12-month marginal bone changes in maxillary and mandibular posterior teeth after FTF are very minimal and driven mainly by bone rather than soft tissue thickness; FTF does not appear to have deleterious effects on adjacent-tooth periodontium.
