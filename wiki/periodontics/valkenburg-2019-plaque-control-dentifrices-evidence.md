## Three-line Summary

Meta-review (umbrella review) of 10 systematic reviews (search to Feb 2017) synthesizing evidence on dentifrice active ingredients (triclosan, stannous fluoride, chlorhexidine, aloe vera) for plaque and gingivitis control in adults.

Toothbrushing with a standard fluoride dentifrice added no significant mechanical plaque-removal benefit over brushing alone (DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91); triclosan-copolymer and stannous fluoride dentifrices had GRADE-strong evidence of benefit on both plaque and gingivitis, chlorhexidine dentifrice weak evidence of a small benefit, and aloe vera very weak evidence.

Dentifrice's chemical actives (triclosan, stannous fluoride), not the paste vehicle itself, drive added antiplaque/antigingivitis benefit; considerable cross-study heterogeneity (I² up to 98%) and industry-linked authorship/publication-bias risk temper the precision of the pooled estimates.

## 세줄요약

성인 대상 치약(dentifrice) 활성성분(트리클로산, 주석불화물, 클로르헥시딘, 알로에베라)의 치태·치은염 조절 근거를 종합한 메타리뷰(우산 리뷰) — 2017년 2월까지 검색된 체계적 문헌고찰 10편 통합.

표준 불소 치약 사용 칫솔질은 치약 없는 칫솔질 대비 기계적 치태 제거에 유의한 부가효과가 없었으나(DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91), 트리클로산-코폴리머·주석불화물 치약은 치태·치은염 모두에서 강한(GRADE) 근거의 효과, 클로르헥시딘 치약은 약한 근거의 소폭 효과, 알로에베라는 매우 약한 근거를 보였다.

치약 자체가 아니라 트리클로산·주석불화물 같은 화학적 활성성분이 항치태·항치은염 부가효과를 이끌며, 근거를 이룬 메타분석들의 상당한 이질성(I² 최대 98%)과 산업 관련 저자·출판 편향 위험은 통합 추정치의 정밀도를 제한한다.

## Summary

This meta-review (an umbrella review of systematic reviews, the dentifrice-focused member of a trio alongside prior meta-reviews on mechanical home-care devices and on mouthwash chemical agents) searched three databases through March 2017 and identified 10 eligible systematic reviews on commercially available dentifrices for plaque and gingivitis control. One review evaluated dentifrice's own mechanical contribution to plaque removal; the rest evaluated single or paired active ingredients (aloe vera, chlorhexidine, stannous fluoride, triclosan) against control/placebo dentifrice. Using AMSTAR/PRISMA-based quality scoring and GRADE evidence grading, the authors calculated weighted-mean pooled difference-of-means (DiffM) across compatible meta-analyses. The headline finding is a clean separation between vehicle and active ingredient: a standard fluoride dentifrice itself adds no significant mechanical plaque-removal benefit over brushing alone, but triclosan-copolymer and stannous fluoride dentifrices show strong evidence of benefit for both plaque and gingivitis relative to a plain fluoride control, chlorhexidine dentifrice shows weak evidence of a small benefit, and aloe vera very weak evidence. A head-to-head comparison (triclosan vs stannous fluoride) found a clinically minor, largely inconclusive difference, with triclosan favored on the Quigley-Hein plaque index and stannous fluoride favored on gingival bleeding score.

## Key Contributions

- Establishes that the **mechanical act of brushing**, not the dentifrice paste itself, accounts for plaque removal — the paste's value lies in delivering chemical actives, not in scrubbing power.
- Produces a **GRADE evidence-strength ranking across four active ingredients**: triclosan and stannous fluoride = strong evidence (moderate magnitude of effect); chlorhexidine = weak evidence (small effect); aloe vera = very weak evidence (very small effect).
- Quantifies **pooled weighted-mean effect sizes** by ingredient and index (Quigley-Hein plaque index, Silness-Löe, Löe-Silness gingival index), enabling direct magnitude comparison across agents.
- Directly contrasts **triclosan vs stannous fluoride** head-to-head, corroborated by an independent network meta-analysis, concluding the clinical difference between the two market-leading actives is minor.
- Completes a **three-part meta-review series** (mechanical home-care devices → mouthwash actives → dentifrice actives) summarizing the full landscape of daily home-care plaque-control evidence.

## Methodology

- **Design**: A priori meta-review protocol; AMSTAR (2007) applied to the review process; PRISMA + AMSTAR 27-item checklist used to score included systematic reviews' methodological quality (risk-of-bias bands: 0-40% high, 40-60% substantial, 60-80% moderate, 80-100% low).
- **Search**: MEDLINE-PubMed, Cochrane Library/DARE, ADA Center for Evidence-Based Dentistry, through March 2017; hand-search of reference lists; PROSPERO checked for ongoing reviews.
- **Selection**: Two independent reviewers, third-reviewer arbitration; 205 unique reviews screened → 12 full texts → **10 systematic reviews included** (2 excluded as non-systematic/narrative).
- **Quantitative synthesis**: Where ≥2 meta-analyses reported a compatible clinical index for the same ingredient, an overall weighted mean DiffM was calculated (weight = number of comparisons per meta-analysis), with heterogeneity assessed by chi-squared/I².
- **Evidence grading**: GRADE framework (study design, risk of bias, consistency, precision, directness, publication bias, magnitude of effect) applied per ingredient.

## Results

- **Mechanical effect of dentifrice**: No significant added mechanical plaque-removal effect vs brushing alone (DiffM 0.00, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.05], P=.91; moderate-quality GRADE evidence).
- **Stannous fluoride (SnF)**: Q&H plaque index overall weighted mean 0.25 (SD 0.08), 95% CI (0.20, 0.29); gingival index overall weighted mean 0.14 (SD 0.02), 95% CI (0.13, 0.15) — both significant across underlying meta-analyses.
- **Triclosan-copolymer**: Q&H plaque index overall weighted mean 0.47 (SD 0.02), 95% CI (0.47, 0.48); gingival index overall weighted mean 0.26 (SD 0.01), 95% CI (0.25, 0.26).
- **Chlorhexidine (CHX) dentifrice**: Q&H plaque index overall weighted mean 0.73 (SD 0.07), 95% CI (0.70, 0.79) from 2 meta-analyses; CHX gel showed no clear trend vs placebo, and CHX dentifrice/gel was less effective than CHX mouthwash.
- **Aloe vera**: only 2 RCTs, heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis; similar efficacy to control reported; evidence "not sufficiently defined."
- **Triclosan vs SnF head-to-head** (Sälzer et al., 15 studies): no significant gingival-index difference (DiffM -0.04, P=.34); gingival bleeding favored SnF (DiffM 0.02, P<.001); Q&H plaque index favored triclosan (DiffM -0.29, P<.001); corroborated by a network meta-analysis (weighted mean difference -0.34, 95% CI [-0.56, -0.12]).
- **Quality of included reviews**: 7/10 low risk of bias, 2 moderate, 1 substantial.
- **Clinical caveat**: stannous fluoride's known staining side effect limits it as a routine universal dentifrice recommendation despite strong efficacy evidence.
- **Key limitations**: considerable heterogeneity (I² up to 98% for plaque, ≥70% for gingivitis scores), publication-bias risk (unpublished data generally excluded from underlying SRs), and author financial ties to dentifrice manufacturers (Colgate, GABA, Oral-B, Procter & Gamble, Sunstar, Unilever, among others).
