## Three-line Summary

Umbrella review (PRISMA/PROSPERO-registered) of 30 systematic reviews on probiotic therapy as an adjunct to nonsurgical treatment in adult patients with periodontal disease and/or peri-implant disease; no cross-SR meta-analysis was possible due to high clinical heterogeneity.

17 of 31 reviews reported clinically relevant benefit; SR-level evidence favors short-term (≤3 month) improvement in PPD, CAL, and BOP on tooth substrate, but benefit is not sustained at 6 months and is markedly weaker/absent on peri-implant substrate; 22/31 SRs had low risk of bias.

Evidence is conflicting, with no definitive conclusion for or against probiotic adjunct therapy; no included SR reported bone loss, implant survival, or tooth-loss outcomes, and high-quality confounder-controlled primary RCTs are needed.

근거가 상충하여 프로바이오틱스 보조요법에 대한 찬반 확정적 결론 불가; 포함된 SR 중 골소실·임플란트 생존율·발치율을 보고한 것은 없었고, 교란변수를 통제한 고품질 1차 RCT가 필요함.

## Summary

This umbrella review (PROSPERO CRD42021254469) systematically compiled 30 systematic reviews evaluating probiotic therapy as an adjuvant to nonsurgical periodontal treatment (NSPT) or peri-implant nonsurgical therapy in adult patients with periodontal or peri-implant disease. Because of high clinical heterogeneity in probiotic species, administration vehicle, dose, and follow-up duration across the underlying primary studies, no quantitative re-pooling across SRs was attempted — the review is a structured qualitative synthesis (JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist, GRADE-style summary-of-findings table). Seventeen of 31 included SRs reported clinically relevant benefit; pooled SR-level signal favored short-term (1–3 month) reductions in probing pocket depth (PPD), gains in clinical attachment level (CAL), and reductions in bleeding on probing (BOP), but this benefit largely did not persist at 6 months, and the peri-implant substrate literature was both sparser and less favorable than the periodontal (tooth) substrate literature. No included SR reported hard clinical endpoints (bone loss, implant survival, tooth loss). The authors conclude the evidence remains conflicting and insufficient to make a definitive recommendation for or against probiotic adjunct therapy, and call for confounder-controlled RCTs with ≥1-year follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Jointly synthesizes probiotic-adjunct evidence across BOTH periodontal (tooth) and peri-implant substrates in a single umbrella review, rather than treating them as separate bodies of literature.
- GRADE-style Summary of Findings table (Table 4) stratifies PPD/CAL/BOP/microbiological outcomes by substrate and direction of effect (favors vs. does not favor probiotics), making the tooth-vs-implant evidence gap explicit.
- Flags that *Lactobacillus reuteri* is the subspecies most consistently linked to clinical improvement across the primary-SR evidence base.
- Explicitly documents the absence of any SR-level data on bone loss, implant survival, or tooth loss — a gap relevant to long-term outcome claims for probiotic adjuncts.

## Methodology

Umbrella review, PRISMA-reported, PROSPERO-registered. Five databases searched to December 2022 (Medline/PubMed, LILACS, Cochrane CENTRAL, Google Scholar first 300 refs, DANS EASY grey-literature archive), plus manual reference screening and author contact. 30 systematic reviews included (risk-of-bias breakdown reported for n=31 in the abstract — see source page for the internal count discrepancy noted in the original paper); underlying primary studies were RCTs. Two independent reviewers extracted data per a JBI systematic-review datasheet and appraised methodological quality with the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Systematic Reviews (Cohen's κ for interrater agreement); disagreements resolved by discussion or a third reviewer. Primary outcomes: PPD, CAL, BOP. Secondary outcomes: plaque/gingival indices, microbiological pathogen counts, immunological markers, bone loss, implant survival, tooth loss, adverse events.

## Results

| Outcome | Total SRs | Favors probiotics | Does not favor | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPD | 26 | 15 (13 tooth + 2 implant) | 11 (7 tooth + 4 implant) | Short-term (≤3mo) benefit, diminishing medium/long term |
| CAL | 19 | 10 (tooth only) | 9 (tooth only) | Benefit at 1–3mo; 6mo benefit unproven |
| BOP | 23 | 13 (10 tooth + 3 implant) | 10 (7 tooth + 3 implant) | Benefit at 6mo unproven |
| Microbiological | 14 | 6 | 8 | Mixed across specific pathogens |
| Bone loss / implant survival / tooth loss | 0 each | — | — | No clinical data reported by any included SR |

Risk of bias across included SRs: 22 low, 7 moderate, 2 high (81% PRISMA-adherent). Representative pooled effect from an included meta-analysis (Hu et al. 2021, 24 RCTs): CAL gain WMD 0.20 mm (95% CI 0.09–0.31), PPD reduction WMD −0.31 mm (95% CI −0.52 to −0.10), BOP reduction WMD −2.98% (95% CI −4.70 to −1.26) at 3 months — not sustained at 6 months. Peri-implant meta-analysis (Gao et al. 2020, 7 RCTs) found no significant PPD benefit of *Lactobacillus* vs. placebo in peri-implant mucositis.

**Bottom line**: results are conflicting; no definitive conclusion for or against probiotic adjunct therapy is possible from current evidence.
