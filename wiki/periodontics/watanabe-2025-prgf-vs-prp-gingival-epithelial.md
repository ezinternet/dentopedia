## One-line Summary
First in vitro study (n=3 donors) comparing PRGF and L-PRP on human gingival epithelial progenitors: PRGF significantly boosts proliferation on days 1–2 (p<0.01) but neither concentrate accelerates wound closure vs. controls.

## Summary
Watanabe et al. (2025) conducted the first in vitro study directly comparing PRGF (Plasma Rich in Growth Factors, leukocyte-poor) and L-PRP (leukocyte-containing platelet-rich plasma) on human gingival epithelial progenitor (HGEPp) cells for proliferation, wound closure, and gene expression. PRGF's leukocyte count (median 0.3 × 10⁴/mL) was markedly lower than L-PRP (6.0 × 10⁴/mL), consistent with IL-1β reduction. PRGF significantly enhanced proliferation on days 1 and 2 (p<0.01), attributed to lower IL-1β (which downregulates cyclin B) rather than TNF-α. Despite the early proliferative advantage, no significant difference in wound closure rate was observed in a 500 µm gap model, suggesting that migration—not proliferation—primarily governs gingival epithelial wound closure kinetics in vitro. EGF concentrations in both concentrates (PRGF: 489 pg/mL; PRP: 279 pg/mL) were below the threshold for integrin β4 upregulation, explaining the null migration finding.

## Key Contributions
- First direct in vitro comparison of PRGF vs. L-PRP effects on gingival epithelial cells
- PRGF superior to L-PRP for early gingival epithelial proliferation (days 1–2); effect disappears by day 3
- Wound closure (migration) not significantly different: proliferation advantage does not translate to faster tissue coverage
- TNF-α and integrin β4 mRNA unchanged by either concentrate — cytoskeletal motility machinery unaffected
- EGF threshold insight: 5,000–10,000 pg/mL needed for integrin β4 upregulation; PRGF/PRP EGF concentrations 10–20× below this threshold

## Methodology
- Cell: HGEPp (normal human gingival epithelium progenitors, pooled; CELLnTEC); seeded 1,000 cells/well
- Donors: 3 healthy males (28, 30, 38 y); blood centrifuged at 580 g × 8 min (Endoret BTI system)
- Proliferation: WST-1 assay (450/620 nm) at days 1, 2, 3, 7, 14; 15% PRGF or PRP supplementation
- Wound healing: NUNC cell-free insert model, 500 µm gap; images at 8–24 h; ImageJ gap area quantification
- Gene expression: qRT-PCR for TNF-α and integrin β4 (normalized to β-actin) at 24 h
- Statistics: Kruskal–Wallis test
