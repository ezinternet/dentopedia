## Summary

This umbrella review synthesizes 72 systematic and narrative reviews to map the efficacy of toothpaste (dentifrice) active ingredients across six clinically relevant oral conditions: gingival/gum health, dental caries, dentine hypersensitivity, tooth surface loss, oral malodour, and tooth whitening/staining. The authors identify 19 distinct active ingredients, including six types of fluoride, and find that efficacy evidence varies substantially — six ingredients had significant supportive evidence for their target condition, while fluoride type in particular showed marked differences in efficacy. The review challenges the clinician assumption that toothpastes are largely interchangeable, arguing instead for condition-specific product selection. ## Key Contributions

- First umbrella-review synthesis spanning six distinct oral condition domains for toothpaste ingredient efficacy in one framework.
- Identifies specific ingredient-condition pairings clinicians can use for tailored recommendations (e.g., condition-specific fluoride selection).
- Surfaces the clinical misconception that all toothpastes perform similarly.

## Methodology

Umbrella review design following a PRISMA-based quality checklist; electronic search via OVID identified 72 eligible reviews (systematic and/or narrative reviews of RCTs, observational, or in-vitro studies) addressing toothpaste active ingredients across the six oral conditions listed above.

## Results

- 19 active ingredients identified, including 6 fluoride types, mapped across the six oral conditions.
- 6 ingredients found to have significant efficacy evidence for their targeted condition.
- Efficacy differences were "particularly evident" among fluoride types — not all fluoride formulations perform equivalently.
