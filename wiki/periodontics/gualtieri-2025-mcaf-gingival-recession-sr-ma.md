## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (17 studies in SR; 9 in MA) comparing modified coronally advanced flap (mCAF) adjunctive techniques for multiple adjacent gingival recession-type (MAGR) defects; mCAF+sCTG was superior to all alternatives for complete root coverage (CRC: OR=1.70; 95% CI 1.18–2.44, p=0.004) and keratinized tissue width increase (ΔKTW: SMD=0.37; 95% CI 0.11–0.63, p=0.005).

sCTG superiority over xenogeneic acellular dermal matrix (XADM) was the most robust finding: CRC (p=0.002) and ΔKTW (p=0.0001), both HIGH evidence; sCTG also outperformed collagen matrix (CM) for ΔKTW (LOW evidence, p=0.0006).

Long-term evidence remains limited to one RCT; mCAF+EMD and double-blade-scalpel sCTG show promise but require more evidence — sCTG remains the evidence-based gold standard for MAGR treatment in 2025.

## Background

Gingival recession (GR) — apical shift of the gingival margin from the cemento-enamel junction — leads to root surface exposure, esthetic compromise, dentinal hypersensitivity, poor plaque control, root caries, and ultimately periodontal support loss. Multiple adjacent gingival recession (MAGR) defects present an amplified surgical challenge due to larger operative fields and greater anatomical variation.

The modified coronally advanced flap (mCAF) with envelope design is the current reference technique for MAGR treatment. This 2025 SR+MA is the first to specifically compare all mCAF adjunctive techniques against sCTG as the reference standard for MAGR defects.

## Methods

- **Protocol**: PROSPERO CRD420251085823
- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cinahl Complete; 2013–2025
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, MAGR treatment, mCAF adjunctive techniques, ≥5 patients, ≥6-month follow-up
- **Primary outcomes**: CRC (complete root coverage), ΔKTW (change in keratinized tissue width)
- **Secondary outcome**: RES (root coverage esthetic score)
- **Risk of bias**: RoB-2; evidence level: GRADEPro GDT
- **Software**: RevMan5.4

## Results

### Included Studies
- SR: 17 RCTs; MA: 9 RCTs (mCAF+sCTG vs alternatives)
- Techniques compared: CM (collagen matrix), XADM (xenogeneic acellular dermal matrix), PRF (platelet-rich fibrin), EMD (enamel matrix derivatives), sCTG harvested with double blade scalpel (DBS)

### Primary Outcomes
| Comparison | Outcome | Statistic | p | Evidence Level |
|-----------|---------|-----------|---|----------------|
| mCAF+sCTG vs alternatives | CRC | OR=1.70 (95% CI 1.18–2.44) | 0.004 | Statistically significant |
| mCAF+sCTG vs alternatives | ΔKTW | SMD=0.37 (95% CI 0.11–0.63) | 0.005 | Statistically significant |
| sCTG vs XADM | CRC | — | 0.002 | HIGH |
| sCTG vs XADM | ΔKTW | — | 0.0001 | HIGH |
| sCTG vs CM | ΔKTW | — | 0.0006 | LOW |

### Secondary Outcomes
- **RES**: No statistically significant difference between techniques
- **mCAF+EMD**: LOW evidence suggesting good results, no significant difference from sCTG
- **mCAF+sCTG (DBS harvest)**: LOW evidence, appears equivalent to conventional sCTG

### Long-term Evidence
- Only 1 RCT demonstrated long-term stable CTG results for root coverage

## Clinical Takeaway

For multiple adjacent gingival recession defects, mCAF with subepithelial connective tissue graft (sCTG) remains the gold standard — with high evidence superiority over XADM for both CRC and KTW augmentation. Collagen matrix and xenogeneic substitutes provide inferior root coverage and keratinized tissue outcomes. Enamel matrix derivatives (EMD) and double-blade scalpel sCTG harvesting show early promise and may become alternatives as evidence accumulates. Aesthetic outcomes (RES) do not significantly differ between techniques — the advantage of sCTG is in tissue quantity, not cosmetic appearance.
