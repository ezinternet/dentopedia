## Three-line Summary

Systematic review (EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guidelines supplement, J Clin Periodontol 2020;47 Suppl 22): 7 studies (from 683 screened), 667 patients, 2,021 teeth with class II–III furcation involvement (FI) undergoing resective periodontal surgery compared with SRP or OFD.

Tooth survival: root amputation/resection/separation 38–94.4% (highly variable), tunnelling 62–67%, OFD 63–85%, SRP 68–80%; 1,515 teeth survived 4–30.8 years; class II FI had better outcomes than class III across all treatment types.

SRP and OFD achieve similar tooth survival rates as resective surgery for class II–III FI — no clear superiority for resective approaches over conservative or less-invasive management.

## Summary

This systematic review, produced as part of the EFP S3 2020 Clinical Practice Guidelines for the treatment of periodontitis, is the most comprehensive evaluation of resective periodontal surgery for furcation-involved teeth. Only 7 studies met inclusion criteria from 683 screened, underscoring the limited controlled-study evidence base for this clinical scenario.

**Core finding**: Tooth survival rates for resective surgery (38–94.4% — extremely heterogeneous) overlap with or fall below SRP (68–80%) and OFD (63–85%), particularly for class III FI. Tunnelling (62–67%) clusters near OFD performance. The massive survival range for resective procedures (38–94.4%) reflects the dominance of operator technique, patient maintenance quality, and defect morphology over treatment category per se.

**Class II vs III FI**: Consistently better outcomes with class II regardless of treatment modality — this gradient supports FI degree as the primary decision variable.

**Clinical implication**: For class II FI, conservative treatment (SRP, OFD) can be attempted before escalating to resective surgery. For class III FI, resective surgery's theoretical advantage (facilitating hygiene) is not clearly evidenced in survival data, but patient-specific factors (molar morphology, strategic importance, hygiene ability) still justify individualized decisions.

**Context within EFP S3**: This SR contributes to the EFP S3 weak recommendation that resective surgery can be considered for selected class II–III FI cases after active periodontal therapy fails, not as first-line.

## Clinical Takeaway

Resective periodontal surgery for class II–III FI does not demonstrate clear survival superiority over SRP or OFD. The choice between conservative and resective management should be individualized based on FI degree, tooth strategic value, root morphology, and patient hygiene compliance. Class II FI responds better to any intervention than class III. Data are insufficient to rank resective procedures against each other (root amputation vs. separation vs. tunnelling).
