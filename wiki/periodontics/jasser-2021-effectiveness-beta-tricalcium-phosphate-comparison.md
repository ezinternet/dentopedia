## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (5 studies from 1979–2010, 171 patients / 254 sites, search to March 2020) of beta-tricalcium phosphate (β-TCP) alone or combined versus allograft, autogenous bone, xenograft, hydroxyapatite or debridement alone in periodontal infra-bony defects around natural teeth; only 1 of 5 was randomized.

β-TCP beat debridement alone (PD reduction 6.20 vs 2.95 mm, CAL gain 5.80 vs 2.30 mm in one trial) but was comparable to or slightly below allograft/autograft; pooled two-wall SMD (2 studies per pool, fixed-effect) was PD 1.555, CAL 0.815, bone fill -1.189, but only bone fill survived the random-effect model, and heterogeneity was I² 73-92%.

β-TCP is a plausible alternative where autograft/allograft is unavailable or cost-limited, but the evidence is old, mostly non-randomized, tiny and internally inconsistent; it cannot support a ranking of β-TCP against other grafts.

## Summary

Jasser et al. systematically reviewed whether the resorbable alloplast β-TCP regenerates periodontal infra-bony defects around natural teeth as well as other graft materials. Of 74 records, 5 studies (1979–2010; 171 patients, 254 sites; 4 prospective, 1 retrospective; only Rajesh 2009 randomized) were included, with β-TCP delivered as pure TCP, biphasic 60% HA/40% β-TCP, β-TCP + calcium sulfate, or a calcium phosphate cement. β-TCP outperformed debridement alone and was broadly comparable to other grafts, while autografts and allografts were nominally better, especially in two-wall defects. Meta-analysis pooled only two studies per outcome and gave fixed-effect signals that mostly vanished under random-effect modeling. The authors conclude that β-TCP is a promising alternative but that adequately powered RCTs are required.

## Key Contributions
- Collects the (sparse) periodontal-defect evidence for β-TCP-based alloplasts against allograft, autograft, xenograft, HA and debridement in one table.
- Stratifies pooled analysis by defect morphology (two-wall vs three-wall) with fixed- and random-effect models side by side, exposing how fragile each pooled signal is.
- States the clinical positioning: β-TCP as a fallback where autograft/allograft is unavailable or too costly, and a candidate for combination with other alloplasts or growth factors.

## Methodology
PRISMA-based SR+MA; Cochrane, MEDLINE (PubMed), Embase and Science Citation Index Expanded plus manual search to March 2020, no language restriction; 74 records, 10 full texts, 5 included (kappa 0.91). Outcomes: PD reduction, CAL gain, bone fill (primary); GR, BOP, plaque index (secondary). SMD pooled in MedCalc 15.0 with Cochran's Q and I² (>50% = high); risk of bias assessed on randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, follow-up completeness, baseline similarity and confounding control. Follow-up 12 months (3 studies), 18 months (1), not applicable (1).

## Results

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strub 1979 (n=8) | TCP vs frozen allograft | 1.8 vs 2.0 | — | re-entry 1.2 vs 1.5 |
| Snyder 1984 (n=10) | TCP only, 18 mo | 3.6 | 1.2 | re-entry 2.8 |
| Zafiropoulos 2007 (n=64) | HA/β-TCP+ASB vs ASB vs ASB+BDX | — | 3.2 vs 3.4 vs 3.2 | re-entry 1.6 vs 2.8 vs 1.5 |

| Sukumar 2010 (n=21) | β-TCP/CaSO4 only | 1.98 | 1.68 | — |

Reading notes: (1) the only pooled result significant under both models is two-wall bone fill, where the signed SMD (-1.189) and raw means (β-TCP 1.2 vs 2.4 mm; 6.5 vs 7.7) point toward the comparator although the Results text says "favoring β-TCP" and the abstract says bone fill was not significant; (2) Table 1 labels "CPC" as cetyl pyridinium chloride although the arm is Chitra calcium phosphate cement; (3) the text calls I² of 73-92% "absence of heterogeneity"; (4) the narrative says all studies had low risk of bias while Table 2 marks randomization, blinding and confounder control "No" for most. These are source inconsistencies, reproduced rather than corrected.
