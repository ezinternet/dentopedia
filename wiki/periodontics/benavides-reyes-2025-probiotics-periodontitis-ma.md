## Three-line Summary

Meta-analysis of 24 RCTs (951 participants; 10 gingivitis, 14 periodontitis) evaluating probiotics as adjunct to periodontal therapy; in periodontitis, PI (SMD=−0.35, p=0.001) and BOP (SMD=−0.32, p=0.021) were significantly reduced but PPD non-significant overall.

Time-dependency observed: PI benefit is greater at >8-week follow-up; BOP benefit is confined to ≤12-week follow-up; PPD shows significance only in ≤12-week studies (SMD=−0.42, p=0.019) — suggesting transient rather than sustained probiotic effect on probing depth.

In gingivitis, no parameter reached significance (high heterogeneity I²=94–96%), and overall GRADE evidence quality is low — probiotics provide a measurable but modest benefit in periodontitis specifically for plaque and bleeding outcomes.

## Background

While [[periodontics/mendoca-2024-probiotics-nspt-nma]] provides the definitive network comparison of probiotic strains, this 2025 meta-analysis complements by stratifying effects by disease stage (gingivitis vs. periodontitis) and by follow-up duration, addressing a gap in the NMA's parallel-group-only design. The key question answered here: does the probiotic benefit on PI and BOP differ between early (gingivitis) and established (periodontitis) disease?

## Methods

- **PROSPERO**: CRD42024509910
- **Search**: PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane, EMBASE; March 2024; English only; last 10 years
- **Inclusion**: RCTs, gingivitis or periodontitis patients, probiotic vs control/placebo, ≥9 participants/group, CONSORT-compliant
- **Outcomes**: PI (%), BOP (%), PPD (mm)
- **Statistics**: Hedges formula (SMD), random-effects model (REML); subgroups by follow-up time
- **Risk of bias**: Cochrane RoB-2; GRADE evidence quality

### Evidence Quality
- GRADE: Low overall; "serious" imprecision and risk of bias
- Publication bias: LFK index suggests possible bias for PI/BOP in gingivitis (not confirmed by Egger/Begg)

## Discussion

The finding that PPD is not significantly reduced overall (only in ≤12-week studies) aligns with the biological expectation that probiotics primarily modify the inflammatory milieu and bacterial adhesion (affecting BOP and PI) rather than inducing pocket depth reduction, which requires tissue remodeling beyond what short-term microbial competition can achieve.

The stark contrast between periodontitis (responsive) and gingivitis (non-responsive) may reflect that established periodontitis provides a more robust inflammatory signal where probiotics' immune-modulatory effects have measurable impact, whereas gingivitis-level inflammation is too mild and variable.

## Clinical Takeaway

Probiotics as SRP adjuncts can reduce plaque accumulation (PI) and gingival bleeding (BOP) in periodontitis patients, with effects most pronounced at moderate follow-up (8–12 weeks). Pocket depth benefit is clinically marginal and not sustained. No clear benefit demonstrated for gingivitis patients. Should be interpreted alongside [[periodontics/mendoca-2024-probiotics-nspt-nma]] for strain-specific guidance (*L. reuteri* preferred).
