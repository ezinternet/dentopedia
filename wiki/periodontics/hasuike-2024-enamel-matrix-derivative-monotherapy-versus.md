## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (Jpn Dent Sci Rev 2024; PROSPERO CRD42021281155) of RCTs with 12-month follow-up comparing enamel matrix derivative (EMD) alone versus EMD plus a bone graft in periodontal intrabony defects: 15 articles from 12 studies (autogenous 2, allograft 1, xenograft 3, alloplastic 6), random-effects pooling in RevMan 5.4.1.

Adding a bone graft gave no significant clinical benefit: PD reduction +0.27 mm (95% CI -0.02 to 0.57; I² 60%), CAL gain +0.05 mm (95% CI -0.39 to 0.50; I² 75%), recession 0.16 mm less with combination (95% CI -0.03 to 0.35; I² 44%); only radiographic defect fill improved (+0.80 mm, I² 3%; alloplastic subgroup +0.88 mm, 95% CI 0.62-1.14).

Graft addition to EMD is not supported for clinical attachment outcomes at 12 months, and the two xenograft trials using minimally invasive surgery (MIST) showed no added benefit, suggesting flap design and wound stability may matter more than graft use; evidence is limited by heterogeneity, moderate risk of bias and few trials per graft type.

## 세줄요약

체계적 문헌고찰 + 메타분석 (Systematic Review + Meta-analysis, SR+MA; Jpn Dent Sci Rev 2024): 치주 골내결손에서 12개월 추적 RCT만 포함해 법랑기질유도체 (Enamel Matrix Derivative, EMD) 단독 대 EMD + 골이식재 병용을 비교 (12개 연구·15편; 자가골 2·동종골 1·이종골 3·합성골 6).

병용의 임상적 추가 이득은 없었음: 치주낭 깊이 (Probing Depth, PD) 감소 +0.27 mm (95% CI -0.02~0.57), 임상부착수준 (Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) 획득 +0.05 mm (95% CI -0.39~0.50), 치은퇴축 차이 0.16 mm (비유의); 방사선학적 결손 충만만 +0.80 mm로 유의 (합성골 하위군 +0.88 mm, 95% CI 0.62~1.14).

12개월 CAL 기준으로는 EMD에 골이식재를 더하는 것이 지지되지 않으며, 최소침습수술 (Minimally Invasive Surgical Technique, MIST) 이종골 2건도 추가 이득이 없었다; 다만 이질성·중등도 편향위험·소수 연구가 한계.

## Summary

Hasuike et al. (Jpn Dent Sci Rev 2024) updated the 2011 and 2014 reviews on whether adding a bone graft to enamel matrix derivative (EMD) improves regeneration of periodontal intrabony defects, restricting inclusion to RCTs with a 12-month follow-up. Searches (PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, CENTRAL to October 2021; PubMed updated 18 January 2024) yielded 15 articles from 12 studies. Pooled analyses found no significant difference between EMD alone and EMD plus graft for PD reduction, CAL gain or recession; only radiographic defect fill was significantly greater with combination therapy, driven chiefly by alloplastic grafts. The authors conclude that bone grafts should be used judiciously with EMD and that minimally invasive flap designs that secure primary closure may make grafts unnecessary even in non-contained defects.

## Key Contributions

- Updates the 2011 and 2014 reviews of EMD + graft versus EMD alone, using a uniform 12-month endpoint (earlier reviews mixed 6-24 months).
- No added clinical benefit of grafting on PD, CAL or recession; benefit confined to radiographic defect fill (+0.80 mm overall).
- Graft-type subgroups: alloplastic grafts improved radiographic fill (+0.88 mm); allograft was the only type with a significant CAL subgroup effect; autogenous bone (2 trials) significant for PD only, with wide CIs.
- Flap design matters: the one xenograft trial in conventional flap surgery showed significant PD, CAL and recession differences, whereas MIST/M-MIST xenograft trials did not.
- Cautions on non-resorbable alloplasts (reported root resorption, ankylosis, infection) and slowly resorbing materials that may impede regeneration.

## Methodology

Design: SR+MA following PRISMA; protocol registered in PROSPERO (CRD42021281155). PICOT: adults with periodontitis and intrabony defects after initial therapy; EMD + any bone graft versus EMD alone; outcomes CAL gain, PD change, recession, radiographic defect fill; 12 months. Only RCTs; furcation/supra-osseous defects and EMD + graft versus graft-alone comparisons excluded. Screening: 136 PubMed, 57 Web of Science, 42 Embase, 31 CENTRAL records (+1 by hand search); 155 articles screened in the 2024 update; 34 full texts assessed; 15 articles / 12 studies included. Cochrane risk-of-bias tool; random-effects mean differences (95% CI) in RevMan 5.4.1; I² for heterogeneity; subgroups by graft type (autogenous, allograft, xenograft, alloplastic). Included: 2 autogenous (EMD + AB), 1 three-arm allograft trial (FDBA, DFDBA), 3 xenograft (DBBM; two with MIST/M-MIST/non-incised papilla), 6 alloplastic projects (four with BoneCeramic 60% HA / 40% beta-TCP, one beta-TCP, one bioactive glass; one split-mouth).

## Results

| Outcome (EMD + graft vs EMD alone, 12 mo) | Pooled result | Heterogeneity | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| PD reduction | +0.27 mm (95% CI -0.02 to 0.57) | I² 60% | ns |
| CAL gain | +0.05 mm (95% CI -0.39 to 0.50) | I² 75% | ns |
| Recession (REC; 10 of 12 studies) | EMD alone 0.16 mm more (95% CI -0.03 to 0.35) | I² 44% | ns |
| Radiographic defect fill (6 studies) | +0.80 mm | I² 3% | significant |
| Radiographic fill, alloplastic subgroup | +0.88 mm (95% CI 0.62 to 1.14) | - | significant |

- PD subgroups: autogenous and allograft significant; xenograft significant only in the conventional-flap trial, not in MIST trials. CAL subgroups: only allograft significant (same xenograft flap pattern). Recession subgroups: no significant differences.
- M-MIST + EMD showed no additional radiographic benefit from graft.
- Risk of bias: moderate overall; blinding unclear in all studies; coin-toss randomization rated high risk for sequence generation and allocation concealment.
- Limitations noted by the authors: conceptual heterogeneity (defect morphology, flap design), moderate bias risk, sparse defect-configuration reporting (walls, depth, angle), few trials per graft subgroup. Funding: Sato Fund and Nihon University Dental Research Center grant; no conflicts declared.
