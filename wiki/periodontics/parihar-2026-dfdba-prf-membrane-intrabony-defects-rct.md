## Three-line Summary

Randomized, double-blinded, split-mouth RCT (n=15 patients / 15 paired intrabony defect sites per group, Stage III/IV Grade B/C periodontitis) comparing DFDBA alone (control) vs DFDBA + PRF membrane (test), followed 6 months.

At 6 months, DFDBA+PRF showed significantly greater PPD reduction (3.47±0.61 mm vs 3.91±0.75 mm, p=0.01, moderate effect Cohen's d=0.64) and CAL gain (final CAL 3.24±0.73 mm vs 4.20±0.76 mm, p=0.04, large effect Cohen's d=1.29); plaque and gingival index showed no significant intergroup difference at any timepoint.

Both protocols produced significant within-group improvement in all parameters (p<0.001); the PRF adjunct benefit is real but modest/adjunct-level, not dramatic — limited by small sample size, 6-month-only follow-up, and absence of histologic confirmation.

## Summary

This split-mouth, double-blinded RCT (15 patients, Stage III/IV Grade B/C periodontitis, one pair of contralateral intrabony defects each) compared demineralized freeze-dried bone allograft (DFDBA) alone against DFDBA combined with an autologous platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) membrane in the surgical management of periodontal intrabony defects. Both treatments produced statistically significant improvements in all clinical parameters from baseline to 6 months. The adjunctive PRF group achieved significantly greater probing pocket depth (PPD) reduction and clinical attachment level (CAL) gain than DFDBA alone by 6 months, while plaque index (PI) and gingival index (GI) — reflecting oral-hygiene/inflammatory status rather than regenerative outcome — showed no significant intergroup difference. The magnitude of benefit is modest to moderate for PPD (Cohen's d=0.64) and large for CAL (Cohen's d=1.29), consistent with PRF acting as a beneficial but not transformative adjunct.

## Key Contributions

- Direct split-mouth comparison isolating the specific contribution of a PRF membrane when added to DFDBA (rather than to OFD alone or a generic bone graft), in a well-defined Stage III/IV periodontitis population.
- Provides effect-size estimates (Cohen's d), not just p-values, allowing the magnitude of PRF's regenerative benefit to be judged: moderate for PPD, large for CAL.
- Shows the PRF benefit is confined to the regenerative/attachment parameters and does not manifest as a measurable difference in plaque control or gingival inflammation when both arms have equally rigorous hygiene reinforcement.
- Reinforces at individual-RCT resolution what the larger PRF literature reports in pooled form: adding PRF to a bone graft material produces a real, if modest, additional clinical gain over the graft alone.

## Methodology

- Randomized, double-blind, split-mouth controlled design; CTRI No. CTRI/2024/03/063595; single institution, April 2024–January 2025.
- 15 systemically healthy patients (8 M/7 F, mean age 40±2.2 y), each contributing one DFDBA-alone site and one DFDBA+PRF site (contralateral, paired infrabony defects; PD ≥5 mm, radiographic defect depth ≥4 mm after Phase I therapy).
- Exclusions: systemic disease affecting healing, bone/wound-healing-interfering medications, pregnancy/lactation, any tobacco use, prior periodontal surgery at the site, single-walled defects, furcation involvement.
- PRF prepared via the low-speed centrifugation concept (10 mL venous blood, no anticoagulant, 3000 rpm x 10 min) and compressed into a membrane; placed over the DFDBA graft in the test sites only.
- Full-thickness flap, curette debridement, primary closure with 3-0 resorbable sutures; standardized post-op antibiotics/NSAID/chlorhexidine regimen for both groups.
- Two blinded examiners (not the operating surgeon) recorded PPD and CAL (UNC-15 probe) plus PI/GI at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months.
- Statistics: Mann-Whitney U (between-group), Friedman test (within-group), Bonferroni-corrected significance; sample size 15/group powered at 95% (α=5%) from prior Agarwal 2016 data.

## Results

| Parameter | DFDBA alone, 6mo | DFDBA+PRF, 6mo | Intergroup p | Effect size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPD reduction | 3.91±0.75 mm | 3.47±0.61 mm | 0.01 | Cohen's d=0.64 (moderate) |
| CAL gain (final CAL) | 4.20±0.76 mm | 3.24±0.73 mm | 0.04 | Cohen's d=1.29 (large) |
| GI | 1.38±0.60 | 1.47±0.60 | 0.322 (NS) | — |
| PI | 1.61±0.55 | 1.58±0.49 | 0.887 (NS) | — |

- Both groups improved significantly from baseline for all four parameters (intragroup p<0.001 each); baseline PPD/CAL were comparable between groups (p>0.05).
- The PPD intergroup difference was not yet significant at 3 months (p=0.068), emerging by 6 months — i.e., the PRF advantage consolidates over time rather than appearing immediately.
- No histologic or advanced-imaging confirmation of true regeneration (vs long-junctional-epithelium repair) was performed; conclusions rest on clinical probing measures only.
- Limitations acknowledged by authors: small sample, 6-month-only follow-up, single-center/single-surgeon design, exclusion of smokers and medically compromised patients.
