## Three-line Summary

A 2024 rapid review (8 studies, low risk of bias) commissioned for Health Canada found no clinically significant benefit of routine scaling and polishing (S&P) for gingivitis or pocket depth in low-risk regular dental attenders over 3 years, but observational data link regular scaling to significantly reduced tooth loss (HR 1.87 without scaling, Lee 2019) and lower AMI incidence (HR 0.543 with regular scaling, Kao 2021). For adults with treated periodontitis, the EFP S3 guideline issues a Grade A strong recommendation for supportive periodontal therapy (SPT) at 3–12 month risk-based intervals, with high-risk patients requiring 3-monthly SPT and lower-risk patients 6–12 monthly, supported by evidence from 17 prospective cohorts showing reduced tooth loss and minimal CAL progression over ≥5 years. No studies were identified for adults with inequitable access to dental care, leaving a critical evidence gap for Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) policy.

## Summary

Matthews and Al-Waeli (2024) conducted a rapid review commissioned by Health Canada to inform the rollout of the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP), examining clinical effectiveness and optimal frequency of dental S&P for adults including low-income populations. The review updates [[lamont-2018-routine-scale-and-polish-periodontal-health]] with 2018–2023 evidence and incorporates EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guideline recommendations.

The review examined three population groups:
1. **All adults** (no/early periodontitis) who are regular dental attenders
2. **Adults with treated periodontitis** (SPT population)
3. **Adults with inequitable access** — no studies identified (critical CDCP policy gap)

### Included Studies

| Study | Design | N | Follow-up | Population | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarkson 2021 (IQuaD) | Cluster RCT | 1,877 randomized; 1,327 analyzed | 3 years | All adults; 63 UK practices | No significant diff. in gingival inflammation across 6-mo, 12-mo, no S&P groups |
| Lamont 2018 | Cochrane SR | Multiple RCTs | Variable | All adults | Little/no diff. gingivitis, PPD; small calculus reduction; patients value scaling |
| Lee 2019 | Korean cohort | 557 (40–75y) | 3 years | All adults | Tooth loss HR 1.87 (95%CI 1.03–3.38) higher without regular scaling |
| Kao 2021 | Taiwan claims | 14,328 (50–64y) | 13 years | All adults | AMI 3.5% (no scaling) vs 1.9% (regular scaling); HR 0.543 (95%CI 0.441–0.670) |
| Manresa 2018 | Cochrane SR | 0 eligible RCTs | — | SPT population | No eligible RCTs for SPT vs no SPT or different SPT intervals |
| EFP S3 Guideline | Clinical practice guideline (SR of 17 cohorts) | Large | ≥5 years | SPT population | Grade A: SPT reduces tooth loss, minimizes CAL; risk-based 3–12 mo intervals |
| Smits 2020 | Netherlands claims | 937,704 | 7 years | Diabetes + SPT | SPT → €8.04/quarter reduction in healthcare costs |
| Kao 2021 (costs) | Taiwan claims | 14,328 | 13 years | All adults | Annual costs lower with regular scaling group |

## Key Contributions

1. **No clinically significant S&P benefit for gingivitis/PPD** in low-risk regular attenders over 3 years — confirms and updates Lamont 2018
2. **Tooth loss and AMI risk reduction** with regular scaling (observational evidence; cannot establish causality alone)
3. **Grade A recommendation synthesized**: EFP S3 guideline establishes risk-based SPT at 3–12 month intervals as standard of care for treated periodontitis
4. **Equity gap identified**: zero studies for adults with inequitable access — major CDCP policy limitation
5. **Cost-benefit evidence**: 6-monthly S&P + personalized OHI = largest incremental net benefit (WTP analysis); SPT reduces healthcare costs in diabetic patients

## Methodology

- **Type**: Rapid review; updated search of databases covering 2018–2023, building on Lamont 2018 Cochrane SR
- **Included studies**: 8 (4 for all adults; 4 for SPT population); all rated low risk of bias
- **Limitations**: Not a full systematic review; no meta-analysis; publication bias not assessed; no studies for equity-focused populations

## Results

### Population 1: All Adults (No/Early Periodontitis)

The IQuaD trial (Clarkson 2021), the largest and highest-quality RCT, randomized 1,877 adults across 63 UK practices to 6-monthly S&P, 12-monthly S&P, or no S&P over 3 years. There was **no statistically or clinically significant difference** between groups for gingival inflammation regardless of interval. Patients in scaling groups reported higher self-perceived oral cleanliness.

Lamont 2018 Cochrane SR (the foundational evidence) similarly found little or no difference in gingivitis or PPD between S&P and no S&P, with only a small reduction in calculus.

However, observational data show long-term systemic benefits:
- **Tooth loss**: HR 1.87 (95% CI 1.03–3.38) higher without regular scaling (Lee 2019, Korean cohort, 3 years)
- **AMI incidence**: 3.5% without scaling vs 1.9% with regular scaling; HR 0.543 (95% CI 0.441–0.670) (Kao 2021, Taiwan, 13 years)

### Population 2: Adults with Treated Periodontitis (SPT)

Manresa 2018 Cochrane SR found no eligible RCTs for SPT vs monitoring-only or different SPT intervals. The evidence base for SPT thus relies on prospective cohort data synthesized in the EFP S3 guideline.

### EFP S3 Clinical Practice Guideline Recommendations for SPT

| Recommendation | Evidence Grade | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Supragingival 전문적 기계적 치태제거 (Professional Mechanical Plaque Removal, PMPR) in Step 1 therapy | Grade A | Stage I–IV periodontitis; all patients |
| Regular SPT reduces tooth loss long-term | Grade A | ≥5 years; SR of 17 prospective cohorts |
| Regular SPT minimizes clinical attachment loss (Clinical Attachment Loss, CAL) progression | Grade A | Long-term data; no evidence of disadvantages (recession/CAL loss) |
| SPT intervals: 3–12 months, risk-tailored | Grade A | High-risk 3-monthly; lower-risk 6–12 monthly |
| Repeated individualized 구강위생교육 (Oral Hygiene Instruction, OHI) at every SPT visit | Grade A | Component of SPT protocol |

**Risk profile factors for SPT interval determination**: smoking status, HbA1c (glycemic control), remaining PPD ≥6mm sites, bleeding on probing (BoP) >30%

### Population 3: Adults with Inequitable Access
No studies identified. This represents a critical evidence gap for CDCP policy, which targets low-income populations.

### Cost-Benefit Summary
- 6-monthly S&P with personalized OHI: largest incremental net benefit when health + non-health costs included (willingness-to-pay analysis)
- SPT in diabetes patients: €8.04 reduction in healthcare costs per quarter (Smits 2020, Netherlands claims, n=937,704)
- Long-term savings from reduced tooth loss and cardiac events offset S&P costs
