## Three-line Summary

Prospective pre-clinical training study at the University of Zurich (3 student cohorts, n=41/34/48) comparing hand-scaling performance before and immediately after an 8.5-hour manual instrumentation course, against experienced-hygienist and layperson controls on a typodont model.

Baseline student scores (29.8-42% tooth surface cleaned) resembled laypeople (27.9%), but post-training scores (61.7-79.5%) improved significantly (P<.001) and were statistically indistinguishable from experienced hygienists (67.4%).

A shortened 8.5-hour pre-clinical course was sufficient to bring novice students to expert-level hand-scaling proficiency, though the outcome is simulation-based (typodont, not live patients) and only the abstract was retrievable.

## 세줄요약

취리히대학교(University of Zurich) 치과대학생 3개 코호트(n=41, 34, 48) 대상 전향적(prospective) 사전임상 술기 연구 — 8.5시간 수기 스케일링(hand scaling) 교육 전후를 비교하고, 숙련 치위생사·비전문가 대조군을 타이포돈트(typodont) 모형에서 함께 측정.

교육 전 학생 점수(치면 세정률 29.8-42%)는 비전문가군(27.9%)과 유사했으나, 교육 후 61.7-79.5%로 유의하게 향상되어(P<.001) 숙련 치위생사군(67.4%)과 통계적으로 유의한 차이가 없었음.

8.5시간의 단축된 사전임상 교육만으로도 학생들이 숙련자 수준의 수기 스케일링(hand scaling) 술기를 습득할 수 있음을 시사하나, 타이포돈트 모형 기반 결과이며 초록만 확보되어 방법론 세부사항은 제한적.

## Summary

Following the Bologna-reform restructuring of pre-clinical curricula at the University of Zurich, this prospective study evaluated whether a shortened 8.5-hour manual training course was sufficient for undergraduate dental students to acquire basic hand-scaling/root-planing competency. Three consecutive cohorts of students (n=41, 34, 48) attempted to remove lacquer concrement from a typodont's maxillary left canine using Gracey and universal (Deppeler M23A) curettes, first at baseline (before any instruction) and again immediately after training; eight experienced dental hygienists and eight laypeople served as positive and negative controls, respectively, using the same instruments and time limit. At baseline, student performance (29.8-42% of tooth surface cleaned) did not differ significantly from laypeople (27.9%), but after training students improved significantly to 61.7-79.5%, statistically indistinguishable from the experienced hygienists' 67.4% (P<.001 vs baseline). The authors conclude that a shortened pre-clinical training period is sufficient for students to acquire the basic scaling/root-planing skills needed before entering clinical training.

## Key Contributions

- Quantifies dental-student hand-scaling skill acquisition after a shortened (8.5-hour) pre-clinical training course, benchmarked against experienced-hygienist (positive control) and layperson (negative control) performance.
- Shows post-training student performance (percentage of tooth surface cleaned, by planimetric analysis) is statistically indistinguishable from experienced dental hygienists.
- Supports the adequacy of reduced pre-clinical training time following Bologna-reform curriculum restructuring, a live curricular-design question for dental schools.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Prospective, controlled, pre/post skill-acquisition study (educational cohort design; no randomization stated).
- **Participants**: 3 consecutive classes of undergraduate dental students (n=41, 34, 48); 8 experienced dental hygienists (positive control); 8 laypeople (negative control).
- **Task**: Timed 5-minute scaling/root-planing session removing lacquer concrement from the maxillary left canine on a typodont, using Gracey and universal (Deppeler M23A) curettes.
- **Timing**: Baseline session prior to any instruction; second session immediately following 8.5 hours of manual pre-clinical training.
- **Outcome measurement**: Instrumented teeth collected, scanned, and planimetrically analyzed for the percentage of tooth surface cleaned.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Baseline student scaling score (% surface cleaned, 3 cohorts) | 29.8% / 31.0% / 42% |
| Baseline layperson score (negative control) | 27.9% (not significantly different from students) |
| Post-training student scaling score (3 cohorts) | 61.7% / 79.5% / 76% |
| Experienced-hygienist score (positive control) | 67.4% |
| Student improvement, baseline vs post-training | P < .001 |
| Post-training students vs experienced hygienists | No statistically significant difference |
