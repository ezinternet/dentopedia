## Three-line Summary

Two-arm RCT (n=120 adults, moderate-to-severe periodontal disease, multi-center India + Saudi Arabia) comparing traditional periodontal surgery (flap surgery/grafts, by periodontal surgeons) against non-surgical therapy (scaling and root planing, by trained dental hygienists), with clinical (pocket depth, attachment level) and patient-reported (pain, discomfort, satisfaction) outcomes at baseline, 3, and 6 months.

Per the tables and the PubMed-indexed abstract, pocket-depth reduction was larger with surgery (6.8→3.7 mm, Δ−3.1 mm) than non-surgical therapy (6.7→4.0 mm, Δ−2.7 mm), and attachment-level gain likewise favored surgery (Δ−2.1 vs Δ−1.6 mm); non-surgical therapy scored lower pain (2.1 vs 3.6/10) and discomfort (2.9 vs 4.2/10) with similar satisfaction (8.4 vs 7.8/10).

Key limitation: the PMC full text is internally inconsistent — its own narrative Results paragraphs cite a *different* set of numbers than Tables 1–3 (which are themselves captioned "arbitrary and for illustrative purposes only"), and the Methods section leaves template placeholders unfilled — treat precise point-estimates with caution.

## 세줄요약

2군 무작위 대조군 연구(Randomized Controlled Trial, RCT) (성인 120명, 중등도-중증 치주질환, 인도·사우디아라비아 다기관) — 전통적 치주수술(flap surgery/graft, 치주전문의 시행) 대 비외과적 치료(스케일링·치근활택술, Scaling and Root Planing, SRP, 훈련된 치위생사 시행) 비교, 임상지표(치주낭 깊이, 임상부착수준)와 환자보고지표(통증·불편감·만족도)를 기저·3개월·6개월에 측정.

표 및 PubMed 등재 초록 기준으로 치주낭 깊이(Periodontal Pocket Depth, PPD) 감소는 수술군에서 더 큼(6.8→3.7mm, Δ−3.1mm) 대 비외과군(6.7→4.0mm, Δ−2.7mm), 임상부착수준(Clinical Attachment Level, CAL) 개선도 수술군 우세(Δ−2.1 대 Δ−1.6mm); 비외과군은 통증(2.1 대 3.6/10)·불편감(2.9 대 4.2/10)이 낮고 만족도(8.4 대 7.8/10)는 유사.

핵심 한계 — PMC 전문 자체가 내적으로 불일치함: 본문 서술문 수치가 Table 1–3 수치와 다르고(표는 "arbitrary and for illustrative purposes only"로 명시), Methods 절에 템플릿 placeholder가 미기재로 남아있어 정확한 수치는 주의해서 다룰 것.

## Summary

This RCT (n=120 adults with moderate-to-severe periodontal disease, recruited across multiple Indian institutions plus one Saudi Arabian site) randomized patients to traditional periodontal surgery (flap surgery and grafts, performed by periodontal surgeons) or non-surgical therapy (scaling and root planing, performed by trained dental hygienists using ultrasonic and hand instruments), then tracked pocket depth, clinical attachment level, and patient-reported pain/discomfort/satisfaction over 6 months. Both arms improved substantially on clinical parameters; per the article's tables and its PubMed-indexed abstract, the surgical arm achieved a numerically larger reduction in pocket depth (Δ−3.1 vs Δ−2.7 mm) and larger attachment-level gain (Δ−2.1 vs Δ−1.6 mm), while the non-surgical arm was better tolerated (lower pain and discomfort) with similar patient satisfaction. Clinically, this supports treating hygienist-delivered SRP as an effective, better-tolerated first-line option, with surgery offering a modest additional clinical margin — consistent with the treatment hierarchy described in [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]] (non-surgical first, surgery adjunctive for residual pockets). A significant caveat limits how much weight to place on the precise numbers: the paper's own narrative Results text reports a different set of PPD/CAL/patient-outcome values than its own tables, the tables are captioned as "arbitrary...for illustrative purposes only," and the Methods section leaves several template placeholders unfilled — hallmarks of a very short, minimally-copyedited supplement article rather than a fully reported RCT.

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head RCT of traditional periodontal surgery versus dental-hygienist-delivered non-surgical SRP in moderate-to-severe periodontal disease, combining clinical parameters with patient-reported outcomes.
- Quantifies an efficacy-vs-comfort trade-off: surgery shows the larger reduction in pocket depth/attachment-level loss; non-surgical therapy shows lower pain and discomfort with comparable satisfaction.
- Adds patient-centered outcome data (pain, discomfort, satisfaction) to a treatment-hierarchy question that older syntheses framed mainly around clinical parameters.

## Methodology

- Two-arm parallel RCT; blinded outcome examiners.
- 120 adults with moderate-to-severe periodontal disease; informed consent obtained; randomization method/allocation concealment not described in available text.
- TSG (traditional surgery group): flap surgery + grafts as indicated, by periodontal surgeons.
- NSTG (non-surgical therapy group): scaling and root planing (ultrasonic + hand instruments), by trained dental hygienists.
- Outcomes at baseline, 3 months, 6 months: periodontal pocket depth (mm), clinical attachment level (mm), and standardized-questionnaire pain/discomfort/satisfaction (1–10 scale).
- Statistics: means/SD, independent t-tests, Chi-square; α = 0.05; no CONSORT diagram, effect sizes/CIs, or sample-size calculation reported in the available text.

## Results

Values reproduce Tables 1–3 (which match the PubMed-indexed abstract). The article's own narrative Results paragraphs give a different, inconsistent set of numbers for the same outcomes — see the [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/vadvadgi-2024-comparing-effectiveness-traditional-periodontal|source page]] Limitations for both sets and further data-quality caveats.

| Outcome | TSG (surgery) | NSTG (non-surgical) |
|---|---|---|
| PPD baseline → 6 mo | 6.8 → 3.7 mm (Δ −3.1 mm) | 6.7 → 4.0 mm (Δ −2.7 mm) |
| CAL baseline → 6 mo | 7.2 → 5.1 mm (Δ −2.1 mm) | 7.1 → 5.5 mm (Δ −1.6 mm) |
| Pain (1–10) | 3.6 | 2.1 |
| Discomfort (1–10) | 4.2 | 2.9 |
| Satisfaction (1–10) | 7.8 | 8.4 |

No between-group p-values or confidence intervals are reported despite the Methods stating t-tests/Chi-square were used. The PubMed abstract's conclusion states surgery produced the greater reduction in pocket depth and attachment loss; the paper's own Discussion/Conclusion instead emphasizes both treatments as "effective" without stating surgical superiority explicitly.

## Related Papers

- [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/unknown-2009-non-surgical-and-surgical-periodontal]] — this 2009 narrative review establishes the treatment hierarchy (non-surgical therapy first-line, surgery adjunctive for residual pockets, similar long-term outcomes in many cases). Vadvadgi 2024 provides RCT-level empirical support for "both approaches improve clinical parameters substantially," and *extends* the older synthesis with quantified patient-reported comfort/satisfaction data the 2009 review did not cover — while also suggesting (per its tables/abstract) a modest edge for surgery on hard clinical endpoints that the 2009 review's "similar outcomes" framing did not sharply quantify.
- [[periodontics/non-surgical-instrumentation/lamont-2018-routine-scale-and-polish-periodontal-health]] — topically adjacent (hygienist-delivered scaling) but a different question: Lamont 2018 is a Cochrane review of *routine* prophylactic scale-and-polish (no root planing) in healthy, low-risk adults without severe periodontitis, finding little effect on gingival health; Vadvadgi 2024 studies *therapeutic* scaling and root planing in patients already diagnosed with moderate-to-severe periodontal disease, where both this trial and the broader literature show clear clinical benefit. Not a direct comparator — included here only to distinguish "routine maintenance cleaning" from "therapeutic SRP for active disease," a distinction relevant to the dental-hygienist-scaling topic sweep this page was ingested under.
