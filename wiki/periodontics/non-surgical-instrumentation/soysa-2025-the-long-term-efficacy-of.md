## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs (52 articles = 43 independent studies; 33 articles in the meta-analysis; ≥6-month follow-up; PROSPERO CRD42021247300) of locally delivered tetracycline-class sustained-release adjuncts (tetracycline fiber, doxycycline gel, minocycline microspheres/ointment) added to scaling and root planing (SRP) in chronic periodontitis, including a smokers vs non-smokers subgroup.

Adjunct benefit was modest but significant: PPD WMD 0.516 mm at 6–9 months and 0.371 mm at ≥12 months; CAL WMD 0.336 mm and 0.310 mm; split-mouth designs inflated effects (meta-regression β=0.422 for PPD). Egger's test showed significant publication bias for PPD and CAL, and most RCTs were at unclear/high risk of bias.

The abstract's claim that non-smokers gained more than smokers long-term (PPD 0.630 vs 0.112 mm) is not supported by the paper's own meta-regression (smoking moderator P=0.0513 for long-term PPD, P=0.566 for CAL; both P>0.05 at 6–9 months), so smoking-specific conclusions are exploratory.

## 세줄요약

SR+MA(RCT 52편 = 독립 연구 43개, 메타분석 33편, 추적 ≥6개월, PROSPERO CRD42021247300): 만성 치주염에서 치석제거·치근활택(SRP)에 국소 서방형 테트라사이클린계(테트라사이클린 파이버·독시사이클린 겔·미노사이클린 마이크로스피어/연고)를 추가한 효과를 평가하고 흡연자 대 비흡연자 하위분석 포함.

추가 효과는 유의하나 작음: 치주낭 깊이(PPD) WMD 6–9개월 0.516 mm·≥12개월 0.371 mm, 임상 부착 수준(CAL) 0.336 mm·0.310 mm; 분할구강(split-mouth) 설계가 효과를 키움(메타회귀 PPD β=0.422); Egger 검정에서 PPD·CAL 출판편향 유의, 대부분 RCT가 비뚤림 위험 불명확/높음.

초록의 "비흡연자가 장기적으로 더 큰 이득(PPD 0.630 vs 0.112 mm)"은 논문 자체 메타회귀(흡연 조절변수 장기 PPD P=0.0513, CAL P=0.566)로 뒷받침되지 않으므로 흡연 관련 결론은 탐색적 수준이다.

## Summary

Soysa et al. (2025, University of Peradeniya) pooled RCTs of sustained-release tetracycline-class local antimicrobials added to SRP in chronic periodontitis: 52 articles (43 independent studies, 17 split-mouth and 26 parallel; 33 articles meta-analyzed). The adjunct gave a statistically significant but small additional PPD reduction (0.516 mm at 6–9 months, 0.371 mm at ≥12 months) and CAL gain (0.336 and 0.310 mm), plus small BOP/PI/GI improvements at both time points. Minocycline (notably minocycline ointment) was the most consistent drug across outcomes and durations; doxycycline's PPD effect was no longer significant at ≥12 months. Heterogeneity was high (I² up to 79%), most trials were at unclear/high risk of bias, and Egger's test indicated publication bias for PPD and CAL, so the pooled estimates likely overstate the true benefit. The smoking analysis rests on 2–4 comparisons per stratum, and the abstract's "non-smokers did better (P<0.05)" statement conflicts with the paper's own meta-regression.

## Key Contributions
- Drug-class-specific (tetracycline, doxycycline, minocycline) and product-level (Actisite, Atridox, Ligosan, Arestin, Dentomycin, minocycline ointment) pooled estimates with a ≥12-month stratum.
- Quantifies a small adjunct effect (~0.3–0.5 mm PPD/CAL) consistent with other local-antimicrobial SRs, and adds secondary outcomes (BOP, PI, GI).
- Split-mouth design is the only significant moderator (inflates PPD/CAL effect); treatment phase and smoking status were not significant moderators.
- Flags a gap: no dedicated furcation-lesion evidence for local tetracyclines.

## Methodology
PRISMA 2020, PROSPERO CRD42021247300; PubMed, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scopus, Embase to Dec 2024, English RCTs, ≥6-month follow-up, adults with chronic periodontitis, SRP + local tetracycline/doxycycline/minocycline vs SRP ± vehicle. Systemic antimicrobials, local monotherapy, non–sustained-release vehicles and diabetic-only studies excluded. WMD of baseline-to-endpoint change (r=0.5 imputed), random-effects, subgroup analysis, meta-regression, Egger + trim-and-fill, leave-one-out sensitivity, Cochrane RoB tool.
