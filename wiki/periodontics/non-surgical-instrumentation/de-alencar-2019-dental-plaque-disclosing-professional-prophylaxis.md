## Three-line Summary

Randomized crossover clinical trial (n=25 children aged 18–36 months, ECC-prevention program, Bauru/São Paulo, Brazil) comparing professional dental prophylaxis with prior 1% malachite green plaque disclosing (GI) versus without disclosing (GII), after a 1-month washout.

Disclosing produced significantly better plaque removal on smooth surfaces (labial/buccal) and occlusal surfaces (Wilcoxon test; lingual/palatal p>0.05) and a significantly shorter prophylaxis duration (391.27 s ±142.2 vs 436.51 s ±128.4), with an inverse plaque-index–duration correlation present only in GI.

Staining before prophylaxis makes professional cleaning in infants more effective and less time-consuming, but access to lingual/palatal surfaces remains the limiting step, and the low baseline plaque index (1.49 smooth, 1.77 occlusal) limits generalizability to higher-caries-risk children.

## 세줄요약

무작위 교차 임상시험(18~36개월 아동 25명, 우식조기예방(ECC) 프로그램, Bauru/상파울루) — 전문가 치아 prophylaxis 전 1% 말라카이트 그린(malachite green) 치태 착색군(GI)과 미착색 대조군(GII)을 1개월 washout 후 비교.

착색군(GI)이 평활면(순협면) 및 교합면에서 치태 제거가 유의하게 우수했고(윌콕슨 검정; 설면/구개면은 p>0.05), prophylaxis 시간도 유의하게 짧았으며(391.27초 ±142.2 vs 436.51초 ±128.4), 치태지수–술식시간 역상관은 GI에서만 관찰됨.

유아 전문가 prophylaxis 전 치태 착색은 제거 효과를 높이고 술식 시간을 줄이지만, 설면/구개면 접근성이 여전히 한계이며 낮은 기저 치태지수(평활면 1.49, 교합면 1.77)로 고위험 아동에의 일반화는 제한적임.

## Summary

This randomized crossover clinical trial (n=25 children aged 18–36 months in an early-childhood-caries prevention program at Bauru, University of São Paulo) tested whether disclosing dental plaque before professional prophylaxis improves the procedure. Each child was evaluated under both conditions — GI, with 1% malachite green disclosing before prophylaxis, and GII, prophylaxis without disclosing — separated by a 1-month washout, with a single operator, a blinded examiner scoring Greene and Vermillion (smooth) and Mestrinho (occlusal) plaque indices, and a third dentist timing the procedure. Plaque removal was significantly better with prior disclosing on smooth (labial/buccal) and occlusal surfaces, but not on lingual/palatal surfaces (p>0.05), and the procedure was significantly faster with disclosing (391.27 s ±142.2 vs 436.51 s ±128.4). Only in the disclosing group was a longer procedure inversely correlated with residual plaque, i.e. time was spent productively. The authors conclude that plaque disclosing in young children is an effective, less time-consuming auxiliary that improves the quality of professional prophylaxis; the low baseline plaque index and small single-center sample limit generalizability.

## Key Contributions

- First study to evaluate plaque disclosing as a technique aid for professional dental prophylaxis in early childhood, rather than only as an education/motivation tool.
- Demonstrates statistically better plaque removal with prior disclosing on smooth (labial/buccal) and occlusal surfaces, but not on lingual/palatal surfaces.
- Shows prior disclosing significantly shortens prophylaxis time (391.27 vs 436.51 s) despite adding a staining step — a chair-time saving.
- Shows that without disclosing, a longer procedure does not translate into cleaner teeth (no plaque–duration correlation in GII), whereas disclosing makes procedure time productive.
- Confirms child behavior (crying/fear) did not affect plaque-removal outcomes under an infant-specialist operator.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Randomized crossover clinical trial; each child assessed under both conditions after a 1-month washout.
- **Population**: 25 children aged 18–36 months (convenience sample from the Baby Clinic ECC-prevention program); inclusion = no caries lesions and at least 4 fully erupted teeth.
- **Arms**: GI = 1% malachite green disclosing before prophylaxis; GII = prophylaxis without disclosing; allocation by opaque envelope (#1/#2).
- **Protocol**: teeth dried with gauze, stained with 1% malachite green (cotton swabs); prophylaxis with low-speed contra-angle handpiece, bristle brush, prophylactic paste. Plaque indices recorded by a blinded examiner who stayed outside the room; a third dentist timed the procedure with a digital stopwatch.
- **Indices**: Greene and Vermillion (smooth surfaces: labial/buccal and lingual/palatal) and Mestrinho, Carvalho and Figueiredo (occlusal surfaces).
- **Calibration**: intraexaminer agreement κ = 87% (10 children).
- **Outcomes**: residual plaque index by surface; prophylaxis duration (seconds); operator-rated difficulty (mild/major); child behavior (positive/negative).
- **Statistics**: Wilcoxon (plaque by surface), Mann–Whitney (behavior), Spearman (plaque vs duration/difficulty); α = 5%.

## Results

| Outcome | With disclosing (GI) | Without disclosing (GII) |
|---|---|---|
| Prophylaxis duration (s) | 391.27 ±142.2 | 436.51 ±128.4 |
| Smooth-surface plaque removal (Wilcoxon) | significantly greater | reference |
| Labial/buccal surfaces | significantly greater | reference |
| Lingual/palatal surfaces | no significant difference (p>0.05) | reference |
| Occlusal-surface plaque removal (Table 1) | significantly greater | reference |
| Plaque index vs duration (Spearman) | inverse — smooth p=0.024, occlusal p=0.037 | none — smooth p=0.235, occlusal p=0.965 |
| Difficulty vs occlusal plaque (Spearman) | positive, p=0.0376 | positive, p=0.000 |
| Behavior effect on plaque index (Mann–Whitney) | none (p>0.05) | none (p>0.05) |

- Baseline habitual plaque index: **1.49 (±0.86)** smooth surfaces; **1.77 (±0.65)** occlusal surfaces.
- Smooth surfaces: GI significantly better than GII (combined and labial/buccal); lingual/palatal no difference.
- Occlusal surfaces: significant difference favoring GI (residual-score distribution 0/0/1 vs 1/0/1 across 126 surfaces).
- Longer duration → less residual plaque only in GI; difficulty correlated with residual occlusal plaque in both groups.
