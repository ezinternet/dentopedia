## Three-line Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 RCTs comparing different doses and durations of adjunctive amoxicillin plus metronidazole (AMX/MET) to full-mouth scaling and root planing (FMSRP) in periodontitis, with primary outcome of clinical attachment level (CAL) gain.

Higher dose (500/500 mg) AMX/MET produced significantly greater CAL gain and probing pocket depth (PPD) reduction at 3 and 6 months compared with lower doses; duration of treatment (3–10 days) did not significantly affect outcomes.

Evidence supports 500/500 mg as the optimal dose for adjunctive AMX/MET with FMSRP; duration recommendation requires further evidence.

## 세줄요약

11편 RCT SR+MA: FMSRP(전악 치석제거·치근활택) 보조 아목시실린(AMX) + 메트로니다졸(MET) 최적 용량·기간 비교, 주결과: 임상부착수준(CAL) 획득.

고용량(500/500 mg) AMX/MET가 3·6개월 CAL 획득·치주낭깊이(PPD) 감소에서 저용량 대비 유의하게 우월; 복용 기간(3–10일) 간 차이는 미미.

FMSRP 보조요법으로 500/500 mg AMX/MET가 6개월 안정 효과를 위한 최적 용량으로 지지됨; 기간 권고안은 추가 근거 필요.

## Summary

Zhao et al. (2021) conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 RCTs to determine the optimal dose and duration of adjunctive amoxicillin plus metronidazole (AMX/MET) when combined with full-mouth scaling and root planing (FMSRP) for periodontitis. The primary outcome was clinical attachment level (CAL) gain, with secondary outcomes of probing pocket depth (PPD) reduction and adverse events. Results showed that the high-dose combination (500/500 mg AMX/MET) produced significantly greater CAL gain and PPD reduction at 3 months, with benefits persisting stably at 6 months. In contrast, comparisons across treatment durations (3, 7, and 10 days) showed minimal differences — no conclusion on optimal duration could be drawn due to limited evidence. Adverse event risk difference between AMX/MET and FMSRP-alone groups was minimal.

## Key Contributions
- Establishes 500/500 mg as the evidence-supported dose for AMX/MET adjunct periodontal therapy
- Demonstrates no significant benefit from extending treatment duration beyond 3 days
- Confirms 6-month stability of clinical attachment gain with high-dose AMX/MET + FMSRP
- Minimal between-group adverse event risk difference supports safety of high-dose regimen

## Methodology
- SR+MA following PRISMA; 11 RCTs eligible
- Databases: 4 electronic databases + manual search of 4 journals
- Inclusion: RCTs comparing AMX/MET adjunct to FMSRP vs FMSRP alone, ≥3 months follow-up
- Primary outcome: CAL gain; Secondary: PPD reduction, adverse events
- Subgroup analysis by dose (high vs low) and duration (3 vs 7 vs 10 days)

## Results
| Comparison | Outcome | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| High dose (500/500 mg) vs lower | CAL gain at 3 mo | Significant |
| High dose (500/500 mg) vs lower | PPD reduction at 3 mo | Significant |
| High dose (500/500 mg) vs lower | CAL gain at 6 mo | Stable, significant |
| 3 vs 7 vs 10 day duration | CAL gain | No significant difference |
| Adverse events | Risk difference | Minimal between groups |
