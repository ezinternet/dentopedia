## One-line Summary
EFP 20th Workshop consensus (70 experts, 21 countries, 2025): manual probing is the reference standard; CBCT not routine; biomarkers and AI show promise but lack clinical diagnostic validation.

## 한줄요약
EFP 20차 워크숍 컨센서스(2025): 수기 탐침이 진단 기준 표준으로 확립, CBCT 일상 사용 불가, 바이오마커·AI는 유망하나 임상 진단 검증 미흡.

## Three-line Summary

European Federation of Periodontology consensus report (J Clin Periodontol 2025;52 Suppl 29:4–33) built on 8 commissioned systematic reviews across 3 working groups with 70 participants, appraising traditional diagnostics, biomarkers and emerging technologies for periodontal diagnosis.

Manual 6-point probing was reaffirmed as the reference standard (error ±1–2 mm) with bleeding on probing carrying the best negative predictive value for stability; CBCT was judged not indicated for routine periodontal diagnosis (selective use for intra-bony defects and furcation involvement); microbial, host-derived and genetic biomarkers were classed as exploratory without validated thresholds; and AI systems, while performing at least as well as experts on image analysis, remain Class I/II diagnostic aids with no independent peer-reviewed validation.

Nothing yet displaces the periodontal probe, so new diagnostic technology should be adopted as an adjunct rather than a replacement — and note that the report was funded by Kenvue, a consumer oral-care company, a declared potential conflict for recommendations touching consumer diagnostics.

## 세줄요약

유럽치주학회 (European Federation of Periodontology, EFP) 합의보고서 (J Clin Periodontol 2025;52 Suppl 29:4–33): 3개 작업그룹·참가자 70명이 의뢰한 체계적 문헌고찰 8편을 토대로 치주 진단의 전통적 방법·바이오마커·신기술을 평가.

수동 6점 탐침 (Manual Probing)이 기준표준 (Reference Standard)으로 재확인됐고(오차 ±1~2 mm), 탐침시 출혈 (Bleeding on Probing, BOP)은 치주 안정성에 대한 음성예측도가 가장 높았다. CBCT는 **일상적 치주 진단에 비적응증**(골내낭·치근이개부에 선택적 사용), 미생물·숙주유래·유전 바이오마커는 검증된 역치가 없는 탐색 단계, 인공지능 (Artificial Intelligence, AI)은 영상 판독에서 전문가에 준하는 성능을 내지만 여전히 Class I/II 진단보조 기기이며 독립적 동료심사 검증이 없다.

아직 **치주탐침을 대체하는 것은 없다** — 신기술은 대체가 아니라 보조로 도입해야 한다. 다만 이 보고서는 소비자 구강관리 기업 Kenvue의 재정 지원을 받았고, 소비자용 진단에 걸리는 권고에서는 이해상충 가능성이 선언돼 있다.

## Summary
The 20th European Workshop on Periodontology (EFP, 2025) produced this consensus report evaluating contemporary and emerging technologies in periodontal diagnosis. Seventy experts from 21 countries, supported by 8 commissioned systematic reviews across 3 working groups, examined traditional diagnostics (WG1), biomarkers (WG2), and emerging technologies including AI (WG3). The report reaffirms manual periodontal probing as the reference standard and 2D periapical radiography as first-line imaging. CBCT is not recommended for routine use but has selective value for intra-bony and furcation defects. Biomarker research (microbial, host-derived, genetic) remains predominantly exploratory with insufficient diagnostic validation. AI-assisted interpretation performs comparably to expert clinicians for radiographic and photographic analysis, but no AI tool has been independently validated for stand-alone diagnostic use.

## Key Contributions
- Formally reaffirms **manual periodontal probing** as the reference standard after evidence review (2018 classification era update)
- Rules out **CBCT for routine periodontal diagnosis** — ionising radiation and cost–benefit ratio do not support it; reserved for selective complex cases
- Characterizes the **biomarker evidence base as exploratory** — microbial thresholds unstable, host-derived biomarker studies underpowered and high-risk-of-bias, genomics only applicable to rare monogenic forms
- Establishes that **AI performs at expert level** for image analysis but no tool meets regulatory or scientific validation thresholds for diagnostic use
- Provides structured **research agenda**: standardized annotated datasets, multi-centre AI trials, longitudinal biomarker validation, interpretable AI models

## Methodology
- Design: Consensus workshop report (EFP, 8 commissioned SRs)
- Participants: 70 experts from 21 countries
- Framework: PICOTS; assessment tools: QUADAS-2, STARD, PRISMA-DTA, TRIPOD+AI
- WG1: traditional probing, 2D radiographs, CBCT, MRI, ultrasound
- WG2: microbial biomarkers (Bostanci 2025 SR), host-derived biomarkers (Rakic 2025 SR), genetic/epigenetic biomarkers (Dommisch 2025 SR)
- WG3: questionnaire-based assessments, AI systems for image and multimodal data

## Results

### WG1 — Traditional Diagnostics
- Manual probing (6 points/tooth) with BOP: reference standard; inherent error ±1–2 mm
- **BOP negative predictive value**: highest among clinical parameters for periodontal stability
- 2D periapical radiograph (paralleling technique): first-line radiographic tool; deficient in 3D accuracy, reproducibility for full-mouth
- **CBCT**: fulfills 3D criteria but radiation dose/cost prohibit routine use; useful for intra-bony defects and furcation classification
- MRI, ultrasound: limited current evidence; promising as technologies mature
- No available technology fulfills all ideal diagnostic imaging criteria

### WG2 — Biomarkers
- **Microbial**: bacterial burden thresholds unstable across assay methodologies; community dysbiosis metrics understudied due to small study sizes
- **Host-derived**: predominantly discovery-phase case-control studies; small n, high risk-of-bias, inconsistent with diagnostic guideline requirements
- **Genetic**: genomic testing applicable only to rare monogenic/syndromic periodontitis; polygenic common forms not diagnostically addressable by genomics; epigenetic biomarkers (DNA methylation, miRNA) emerging but unvalidated

### WG3 — Emerging Technologies / AI
- AI performs **at least as well as experts** for radiographic image analysis; may outperform in some scenarios
- Commercially available AI: Class I/II devices — diagnostic aids, not approved stand-alone diagnostics; lacks independent peer-reviewed validation
- No AI method validated for routine clinical diagnostic use
- Multimodal AI integration (imaging + clinical + omics): not yet superior to imaging alone
- Applied data science in periodontology is described as "in its infancy"
