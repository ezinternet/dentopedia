## Three-line Summary

Pilot in vivo randomized crossover study (n=10 hygienists, 5 with musculoskeletal disorders [MSD] / 5 without) comparing the ergonomic performance of three Barnhart 5/6 periodontal curette handle designs — a novel adaptive silicone-overlay handle vs. two conventional rigid handles — during a standardized typodont scaling task.

The adaptive handle (Curette A) significantly reduced instrumentation-related fatigue and improved comfort in both groups, and significantly cut measured muscle work (surface EMG of 4 hand/forearm muscles) in non-MSD testers, without sacrificing tactile feedback; hygienists with MSDs expended roughly double the muscle work and reported ~68% worse comfort / ~99% worse fatigue than non-MSD colleagues regardless of which curette was used.

Pilot-scale (n=10) findings support adaptive/cushioned handle designs as a mitigation for MSD-related instrumentation burden, but the study used typodont manikins rather than live patients, could not blind testers to instrument identity, and awaits confirmation in larger follow-up trials already noted as underway by the authors.

## 세줄요약

파일럿 인비보(in vivo) 무작위 교차설계 연구(치위생사 n=10, 근골격계질환(Musculoskeletal Disorder, MSD) 있는 군 5명·없는 군 5명)로, 신형 적응형(Adaptive) 실리콘 오버레이 손잡이 큐렛과 기존 경성(rigid) 손잡이 큐렛 2종(Barnhart 5/6)의 인간공학적 성능을 표준화된 타이포던트(Typodont) 스케일링 과제로 비교.

적응형 큐렛(Curette A)은 양 군 모두에서 피로도를 유의하게 줄이고 편안함을 개선했으며, 비-MSD 군에서는 표면근전도(Surface Electromyography, sEMG, 손/전완 4개 근육) 측정 근육 작업량도 유의하게 감소시켰고 촉각 피드백은 손상되지 않았음; MSD 군은 큐렛 종류와 무관하게 비-MSD 군보다 근육 작업량이 약 2배, 편안함은 평균 68% 나쁘고 피로도는 평균 99% 나쁨.

소규모(n=10) 파일럿 결과로 적응형·쿠션형 손잡이 설계가 MSD 관련 시술 부담 완화에 도움될 수 있음을 시사하나, 실제 환자가 아닌 타이포던트 모형 사용, 술자 눈가림 불가능, 대규모 후속연구 필요라는 한계가 있음.

## Summary

This pilot in vivo study (UC Irvine, IRB-exempt) compared the ergonomic performance of three Barnhart 5/6 periodontal curette handle designs in 10 right-handed dental hygienists — 5 with a diagnosed musculoskeletal disorder (MSD)/repetitive stress injury and 5 without — each testing all 3 curettes in randomized order on standardized typodont models. The novel design (Curette A) uses a flexible, adjustable core with a silicone overlay that conforms to the hand and fingers, versus two conventional rigid handles (a resin handle and a silicone-covered rigid handle). Outcomes combined objective surface electromyography (sEMG) of four gripping/manipulating muscles, normalized to each muscle's maximum voluntary contraction, with subjective 0–10 VAS ratings of comfort, fatigue, and tactile sensitivity. The adaptive handle significantly improved comfort and fatigue scores in both MSD and non-MSD hygienists, and significantly reduced total muscle work in non-MSD testers, while tactile sensitivity was preserved across all three instruments. Hygienists with MSDs performed almost double the muscle work and reported roughly half the comfort and twice the fatigue of their non-MSD colleagues, regardless of which curette was used — underscoring MSD status itself as a major driver of instrumentation burden, independent of handle design. Clinically, this pilot suggests adaptive, larger-contact-area handle geometry is a promising ergonomic mitigation for periodontal instrumentation, particularly for clinicians already affected by MSDs, though confirmation requires larger, longer-duration clinical follow-up.

## Key Contributions

- Head-to-head comparison of a novel adaptive/bendable-core silicone-overlay curette handle against two conventional rigid handle designs, using a combined objective (sEMG) + subjective (VAS) ergonomic assessment.
- One of the first instrumentation-ergonomics studies to stratify outcomes by clinician MSD status, not just by instrument design — showing MSD status itself roughly doubles muscle work and worsens comfort/fatigue regardless of curette.
- Adaptive handle preserved tactile feedback (no significant cross-instrument difference in tactile-sensitivity VAS) while significantly improving comfort/fatigue in both groups and muscle work in non-MSD testers — arguing against a tactile-feedback trade-off for adaptive designs.
- Confirms, in a controlled within-subject design, that a wider silicone-covered rigid handle (Curette C) outperforms a narrower resin rigid handle (Curette B) specifically on palm comfort.

## Methodology

- Pilot in vivo, within-subject randomized crossover design; IRB-exempt (UC Irvine, de-identified data only).
- 10 right-handed hygienists (≥5 yr clinical practice, ≥3 days/week, periodontal-instrumentation instructors at 6 California dental hygiene schools): 5 with diagnosed MSD/RSI/chronic hand pain, 5 without any hand/wrist/finger injury in the prior 6 months.
- Each tester used all 3 curettes (Barnhart 5/6 pattern) in randomized sequence on typodont manikins with standardized artificial calculus/biofilm; 20-minute rest between arms; 8-minute standardized scaling protocol per curette across 8 defined tooth-surface segments.
- Outcomes: sEMG of 4 muscles (APB, FDI, FPL, EDC) normalized to maximum voluntary isometric contraction (MVC); hard-copy 0–10 VAS for comfort, fatigue, and tactile sensitivity; open-ended comments. Testers unblinded to instrument identity; data extraction/analysis performed by a single blinded, pre-calibrated investigator.
- Statistics: General Linear Model (GLIM) with pairwise comparisons and Tukey's post hoc test, α < 0.05 (SPSS 19).

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Comfort & fatigue, non-MSD testers | Adaptive Curette A significantly better than rigid Curettes B and C on all categories; tactile sensitivity not significantly different across instruments |
| Comfort & fatigue, MSD testers | Curette A significantly better than Curette B on all categories; A vs. C on fatigue favored A but not significant; tactile sensitivity not significantly different |
| Palm comfort, rigid B vs. C | Silicone-covered rigid Curette C significantly better than resin rigid Curette B (both groups) |
| Comfort, MSD vs. non-MSD (pooled, all instruments) | 57–74% worse (mean 68%) in hygienists with MSDs |
| Fatigue, MSD vs. non-MSD (pooled, all instruments) | 97–100% worse (mean ~99%) in hygienists with MSDs |
| Muscle work (sEMG), non-MSD testers | Curette A significantly less total muscle work than Curette B or C |
| Muscle work (sEMG), MSD testers | Curette A lowest, Curette B highest, differences not significant (inter-subject SD up to 28%) |
| Muscle work, MSD vs. non-MSD | Significantly greater in MSD group regardless of curette used |
