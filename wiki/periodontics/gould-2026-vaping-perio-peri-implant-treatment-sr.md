## Three-line Summary

SR (Clin Exp Dent Res 2026): searched through June 2026; systematic review of vaping's effect on periodontal and peri-implant treatment outcomes (gingivitis, periodontitis, peri-implant disease); PROSPERO CRD42024506111.

Vape users trend toward worse treatment outcomes vs non-smokers, similarly to cigarette smokers; immunological responses also appear less favorable; certainty of evidence is low to very low (observational designs, heterogeneous exposure definitions).

Cannot make definitive causal claims; clinicians should counsel patients to treat vaping with the same caution as conventional smoking — it is not a proven safer alternative in the context of periodontal or peri-implant therapy.

## Summary

Gould et al. (Clin Exp Dent Res 2026) conducted a SR of studies examining vaping's effect on periodontal and peri-implant treatment outcomes through June 2026. Databases included Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science. The PICO question compared vaping patients (treatment) vs smokers, former smokers, or non-smokers (control) in clinical and immunological outcomes. Results suggest vape users may experience treatment outcomes trending toward those of cigarette smokers — both groups performing worse than non-smokers. However, certainty of evidence is low to very low due to the limited number of eligible studies (predominantly observational), heterogeneity in vaping exposure definitions, and short follow-up periods. MA was not feasible. The authors urge caution in extrapolating findings and call for longitudinal interventional studies with standardized exposure characterization.

## Key Contributions

- First SR specifically on vaping's effect on perio AND peri-implant treatment outcomes
- Vape ≈ cigarette smoker in treatment response (both worse than non-smoker) — pattern, not proven causality
- Immunological signal: vaping affects oral cytokine levels (IL-1β, PGE2 changes) — less characterized than smoking
- Direct clinical recommendation: apply same counseling caution to vapers as to conventional smokers
- Establishes evidence gap — calls for RCTs and long-term cohorts

## Methodology

SR (PRISMA); Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science; no date restrictions; through June 30, 2026; gingivitis, periodontitis, peri-implant disease; Risk of bias: Newcastle-Ottawa Scale; PROSPERO CRD42024506111.

## Results

| Comparison | Direction | Certainty |
|---|---|---|
| Vapers vs non-smokers | Worse outcomes (trend) | Very low |
| Vapers vs cigarette smokers | Similar outcomes | Very low |
| Immunological response | Less favorable in vapers vs non-smokers | Very low |

No MA possible — insufficient homogeneous data.
