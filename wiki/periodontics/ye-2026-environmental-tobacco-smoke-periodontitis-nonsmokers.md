## Three-line Summary

SR+MA (14 studies, 115,925 non-smoking adults, search to June 2025) — first meta-analytic test of whether environmental tobacco smoke (ETS/secondhand smoke) exposure alone is associated with periodontitis and tooth loss in people who have never smoked.

ETS exposure was significantly associated with periodontitis (OR 1.47, 95% CI 1.22-1.76) but not tooth loss (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80-1.31); association was stronger with cotinine (biomarker) assessment than self-report, and stronger in men than women.

Extends the smoking-periodontal harm model beyond active smokers to household/occupational passive exposure — relevant for risk counselling of non-smoking patients who live or work with smokers, and supports smoke-free-environment policy as periodontitis prevention.

## Summary
This is the first SR+MA to isolate ETS exposure's association with periodontitis specifically in confirmed non-smokers (removing active-smoking confounding present in most prior smoking-periodontal literature). Pooling 14 studies and >115,000 participants, it finds a significant periodontitis association but a null tooth-loss association, with notable effect-modification by exposure-assessment method (cotinine > self-report) and sex (men positive, women null).

## Key Contributions
- First quantitative ETS-periodontitis pooled estimate restricted to genuine non-smokers.
- Demonstrates that objective (cotinine) exposure assessment yields stronger, more credible associations than self-report — a methodological lesson for future secondhand-smoke research generally.
- Surfaces a sex-differential association with plausible mechanistic hypotheses (nicotine metabolism rate, under-reported exposure in women, hormonal modulation), flagging an area for future stratified research.

## Methodology
PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, Cochrane Library searched for English-language publications to June 9, 2025. 14 studies / 115,925 adults (18+) meta-analyzed for OR of periodontitis and tooth loss vs ETS exposure. Subgroup analyses: region, age, sex, exposure-assessment method (self-report vs cotinine), adjustment for diabetes/oral hygiene, study recency.

## Results
[확인, 관찰연구 기반] Periodontitis: OR 1.47 (95% CI 1.22-1.76), significant. Tooth loss: OR 1.03 (95% CI 0.80-1.31), null. Cotinine-based assessment showed stronger association than self-report. Men: significant; women: null. Studies from 2015 onward showed somewhat weaker effects than earlier studies (possibly better confounder control or publication-bias artifact in earlier literature). Age-stratified subgroup analysis not feasible due to inconsistent reporting.

## Limitations
- Predominantly cross-sectional — causality/temporal sequence unresolved (reverse causation considered but judged unlikely).
- No dose-response analysis possible (insufficient exposure-dose/duration data across studies).
- English-language restriction may introduce language/publication bias.
- Substantial heterogeneity remained largely unexplained despite extensive subgroup analysis.
- Self-reported ETS exposure (majority of studies) is an information-bias source; unmeasured co-exposures (air pollution, occupational hazards) not adjustable.

## Clinical Implication (Korean Practice)
For non-smoking patients reporting a smoking spouse/household member or workplace exposure, this SR+MA supports treating passive smoke exposure as a periodontitis risk factor worth documenting in history-taking — distinct from, but analogous to, active-smoking risk counselling (see [[periodontics/apatzidou-2022-cigarette-smoking-periodontal-implant-review]]). Because the tooth-loss association was null and causality is not firmly established, avoid overstating certainty; frame as "associated with, plausible risk factor" rather than "proven cause."
