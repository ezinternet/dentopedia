## Three-line Summary

Bench simulation study using a dental manikin and two in-situ optical methods (digital inline holography, DIH; laser sheet imaging, LSI) to characterize droplet size/velocity from simulated ultrasonic supragingival scaling and to evaluate the capture efficiency of aerosol-mitigation devices.

More than 99% of droplets measured 12–200 μm and over 65% traveled at less than 1 m/s; a saliva ejector (SE) reduced overall aerosol/spatter signal by 63%, a high-volume evacuator (HVE) by 88%, and an extraoral local extractor (ELE) by up to 96% at its high-flow setting with the nozzle 14 cm from the manikin's mouth.

A properly positioned, hands-free ELE outperformed HVE and SE without requiring a chairside assistant, but capture efficiency fell sharply as nozzle distance increased (ELE high-flow: 96%→56% when moved from 14 cm to 18 cm), and the manikin-only bench design cannot itself confirm reduced infectious risk.

## 세줄요약

치과 마네킹과 두 가지 실시간 광학기법 — 디지털 인라인 홀로그래피(Digital Inline Holography, DIH), 레이저 시트 이미징(Laser Sheet Imaging, LSI) — 을 이용해 모의 초음파 치은연상 스케일링에서 발생하는 물방울 크기·속도를 특성화하고 에어로졸(Aerosol) 저감 장치의 포집효율을 평가한 실험실 벤치 연구.

물방울의 99% 이상이 12–200 μm 범위였고 65% 이상이 속도 1 m/s 미만이었으며; 타액흡입기(Saliva Ejector, SE)는 전체 신호를 63%, 고용량흡인(High-Volume Evacuation, HVE)은 88%, 구강외 국소흡인장치(Extraoral Local Extractor, ELE)는 노즐을 마네킹 입에서 14 cm에 두고 고유량으로 가동했을 때 최대 96%까지 저감시켰다.

손이 자유로운 ELE를 적절히 배치하면 보조인력 없이도 HVE·SE보다 우수한 저감 효과를 냈지만, 노즐 거리가 멀어지면(14cm→18cm) 포집효율이 급격히 떨어졌고(ELE 고유량 96%→56%), 마네킹만을 이용한 벤치 연구이므로 실제 감염 위험 감소까지 입증한 것은 아니다.

## Summary

Motivated by COVID-19-era concerns about ultrasonic scaling as an aerosol-generating procedure (AGP), this bench study used a dental manikin plus two complementary in-situ optical methods — near-field digital inline holography (DIH) for droplet size/speed and far-field laser sheet imaging (LSI) for plume-scale visualization — to characterize aerosols and spatter from simulated supragingival ultrasonic scaling and to quantify the capture efficiency of aerosol-mitigation devices. Droplets spanned 12–200+ μm (>99% within that range) and moved mostly slowly (>65% < 1 m/s), which the authors argue favors point-of-source suction over whole-room ventilation. Among tested devices, a saliva ejector (SE) captured 63%, a high-volume evacuator (HVE) 88%, and a hands-free extraoral local extractor (ELE) up to 96% at its high-flow setting and optimal 14-cm nozzle distance — but ELE efficiency dropped steeply (to 56%) when the nozzle was moved just 4 cm farther away, underscoring that device positioning is as clinically important as device choice.

## Key Contributions

- First characterization of **both** droplet size and velocity for ultrasonic-scaler aerosols/spatter using in-situ optical methods (DIH + LSI), rather than surface-collection methods that undersample small aerosols (< ~50 μm).
- First quantitative capture-efficiency evaluation of an extraoral local extractor (ELE) for ultrasonic scaling, including flow-rate and nozzle-distance effects — a device category the authors state lacked published effectiveness evidence before this study.
- Shows droplet velocity is low (>65% < 1 m/s, omnidirectional), supporting point-of-source suction strategies over whole-room ventilation for this specific procedure.
- Demonstrates that combining ELE with SE or HVE gives no meaningful additional capture benefit over a well-positioned, high-flow ELE alone.

## Methodology

- **Design**: Laboratory bench simulation on a dental manikin (thermoplastic teeth) — not a clinical/patient study.
- **Simulated procedure**: A dental hygienist performed simulated supragingival scaling of the facial surfaces of the maxillary central incisors (#8/#9) and lingual surfaces of the mandibular central incisors (#24/#25) — the regions the authors identify as generating the most spatter/aerosol clinically — using an ultrasonic scaler (Cavitron Plus, 30K FSI-1000 inserts; Dentsply Sirona) at medium power, ~36 mL/min water flow; 3 trials × 10-second videos per site.
- **DIH**: Near-field holographic setup (sample volume 15.6 × 13.0 × 250 mm, ~8 mm above the oral cavity; 35 fps, 13-μs exposure), processed with the hybrid hologram method of Shao et al. to extract particle size distributions.
- **LSI**: Far-field imaging (high-resolution camera + image intensifier + light sheet, 40 × 40 cm field of view) with background subtraction and entropy-filter segmentation; capture efficiency derived from total scattered-light intensity vs. a no-mitigation control.
- **Mitigation devices**: saliva ejector (SE), high-volume evacuator (HVE), and an extraoral local extractor (ELE; DentalPro Aerosol UVC, BOFA International) at low (~100 m³/h) and high (~220 m³/h) flow, tested at 14 cm and 18 cm nozzle-to-mouth distance, alone and in combination.
- **Cross-check**: An aerodynamic particle sizer (TSI 3321) independently sampled residual (post-evaporation) particles 0.7–3 μm at the DIH location.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Droplet size range (DIH) | > 99% within 12–200 μm; number-mode 55 μm (facial, teeth #8/#9) vs. 34 μm (lingual, teeth #24/#25) |
| Droplet velocity (DIH) | > 65% < 1 m/s; < 2% > 3 m/s; omnidirectional |
| Size-dependent capture (DIH, non-SE devices) | > 95% up to 90 μm; ~80% at 120 μm; ~50% at 150 μm |
| Capture efficiency — Saliva Ejector (SE) | 63% (LSI, overall) — lowest of devices tested |
| Capture efficiency — HVE | 88% (LSI, overall) |
| Capture efficiency — ELE, low-flow, 14 cm | 74% (LSI, overall) |
| Capture efficiency — ELE, high-flow, 14 cm | 96% (LSI, overall) — best single device |
| ELE + SE or + HVE (high-flow, 14 cm) | 94% / 95% — no added benefit over ELE alone |
| ELE positioning effect (14 cm → 18 cm) | 74%→38% (low-flow); 96%→56% (high-flow) |
| Small-particle cross-check (0.7–3 μm, aerodynamic particle sizer) | > 95% capture, consistent with DIH curve |
| Noise at manikin's ear (scaler only → scaler + ELE high-flow) | 81.1 dB → 86.8 dB |
