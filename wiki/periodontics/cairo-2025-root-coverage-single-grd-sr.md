## Three-line Summary

PRISMA 2020-compliant SR of 50 RCTs (1820 subjects, 2219 single gingival recession defects); root coverage surgery significantly improved both clinician-reported esthetic scores (RES) and patient-reported esthetic perception (VAS), with bilaminar techniques (CAF+sCTG) superior to monolaminar (CAF alone) for both outcomes.

Lateral flap displacement techniques yielded higher RES than coronal displacement; meta-regression showed significant positive association between mean root coverage (MRC%) and RES, but MRC% did NOT correlate with patient esthetic satisfaction — patients and clinicians value different things.

Dentinal hypersensitivity was substantially reduced by surgical root coverage regardless of modality — an underrecognized but consistent clinical benefit beyond esthetics.

## Background

Single gingival recession defects (single GRDs) are treated by root coverage periodontal plastic surgery (RCPPS), with outcomes traditionally measured by clinician-reported parameters (probing depth, root coverage %, keratinized tissue width). Growing recognition of the importance of patient-reported outcomes (PROs) — particularly esthetic satisfaction — has led to dual-outcome frameworks in recent trials.

This 2025 SR (PROSPERO CRD517050) is the largest synthesis to date, explicitly integrating both clinician-reported esthetic scores (RES: 0–10 scale) and patient-reported esthetic perception/satisfaction (VAS) across 50 RCTs. Complementary to [[periodontics/gualtieri-2025-mcaf-gingival-recession-sr-ma]] which focuses on multiple adjacent defects (MAGR).

## Key Findings

### Technique Hierarchy

**Bilaminar vs monolaminar (CAF+sCTG vs CAF alone)**:
- RES: Bilaminar significantly superior
- VAS: Bilaminar significantly superior
- Both outcomes: bilaminar consistently better

**Soft tissue substitutes (CAF + substitute)**:
- VAS: Beneficial effect (significantly better than monolaminar)
- RES: No significant additional benefit over CAF alone

**Flap displacement direction**:
- Lateral flap > coronal flap: superior mean RES values
- Likely due to improved blood supply and tissue coverage

### Meta-regression
- Mean root coverage % ↑ → RES ↑: **statistically significant** positive association
- Mean root coverage % ↑ → patient esthetic perception: **NOT statistically significant**
  - This dissociation implies patients' satisfaction relates to factors beyond measurable coverage (e.g., pain, scar, contour)

### Dentinal Hypersensitivity
- Substantially reduced regardless of treatment modality
- Consistent and clinically meaningful secondary benefit

## Clinical Takeaway

For single gingival recession defects:
1. **Bilaminar (CAF+sCTG)** is the evidence-based choice when both clinician and patient esthetic outcomes are priorities — superior over both monolaminar (CAF) and substitutes for RES
2. **Soft tissue substitutes** (collagen matrices, xenogeneic dermal matrix) achieve patient-reported esthetic satisfaction comparable to bilaminar — acceptable when donor site morbidity is a concern
3. **Lateral flap techniques** should be considered for superior RES in appropriate anatomy
4. **Dentinal hypersensitivity relief** is a consistent benefit to communicate to patients pre-operatively
5. Do not conflate root coverage percentage with patient satisfaction — they measure different constructs
