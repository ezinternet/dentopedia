## Three-line Summary

Retrospective cross-sectional study (n=445 teeth from a Hong Kong university postgraduate endodontic clinic, 2019–2024) using combined periapical radiograph + high-resolution small-FOV CBCT to estimate cemental tear prevalence.

Prevalence was 5.6% (25/445); multivariate logistic regression identified four independent risk factors: incisor tooth type (molars OR 0.08), tooth mobility (Grade 3 OR 48.27), severe crestal bone loss ≥70% (OR 10.83), and adequate root canal obturation quality (OR 8.38); periapical radiograph sensitivity was only 0.60 vs CBCT.

CBCT is the preferred imaging modality because periapical films miss 40% of confirmed cemental tear cases, and the majority of cases (76%) present with extensive apico-marginal bone loss (Class 2/4, Stage C/D) — underscoring the diagnostic challenge in this referral-level population.

## Summary

This retrospective, cross-sectional study is the first to combine periapical radiographs (PR) and high-resolution small-FOV CBCT to estimate cemental tear prevalence, finding 5.6% among 445 patients consecutively referred to a University Postgraduate Endodontic Clinic in Hong Kong (2019-2024) — nearly triple the 0.89-1.9% reported by two prior single-modality studies. On multivariate logistic regression, four factors remained independently significant: tooth type (incisors far more affected than molars), tooth mobility (odds increasing with mobility grade), severe crestal bone loss (≥70%), and — a novel finding — adequate (rather than inadequate) root canal filling quality. Applying the Lee et al. (2021) Class/Stage classification to a consecutive clinical cohort (rather than pooled case reports), the study shows most cases (76%) fall into Class 2/4 and Stage C/D, i.e. extensive apico-marginal bone loss across multiple root surfaces — explaining why these teeth are so commonly misdiagnosed as primary endodontic or periodontal disease before referral. PR alone missed 40% of confirmed cases, underscoring CBCT's role as the preferred imaging modality.

## Key Contributions

- Highest-quality prevalence estimate to date (5.6%) from combined PR+CBCT imaging in a consecutive clinical cohort, versus 0.89-1.9% from prior PR-only or lower-resolution/larger-FOV CBCT-only studies.
- First multivariate risk-factor model for cemental tear, isolating tooth type, mobility, crestal bone loss severity, and root filling quality as independent predictors.
- First quantification of PR diagnostic accuracy against CBCT as reference standard for cemental tear (sensitivity 0.60, specificity 1.00).
- Real-world application of the Lee et al. (2021) Class/Stage classification to a consecutive referral cohort, showing 76% concentrate in the most extensive categories (Class 2/4, Stage C/D).
- Novel association between adequate root canal obturation quality and cemental tear risk (OR 8.38), raising a mechanistic hypothesis about obturation-technique-induced thermal/mechanical root stress warranting further study.

## Methodology

Retrospective, observational, cross-sectional design at a University Postgraduate Endodontic Clinic (Hong Kong), 1 Sept 2019 – 31 March 2024, IRB-approved (UW 20-447), PROBE-guideline reporting. 575 patients screened → 445 patients/teeth (one tooth per patient) met inclusion. Sample size powered for 5% anticipated prevalence at 95% CI/2% precision. Imaging: paralleling-technique digital PR plus high-resolution small-FOV CBCT (Veraview X800, 40×40mm FOV, 0.08mm voxel, 1.5mm reformatted slices). Single experienced endodontist assessor (>25y experience); intra-observer Kappa 1.0 on 10% re-check. 30 candidate factors tested via Pearson chi-squared/exact univariate analysis, significant factors entered into backward-stepwise multivariate logistic regression (α=0.05). PR diagnostic accuracy computed against CBCT as reference standard.

## Results

- Prevalence: 25/445 (5.6%).
- Multivariate-significant risk factors: tooth type (molars OR 0.08 vs incisors, 95% CI 0.02–0.34, p<0.001); tooth mobility (Grade 1 OR 26.87, Grade 2 OR 18.69, Grade 3 OR 48.27, all vs no mobility, p<0.05); severe crestal bone loss ≥70% (OR 10.83, CI 2.85–41.21, p<0.001 vs mild ≤30%); adequate root filling quality (OR 8.38, CI 1.73–40.69, p=0.008 vs inadequate).
- Classification distribution: 76% Class 2 or 4; 76% Stage C or D.
- Mean age 58.7y; no significant age or gender association.
- 72% of cemental-tear cases had root canal treatment initiated or completed at time of referral.
- PR vs CBCT (reference standard) diagnostic accuracy: sensitivity 0.60, specificity 1.00, PPV 1.00, NPV 0.98; 40% of confirmed cases (10/25) detectable only via CBCT.
- Gold-standard direct inspection/histopathologic confirmation achieved in 60% (15/25) of cases.
