## Three-line Summary

Systematic review + meta-analysis (SR+MA; Odontology 2026; Embase/PubMed/Cochrane Library to April 2025) of RCTs assessing bioactive glass (BG) as a standalone intervention for periodontal regeneration; 12 RCTs, 234 patients, 406 periodontal defects, Cochrane standards, random-effects pooling in RevMan v5.4.

BG significantly improved probing depth (PD) reduction (MD = 1.27 mm; p = 0.0006) and clinical attachment level (CAL) gain (MD = 1.49 mm; p = 0.0005), with moderate-to-high heterogeneity and no significant publication bias.

BG looks like a credible osteoconductive regenerative adjunct for periodontal defects, but the pooled estimate rests on 12 small RCTs with moderate-to-high heterogeneity, and this abstract-only page cannot verify risk of bias, comparator arms, follow-up timepoints or subgroup structure.

## Summary

Jafari and colleagues (Odontology 2026) pooled the clinical efficacy of bioactive glass (BG) in periodontal regeneration, framed against the limitation of conventional therapy: open flap debridement (OFD) controls inflammation but provides limited regeneration, whereas BG is a bioactive and osteoconductive material that has emerged as a promising regenerative agent. The review targets the two standard clinical endpoints of periodontal regeneration — probing depth (PD) reduction and clinical attachment level (CAL) gain — in periodontal lesions, and restricted inclusion to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) using BG as a standalone intervention. Twelve RCTs covering 234 patients and 406 periodontal defects were pooled with a random-effects model. BG improved PD reduction by a mean of 1.27 mm (p = 0.0006) and CAL gain by a mean of 1.49 mm (p = 0.0005), with moderate-to-high heterogeneity between studies and no significant publication bias. The authors conclude that BG significantly enhances clinical outcomes in periodontal regeneration and has strong potential as an effective regenerative material, while calling for further high-quality trials.

## Key Contributions

- **Material-specific pooled effect for a periodontal indication** — the wiki previously held BG only as a material class (alloplast) in narrative reviews; this is the first BG clinical-efficacy estimate for periodontal regeneration held here.
- **Absolute millimetre effects on both standard endpoints** — PD reduction and CAL gain as mean differences, directly comparable to the grafting-vs-OFD range in [[periodontics/host-modulation/stavropoulos-2021-periodontal-regeneration-intrabony-nma]].
- **Standalone-intervention design** — BG is evaluated as its own intervention rather than as an add-on, which keeps the pooled contrast interpretable against debridement-type controls.
- **Heterogeneity and publication bias reported together** — the pooled numbers are accompanied by their calibration limits rather than presented bare.
- **Mechanistic framing consistent with the alloplast taxonomy** — BG is *bioactive and osteoconductive*; in [[bone-regeneration/haugen-2026-bone-grafts-everything-you-need]] BG is classed as an alloplast that is osteoconductive, osteoinductive only through surface ion release, and carries no osteogenic cell fraction.

## Methodology

- **Design**: systematic review + meta-analysis following Cochrane Collaboration standards.
- **Databases / search date**: Embase, PubMed, Cochrane Library; search up to April 2025.
- **Eligibility**: RCTs assessing BG as a standalone intervention for periodontal regeneration; studies reporting PD and/or CAL outcomes.
- **Review process**: two reviewers independently extracted data and assessed risk of bias.
- **Analysis**: random-effects meta-analysis in RevMan (Review Manager) v5.4, used to address study heterogeneity.
- **Pooled base**: 12 RCTs, 234 patients, 406 periodontal defects.
- **Outcomes**: probing depth (PD) reduction; clinical attachment level (CAL) gain.
- **Not recoverable from the abstract**: comparator arms pooled against BG, follow-up duration, number of studies per pool, I² values, risk-of-bias ratings, subgroup definitions, safety outcomes.

## Results

| Outcome | Pooled effect | p-value | Heterogeneity | Publication bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Probing depth (PD) reduction | MD = 1.27 mm | 0.0006 | moderate-to-high (no I² reported) | not significant |
| Clinical attachment level (CAL) gain | MD = 1.49 mm | 0.0005 | moderate-to-high (no I² reported) | not significant |

- Direction: both pooled effects favour BG and both are statistically significant.
- Magnitude context: the CAL gain of 1.49 mm sits inside the +1.26 to +2.66 mm CAL-gain range reported for regenerative approaches versus OFD in [[periodontics/host-modulation/stavropoulos-2021-periodontal-regeneration-intrabony-nma]] (primary-study overlap between the two reviews cannot be excluded from the abstract).
- Authors' conclusion: BG significantly enhances clinical outcomes in periodontal regeneration; despite variability among studies it has strong potential as an effective regenerative material, warranting further high-quality trials.

## Limitations

- **Abstract-only** — risk-of-bias judgements, forest plots, I² values, subgroup structure, comparator definitions, follow-up times and adverse events are unavailable; this page cannot support claims about long-term stability, defect-morphology subgroups, or head-to-head comparison with EMD, PRF or other grafts.
- **Moderate-to-high heterogeneity** — the MD values are averages over materially different protocols, not one reproducible effect.
- **Small base** — 12 RCTs, 234 patients, 406 defects; individual trials are correspondingly small.
- **Publication-bias method unstated** in the abstract, so the reassurance is only as strong as an unverified test.
- **No radiographic, tooth-survival, cost or morbidity outcome** is mentioned in the abstract.
