## Three-line Summary

Multi-centre, quasi-randomized (clinician-level cluster) two-armed field effectiveness trial in Swedish general dental practice: 95 dental hygienists (59 clinics) treated 615 periodontitis patients (GPIC n=280, CNST n=335) under a non-inferiority design, comparing a guided patient-education-first single-session protocol against conventional multi-visit scaling and root planing.

At 6 months, pocket closure (PPD ≤4 mm) was ~69–72% overall with no significant between-group difference (shallow sites 72% GPIC vs 75% CNST; deep sites 30% vs 33%), while GPIC required significantly less total treatment time (134±40 vs 161±61 min, p<.001) and fewer instrumentation sessions.

Efficacy-trial-level non-surgical periodontal outcomes reproduce in real-world dental-hygienist-delivered general practice; smoking, older age, and molar tooth type independently lowered the odds of pocket closure, supporting smoking-cessation counselling and avoidance of over-instrumentation.

## Summary

This Swedish multi-centre field study asked whether the guided periodontal infection control (GPIC) protocol — patient education until adequate self-performed hygiene is demonstrated, followed by a single session of full-mouth ultrasonic debridement — is non-inferior to conventional non-surgical therapy (CNST, multi-visit scaling and root planing at clinician discretion) when both are delivered by ordinary dental hygienists in general practice rather than by trial-calibrated specialists. Ninety-five dental hygienists across 59 clinics in Region Västra Götaland treated 615 periodontitis patients under a quasi-randomized (clinician-level), non-inferiority design. At 6 months, pocket closure (PPD ≤4 mm) reached roughly 69–72% overall with no significant difference between protocols at any pocket-depth or disease-severity stratum, closely reproducing benchmarks from prior efficacy trials and systematic reviews. GPIC, however, was markedly more time-efficient — about 17% less total chairtime, fewer sessions, and less local anaesthesia — without sacrificing outcomes. Multilevel regression identified baseline pocket depth, current smoking, older age, and molar tooth type as independent negative predictors of pocket closure, while treatment group and BMI had no significant effect. The clinical implication: non-surgical periodontal therapy efficacy generalizes to everyday hygienist-delivered practice, and a structured, education-first, single-session protocol can match conventional multi-visit SRP while using less chairside time.

## Key Contributions

- First large-scale **effectiveness** (not efficacy) comparison of GPIC vs CNST, conducted entirely in general dental practice by practising dental hygienists.
- Shows a single-session, patient-education-first protocol (GPIC) achieves outcomes statistically indistinguishable from multi-visit conventional SRP (CNST), at ~17% less total chairtime and markedly less local anaesthesia.
- Quantifies time-efficiency directly: 9.5±10.5 min of instrumentation per closed pocket (GPIC) vs 14.5±20.8 min (CNST), p=.001.
- Multilevel modelling isolates independent site-level predictors of pocket closure: baseline PPD (OR 0.33/mm), current smoking (OR 0.56), age (OR 0.97/year), and molar vs anterior tooth (OR 0.41) — treatment group and BMI were not significant.
- Confirms, at effectiveness scale and with 95 clinicians of varying experience, that the ~70% pocket-closure rate from prior efficacy RCTs is reproducible under real-world general-practice conditions.

## Methodology

- Multi-centre, quasi-randomized (clinician-level, stratified by geography/clinic sociodemographics), two-armed non-inferiority field trial. Approved by the Regional Ethical Review Board, Gothenburg (Dnr: 288-13); registered at ClinicalTrials.gov.
- 95 of 120 invited dental hygienists (59 clinics), Public Dental Service, Region Västra Götaland, Sweden, each randomized to GPIC (n=47 DHs) or CNST (n=48 DHs) after a 1-day GCP/protocol/calibration training session.
- Adults with periodontitis, ≥18 teeth, ≥5 teeth with proximal PPD ≥5 mm + BoP; no subgingival instrumentation in the prior 6 months. Recruited June 2014–December 2017; modified intention-to-treat analysis, n=615 (GPIC 280, CNST 335) of 689 enrolled.
- Power calculation: n=506 (253/arm) for 80% power at α<.05 to detect a 5-point between-group difference in pocket closure; target enrolment inflated to 700 for anticipated dropout.
- **GPIC**: education/motivation visits until full-mouth plaque score <30%, then one session of full-mouth ultrasonic debridement (EMS Piezon).
- **CNST**: integrated education + SRP across consecutive visits (typically 1–2 weeks apart), session count at DH discretion ("business as usual").
- Both arms: oral-hygiene check at 2–4 weeks; re-evaluation/re-instrumentation of residual PPD ≥5 mm + inflammation sites at 3 months; final evaluation at 6 months.
- PPD/BoP measured at 4 sites/tooth (standardized probe) at baseline, 3, and 6 months; treatment time and anaesthesia use logged per session; patient-reported oral-health change on a 5-point scale at 6 months.
- Statistics: modified ITT with last-observation-carried-forward; t-test/chi-square for group comparisons; 3-level (clinician/patient/tooth-site) logistic multilevel model for pocket-closure probability; 2-level linear multilevel model for residual-pocket burden; partial correlation between clinical and patient-reported outcomes.

## Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Pocket closure (PPD ≤4mm) at 6 months, overall | ~69–72%, no significant GPIC vs CNST difference |
| Pocket closure, shallow sites (5–6mm) | 72% (GPIC) vs 75% (CNST), ns |
| Pocket closure, deep sites (≥7mm) | 30% (GPIC) vs 33% (CNST), ns |
| Total treatment time (baseline+3mo) | 134.0±40.1 min (GPIC) vs 160.9±61.3 min (CNST), p<.001 |
| Instrumentation time per closed pocket | 9.5±10.5 min (GPIC) vs 14.5±20.8 min (CNST), p=.001 |
| Sessions to complete baseline instrumentation | 100% single session (GPIC) vs 29.0% single session (CNST) |
| Local anaesthesia (initial phase, no anaesthesia used) | 70.0% (GPIC) vs 59.9% (CNST), p<.001 |
| Patients rating oral health "much/very much improved" | >75% overall, no significant group difference |
| Pocket closure OR per mm baseline PPD (multilevel model) | OR 0.33 (95% CI 0.31–0.36) |
| Pocket closure OR, current smoker vs non-smoker | OR 0.56 (95% CI 0.37–0.85) |
| Pocket closure OR, molar vs anterior tooth | OR 0.41 (95% CI 0.35–0.48) |
| Pocket closure OR, treatment group (GPIC vs CNST) | not significant (p=.117) |
| Adverse events | none reported in either arm |

- [[periodontics/lamont-2018-routine-scale-and-polish-periodontal-health]] — contrast, not reinforcement: Lamont's Cochrane review finds scheduled routine scale-and-polish gives little/no benefit in *low-risk healthy* adults without severe periodontitis; this study is therapeutic subgingival instrumentation in *diagnosed periodontitis* patients (PPD≥5mm+BoP), where non-surgical treatment produces a large, clinically meaningful effect (~70% pocket closure). The two together delineate where non-surgical hygienist-delivered instrumentation does (diagnosed disease) and does not (routine prophylaxis in low-risk adults) show benefit.
