## Three-line Summary
Hypothesis-building narrative review (two reviewers, Jan 2022–Apr 2025, five databases) evaluating non-invasive blood perfusion imaging for periodontal microsurgery; the search found no direct empirical evidence for LSCI/US in microsurgical periodontal wound healing.
LSCI (~10 μm spatial resolution, 113 fps, ~1 mm depth) and US (B-mode + color flow, 64 μm axial resolution) emerged as the leading complementary modalities; an illustrative grafted-ridge case showed mid-occlusal ~220 PU versus facial apical ~360 PU perfusion.
The authors hypothesize microsurgery improves healing by limiting surgical trauma and flap tension (flap elevation can cut perfusion by 50% at 24 h); large controlled human studies and updated diagnostic protocols are required.

## Summary
This hypothesis-building narrative review (Rodriguez et al., Clinical Advances in Periodontics 2026;16(Suppl. 1):S22–S34) asked which non-invasive perfusion-imaging modalities can assess wound healing in periodontal microsurgery. Two reviewers searched five databases from January 2022 to April 2025 and identified laser speckle contrast imaging (LSCI) and ultrasound (US) as the leading, complementary techniques: LSCI maps superficial gingival perfusion (~10 μm spatial, 113 fps, ~1 mm depth, arbitrary perfusion units), while US adds cross-sectional anatomy plus color-flow velocity/power (64 μm axial resolution). Human and preclinical studies showed both track perfusion changes during compression, inflammation and graft healing, and an illustrative grafted-ridge case spatially correlated the two. Critically, the search returned no studies applying either modality to microsurgical periodontal wound healing, so the authors frame a testable hypothesis — microsurgery preserves perfusion by minimizing surgical trauma and managing flap tension (residual tension ≤0.1 N; 7-0 polypropylene fails at ~3.7 N) — and call for large controlled human studies before clinical adoption.

## Key Contributions
- Positions LSCI + US as complementary chairside perfusion tools: LSCI for superficial projection imaging, US for deeper cross-sectional anatomy and flow direction/velocity/volume.
- Documents the evidence vacuum (no LSCI/US studies in microsurgical periodontal wound healing) and converts it into an explicit, testable trauma–tension–perfusion–healing hypothesis.
- Proposes a standardized ROI-based diagnostic framework, demonstrated on a grafted edentulous ridge (site #8, 5 months post-augmentation) with spatial US–LSCI correlation.
- Integrates quantitative surgical thresholds (≤0.1 N / 10 g residual tension; ~3.7 N / 370 g 7-0 polypropylene breaking force) into the perfusion–healing model.

## Methodology
Design: hypothesis-building narrative review (not a systematic review; inherent bias acknowledged) by two reviewers (A.R., O.K.), plus one exploratory clinical case from an NIH-funded, IRB-approved feasibility study (HUM00203875). Databases: NLM PubMed, Embase, EBSCOhost CINAHL, EBSCOhost Dentistry and Oral Sciences Source, Cochrane CENTRAL. Search window: January 2022 – April 2025. No fixed study count; narrative synthesis of human LSCI cohorts (n = 7, 21, 31), a US pilot (n = 5), a periapical cohort (n = 15), mini-pig work (n = 8), and one case (30-year-old female). Outcomes: LSCI perfusion units (PU), US color-flow velocity/power and color pixel density (CPDV/CPDP), reperfusion time, healing scores and soft-tissue thickness.

## Results
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| LSCI resolution / depth | ~10 μm spatial, 113 fps temporal, ~1 mm penetration (Table 1 states ~100 μm — internal inconsistency) |
| US resolution | 64 μm axial; L30-8 array, 12–24 MHz second harmonic, 90 dB velocity / 40 dB power gain cine-loops |
| LSCI compression (Fazekas, n=21) | Higher resting flow apical to papillae; hyperemia more pronounced/longer in males |
| LSCI donor-site healing (Molnár, n=7) | Reperfusion time strongly correlated with healing scores; delayed normalization vs primary healing |
| US pilot (n=5, CAF+CTG) | CV/CP peaked 199.25% above baseline at 1 week/1 month; declined by 6 and 12 months |
| US RCT (Tavelli 2025) | Tunneling > CAF for interproximal perfusion and early graft vascularization; correlated with 12-month outcomes |
| Illustrative case (grafted ridge) | Mid-occlusal ~220 PU; facial crestal bone ~280 PU vs apical ~360 PU |
| Flap elevation | Reduces baseline perfusion by ~50% at 24 h; longer vertical incisions → poorer revascularization |
| Central finding | No hits for LSCI/US in microsurgical periodontal wound-healing evaluation |
