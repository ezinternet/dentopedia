## Three-line Summary

Narrative review summarizing dental plaque/biofilm indices used to evaluate home oral hygiene effectiveness, classifying them into non-quantitative (area/numerical, gingival thickness, dichotomous) and quantitative (weight, planimetric, QLF, automated, 3D-coordinate) methods.

Non-quantitative indices (Plaque Index/PlI, Quigley-Hein/Turesky-modified QHPI, Navy Plaque Index and its RMNPI/MNPI modifications, O'Leary Plaque Control Record, OHI/OHI-S) remain the most widely used because they need no special instruments; quantitative methods (QLF, automated RGB/HSI photographic analysis, 3D coordinate-measuring machine) offer greater objectivity but require dedicated equipment and are largely confined to research.

Clinicians/researchers must select the index matching their purpose (epidemiologic survey vs clinical trial vs chairside motivation) since only general findings are comparable across different indices, and ad hoc index modification should be avoided; a Full-Mouth Plaque Score of 20-25% is an acceptable maintenance threshold, tightening to ≤15% before regenerative periodontal surgery.

## Summary

This narrative review by D'Elia et al. (Sapienza University of Rome, 2023) provides a single organizing taxonomy for the many plaque/biofilm indices scattered across the periodontal and preventive-dentistry literature. Dental plaque is framed as a structured biofilm whose dysbiosis drives both caries and periodontal disease, which is why accurately scoring biofilm burden — via a well-chosen index — matters for both epidemiology and chairside patient motivation. The review's central axis is objectivity: **non-quantitative** indices depend on clinician judgment of presence/extent/thickness (fast, instrument-free, but subjective and examiner-dependent), while **quantitative** indices use objectifiable measurement (weight, photographic planimetry, fluorescence, 3D coordinates) at the cost of equipment, training, and time.

## Key Contributions

- Organizes plaque indices first by objectivity (non-quantitative vs quantitative), then by scoring criterion (area/extent, thickness, weight, fluorescence, presence/absence, 3D volume) — a practical index-selection framework.
- Traces the historical modification chains of the most-cited indices:
  - Ramfjord Plaque Component (1959) → Shick-Ash modification (buccal/lingual gingival-third only)
  - Green-Vermillion OHI (1960) → Simplified OHI-S/DI-S → Glass Index
  - Quigley-Hein Plaque Index (1962) → **Turesky modification (1970, TQHPI)** — the most-used index in clinical trials
  - Navy Plaque Index (1973) → Elliott modification (MNPI) and **Rustogi Modified Navy Plaque Index (RMNPI)** — both partition each tooth into 9 areas, weighting gingival-margin-adjacent deposits
- Details disclosing-agent chemistry/history (iodine, Bismarck brown, erythrosine, methylene blue, basic fuchsin, two-tone solutions) as the practical prerequisite for scoring most non-quantitative indices.
- States explicit Full-Mouth Plaque Score (FMPS) clinical decision thresholds: **20-25%** acceptable for periodontal maintenance; **≤15%** (plaque-free at the surgical site) required before regenerative periodontal surgery, since lower biofilm burden is associated with greater clinical attachment gain.
- Surveys objective/emerging techniques — quantitative light-induced fluorescence (QLF), automated RGB/HSI or "yellow-pixel" photographic analysis, and CMM-based 3D plaque quantification — noting QLF's added value of simultaneous early-caries detection.
- Proposes five "ideal index" criteria: simplicity, minimal time/instrumentation, clearly defined reproducible scoring criteria, amenability to statistical analysis, and uniform sensitivity across the scale.

## Methodology

Narrative (non-systematic) literature review; no PRISMA flow diagram, defined search strategy, or formal quality appraisal is reported. The authors synthesize decades of periodontal/preventive-dentistry literature on plaque-index development into two summary tables (non-quantitative; quantitative) and accompanying schematic figures (not reproduced in the plain-text PMC full text obtained for this ingest).

## Results

No original clinical data — this is a methods-taxonomy synthesis. Key take-home points:
- Non-quantitative indices dominate both clinical and research use precisely because no special instruments are needed, trading off examiner-dependent subjectivity.
- Quantitative methods (weight, planimetric, QLF, automated, 3D-CMM) improve objectivity/reproducibility but remain mostly research-only tools due to equipment/training burden.
- Dichotomous indices (e.g., O'Leary Plaque Control Record, FMPS) suit chairside motivation and hygiene-instruction feedback; severity-graded indices (TQHPI, RMNPI) suit clinical trials and epidemiologic surveys.
- Findings across different plaque indices are only broadly comparable, not interchangeable — a caution directly relevant when interpreting interdental-cleaning-device RCTs in this wiki that each use a different index (RMNPI, TQHPI, OHI-S, etc.).
