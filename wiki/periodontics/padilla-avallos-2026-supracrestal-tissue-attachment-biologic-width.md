## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (Odovtos, Online First 2026, OCEBM appraisal of PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science evidence) comparing conventional, laser-assisted, and digitally guided management of the supracrestal tissue attachment (STA, formerly biologic width) in clinical practice.

Conventional surgery (gingivectomy, access flap, osteotomy, osteoplasty) remains reliable at Level 2/Grade B; digitally guided workflows (CBCT + intraoral scanning + CAD/CAM surgical guides) deliver precision and reproducibility (Level 2/Grade B), especially in esthetic crown lengthening; laser (diode, Er:YAG) improves soft-tissue precision, hemostasis, and morbidity but lacks osseous-surgery validation (Level 3/Grade C).

Physiological STA vertical dimension 2.73–3.0 mm; pre-surgical periodontal stabilization (Level 1/Grade A) is mandatory; deep margin elevation Level 2/Grade B; AI-augmented CBCT/scanning planning is the emerging frontier.

## Overview

The supracrestal tissue attachment — the dentogingival unit historically called "biologic width" — is a critical determinant of periodontal health and restorative predictability. This narrative review (ODOVTOS-Int. J. Dent. Sc., Online First 2026; received 2025-09-24, accepted 2026-02-27) critically appraises conventional and technology-assisted strategies for its preservation, using the Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine (OCEBM) framework and synthesizing findings into comparative and recommendation tables.

## Key Findings

- **STA composition and dimension**: sulcular epithelium + junctional epithelium + supracrestal connective tissue forming the biologic interface between tooth and oral environment; physiological vertical dimension **2.73–3.0 mm** is the surgical re-establishment target. Violation (iatrogenic or pathologic) → chronic inflammation, gingival overgrowth, or recession.
- **Conventional techniques (Level 2/Grade B)**: gingivectomy, access flaps, osteotomy, osteoplasty remain reliable when guided by biologic and prosthetic principles — but outcomes depend on phenotype (gingival biotype) and surgical design; risk of gingival rebound.
- **Digitally guided workflows (Level 2/Grade B)**: CBCT + intraoral scanning + CAD/CAM surgical guides enable precise soft-tissue mapping and transfer of virtual planning to the operative field; reduce operator variability; indicated for prosthetically driven crown lengthening in esthetic zones, multitooth cases, interdisciplinary margin-symmetry cases. Contraindications: active periodontal disease, severe tooth mobility, poor guide fit, absent digital infrastructure.
- **Laser-assisted procedures (Level 3/Grade C)**: diode and Er:YAG lasers offer improved precision, effective hemostasis, reduced edema/pain/bleeding for soft-tissue contouring; limited evidence for osseous cutting; certified training and technique sensitivity required.
- **Diagnostic anchors**: periodontal clinical + radiographic evaluation Level 1/Grade A (CBCT improves precision); 2017 periodontal/peri-implant disease classification Level 1/Grade A as standardized framework; **CBCT soft-tissue retraction only Level 3/Grade C** (limited clinical validation).
- **Adjunctive strategies**: deep margin elevation Level 2/Grade B (facilitates provisional stability; limited long-term data); pre-surgical periodontal stabilization Level 1/Grade A mandatory for predictable healing.
- **Frontier**: integration of AI with CBCT and digital scanning for personalized planning.

## Clinical Implications

1. Diagnose STA violation with clinical + radiographic evaluation and bone sounding before any restorative margin decision; CBCT adds precision but its soft-tissue visualization remains weakly validated.
2. Choose the surgical platform by indication: conventional surgery for broad applicability, digital guides for esthetic/interdisciplinary crown lengthening, lasers for minimally invasive soft-tissue contouring — no single modality dominates.
3. Never skip pre-surgical periodontal stabilization; plan for the physiological 2.73–3.0 mm STA dimension regardless of technique.
