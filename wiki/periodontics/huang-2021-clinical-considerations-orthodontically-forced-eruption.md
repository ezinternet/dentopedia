## Three-line Summary

Narrative review of orthodontic forced eruption (extrusion) as alternative to resective crown lengthening when caries/fracture violates biologic width or ferrule is inadequate

Preserves supporting bone on adjacent teeth (vs osseous surgery), improves crown-to-root ratio, avoids recession/sensitivity; requires fiberotomy to prevent supracrestal tissue relocation; Salama extraction-site + Hochman soft-tissue classification guide case selection

Extrusion protocol ~1-2 mm/month with light forces (15g anterior / 50g posterior); contraindicated in active periodontitis (coronal relocation only, no attachment gain) and Salama Type 3 sites (severe vertical defect → GTR)

## Summary

This narrative review consolidates when **orthodontic forced eruption (extrusion)** should be used as a restorative adjunct instead of resective crown lengthening. When caries, horizontal fracture, resorption, or perforation invades the supracrestal tissue attachment (biologic width) — or when a full-coverage restoration lacks an adequate ferrule (target ≈1.5–2 mm ferrule plus ~4.5 mm supra-alveolar tooth structure, of which ~2 mm is biologic width) — the crown must be lengthened. Compared with osseous surgery, which resects bone from the adjacent teeth to build positive architecture, forced eruption preserves supporting bone, spares neighboring teeth, avoids recession/sensitivity, and yields a better crown-to-root ratio and esthetics. The two clinical goals require opposite tissue responses: for **ferrule/crown lengthening** the bone and gingiva must *not* follow the tooth, so supracrestal **fiberotomy** is done to limit osteoid; for **implant site development** the bone *should* follow, as PDL tension deposits crestal bone (Salama 1993). The review details indications and contraindications (absolute contraindication: absence of a healthy attachment apparatus, i.e., active periodontitis, where extrusion causes only coronal relocation with clinical attachment loss), the Salama extraction-site classification (Type 1/2/3; Type 2 convertible to Type 1; Type 3 contraindicated → GTR), the Hochman soft-tissue classification, appliance options (fixed vs clear aligner), and an extrusion protocol of ~1–2 mm/month with light forces (≈15 g anterior / 50 g posterior).

## Key Contributions

- Positions forced eruption as a **conservative alternative to resective crown lengthening** for biologic-width (SCTA) violation and inadequate ferrule.
- Clarifies the **opposite tissue goals**: fiberotomy to *stop* tissue following (crown lengthening) vs PDL tension to *pull* bone up (implant site development).
- Summarizes **Salama 1993** extraction-site classes and **Hochman** soft-tissue-response classes for case selection.
- Gives a usable protocol: 1–2 mm/month, ~15 g/50 g forces, fixed vs aligner appliances, 1–6 month stabilization.

## Methodology

- Narrative literature review (no PRISMA protocol, no meta-analysis) integrating historical, clinical, and animal evidence.
- Structured by indication (ferrule/crown lengthening; implant site development), plus biology of extrusion and methodology/efficacy.
- Evidence cited includes animal models (Berglundh 1991 dog: 4.5 mm extrusion/16 wk, PD unchanged, recession 0.5 mm) and a prior systematic review (Somar: 0.5–1 mm/month, bone-augmentation efficacy 69–100%).

## Results

| Dimension | Forced eruption | Resective crown lengthening |
|---|---|---|
| Supporting bone | Preserved | Resected (also adjacent teeth) |
| Adjacent teeth | Spared | Involved (positive architecture) |
| Recession/sensitivity | Avoided | Possible |
| Crown-to-root ratio | Improved (e.g., 4:5→4:4) | Worsened (e.g., 5:4) |
| Treatment time | Prolonged | Short |
| Added procedures | Fiberotomy ± later crown lengthening; possible endo | Single surgery |

**Contraindications**: active periodontal inflammation (absolute — extrusion → coronal relocation + CAL), ankylosis, hypercementosis, vertical root fracture, root proximity/furcation, extremely short roots, insufficient prosthetic space. **Salama Type 3** sites (loss of labial plate, severe vertical/buccolingual defect) are contraindicated → guided tissue regeneration.
