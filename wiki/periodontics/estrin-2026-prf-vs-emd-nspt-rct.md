## Three-line Summary

Split-mouth RCT (Oral Health Prev Dent 2026): 15 patients, Stage II/III periodontitis; PRF vs EMD as adjuncts to NSPT (SRP) at 6 months — first direct comparison of these two biologics.

Both PRF and EMD significantly improved CAL, PPD, and BOP from baseline; no statistically significant difference between the two materials for any outcome.

PRF and EMD are interchangeable as biologic adjuncts to NSPT in moderate periodontitis — selection may be based on cost, availability, and handling characteristics.

## Summary

Estrin et al. (Oral Health Prev Dent 2026) conducted a split-mouth RCT in 15 patients with Stage II/III, Grade A/B periodontitis comparing adjunctive PRF and EMD applied during NSPT. SRP was performed using ultrasonic instruments, hand curettes, and adjunctive laser (Fotona), with EMD or PRF randomly assigned to contralateral quadrant pairs. At 6 months, both PRF and EMD produced statistically significant improvements in CAL, PPD, and BOP from baseline, with no significant difference between the two biologics on any outcome. The authors note that PRF provides a practical advantage when moisture control is difficult (no root conditioning required), while EMD is ideally applied to conditioned, relatively dry root surfaces. Both are effective adjuncts regardless of these handling differences.

## Key Contributions

- First RCT directly comparing PRF vs EMD as NSPT adjuncts (rather than vs control)
- Comparable efficacy: both improve CAL, PPD, BOP; no significant between-group difference
- EMD effective flaplessly — biologic advantage persists even without surgical access
- Clinical implication: interchangeable; selection based on clinician preference, cost, patient factors
- Split-mouth design provides strong internal validity despite small n

## Methodology

Split-mouth RCT; private practice (Florida, USA); Dec 2024–Apr 2025; n=15 Stage II/III Grade A/B periodontitis; SRP + EMD vs SRP + PRF (contralateral quadrant allocation); Fotona laser adjunct; 6-month follow-up; outcomes: PD, GR, CAL, BOP.
