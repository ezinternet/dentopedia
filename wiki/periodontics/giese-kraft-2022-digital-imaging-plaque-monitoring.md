## Three-line Summary

Prospective in vivo study (n=20 healthy adults, Germany) comparing clinical RMNPI plaque scoring against plaque scores derived from 2D intraoral-camera images and 3D intraoral-scanner images, at habitual plaque, after 72h no oral hygiene, and after habitual brushing.

Both 2D and 3D image-based plaque scores tracked the same significant within-subject changes as the clinical reference (each p<0.0001), with substantial-to-almost-perfect Kappa agreement overall (2D agreed slightly better than 3D, especially on oral/lingual surfaces where scanner-head geometry limited visualization).

Intraoral 2D images give more precise plaque scoring but are slow to acquire per tooth; intraoral scanners image the whole arch quickly with easy multi-angle review at some cost to image sharpness — both are reliable enough for research and clinical plaque documentation, but the study is limited to young subjects with minimal restorations/recession.

## 세줄요약

전향적 생체내 연구(Prospective in vivo study, n=20명 건강 성인, 독일): 임상 러스토기 변형 네이비 치태지수(Rustogi Modification of Navy Plaque Index, RMNPI)와 2D 구강내 카메라 영상·3D 구강스캐너(Intraoral Scanner) 영상 기반 치태 점수를 습관적 치태, 72시간 구강위생 중단 후, 습관적 칫솔질 후 세 시점에서 비교.

2D·3D 영상 기반 치태 점수 모두 임상 기준과 동일한 유의한 시점 간 변화를 보였고(각 p<0.0001), 전체적으로 substantial~almost perfect 수준의 카파(Kappa) 일치도를 보였다(2D가 3D보다 약간 우수, 특히 스캐너 헤드 형태 제약이 있는 구개/설측면에서 3D 일치도 저하).

2D 구강내 영상은 더 정밀한 치태 판정이 가능하지만 치아당 촬영 시간이 오래 걸리고, 구강스캐너는 전체 치열을 빠르게 스캔하고 다각도 검토가 쉬운 대신 영상 선명도가 다소 떨어진다 — 두 방법 모두 연구·임상 치태 문서화에 신뢰할 만하나, 젊고 수복물/치은퇴축이 적은 대상자로 제한된 연구.

## Summary

Chairside plaque indices (like RMNPI) score plaque as it is disclosed, but the result is only a number recorded at the moment of examination — it cannot be re-inspected, re-coded into another index, or revisited for a new research question later. This prospective in vivo study in 20 healthy German adults tested whether disclosed plaque can instead be captured and scored reliably as permanent digital images: 2D images from an intraoral camera (Carestream CS 1500) and 3D scans from an intraoral scanner (Carestream CS 3600), using the clinical RMNPI examination as the reference standard. Plaque was measured at three time points that intentionally span a wide range — habitual levels, after 72h without oral hygiene, and after a subsequent habitual toothbrushing — to test not just detection but monitoring of change over time.

Both digital methods reproduced the same statistically significant plaque trajectory seen clinically (rise after abstention, fall after brushing, each p<0.0001), and agreement with the clinical reference was substantial to almost perfect by Kappa statistics for most of the 9 RMNPI areas. 2D images agreed slightly better than 3D overall; the gap was concentrated on oral (lingual) surfaces, where intraoral-scanner-head geometry made some areas harder to capture, dragging 3D agreement down to only moderate (κ=0.506) or fair (κ=0.269) in two areas. A small, clinically minor systematic bias toward higher plaque percentages on both image modalities (vs. clinical exam) was attributed to image magnification and idealized illumination rather than a true measurement flaw.

## Key Contributions

- Validates digital 2D (intraoral camera) and 3D (intraoral scanner) images as reliable substitutes for chairside-only RMNPI plaque scoring, enabling permanent, re-analyzable plaque documentation.
- Shows the specific failure mode of 3D scan-based plaque scoring: oral/lingual surfaces suffer more than vestibular surfaces because of scanner-head size/geometry, dropping agreement from "substantial" to "moderate/fair" in some areas.
- Quantifies a small but consistent upward bias of image-based scores relative to clinical examination via Bland-Altman analysis, useful for anyone comparing absolute plaque percentages across these three measurement modes.
- Establishes intraoral-scanner-based plaque scoring (a fast, whole-arch, rotatable 3D record) as clinically usable despite lower image sharpness than 2D — foundational for later scan-based plaque metrics such as PSPI in [[interdental-cleaning/jung-2025-flossing-performance-plaque-removal]].

## Methodology

- Prospective, single-arm, repeated-measures in vivo study; n=20 (16F/4M), mean age 27.5±1.2y, convenience sample of healthy young adults with full dentition and minimal restorations.
- Three visits: T1 habitual plaque (baseline) → T2 after 72h without any oral hygiene → T3 after subsequent habitual toothbrushing.
- Plaque disclosed with Mira-2-Ton solution; RMNPI scored on Ramfjord teeth (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44), vestibular + oral surfaces, 9 dichotomous (0/1) areas per surface.
- Immediately after clinical scoring: 4 intraoral 2D photos per tooth (CS 1500) and one full-arch 3D scan (CS 3600), scored separately by 2 calibrated examiners on a standardized monitor via CS Mesh Viewer.
- Reliability tracked with Cohen's Kappa (inter- and intra-examiner) at each time point; agreement between methods assessed with Kappa per RMNPI area and with Bland-Altman plots (bias + proportional-bias regression) against the clinical reference.

## Results

- RMNPI positive-area percentages (mean±SD, all surfaces): T1 clinical 62.1(10.6) / 2D 65.1(10.0) / 3D 64.4(10.6); T2 76.9(8.0) / 77.9(8.6) / 77.5(9.4); T3 56.3(11.1) / 58.2(12.1) / 61.2(10.8) — all three methods detected the same significant rise (T1→T2) and fall (T2→T3), each p<0.0001.
- Vestibular surfaces showed larger plaque swings than oral surfaces across all methods; the upper first molar vestibular surface showed the numerically largest increase after 72h abstention.
- Kappa agreement (pooled): 2D vs. clinical 0.681–0.909 across the 9 areas; 3D vs. clinical 0.269 (fair, oral area D) to 0.864 (almost perfect, area E); 2D vs. 3D 0.634–0.919 — 2D agreed with the clinical reference slightly better than 3D overall.
- Bland-Altman bias vs. clinical exam: 2D −0.5±2.1 (oral, n.s.) / −1.5±2.2 (vestibular, p<0.001); 3D −2.6±4.9 (oral, p<0.001) / −1.5±2.4 (vestibular, p<0.001); a small proportional bias existed only for 3D vestibular scores (overestimate at low, underestimate at high plaque levels).
- Inter-/intra-examiner Kappa stayed in the 0.72–0.97 range throughout, confirming adequate calibration for both image modalities and the clinical exam.
