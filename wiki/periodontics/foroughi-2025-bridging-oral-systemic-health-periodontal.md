## Three-line Summary

Narrative review (PubMed/Scopus/Google Scholar synthesis) mapping periodontal disease's systemic associations across seven conditions — CVD, diabetes, adverse pregnancy, Alzheimer's, GI/oral cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, respiratory infection — and the diagnostic-technology landscape for chairside biomarker detection.

Key biomarker compartments: saliva (IL-1β, TNF-α, MMPs, microbial DNA), blood (CRP, IL-6, AGEs), and GCF (RANKL:OPG, MMP-8/-9); pooled estimates from cited literature include 12% higher CHD risk, 3× respiratory pneumonia risk, and GI cancer risk elevations of +9–39%.

AI-integrated multiplex point-of-care platforms (lateral flow assays, microfluidics/lab-on-a-chip, biosensors) are proposed for comprehensive early chairside risk stratification, but these associations remain observational and causation is unproven; no cut-offs are standardized for oral-fluid biomarkers.

## Summary

This is a broad narrative review (Foroughi et al., *Infection* 2025) tying periodontal disease to systemic health and, most usefully for this wiki, mapping the **biomarker and diagnostic-technology landscape** that could bring periodontal/systemic risk assessment to the chairside. Being a narrative review, its associations are illustrative rather than pooled — causation is explicitly left unproven (confounding, reverse causation, need for longitudinal interventional trials).

The clinical throughline: periodontal dysbiosis and chronic inflammation raise circulating inflammatory mediators (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, CRP) and disseminate pathogens (Pg, Fn, Td, Aa, Tf) via bacteremia, which are then implicated across cardiovascular disease, diabetes, adverse pregnancy outcomes, Alzheimer's, GI/oral cancers, rheumatoid arthritis, and respiratory infection. The diagnostic argument is that these same mediators are measurable non-invasively in **saliva, blood, and GCF**, and that current single-parameter labside assays (ELISA, PCR) should be complemented by **multiplex point-of-care platforms** integrating AI.

## Key Contributions

- **Compartmentalized biomarker map.** Saliva: IL-1β, TNF-α, MMPs, microbial DNA (non-invasive, accessible). Blood: CRP, IL-6, AGEs (systemic inflammatory load; fall after periodontal treatment). **GCF: RANKL, OPG, MMP-8, MMP-9** — the RANKL:OPG ratio is presented as a bone-turnover marker reflecting osteoclast-driven resorption, collectable non-invasively for monitoring inflammation and bone destruction.
- **Sensitivity/specificity framing.** Salivary IL-1β and TNF-α are high-sensitivity markers for active inflammation (useful for early detection) but lose specificity under systemic inflammation; the GCF RANKL:OPG ratio is reliably specific for bone resorption/progression; CRP+IL-6 combined is a significant CVD predictor.
- **Labside vs chairside taxonomy.** ELISA and PCR are the accuracy gold standards but slow, costly, centralized, and single-parameter. Lateral flow assays, microfluidics/lab-on-a-chip, and biosensors trade some specificity for speed, portability, low sample volume, and multiplexing.
- **Case for AI-integrated multiplex devices.** Simultaneous detection of cytokines + microbial DNA + bone-resorption markers on one smartphone-linked portable platform is proposed as the route to comprehensive early detection and personalized risk stratification.

## Methodology

Narrative synthesis of PubMed/Medline, Scopus, and Google Scholar — landmark studies, recent advances, and representative evidence selected for a broad overview. No systematic search, no PRISMA, no meta-analytic pooling, and no formal risk-of-bias appraisal. The review generates no new data; all quantitative figures are reproduced from cited primary/secondary sources. Full text retrieved from PMC.

## Results

**Systemic association estimates (from cited sources, not newly pooled):**
- CVD: 5-cohort meta-analysis (n=86,092) → 12% higher CHD risk; case–control (n=1,423) → OR 2.22; Pg/Aa/Tf DNA detected in atheromatous plaques.
- Diabetes: diabetics ~3× more likely to develop periodontitis; type 1 diabetes periodontitis 58% vs 15% (non-diabetic).
- Cancer: GI cancer risk elevated — esophageal +39%, gastric +13%, colorectal +21%, pancreatic +35%, liver +9%.
- Respiratory: periodontitis → ~3× nosocomial pneumonia risk; dental plaque is a genetically confirmed reservoir for respiratory pathogens.

**Diagnostics:**
- **Labside** — ELISA: antibody–antigen, colorimetric/fluorescent/chemiluminescent readout, high specificity/reproducibility, slow, centralized. PCR: high-sensitivity pathogen-DNA detection (Pg, Td, Fn), contamination-sensitive, expensive.
- **Chairside** — LFAs: immunochromatographic strips, results in minutes, inexpensive, lower specificity than ELISA/PCR (commercial exemplars OraQuick, BinaxNOW). Microfluidics/lab-on-a-chip: multiplex, microliter sample volumes (GeneXpert and similar). Biosensors: bio-recognition element + transducer, real-time, miniaturizable/wireless (Simoa, i-STAT).

**Treatment impact on systemic markers:** SRP (gold-standard non-surgical) lowers CRP and IL-6; adjuncts include chlorhexidine chips, minocycline microspheres, systemic amoxicillin+metronidazole for aggressive/refractory disease, surgery (flap/GTR/graft) for advanced cases, and host-modulation with sub-antimicrobial-dose doxycycline; LANAP offered as a minimally invasive option.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors:** observational associations without established causation; biomarker translation hampered by inter-individual variability, absent standardized cut-offs, sample-collection heterogeneity, and research-only validation; chairside platforms need cost/manufacturing/reliability improvement and regulatory clearance.
