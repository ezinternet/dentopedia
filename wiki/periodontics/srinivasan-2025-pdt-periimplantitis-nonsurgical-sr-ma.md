## Three-line Summary

SR+MA of 25 studies evaluating photodynamic therapy (PDT) as adjunct to non-surgical mechanical debridement (MD) for peri-implantitis; at 3 months, MD+PDT provided significantly greater improvements than MD alone in BOP (SMD=0.90, p=0.005), PD (SMD=1.15, p<0.0001), PI (SMD=0.74, p=0.0004), and crestal bone loss (CBL SMD=0.38, p=0.04).

Benefits were less pronounced for CAL, mucosal recession, and inflammatory biomarkers; at extended follow-up periods, no significant additional advantages of PDT were maintained — the adjunct benefit is short-term and site-specific rather than sustained or systemic.

Complements the [[wiki/implants/peri-implantitis/jervoe-storm-2024-photodynamic-therapy-periodontal-periimplant-diseases]] Cochrane review (which identified a peri-implantitis evidence gap) by specifically quantifying PDT's short-term clinical benefit in this population using 25 studies.

## Background

Peri-implantitis, characterized by inflammation and progressive bone loss around dental implants, is increasingly prevalent and difficult to manage. The [[wiki/implants/peri-implantitis/jervoe-storm-2024-photodynamic-therapy-periodontal-periimplant-diseases]] Cochrane review (2024) found only one small peri-implant mucositis study and identified peri-implantitis evidence as a critical gap. This 2025 SR+MA specifically fills that gap with 25 studies focused on non-surgical management.

Antimicrobial photodynamic therapy (aPDT) works by activating a photosensitizing agent (typically toluidine blue, methylene blue, or indocyanine green) with a specific wavelength light source, generating reactive oxygen species that kill bacteria in the biofilm without antibiotic resistance pressure.

## Methods

- **Search**: PubMed, Web of Science, CENTRAL; literature to November 2023; 4675 screened
- **Included**: 25 studies (RCTs and other designs)
- **Outcomes**: BOP, PD/PPD, PI, CBL, CAL, mucosal recession, inflammatory biomarkers
- **Comparator**: MD alone or other non-surgical treatments

## Results

### 3-Month Follow-up (MD+PDT vs MD alone — RCT meta-analysis)
| Outcome | SMD | 95% CI | p |
|---------|-----|---------|---|
| BOP | 0.90 | −1.53 to −0.28 | 0.005 |
| PD | 1.15 | −1.72 to −0.58 | <0.0001 |
| PI | 0.74 | −1.15 to −0.33 | 0.0004 |
| CBL | 0.38 | −0.73 to −0.03 | 0.04 |

### Less Pronounced Benefits
- CAL: less pronounced additional effect
- Mucosal recession: limited additional effect
- Inflammatory biomarkers: limited additional effect

### Baseline vs Follow-up
- Both MD alone AND MD+PDT: significant improvement from baseline
- PDT advantage: primarily in short-term 3-month window

### Extended Follow-up
- No significant additional PDT advantages maintained at longer follow-up

## Clinical Takeaway

Adjunct PDT can meaningfully improve short-term peri-implant clinical parameters during non-surgical management of peri-implantitis, particularly BOP, probing depth, and crestal bone stability at 3 months. The benefit is not sustained at extended follow-up and does not extend reliably to CAL. Given the absence of antibiotic resistance concerns and the low adverse effect profile, PDT is a reasonable adjunct in the active non-surgical phase of peri-implantitis management, but should not replace or substitute surgical approaches in advanced disease.
