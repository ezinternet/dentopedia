## Three-line Summary

Prospective single-arm (before–after) 5-month oral health program in nine schoolchildren (six girls; grades 1–6) without untreated decayed teeth living in a children's home in Japan, combining group and individual instruction with 3 months of weekly self-checking using plaque disclosing solution.

Mean Plaque Control Record (PCR) fell from 66.1% at baseline and 60.7% at 1 month to 38.7% after 3 months of self-checking (P = 0.009 and P = 0.007, paired t-test), and Patient Hygiene Performance (PHP) fell from 2.8 at baseline and 2.7 at 1 month to 1.4 at 4 months (P = 0.012 and P = 0.018, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

Self-checking with disclosing solution may improve oral hygiene among schoolchildren in a children's home, but the study is small (n = 9), uncontrolled, and the improvement ratio did not correlate with school grade (r = 0.63, P = 0.070).

## Summary

This prospective, single-arm (before–after) 5-month program from Hiroshima University tested whether self-checking with a plaque disclosing solution improves oral hygiene in nine schoolchildren (six girls, grades 1–6) without untreated decay who were living in a Japanese children's home. After baseline group education and individual toothbrushing instruction at 1 month, the children self-checked their plaque with a disclosing agent once a week for 3 months, with PCR and PHP recorded weekly by a hygienist. Mean PCR fell from 66.1% at baseline (60.7% at 1 month) to 38.7% after 3 months of self-checking (P = 0.009 and P = 0.007), and PHP fell from 2.8 (2.7 at 1 month) to 1.4 (P = 0.012 and P = 0.018). The average improvement ratio was 0.40 ± 0.35 and did not correlate significantly with school grade (r = 0.63, P = 0.070). The authors conclude that self-checking with disclosing solution may be effective in this population, while noting the absence of a control group and the small sample as key limits.

## Key Contributions

- First study, to the authors' knowledge, evaluating the effect of self-checking of oral health among children in a children's home.
- Demonstrates a significant reduction in both PCR (66.1%/60.7% → 38.7%) and PHP (2.8/2.7 → 1.4) after 3 months of weekly disclosing-solution self-checking alongside instruction.
- Reports an improvement ratio of 0.40 ± 0.35 with no significant grade–improvement correlation.

## Methodology

- **Design**: prospective single-arm (before–after) interventional program, no control group; 5 months; 2016–2017.
- **Participants**: n = 9 schoolchildren (6 girls) without untreated decayed teeth from one children's home in Japan; grades 1 (n = 2), 3 (1), 4 (3), 5 (1), 6 (2).
- **Program**: baseline group education (20-min play + teaching model) → individual toothbrushing instruction at 1 month → 3 months of weekly self-checking with disclosing solution after brushing (PCR/PHP recorded weekly, no individual instruction) → individual instruction again → final report at 5 months. All instruction by one dental hygienist.
- **Outcomes**: PCR and PHP; self-administered oral health questionnaire.
- **Statistics**: paired t-test, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Cochran's Q, Friedman, McNemar, Mann–Whitney U, Pearson's correlation; JMP Pro 12; P < 0.05.
- **Ethics**: Hiroshima University Ethical Committee No. 1814; informed consent from participants and guardians.

- PCR decreased gradually over the first 5 weeks of the 3-month self-checking period, with no characteristic changes afterward.
- PCR and PHP were lower in girls than boys at 4 months but without a significant difference.
- Questionnaire: 6/9 (66.7%) brushed three times daily throughout; using a mirror was 55.6% → 66.7% → 44.4%; monthly toothbrush replacement rose from 18.2% to 55.6% (n.s.); toothpaste-every-time rose 88.9% → 100% (P = 0.37); fluoride toothpaste use 44.4% → 66.7% → 55.6% (P = 0.47); dental floss was a common extra appliance among six children at 4 months.
