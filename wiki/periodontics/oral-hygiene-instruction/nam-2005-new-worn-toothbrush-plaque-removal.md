## Three-line Summary

This single-center, single-examiner blind, randomized, crossover controlled clinical trial in 40 healthy university students (mean age 25.1 years) compared the single-use plaque removal efficacy of new versus 3-month-old naturally worn manual toothbrushes using the Patient Hygiene Performance (PHP) index.

New toothbrushes achieved significantly greater plaque reduction than 3-month-old brushes for whole-mouth sites (58.0% vs 42.9%, p=0.0001), proximal sites (45.2% vs 24.6%, p=0.0001), and marginal sites (54.0% vs 37.8%, p=0.0001), representing 15.1%, 20.6%, and 16.3% greater mean reductions respectively.

Worn toothbrushes are significantly less effective for plaque removal, particularly in proximal and marginal areas, supporting the recommendation that toothbrushes be replaced every 3 months to maintain optimal plaque control.

## Summary

This single-center, examiner-blind, randomized crossover study compared the single-use plaque removal efficacy of new versus naturally worn toothbrushes. Forty healthy university students (mean age 25.1 years) with pocket depths ≤3mm and all six Ramfjord teeth present participated. Subjects used the same toothbrush for 3 months to create worn brushes, then were randomized to either New/Worn or Worn/New sequence. At experimental visits, subjects refrained from brushing for 48 hours to allow plaque accumulation, then brushed with the assigned brush using their habitual technique. Plaque was scored using the PHP index before and after brushing. Brushing surface area was measured digitally to quantify wear. New brushes achieved significantly greater plaque reductions than worn brushes for whole-mouth (58.0% vs 42.9%), proximal (45.2% vs 24.6%), and marginal sites (54.0% vs 37.8%), all p=0.0001. No significant differences were found for non-proximal (p=0.071) or non-marginal sites (p=0.317). The mean increase in brushing surface area for 3-month-old brushes was 50.6% (range 0.2-112.1%). Linear correlations between wear and plaque reduction (r=-0.58 for whole-sites, r=-0.50 for marginal sites) were not statistically significant. The authors concluded that worn toothbrushes are significantly less effective for plaque removal, particularly in proximal and marginal areas, supporting the 3-month replacement recommendation.

## Key Contributions

- New toothbrushes showed significantly greater plaque reduction than 3-month-old worn brushes for whole-mouth, proximal, and marginal sites.
- Proximal site plaque reduction was 20.6% greater with new versus worn brushes (45.2% vs 24.6%, p=0.0001).
- Whole-mouth and marginal site reductions were 15.1% and 16.3% greater with new brushes.
- No significant differences in non-proximal or non-marginal sites, suggesting wear primarily affects hard-to-reach areas.
- Brushing surface area increased by mean 50.6% after 3 months, with wide individual variation.
- Correlation between wear and efficacy was not statistically significant due to confounding factors (brushing habits, technique, force).

## Methodology

This was a single-center, single-examiner blind, randomized, crossover controlled clinical trial. Forty-two healthy subjects were initially recruited from Seoul National University's Dental College, with 40 completing the study (27 males, 13 females; mean age 25.1 years, range 23-37). Inclusion criteria required presence of all six Ramfjord teeth (16, 21, 24, 36, 41, 44), pocket depth ≤3mm, no crowns/prostheses/orthodontic appliances, and no restorations involving study tooth surfaces. At visit 1, subjects received identical toothbrushes (Oral-B Advantage®) and were instructed to use them for 3 months. At visit 2 (3 months later), subjects were randomly assigned to two sequences: Group I (New/Worn, n=19) and Group II (Worn/New, n=21). Professional plaque and calculus removal established baseline plaque score of 0, followed by 48 hours of no brushing for plaque accumulation. At visit 3, plaque was scored, subjects brushed with assigned brush using familiar technique, and plaque was re-scored. A 2-week washout period separated experimental periods. At visit 4, professional plaque control was performed, and visit 5 repeated the protocol with the opposite brush. Plaque was assessed using the PHP index with erythrosin solution disclosure. Five sections per tooth surface (mesial, distal, middle third subdivided into gingival, middle, occlusal) were scored. Sites analyzed included whole, proximal (mesial+distal), marginal (proximal+gingival), non-proximal, and non-marginal. Toothbrush wear was quantified by measuring brushing surface area increase from standardized digital photographs using image analysis software. Statistical analysis used paired t-tests and Pearson's correlation, with two-sided p<0.05 considered significant.

Both new and worn toothbrushes demonstrated statistically significant reductions in whole-mouth, proximal, and marginal plaque from pre- to post-brushing (all p=0.0001). New brushes achieved significantly greater mean plaque reductions than worn brushes for whole-mouth (15.1% greater), proximal (20.6% greater), and marginal sites (16.3% greater), with all differences statistically significant (p=0.0001).
