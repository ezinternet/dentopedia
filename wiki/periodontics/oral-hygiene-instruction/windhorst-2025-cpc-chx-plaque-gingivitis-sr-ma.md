## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the effectiveness of cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) mouthwash versus chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthwash on plaque and gingivitis scores in 14 RCTs (18 comparisons) from both brushing and non-brushing situations. In non-brushing studies, CHX-MW showed a small statistically significant favourable effect over CPC-MW for plaque index (WMD = 0.55, p = 0.003, 95% CI: 0.19 to 0.91). In brushing studies, no significant differences were found between CPC-MW and CHX-MW for plaque (WMD = 0.04, p = 0.11) or gingival index (WMD = 0.02, p = 0.20).

There is moderate-certainty evidence that CPC-MW can serve as a substitute for CHX-MW in brushing situations, but CHX-MW remains superior when plaque control is the main focus without brushing.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis compared the effectiveness of cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) and chlorhexidine (CHX) mouthwashes on plaque and gingivitis scores. Fourteen randomized controlled trials (18 comparisons) were included, with 12 non-brushing and 6 brushing comparisons. In non-brushing studies, CHX-MW showed a small statistically significant advantage over CPC-MW for plaque index (WMD = 0.55, p = 0.003), with considerable heterogeneity (I² = 93%). Subgroup analyses by concentration consistently favoured CHX. In brushing studies, no significant differences were observed for plaque (WMD = 0.04, p = 0.11) or gingival index (WMD = 0.02, p = 0.20). Staining was less with CPC than CHX. The authors concluded that CPC-MW can serve as a substitute for CHX-MW in brushing situations, while CHX-MW remains the first choice for plaque control in non-brushing situations such as post-surgical wound healing.

## Key Contributions

- CHX mouthwash superior to CPC in non-brushing plaque control (WMD = 0.55, p = 0.003)
- No significant difference between CPC and CHX in brushing situations for plaque or gingivitis
- CPC causes less tooth staining than CHX (reported in 2 studies)
- CPC-MW is a reliable long-term alternative to CHX-MW for plaque/gingivitis prevention
- CHX-MW remains first choice for post-surgical wound healing where brushing is not possible

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA and Cochrane Handbook guidelines with a registered protocol (PROSPERO CRD42023444797). A structured search was conducted in MEDLINE-PubMed and Cochrane-CENTRAL up to May 2023. Eligible studies were RCTs or CCTs comparing CPC-MW versus CHX-MW in adult patients (≥18 years) with ≥20 teeth, in brushing or non-brushing models. Fourteen publications providing 18 comparisons were included. Risk of bias was assessed using Cochrane RoB2 tool (1 low risk, 3 moderate, 10 high risk). Meta-analysis used random-effects models (or fixed-effects for <3 studies) with WMD and 95% CI. Heterogeneity was assessed with I². GRADE was used for evidence quality rating (moderate certainty).
