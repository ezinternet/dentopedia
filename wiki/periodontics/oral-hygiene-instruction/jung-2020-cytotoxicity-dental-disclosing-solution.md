## Three-line Summary

In-vitro study (cultured human gingival epithelial cells + mouse head/neck tissue + porcine skin) tested an erythrosine-based 2-Tone (Blue-Violet) disclosing solution at 200×/100×/50× dilutions.

At 50× dilution, the solution induced apoptosis (increased Sub G1 on flow cytometry, positive TUNEL, loss of intercellular adhesion, p < .001) and impaired wound healing; it stained nuclei, cytoplasm, soft tissue and cortical bone and was not removable by any tested drink/oral cleanser.

Erythrosine-based disclosing solutions are cytotoxic to gingival epithelium and difficult to decolorize, supporting a shift toward biocompatible alternatives and caution in inflamed, dry or poorly maintained mouths.

## Summary

This in-vitro study evaluated the cytotoxicity of a commercial 2-Tone (Blue-Violet) erythrosine-based dental disclosing solution using cultured human gingival epithelial cells, mouse head/neck tissue sections and porcine skin. The solution strongly stained gingival epithelial cell nuclei and cytoplasm, and broadly stained skeletal muscle, collagenous fibers, fatty marrow, hematopoietic tissue and cortical bone in mouse sections to an extent similar to H&E staining. In gingival epithelial cells, at a 1:50 dilution (v/v) the solution significantly impaired wound healing, reduced cell viability by MTT (p < .001), increased the apoptotic Sub G1 fraction on flow cytometry, produced positive TUNEL staining colocalized with DAPI and caused loss of intercellular adhesion with spindly/irregular morphology (p < .001). At higher dilutions (1:100, 1:200) effects were milder or absent. The stain applied to porcine skin was not removed by any of the ten tested drinks/oral cleansers (or absolute alcohol) better than distilled water. The authors conclude that erythrosine-based disclosing solutions are cytotoxic to gingival epithelium via apoptosis and are difficult to decolorize, and they advocate developing safer alternatives, pursuing easy-decolorizing methods (e.g., activated-carbon adsorption) and restricting use in patients with oral inflammation, oral dryness or poor oral hygiene.

## Key Contributions

- Broad tissue-staining range documented (cells, soft tissue and cortical bone) with similarity to H&E.
- Convergent evidence (MTT, flow cytometry Sub G1, TUNEL, morphology, wound healing) showing **apoptosis** as the cytotoxic mechanism in gingival epithelial cells.
- Quantitatively graded concentration-dependence: effects significant at **50× dilution** (1:50 v/v), minimal or absent at **100×** and **200×**.
- Irreversible/poor decolorizability: none of ten tested solutions outperformed distilled water.
- Practical safety recommendation: safer alternatives needed; expert guidance and caution in vulnerable patients.

## Methodology

- **Cells**: human gingival epithelial cells from a non-mucosal-disease wisdom-tooth extraction donor (Dept. of Oral Pathology, Yonsei University); DMEM:F-12 (3:1)+10% FBS, 37°C/5% CO2.
- **Agent**: 2-Tone (Blue-Violet) erythrosine-based disclosing solution (Young Dental Manufacturing); stock diluted 200×/100×/50× in complete medium (v/v).
- **Cell staining**: 5 min treatment, light microscopy.
- **Tissue staining**: deparaffinized mouse head/neck sections stained 5 min, washed in PBS (×3), compared to H&E.
- **Wound healing**: confluent monolayer scraped with sterile pipette tip; 24 h observation.
- **MTT**: 5 × 10³ cells/well, 24 h treatment, 5 mg/ml MTT 1 h, formazan in 200 µl DMSO, A570.
- **Cell cycle**: 24 h treatment, 70% ethanol fixation, PI 40 µg/ml + RNase 200 µg/ml, FACScan flow cytometry (CellQuest/Modfit LT).
- **TUNEL**: 1 × 10³ cells/well, 24 h treatment; fixed 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized Triton-X100; Alexa Fluor 647 phalloidin 4 h, Roche In Situ Cell Death Detection Kit, DAPI counterstain; fluorescence microscopy.
- **Decolorization**: porcine skin stained 5 min, excess absorbed; treated 30 min with agitation using ten solutions (drinking vinegar, orange juice, Listerine, milk, distilled water, green tea, Pocari Sweat, bottled water, toothpaste, alkaline water); density quantified by ImageJ.
- **Statistics**: means ± SD, n=3 experiments; one-way ANOVA + Tukey post-hoc; Mann–Whitney for TUNEL; p < .05.

## Results

- **Staining**: strong nuclear/chromosomal and cytoplasmic staining in gingival epithelial cells; broad staining of skeletal muscle, collagenous fibers, fatty marrow, cellular hematopoietic tissue and cortical bone in mouse head/neck sections — extent/degree comparable to H&E.
- **Wound healing (24 h)**: control fully healed; concentration-dependent delay; **wound-healing failure at 50× dilution** (p < .001 vs control).
- **Cell viability (MTT, 24 h)**: similar to control at 200× and 100×; **significant reduction at 50×** (p < .001 vs control).
- **Cell cycle (flow cytometry)**: control-like at 200×; altered at 100×; **significant increase in Sub G1 (apoptotic) fraction at 50×**.
- **Morphology & TUNEL (50×)**: loss of intercellular adhesion, cells spindly/irregular; TUNEL-positive signal colocalized with DAPI-stained nuclei; **higher TUNEL signal than control** (p < .001).
- **Decolorization**: none of the ten tested solutions significantly bleached the stain more than distilled water (p > .05); absolute alcohol also ineffective (reported in discussion).
