## Three-line Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis included 13 RCTs (16 comparisons) evaluating chlorhexidine mouthwash with anti-discoloration system (CHX-MW+ADS) versus CHX-MW without ADS on tooth staining, plaque, and gingivitis in non-brushing and brushing models. In non-brushing studies, CHX-MW+ADS significantly reduced tooth staining (SMD = -3.19, p = 0.0005, 95% CI: -3.98 to -1.41) while showing no significant differences in plaque (DiffM = 0.10, p = 0.45) or gingival index (DiffM = 0.04, p = 0.15). In brushing studies, no significant differences were found for staining, plaque, or gingivitis between CHX-MW+ADS and CHX-MW.

There is moderate-quality evidence that ADS reduces tooth staining without compromising the anti-plaque and anti-gingivitis efficacy of CHX mouthwash in both non-brushing and brushing studies.

## Summary

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated whether chlorhexidine mouthwash with an anti-discoloration system (CHX-MW+ADS) reduces tooth staining without compromising the anti-plaque and anti-gingivitis efficacy of chlorhexidine. Thirteen randomized controlled trials (16 comparisons) were included, categorized into non-brushing (6 comparisons) and brushing (10 comparisons) studies. Meta-analysis demonstrated that in non-brushing studies, CHX-MW+ADS significantly reduced tooth staining compared to CHX-MW alone (SMD = -3.19, p = 0.0005), while no significant differences were observed for plaque index (DiffM = 0.10, p = 0.45) or gingival index (DiffM = 0.04, p = 0.15). In brushing studies, no significant differences were found for any outcome. The authors concluded that ADS effectively reduces CHX-induced tooth staining without affecting its clinical efficacy, providing moderate-quality evidence that challenges the "if it does not stain, it does not work" paradigm.

## Key Contributions

- ADS significantly reduces tooth staining from CHX mouthwash in non-brushing models (SMD = -3.19)
- ADS does not compromise CHX anti-plaque efficacy (plaque: DiffM = 0.10 in non-brushing, 0.01 in brushing)
- ADS does not compromise CHX anti-gingivitis efficacy (gingival index: DiffM = 0.04 in non-brushing, 0.00 in brushing)
- Moderate-quality evidence supports clinical use of CHX-MW+ADS to improve patient compliance
- The "if it does not stain, it does not work" assumption is challenged

## Methodology

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA and Cochrane Handbook guidelines. A structured search was conducted in MEDLINE-PubMed and Cochrane-CENTRAL from inception to October 2018 (updated February 2019). Eligible studies were RCTs or CCTs comparing CHX-MW+ADS versus CHX-MW without ADS, with daily rinsing ≥2×/day, reporting outcomes of discoloration, plaque, bleeding, or gingivitis. Thirteen publications providing 16 comparisons were included: 6 non-brushing and 10 brushing studies. Risk of bias was assessed using a customized 7-criteria checklist; most studies had low-moderate risk. Meta-analysis used random-effects models (or fixed-effects for <4 studies). Heterogeneity was assessed via Chi², I², and Tau². GRADE was used for evidence quality rating.
