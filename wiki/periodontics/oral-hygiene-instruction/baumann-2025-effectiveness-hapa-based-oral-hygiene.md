## Three-line Summary
RCT in 62 analyzed community-dwelling adults aged 65-87 (30 HAPA-based vs 32 standard instruction; 67 randomized, 7.5% drop-out) at one German university dental clinic, testing stage-tailored (Preintender/Intender/Actor) interdental-brush instruction under the Health Action Process Approach (HAPA), with a 12-week follow-up.
Gingival Bleeding Index (GBI) fell in both groups with no between-group difference (F(1,57)=0.15, p=.70); Plaque Control Record (PCR) fell only with HAPA (62.27 to 57.00%; control 59.47 to 60.69%; between-group p=.026, eta2=0.083), and self-reported interdental brush use rose from 4.30 to 9.93 times/week (eta2=0.61; control not measured).
Adding stage-tailored planning to standard interdental-brush instruction may help older adults' plaque control and interdental habits, but limits are a small sample, self-reported behavior in one arm only, no participant blinding, 12-week follow-up, and a motivated university-clinic sample.

## Summary
This single-center, two-arm RCT from Frankfurt compared standard interdental-brush-focused oral hygiene instruction with the same instruction adapted to Health Action Process Approach (HAPA) stages in adults aged 65 and older. Sixty-seven patients were randomized and 62 (30 HAPA, 32 control; age 65-87) were analyzed at 12 weeks. Gingival bleeding (GBI) improved in both groups with no between-group difference, whereas plaque (PCR) fell only in the HAPA group (62.27 to 57.00%, between-group p=.026, eta2=0.083). Self-reported interdental brush use in the HAPA arm doubled (4.30 to 9.93 times/week), accompanied by gains in self-efficacy and behavioral control. Small sample, self-reported behavior collected only in the intervention arm, unblinded participants, and a highly motivated university-clinic sample limit inference.

## Key Contributions
- First RCT in the wiki testing a behavior-theory (HAPA) instruction against standard instruction in patients 65+.
- Separates a plaque benefit (PCR, HAPA only) from a bleeding benefit (GBI, both groups equal), so standard instruction plus professional cleaning already improves gingival inflammation.
- Shows mediator shifts (action self-efficacy, behavioral control, planning) without change in intention or risk perception.

## Methodology
Parallel RCT, simple 1:1 randomization with sealed envelopes; follow-up examiners blinded. Inclusion: 65+, at least 10 interproximal spaces; exclusion: professional cleaning within 6 months, prior periodontal-department treatment. Both groups: about 65-min session, individually fitted interdental brushes, professional cleaning. HAPA group: 15-min questionnaire classifying Preintender/Intender/Actor (score 0-69), stage-tailored instruction, written personal goal. Outcomes GBI and PCR at 12 weeks (mean 12.29 weeks); ANCOVA adjusted for baseline and age; Bonferroni for multiple tests.

## Results
| Outcome | HAPA (n=30) | Control (n=32) | Between-group |
|---|---|---|---|
| GBI % | 9.5 to 5.70 (d=0.71) | 11.34 to 5.56 (d=0.65) | p=.70, eta2=0.003 |
| PCR % | 62.27 to 57.00 (d=0.42, p=.030) | 59.47 to 60.69 (n.s.) | p=.026, eta2=0.083 |
| Interdental brush use/week | 4.30 to 9.93 (eta2=0.61) | not measured | not testable |
| Stage distribution | Actor 13 to 20 (+53.8%); Preintender 3 to 0 | not applicable | chi2 p=.019 (within HAPA arm) |

Brushing frequency did not change (12.8 to 13.1/week, p=.482). Stage subgroups (n=3/14/13) showed no significant differences in GBI or PCR. No adverse events. Drop-out 7.5% (5/67).

## Clinical Insights
- For a 65+ patient, standard interdental-brush fitting plus professional cleaning already lowered GBI about as much as the HAPA version in this trial; the HAPA increment was on plaque (about 5 points) and self-reported habit.
- A practical takeaway is a brief stage questionnaire, a written personal goal and coping planning ("what obstacles, how will I counter them") added to the usual demonstration, but effect durability beyond 12 weeks is unknown and the behavioral gain lacks a control comparison.
- Recruits were motivated university-clinic patients with many teeth (mean 22.5 interproximal spaces); results may not extend to frail, cognitively impaired or dentally disadvantaged older adults.
