## Three-line Summary

Multi-institution Chinese expert consensus (Int J Oral Sci 2025) synthesizing epidemiology, histologic pathogenesis, predisposing factors, diagnostic criteria, and a location-based treatment decision tree for cemental tear — a cementum detachment at the cemento-dentinal junction distinct from vertical root fracture, primary endodontic disease, and periodontitis.

Prevalence is 0.89–1.9% by radiography; fragment location drives outcomes (coronal 60%, middle 66.7%, apical 11.1% healing); complete removal of torn fragments is the central therapeutic principle, with 94% functional tooth retention at 1 year after complete removal.

CBCT is the preferred imaging modality; histopathology is the gold standard; the consensus positions most cemental-tear teeth as retainable with regenerative surgery rather than extraction by default.

## Summary

Cemental tear is a partial or complete detachment of cementum along the cemento-dentinal junction (CDJ) or along incremental lines within the cementum body — a specific root-surface fracture of cementum that is easily misdiagnosed as vertical root fracture (VRF), primary endodontic disease, or periodontitis. This expert consensus, published in the *International Journal of Oral Science*, consolidates a fragmented case-report literature into a single clinical guideline: it frames pathogenesis as internal (structural weakness of the CDJ and secondary cementum) plus external (occlusal or dental trauma) factors, lists seven possible predisposing factors under an explicit Oxford CEBM Level-4 evidence caveat, and defines a diagnostic hierarchy of history → clinical exam → CBCT → histopathology (the gold standard). Management centers on complete removal of torn fragments, with a decision tree keyed to fragment location (cervical/middle/apical third) and to intrabony-defect features that select the regenerative approach (EMD, GTR, bone grafts). Prognosis is location-dependent, and 94% of teeth are functionally retained at 1 year after complete fragment removal — so active intervention, rather than extraction by default, is recommended.

## Key Contributions

- Provides the first standardized expert-consensus clinical guideline dedicated to cemental tear, addressing the low clinical awareness that drives misdiagnosis and unnecessary extraction.
- Establishes the histologic weak-point rationale: the CDJ (100–200 µm) with its 10–50 µm proteoglycan-rich hygroscopic layer, plus age-related loss of CDJ hygroscopicity and glycoprotein adhesion, predispose cementum to detachment.
- Supplies an explicit differential-diagnosis framework separating cemental tear from VRF, primary endodontic disease, and periodontitis, with radiographic and pulp-vitality discriminators.
- Introduces a location-based treatment decision tree linking fragment accessibility to non-surgical vs surgical care and to intrabony-defect–specific regeneration (depth/width/wall count thresholds).
- Quantifies prognosis: non-surgical cure 28.6%, surgical healing 57.7%, and 94% 1-year functional retention after complete removal, reframing many "hopeless" teeth as retainable.

## Methodology

Narrative expert-consensus review (not a systematic review or meta-analysis). The underlying evidence base is dominated by case reports and small retrospective series (Oxford CEBM Level 4). The consensus organizes existing knowledge across epidemiology, cementum histology, pathogenesis/predisposing factors, clinical/radiographic/histopathologic manifestations, diagnosis, differential diagnosis, treatment modalities, and outcomes, and derives its regenerative thresholds by extrapolation from general periodontal intrabony-defect regeneration literature rather than cemental-tear–specific trials.

## Results

- **Prevalence**: 0.89% (periapical radiographs of 4,629 teeth; Keskin et al.) to 1.9% (813 CBCT images; Özkan et al.), both from Turkey and both radiography-only (no clinical confirmation).
- **Anatomy**: cementum ~20–50 µm cervically, ~150–200 µm at the apex, thickening 3–5× with age; fragment detachment at CDJ 77.6% vs within cementum 22.4%.
- **Associated lesions**: periodontal ~86%, periapical ~65%; surrounding soft tissue granulation 92.3% vs cystic 7.7%.
- **Demographics**: male predominance in some series (77.5% Lin; 63% Lee) but non-significant in others (Keskin, Özkan); peaks after age 60.
- **Diagnosis**: "prickle-like"/"flake-like" radiopaque fragment near the root surface within a radiolucent lesion; small-FOV CBCT preferred over periapical film; histopathology is the definitive gold standard; methylene blue staining aids intraoperative fragment detection.
- **Treatment thresholds**: intrabony depth <3 mm → resective, ≥3 mm → regenerative (EMD/GTR); wall/width rules escalate to combined bone graft + biologics + GTR for 1-wall defects; apical regeneration favored when periapical defect ≥10 mm diameter or residual buccal plate ≤3 mm.
- **Outcomes**: non-surgical cure 28.6%; surgical healing 57.7%; location-based healing 60% (coronal third), 66.7% (middle third), 11.1% (apical third); 94% functional retention at 1 year after complete fragment removal.
