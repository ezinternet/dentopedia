## One-line Summary

Classic 1981 narrative review arguing that attempts to keratinize sulcular/junctional epithelium are unjustified because non-keratinized epithelia can also form permeability barriers, and because JE's undifferentiated state is essential for maintaining tooth attachment.

## Summary

In 1981, significant research effort was being directed at inducing keratinization of the sulcular epithelium on the assumption that a cornified surface would better resist penetration by bacterial antigens and toxins from the gingival sulcus. Squier argues against this rationale on two grounds. First, non-keratinized epithelia have been shown to resist penetration of certain substances — the permeability barrier is not solely a keratinization-dependent phenomenon. Second, and critically, the formation of a surface barrier is a concomitant of terminal epithelial differentiation, while epithelial attachment to the tooth surface (via hemidesmosomes and internal basal lamina) is a property of relatively undifferentiated epithelial cells. Attempting to induce JE to differentiate could therefore result in a loss of epithelial attachment — an unacceptable biological trade-off. The paper provides an important conceptual foundation for understanding why the JE remains non-keratinized: its undifferentiated state is a functional requirement, not a vulnerability.

## Key Contributions

- Challenges prevailing assumption that **keratinization improves sulcular epithelial barrier function**
- Establishes that **non-keratinized epithelia have barrier properties** — keratinization ≠ impermeability
- Identifies the fundamental conflict: **differentiation (barrier formation) vs undifferentiation (attachment function)** in JE
- Implies JE's fast renewal and non-keratinized state are **biologically necessary** — not defects to be corrected
- Foundational conceptual paper for periodontal biology of the sulcular region

## Results

- Non-keratinized epithelia can resist penetration of certain substances — barrier ≠ keratinization
- Epithelial **attachment to tooth** (JE function) requires undifferentiated cell state
- Inducing terminal differentiation in JE = risk of losing epithelial attachment
- Attempts to keratinize sulcular region are therefore **theoretically unjustified**
