## Three-line Summary

Double-blinded RCT, n=90 active-SLE patients with periodontitis (45 SRP+OHI vs 45 OHI-only), 3-month follow-up.

SRP group showed clinically meaningful periodontal improvement (PD −2.10mm, CAL −2.02mm) and a significant between-group CRP reduction (P=0.032), but SLEDAI change did not differ between groups (P=0.894).

Nonsurgical periodontal treatment reduces acute-phase inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, ESR trend) in SLE patients but does not demonstrably alter overall lupus disease activity index at 3 months — supports periodontal care as an adjunct, not a disease-activity-modifying intervention.

## Summary

This double-blinded RCT (n=90) tested whether nonsurgical periodontal treatment (scaling and root planing, SRP) affects serum inflammatory markers and disease activity in patients with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and coexisting periodontitis. Patients were randomized to SRP + oral hygiene instructions (OHI) (case, n=45) or OHI-only (control, n=45), with periodontal parameters (PD, CAL, bleeding index, plaque index), CRP, ESR, and SLEDAI measured at baseline and 3 months. The case group showed clinically meaningful periodontal improvement and a statistically significant reduction in CRP relative to control (P=0.032); ESR trended lower but did not reach between-group significance (P=0.180). SLEDAI decreased in both groups over the study period with no significant between-group difference (P=0.894 for the difference-in-difference), indicating periodontal treatment's anti-inflammatory effect did not translate into a detectable change in overall lupus disease activity at this timepoint.

## Key Contributions

- Adequately powered RCT (n=90) — roughly triple the sample size of the only comparable prior trial (Fabbri et al., n=32/17) — directly comparing SRP+OHI vs OHI-only in active SLE with periodontitis.
- Found periodontal treatment produced a statistically significant between-group CRP reduction (P=0.032), contradicting Fabbri et al.'s null CRP/ESR finding.
- Found SLEDAI improved in both groups with no significant between-group difference, contradicting Fabbri et al.'s finding of a significant SLEDAI benefit from periodontal treatment — an open contradiction in this small literature.
- Documents a likely Hawthorne effect: the OHI-only control group's periodontal indices improved statistically but not clinically, versus the SRP group's clinically meaningful improvement from actual calculus removal.

## Methodology

Single-center, double-blinded RCT (Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials), Jan 2019–Feb 2020, rheumatology clinic. Inclusion: ACR-diagnosed active SLE (SLEDAI >2), periodontitis stage 2–4/grade B–C, ≥20 teeth. Exclusion: overlapping systemic autoimmune/metabolic disease, smoking, pregnancy, recent radio/chemotherapy, recent periodontal treatment or antibiotics, anticoagulant/cytotoxic drug use, medication changes during the study. Case group: OHI + full-mouth SRP (ultrasonic instrument). Control group: OHI only. Periodontal re-examiner and SLEDAI assessor both blinded to group assignment. Outcomes at baseline and 3 months: PD, CAL, bleeding index, O'Leary plaque index, serum CRP, ESR, SLEDAI. Statistics: SPSS23, Chi-square, independent t-test, Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon signed-rank, P<0.05 significance.

## Results

| Parameter | Case (baseline→3mo) | Control (baseline→3mo) | Between-group P (3mo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SLEDAI | 3.31→2.37 | 3.22→2.46 | 0.499 (diff-in-diff P=0.894) |
| CRP | 2.87→1.64 (within P=0.001) | 4.23→3.66 (within P=0.588, ns) | 0.032 |
| ESR | 20.64→14.77 (within P=0.001) | 20.82→18.6 (within P=0.109, ns) | 0.180 |
| PD (mm) | 4.28→2.17 | 4.15→4.08 | 0.001 |
| CAL (mm) | 3.74→1.71 | 3.97→3.8 | 0.001 |
| Bleeding index | 42.02→14.11 | 41.95→40.55 | 0.001 |
| Plaque index | 63.33→17.44 | 63.68→62.04 | 0.001 |

No significant baseline differences in age, sex, or any periodontal/serologic parameter between groups. Periodontal parameters improved in both groups, but only the case (SRP) group reached clinically meaningful magnitude; control-group changes were statistically significant but clinically negligible, attributed to a Hawthorne effect from oral hygiene instruction and being observed. Elevated CRP in SLE patients is discussed as more likely reflecting concurrent infection (e.g., periodontal) than SLE activity itself, supporting periodontal infection as a driver of the acute-phase-reactant signal independent of SLEDAI.
