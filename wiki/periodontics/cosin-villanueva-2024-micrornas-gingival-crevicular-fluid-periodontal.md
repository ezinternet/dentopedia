## Three-line Summary

PRISMA systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024544648) of 16 case-control studies (3222 records screened; n=18–216 per study) evaluating GCF miRNAs as diagnostic biomarkers for periodontal disease; meta-analysis was not feasible due to high heterogeneity.

Using AUC >0.8 as the acceptability threshold, miR-146a, miR-200b (especially miR-200b-3p), miR-223, and miR-23a show acceptable diagnostic plausibility for periodontitis, while miR-203 (underexpressed, targets VEGFA) does not qualify and is instead a candidate therapeutic target.

Standardized study designs, larger cohorts, and validated expression-unit reporting are needed before any GCF miRNA can be adopted as a clinical periodontal biomarker.

## Summary

Periodontitis is an immunoinflammatory disease in which any change in gene expression can alter inflammatory and immune responses. microRNAs (miRNAs) — short (19–24 nt) non-coding RNAs that repress gene expression post-transcriptionally — are an epigenetic mechanism that is dysregulated in periodontal disease and detectable in biofluids. Among biofluids, **gingival crevicular fluid (GCF)** is the most periodontium-relevant: it flows passively from the gingival sulcus and changes with local inflammatory status, making it a minimally invasive, site-specific sample.

This PRISMA 2020 systematic review (PROSPERO CRD42024544648) asked which GCF miRNAs can serve as diagnostic biomarkers to distinguish periodontal disease from health, and which have the highest diagnostic performance. From 3222 records, **16 case-control studies** were included. The literature was too heterogeneous (different miRNAs, missing numeric data, non-convertible expression units) to permit a meta-analysis, so the synthesis is qualitative.

The recurrently studied and clinically relevant miRNAs are **miR-146a, miR-200b (especially miR-200b-3p), miR-223, miR-23a, and miR-203**. Taking **AUC > 0.8** as the threshold for acceptable diagnostic plausibility, **all of these except miR-203** qualify as candidate diagnostic biomarkers for periodontitis. miR-203 is instead of interest as a protective factor / therapeutic target (it targets VEGFA and is underexpressed in disease).

## Key Contributions

- First GCF-restricted synthesis (excluding serum, saliva, gingival tissue, in-vitro) of miRNAs as **diagnostic** periodontal biomarkers.
- Explicit diagnostic-plausibility ranking by AUC > 0.8: **miR-146a, miR-200b (miR-200b-3p), miR-223, miR-23a acceptable; miR-203 not**.
- Documents direction of dysregulation, correlation with disease severity, normalization after non-surgical therapy, and links to systemic comorbidities (T2DM, CVD, RA).

## Methodology

- **PECO**: humans (P); miRNA expression in GCF (E); healthy individuals (C); diagnosis of periodontal disease (O).
- **Databases** (26 Jan 2024, updated 30 May 2024): PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, Google Scholar — no language/time limits — plus manual reference-list search.
- **Inclusion**: cross-sectional / case-control / cohort / experimental human studies, GCF samples, periodontitis or gingivitis, analyzing miRNAs. **Exclusion**: no control group; in-vitro studies.
- **Flow**: 3222 records → 2091 duplicates removed → 1131 screened → 25 full-text → 9 excluded → **16 included** (all case-control).
- **Quality**: Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (max 9); dual-reviewer with a third as tiebreaker — 13/16 good, 3/16 fair.
- **No meta-analysis** possible; PRISMA items 13, 14, 20, 22, 23b unfulfilled.

## Results

- **Study characteristics**: 16 observational case-control studies; sample sizes 18–216; mean ages 27.8–65.3 y. Two-thirds used the 2018 periodontal classification, one-third the Armitage classification. Most miRNAs were **upregulated** in disease, with some contradictory reports.
- **miR-200b / miR-200b-3p** (most studied with miR-146a): immune-cell differentiation, early inflammation; generally elevated in GCF; even higher with comorbid periodontitis + T2DM or + CVD (Liu 2022: periodontitis 3.06 ± 0.21, T2DM 2.74 ± 0.22, comorbid 3.51 ± 0.30, significant). Saito 2017 reported lower levels (small n).
- **miR-146a**: TLR-signaling/cytokine modulation; upregulated in Almiñana-Pastor 2023 and Radović 2018 (normalized after non-surgical therapy), but **underexpressed** in periodontitis in Rovas 2022 — direction not conclusively settled in GCF; larger studies needed.
- **miR-23a**: inhibits osteogenesis (destructive); increased in mild-stage periodontitis (Costantini 2023) and in GCF + PDLSC cultures (Zhang 2019, decreased after therapy) — possible early bone-metabolism initiator.
- **miR-203**: targets VEGFA, inhibits angiogenesis; **underexpressed** across all 3 studies including it; negative correlation with TNF-α → proposed **protective** role / therapeutic target, not a diagnostic marker.
- **miR-223**: innate-immunity and osteoclast-differentiation regulator; upregulated in GCF (higher with concomitant DM); positive severity correlation in one study, none in Costantini 2023.
- **Diagnostic performance**: with **AUC > 0.8** as the acceptability threshold, miRNAs qualifying include miR-146a, miR-23a, miR-28-5p, miR-155, miR-125b-1-3p, miR-200c-3p, miR-200b, miR-30b-3p, miR-200a-5p, miR-1226, miR-200a-3p, miR-200b-5p, miR-223, miR-223-5p, miR-200c-5p, miR-200b-3p.
- **Conclusion**: miR-146a, miR-200b (miR-200b-3p), miR-223, and miR-23a have acceptable diagnostic plausibility for periodontitis; **miR-203 does not** (protective/therapeutic-target candidate). Heterogeneity blocks quantitative synthesis; standardized, homogeneous studies are needed.
- **Caveats**: only 3 studies included early (stage I) periodontitis; no study compared miRNA across periodontitis grades; only 2 measured miRNA after periodontal treatment; only 1 used high-throughput sequencing.
