## Summary
This 2013 perspective article in *Wound Repair and Regeneration* reviews why oral mucosal wounds heal faster and with minimal scarring compared with dermal (skin) wounds, which are prone to fibrotic scar formation from an imbalance between extracellular matrix (ECM, 세포외기질) synthesis and degradation. Drawing on the comparative literature, the authors describe oral wounds as containing fewer immune mediators, fewer blood vessels, and fewer profibrotic mediators than dermal wounds, while showing more bone marrow-derived cell recruitment, a higher reepithelialization rate, and faster fibroblast proliferation. ECM composition also differs, notably in procollagen I and tenascin-C (테나신-C) expression. The review does not present new primary data; it is a synthesis intended to point toward mechanisms — spanning ECM, immune cells, growth factors, and fibroblast phenotype — that could inform future antifibrotic strategies for dermal scarring. The exact mechanisms of scarless oral healing are explicitly noted as unresolved.

## Key Contributions
- Consolidates dermal-vs-oral-mucosal wound-healing differences into a single comparative framework (ECM, immune cells, vasculature, fibroblasts, bone marrow-derived cells).
- Positions bone-marrow-derived cell recruitment as a notable, less-commonly-discussed contributor to scarless oral healing.
- Proposes scarless oral mucosal healing as a translational model for developing therapies to prevent dermal fibrotic scarring.
