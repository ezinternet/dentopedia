## Three-line Summary

Retrospective case series (single periodontist, single Korean clinic, 2019–2020; 63 of 83 screened patients) of acute periodontal abscesses at molars (PD ≥6 mm, pus, bone loss) treated with drainage plus one of three adjuncts chosen by patient preference: saline irrigation alone (control, n=20), 2% minocycline ointment (TM, n=21), or 300 mg/mL doxycycline solution (TD, n=22), repeated at 1 week and followed to 4 weeks.

At 4 weeks, abscess-side probing depth (PD) fell 1.09 mm (control), 1.88 mm (TM) and 2.88 mm (TD), and abscess-side bleeding on probing (BOP) fell 45.00%, 73.02% and 95.45%; both antibiotic groups beat control (TM vs control PD P=0.024, BOP P=0.009) and TD beat TM (PD P<0.001, BOP P=0.031), while plaque, recession and tooth mobility (Periotest value, PTV) did not differ between groups.

Allocation was by patient choice with no randomization or blinding, one examiner, 4-week follow-up and no microbiology, so the ranking (and especially the doxycycline-over-minocycline claim, from a compounded non-commercial solution) is hypothesis-generating; all three arms improved, including saline-only.

## Summary

This retrospective case series from a single Korean clinic compared three adjuncts to drainage in 63 patients with acute periodontal abscesses on molars: saline irrigation alone, 2% minocycline ointment, and 300 mg/mL doxycycline solution, each applied at baseline and again at 1 week. By 4 weeks all groups had improved, but abscess-side PD fell 1.09, 1.88 and 2.88 mm and abscess-side BOP fell 45.00%, 73.02% and 95.45% in the control, minocycline and doxycycline groups. Both local tetracycline-class adjuncts therefore did better than drainage plus saline, and doxycycline did better than minocycline. Because patients chose their own treatment, there was no randomization or blinding, follow-up was only 4 weeks, and the doxycycline preparation was a compounded solution, the findings suggest a benefit of local adjunct therapy but do not establish a drug ranking.

## Key Contributions

- Only head-to-head comparison in the wiki of 2% minocycline ointment vs local doxycycline vs drainage alone in acute periodontal abscess.
- Quantifies abscess-specific outcomes: abscess-side PD reduction 2.88 / 1.88 / 1.09 mm and BOP reduction 95.45 / 73.02 / 45.00% (TD / TM / control) over 4 weeks.
- Shows that PI, GR and PTV did not differ between groups, so differences in PD/BOP/CAL are not explained by plaque or recession.

## Methodology

Retrospective case series (STROBE), Jan 2019-Dec 2020, one periodontist, 63 of 83 patients eligible; groups: control n=20, 2% minocycline ointment (TM) n=21, 300 mg/mL doxycycline (TD) n=22, assigned by patient preference. Afebrile patients with a molar abscess (PD ≥6 mm, pus, bone loss); smokers, uncontrolled systemic disease and antibiotic exposure within 1 month excluded. Drainage under local anesthesia at visit 1; adjunct applied at visits 1 and 2 (1 week; full-mouth root planing at visit 2); outcomes at 4 weeks. Primary outcome PD; secondary BOP, plaque index, recession, CAL, Periotest value. Patient-level repeated-measures ANOVA and linear mixed model, Bonferroni post hoc.

## Results

| Outcome (baseline to 4 weeks) | Control | 2% minocycline ointment | Doxycycline 300 mg/mL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abscess-side PD reduction (mm) | 1.09 | 1.88 | 2.88 |
| Abscess-side BOP reduction (%) | 45.00 | 73.02 | 95.45 |
| Whole-tooth mean PD (mm) | 6.97 to 6.22 | 6.66 to 5.41 | 7.03 to 5.29 |
| Whole-tooth mean BOP (%) | 80.83 to 39.17 | 83.33 to 20.63 | 78.79 to 6.82 |
| Mean CAL gain (mm) | 0.40 | 0.86 | 1.31 |
| Deepest-site PD (mm) | 9.99 to 8.35 | 9.47 to 7.34 | 10.13 to 6.90 |
| Periotest value | 28.28 to 18.50 | 27.04 to 17.74 | 25.25 to 13.57 |

- Abscess-side PD (linear mixed model): control vs TM P=0.024; control vs TD P<0.001; TD vs TM P<0.001. Abscess-side BOP: control vs TM P=0.009; control vs TD P<0.001; TD vs TM P=0.031.
- CAL gain: TD vs control P<0.001, TD vs TM P=0.002. No significant between-group differences in plaque index, recession or Periotest change.
- Deepest-site PD change (the per-tooth deepest site): TM vs control was not significant in either period (visit 1-2 P=0.528; visit 2-3 P=0.488), whereas TD vs control was (P<0.001 and P=0.001); the minocycline advantage over saline is therefore shown on abscess-side PD/BOP and CAL, not on deepest-site PD.
- Residual mild pain at 1 week: 5 (control), 3 (TM), 4 (TD) patients; none at 4 weeks.

## Limitations and Interpretation

- Non-random allocation by patient choice, single clinician-examiner, no blinding, 4-week follow-up, no microbiology.
- Doxycycline arm was a clinic-compounded solution (100 mg capsule powder dissolved in saline, stated as 300 mg/mL) applied as a 5-minute irrigation; minocycline was a 2% ointment left in the pocket. Vehicle, contact time and dose differ, so this is not a clean drug-vs-drug comparison, and the compounded preparation is not an available product equivalent.
- All three arms improved significantly, including saline-only; most patients had generalized stage 3 periodontitis and received full-mouth root planing at 1 week, which contributes to the 4-week gains in every arm.
- No harms analysis was reported beyond pain; the paper's statement that systemic effects are minimal is an inference from other studies, not measured here.
- Evidence for the minocycline ointment in this indication comes from this single retrospective study and one earlier study cited in its discussion (Eguchi), so certainty is low.
