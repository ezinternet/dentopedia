## Three-line Summary
In vitro study (Folia Biologica, Kraków 2005) comparing primary human keratinocytes isolated from skin (epidermis) and buccal (oral) mucosa biopsies from 12 healthy donors, assessing morphology, motility, and proliferation under identical culture conditions.
Oral mucosa keratinocytes migrated ~12% faster (1.73 vs 1.55 μm/min), moved in straighter trajectories (coefficient of dislocation efficiency 0.55 vs 0.36), and grew to 20% higher density by 24h than skin keratinocytes; cell size/shape parameters did not differ significantly.
Fibroblast-conditioned medium suppressed oral mucosal keratinocyte migration and proliferation back toward unsupplemented-medium levels, pointing to paracrine fibroblast regulation; supports using cultured autologous oral mucosal keratinocytes as an alternative cell source for skin wound/burn grafting.

## 세줄요약
Folia Biologica(Kraków, 2005)의 체외(in vitro) 연구로, 건강한 공여자 12명의 피부(표피)와 구강(협측) 점막 생검에서 분리한 1차 배양 각질세포(케라티노사이트, Keratinocyte)를 동일 조건에서 형태·이동성·증식능으로 비교했다.
구강점막 각질세포는 피부 각질세포보다 이동속도가 약 12% 빨랐고(1.73 vs 1.55 μm/min), 이동경로가 더 직선적이었으며(전위효율계수, CDE 0.55 vs 0.36), 24시간째 세포밀도가 20% 더 높았다. 세포 크기·형태는 두 유형 간 유의차가 없었다.
섬유아세포(Fibroblast) 조건배지가 구강점막 각질세포의 이동성·증식을 무보충 배지 수준으로 억제해 방사성(Paracrine) 조절 가능성을 시사하며, 피부 생검이 어렵거나 신속한 세포 확장이 필요한 화상·피부창상 이식에서 구강점막 각질세포가 대체 세포원이 될 수 있음을 뒷받침한다.

## Summary
This in-vitro study directly compared primary human keratinocytes isolated from skin and oral (buccal) mucosa biopsies of 12 healthy donors, using computer-aided image and time-lapse motility analysis under identical culture conditions. Oral mucosa keratinocytes proliferated faster (20% higher density at 24h) and migrated faster and more directionally (higher trajectory speed and coefficient of dislocation efficiency) than skin keratinocytes, despite showing no significant differences in cell size or shape. Fibroblast-conditioned medium suppressed oral mucosal keratinocyte motility and growth toward unsupplemented-medium levels, implicating a paracrine fibroblast-keratinocyte regulatory loop. The findings give a cellular-biology basis for the well-recognized clinical observation that oral wound healing is faster and less scar-prone than skin healing, and support the use of expanded autologous oral mucosal keratinocytes as a graft cell source for cutaneous wound/burn repair when skin donor sites are limited.

## Key Contributions
- Direct, same-protocol comparison of skin vs oral mucosa keratinocyte proliferation, morphology, and quantified motility (trajectory tracking, not just endpoint migration).
- Actin cytoskeleton organization differs by tissue origin (peripheral F-actin accumulation in fast-moving oral keratinocytes vs central lamellar bundles in skin keratinocytes), giving a structural correlate to the motility difference.
- Fibroblast-conditioned medium experiment shows paracrine suppression of oral keratinocyte motility/proliferation, a mechanistic clue relevant to epithelial-mesenchymal crosstalk in oral wound healing.

## Methodology
In-vitro experimental design; keratinocytes isolated from skin (n=6 donors) and buccal mucosa (n=6 donors) biopsies, primary and secondary culture in serum-free keratinocyte medium. Motility assessed via time-lapse imaging (3-min intervals, 120 min, 100 cell tracks/condition) computing trajectory length, net displacement, average speed, and coefficient of dislocation efficiency (CDE). Proliferation measured by cell counts at 24/48/72h. Morphology quantified via CYTOCYCLE 2.0 image analysis (surface area, extension, dispersion, elongation). F-actin visualized with TRITC-phalloidin. Oral mucosa fibroblast-conditioned medium tested for paracrine effects on oral keratinocyte behavior.

## Results
| Outcome | Skin keratinocytes | Oral mucosa keratinocytes |
|---|---|---|
| Average migration speed | 1.55 ± 0.04 μm/min | 1.73 ± 0.04 μm/min* |
| Coefficient of dislocation efficiency (CDE) | 0.36 ± 0.02 | 0.55 ± 0.02* |
| Total final displacement | 67.99 ± 4.48 μm | 113.58 ± 6.23 μm* |
| Cell density growth (24h) | baseline | ~20% higher |
| Mean cell surface area | 1639 ± 472 μm² | 1618 ± 481 μm² (NS) |

*p<0.05 vs skin keratinocytes. Fibroblast-conditioned medium reduced oral mucosal keratinocyte speed to 1.24 ± 0.03 μm/min and CDE to 0.26 ± 0.01 (both p<0.05 vs oral mucosa control medium).
